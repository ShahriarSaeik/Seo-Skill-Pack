## ARTICLE bangladesh-local-seo

### Local SEO in Bangladesh: Practical Notes

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
The local SEO fundamentals (Google Business Profile, reviews, consistent NAP, a good website) work in Bangladesh too, with a few local twists: people search in English, Bangla and transliterated Bangla; addresses are informal, so the map pin matters more; Facebook pages and marketplace/delivery listings act as important citations; and reviews are easiest to collect through Messenger and WhatsApp.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Research keywords three ways.** For each service, check autocomplete for the English term, the Bangla term and the transliteration (e.g., "saree shop Dhanmondi", "শাড়ির দোকান ধানমন্ডি", "sharee dokan"). Use the most common form in your page titles and headings, and cover the others naturally in body text and FAQs.
2. **Fix the address and pin.** Write the address in one consistent format everywhere (House, Road, Block/Sector, Area, City, postcode), and drag the map pin to the exact entrance. Add a short landmark note in your website's contact section ("opposite X mosque") for customers; keep the GBP address field itself clean.
3. **Business name: match your signboard.** If your signboard is in Bangla and English, use the name customers know. Don't add keywords ("Best Biryani House Dhaka").
4. **Treat key platforms as citations.** Keep the same name, address, phone (+880 format) and hours on: your Facebook page, Instagram, food delivery apps for restaurants, online marketplaces for shops, and relevant local directories. Link back to your website where allowed.
5. **Collect reviews where customers already talk to you.** Send your direct Google review link via Messenger/WhatsApp/SMS after delivery or a visit. Ask everyone, not only happy customers, and reply to every review (Bangla or English, matching the reviewer).
6. **Make the website locally useful.** One page per service and per branch, with real photos, prices or price ranges, delivery areas, accepted payment methods (cash, cards, mobile wallets), and a click-to-call / WhatsApp button.
7. **Post updates on your Business Profile** for offers and events (e.g., Eid, Pohela Boishakh, winter collections), 4–8 weeks before seasonal demand.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Keyword list in English + Bangla + transliteration for each service
- [ ] One consistent address format; pin on the exact entrance
- [ ] Business name matches signboard, no keywords
- [ ] Same NAP and hours on Facebook, Instagram, delivery apps/marketplaces and directories
- [ ] Review link shared via Messenger/WhatsApp after every order or visit
- [ ] Replies to all reviews in the reviewer's language
- [ ] Service and branch pages with photos, delivery areas and payment methods
- [ ] Seasonal Business Profile posts planned before Eid, Pohela Boishakh and winter

## Data points & benchmarks
- No reliable public benchmark exists for Bangladesh local pack behaviour; test your own target searches from the target area and track them monthly.
- The same Google local ranking factors (relevance, distance, prominence) apply worldwide.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Relying only on a Facebook page with no Google Business Profile or website
- Different spellings of the business name or address across platforms
- Asking only for 5-star reviews, offering discounts for reviews, or buying reviews
- Keyword-stuffed business names to rank in other areas
- Targeting only English keywords when customers search in Bangla

## Key principles
- Global fundamentals, local habits: meet customers in the language and channels they use.
- Consistency beats volume: one accurate identity everywhere.
- Reviews are a habit, not a campaign.

## ARTICLE franchise-seo

### Franchise SEO: Local and National Growth Strategies for Franchises

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Franchise SEO is NOT local SEO multiplied by locations — it's a two-level strategy: hyper-local visibility for each franchisee (NAP, location pages, business profiles, social profiles, directories) and national brand visibility (scaled keyword research, site structure choice, regional content hubs, per-region tracking). The biggest wins come from treating each location as a "micro-brand" while leveraging central authority.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Local level (per franchise):**
1. **Consistent NAP** — business name = "[Brand] [Location]" (e.g., "[Brand] Arlington") only if that is how the location is genuinely named and signed — never add keywords or places just to rank; use a real, staffed address (virtual offices and PO boxes aren't allowed on Google Business Profile), not the franchisee's home; use call-tracking local numbers that forward to franchisee mobiles
2. **Dedicated landing page per franchise** — template + unique elements: localized H1/description, location contact details, schedule/booking, local team photos, social proof
3. **Business profiles on Google, Bing, Apple** — fill everything; link to the LOCATION page, never the homepage
4. **Social profiles per location** — interlink location socials ↔ location landing page (builds micro-brand authority)
5. **Local directory listings per location** — find via a link-intersect analysis (competitor location pages); keep everything hyper-local (use your country's relevant directories)

**National level (whole brand):**
1. **Scale keyword research** — run bulk keyword research on your service keywords in a keyword research tool; filter for all suburbs/cities/states/ZIPs/abbreviations (match any of those words); segment by location
2. **Choose site structure:**
   - Option 1: One website, one page per franchise (simple, leverages brand authority, weak for non-branded keywords) — /location/franchise-1
   - Option 2: One website, subfolder hub per franchise — /new-york/plumbing (best local+national balance; risk of duplication) ← recommended
   - Option 3: Separate website/subdomain per location (max franchisee control, max technical overhead) — no inherent SEO benefit, only choose for non-SEO reasons
3. **Create regional content + internal links** — city/state pages listing all locations in the region; link region→franchises, franchises→region, cross-link nearby regions
4. **Track per-region performance** — separate projects or groups per region, a rank tracking tool with region tags (tag competitors for share of voice), or API data in custom dashboards

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Standardize naming: Brand + Location across all listings
- [ ] Set up call tracking via dedicated software: public local numbers → forward to franchisees; connect to analytics
- [ ] Build location page template: localized H1, conversion elements, map, premises/staff photos, services, location-specific reviews
- [ ] Create GBP + Bing Places + Apple Business Connect per location; link to location page
- [ ] Create per-location social profiles where allowed; interlink with location pages only
- [ ] Run a link-intersect analysis on competitor location pages for directory opportunities; submit hyper-local details
- [ ] Identify country-relative search patterns (e.g., US: "mold remediation"; AU: "mold removal") as national baseline
- [ ] Bulk keyword research: services list + location filter (suburbs, cities, states, ZIPs, abbreviations)
- [ ] Segment keywords by location AND service; map to correct pages
- [ ] Pick site structure (recommended: subfolder hubs per location, only where unique content/E-E-A-T is possible)
- [ ] Build regional hub pages for any city with ≥1 franchise + search volume
- [ ] Programmatically internal-link: region ↔ franchisees ↔ nearby regions
- [ ] Track by region: separate projects (small), a rank tracking tool with tags + competitor share of voice (large), API (custom dashboards)
- [ ] Diagnose duplicate content with a site crawler's duplicate-content check
- [ ] Distribute a brand guide; consider headless CMS for franchisee edits; centralize marketing governance

## Data points & benchmarks
- One fitness franchise's location pages (template + unique elements) reportedly grew to ~45,000 monthly visits in ~10 months (one case, not a benchmark)
- "Mold remediation" vs "mold removal": US prefers "remediation"; AU searches "removal" ~10x more than "remediation" — relative search patterns differ by country
- One study found no discernible SEO difference between subdomains and subfolders — choose based on CMS/operations

## Mistakes to avoid
- Using franchisee personal details (mobile, home address) — ownership changes break NAP consistency
- Linking all business listings/socials to the national homepage — dilutes local relevance
- Mass-producing templated location pages without unique content — duplicate content/doorway page risk
- Spinning the same content across location hubs
- Only creating pages for exact suburbs — missing city/state-level keyword demand
- Giving every franchisee their own website "for SEO" without operational justification — technical debt
- Locking franchisees out of marketing entirely — hurts national visibility; govern instead with brand guides
- Quoting franchise SEO work before understanding the custom CMS's limitations

## Key principles
- Franchise SEO operates at two levels: hyper-local micro-brands + national brand authority.
- Business listings and social profiles should form a per-location link network pointing at the location page.
- Granular location keywords still matter — for Ads/SEO unification, accurate local reporting, and untapped regional opportunities.
- Structure choice is a business/CMS decision first; SEO benefit between subfolder and subdomain is negligible.
- Centralize governance (brand guides, headless CMS, shared agency) rather than restricting franchisees.

## ARTICLE gbp-listing-not-showing

### Google Business Profile Not Showing on Maps? A Troubleshooting Checklist

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Most "we're not on Google Maps" problems are one of six things: no profile, an unverified profile, a suspended profile, a duplicate, a weak category/name match for the search, or simple distance (Maps favours businesses near the searcher). Check them in that order. Fix the cheap ones today; relevance and reviews take weeks.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Does a profile exist?** Search Google Maps for your exact business name and street. If nothing appears, create one at business.google.com (or claim the existing one if it shows "Own this business?").
2. **Is it verified?** In the Business Profile dashboard, look for a "Verify" or "Get verified" prompt. Google chooses the method (video recording, phone/SMS, email or postcard) per business. Unverified profiles rarely show for non-brand searches.
3. **Is it suspended or limited?** A suspension notice appears in the dashboard. Common causes: keywords added to the business name, a virtual office or address customers can't visit, a service-area business showing a home address, the wrong category, or several profiles for one location. Fix the cause, then submit a reinstatement request with proof (shop-front photo with signage, trade licence, utility bill).
4. **Is there a duplicate?** Search Maps for variations of your name and phone. Duplicates split reviews and can suppress both listings. Report the duplicate (or ask support to merge it) instead of creating another profile.
5. **Do you match the search?** Search the exact phrase customers use (e.g., "restaurant in Gulshan"). Check: the primary category is the most specific one (e.g., "Thai restaurant" rather than "Restaurant"), the business name is your real-world name, hours are set, and your website mentions the service and area.
6. **Is it distance?** Maps results change with the searcher's location. Test from (or set the map to) the area you serve. A business 5 km away often won't show for "near me" searches in another neighbourhood, and that's normal.
7. **Then build prominence:** steady genuine reviews, photos, consistent name/address/phone (NAP) across directories and social profiles, and local links.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Profile exists and is claimed by the owner's Google account (add managers, never share the password)
- [ ] Verified (dashboard shows no verification prompt)
- [ ] No suspension notice; name has no added keywords
- [ ] One profile per real location; duplicates reported
- [ ] Most specific primary category + relevant secondary categories
- [ ] Map pin placed exactly on the entrance
- [ ] Hours, phone, website and photos complete
- [ ] Tested the target search from inside the service area
- [ ] Review request routine in place (direct review link)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Google's local ranking is based on relevance, distance and prominence (Google's own documentation); distance can't be "optimized" except by having a real location near searchers.
- Newly verified profiles often take days to a few weeks to show consistently for non-brand searches.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Creating a new profile because the old one "disappeared" (creates duplicates and can trigger suspension)
- Adding city or service keywords to the business name
- Using a virtual office or a friend's address to appear in another area
- Buying or gating reviews (violates Google's policies; can remove reviews or the profile)
- Judging rankings only from your own phone at home

## Key principles
- Check eligibility (exists, verified, not suspended, not duplicated) before working on rankings.
- Relevance is in your control; distance isn't; prominence is earned over time.
- Honesty is the fastest route back from a suspension: fix the real cause and provide proof.

## ARTICLE google-maps-seo

### Google Maps SEO: 5 Proven Strategies to Rank Higher

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Google Maps rankings are driven by three factors — relevance, distance, and prominence — and you can influence two of them. The playbook: fully optimize your Google Business Profile, generate reviews consistently, optimize your website for local keywords, build citations + local backlinks, and monitor your profile for sabotage.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Google ranks local businesses on:
1. **Relevance** — how well the listing matches the query
2. **Distance** — proximity to searcher (or specified location)
3. **Prominence** — how well-known/trusted the business is (reviews, citations, online presence)

The 5 strategies:
1. **Perfect your GBP setup** — claim/verify, pick the most specific primary category, show your address only if customers visit you there (Google's rule — service-area businesses that work from home must hide it; hybrids show it), set accurate hours, complete every field, write an accurate description that uses natural keywords, add services, upload 20-30+ photos, seed your own Q&A only if your profile still shows it
2. **Build trust through reviews** — generate consistently (not in bursts), respond to every review personally within 24-48h
3. **Optimize your website for local search** — local keywords in title/H1/H2, dedicated service pages, LocalBusiness schema, embedded Google Map
4. **Strengthen off-site presence** — consistent-NAP citations, local backlinks (chambers, sponsorships, data-driven local PR), "best of" list placements (links gained through paid sponsorships or other value exchange should use rel="sponsored" or nofollow)
5. **Maintain and monitor rankings** — watch for unauthorized GBP edits, keep NAP accurate across locations

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Claim and verify your Google Business Profile (postcard verification takes 5-14 days)
- [ ] Choose the most specific primary category + 2-3 secondary categories
- [ ] Storefront/hybrid: show your address and verify the map pin is exact. Service-area business without customer visits: hide the address and set accurate service areas (required by Google's guidelines)
- [ ] Set regular hours for every day + special holiday hours; avoid "hours might differ" labels
- [ ] Complete every GBP field: local-area phone (not toll-free), URL, 750-char description, attributes, opening date, service areas
- [ ] Write keyword-rich description with natural local/service keywords (no stuffing)
- [ ] Add predefined services (dine-in, takeout, delivery, catering, etc.)
- [ ] Upload 20-30 photos minimum: 5+ exterior, 5+ interior, 8-10 product, 2-3 team
- [ ] If your profile still has Q&A (Google has been phasing it out), seed it with thorough owner answers
- [ ] Generate 2-3 reviews per week via QR codes on tables/receipts; train staff to ask (never offer incentives, write fake reviews, or filter out unhappy customers)
- [ ] Respond to every review (positive and negative) within 24-48h, personally, no templates
- [ ] Title tags + H1/H2 with local keywords; NAP on site matches GBP exactly
- [ ] Create dedicated service pages (400-800 words unique content, photos, target keyword + location)
- [ ] Add LocalBusiness schema (name, address, phone, hours, price range, geo-coordinates); validate with Rich Results Test
- [ ] Embed a Google Map of your location on the site
- [ ] Build citations on Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, TripAdvisor, Foursquare + industry/local directories with identical NAP (punctuation matters) — US examples; use your country's equivalents
- [ ] Join chamber of commerce; sponsor local events/teams/charities; partner with complementary businesses
- [ ] Pitch local news with data-led stories (e.g., public data analysis relevant to locals)
- [ ] Find "best of" lists via link-intersect style analysis (sites linking to competitors but not you) and pitch inclusion on merit (disclose any free product or paid placement)
- [ ] Monitor GBP for suggested edits/sabotage, especially across multiple locations

## Data points & benchmarks
- Primary GBP category is the **#1 ranking factor** for Google Maps (2026 local search ranking factors survey)
- ~90% of consumers use Google Maps; 46% of all Google searches have local intent (older industry statistics)
- Only 7.9% of local searches triggered an AI Overview in one industry study — local search appears less disrupted by AI so far
- "Quality/Authority of Unstructured Citations" is the **#4 ranking factor for AI/LLM visibility** (same survey)
- Review sweet spots: 2-3 reviews/week consistently, 4.5+ star rating
- Review response time: within 24-48 hours
- Photo benchmark: 20-30 minimum; description max 750 characters
- Expected effort: 10-15 hours upfront setup, then 3-5 hours/week ongoing
- Google prioritizes businesses open at search time — an open competitor outranks a closed one at 8 PM

## Mistakes to avoid
- Keyword stuffing your business name ("Joe's Pizza | Best Pizza Canary Wharf...") — guideline violation, suspension risk
- Choosing unrelated categories — "category confusion" is a listed suspension risk factor
- Showing a home address customers can't visit (guideline violation → suspension risk). Note: proximity to the searcher still matters, so a real, staffed location in the target city helps — but only if customers can actually visit it
- Inconsistent NAP across GBP/website/third parties ("123 Main Street" vs "123 Main St") — triggers "hours might differ" and distrust
- Review bursts (20 in one week then nothing) — consistency beats volume
- Template review responses — personal replies convert better
- Leaving Q&A unmanaged where it still exists — competitors can answer questions on your listing

## Key principles
- "Just having a Google Business Profile doesn't mean Google will rank you."
- Proximity is only one factor — relevance and prominence are winnable.
- Photos that match target keywords perform better (e.g., wood-fired oven shots for "wood-fired pizza").
- Local has so far been less affected by AI Overviews than other verticals — but citations now also drive LLM/AI visibility.
- "Good SEO is just good marketing. Build a great business, serve your customers well, and the rankings will follow."

### How to Optimize Your Google Business Profile in 30 Minutes

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A fully optimized Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) improves local rankings and visibility — and takes ~30 minutes. Work through 14 settings: claim the profile, pick the most specific category, set business type + service areas, hours (incl. holiday), contact details, photos, products/services, reviews, Q&A (only if still shown), posts, messages (discontinued — use other contact paths), and mirror everything on your website.

## Framework / Step-by-step
14-step optimization order:
1. **Create or claim your Business Profile** — business name must be the real-world name (no keyword stuffing — suspension risk)
2. **Set business category** — categories describe what you ARE, not what you sell. Choose the most specific valid option (e.g., "chartered accountant" > "accountant") — specificity wins broader queries too. Add secondary categories only for genuinely different services (bookkeeping, payroll)
3. **Choose business type** — storefront (set address, no service area), service-area (set up to 20 service areas, clear/hide address), hybrid (both; keep address visible)
4. **Add business hours** — add split rows for lunch/dinner closings
5. **Set holiday hours** — confirm special hours even if unchanged, or Google shows a warning on holidays
6. **Add contact details** — website + phone; at least one is mandatory
7. **Add photos** — logo, cover, 3+ exterior, 3+ interior, 3+ product, 3+ at-work, 3+ team; name files descriptively
8. **Add products and services** — full details (name, price, category, description, link); copy from your website (no duplicate-content issue)
9. **Get more reviews** — ask happy customers, share the review link, print "leave a review" cards with QR codes, put link in email signature. No incentives (ToS violation)
10. **Answer questions** — if your profile still has Q&A, enable notifications and seed FAQs from your business account so answers are tagged as from the owner (legacy: Google has been phasing out GBP Q&A — do this only if your profile still shows a Q&A section). Either way, put FAQ answers on your website, where AI and Google can read them
11. **Post updates** — offers/events with precise, timely headlines; only post when you have something meaningful
12. **Messages (discontinued)** — Google removed GBP chat/messaging in July 2024; make phone, booking link and website the contact paths instead
13. **Fill everything else in** — menus, booking links, attributes
14. **Optimize your website** — Google pulls services from your site into profile matching; mention all products/services searchers look for

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Claim existing profile (don't create duplicates) via google.com/business
- [ ] Use real business name only — no keyword additions
- [ ] Pick most specific primary category; add secondary only for distinct offerings
- [ ] Service-area business: set 1-2 accurate service areas (within ~2 hours' drive), clear address
- [ ] Add regular hours with split shifts; confirm all holiday/special hours
- [ ] Add website + local phone
- [ ] Upload logo, cover photo, 3+ each: exterior (multiple angles/times of day), interior, product, at-work, team photos; use descriptive filenames
- [ ] List products/services with full details + links to site pages
- [ ] Generate reviews: ask in person, share review link, review cards with QR, email signature link
- [ ] If Q&A is still available on your profile: turn on notifications; pre-answer FAQs from the owner account
- [ ] Publish posts only when meaningful: specific offers ("50% off all drinks 5-6 PM Saturdays"), good photos, timely
- [ ] Make sure call, booking and website buttons work (GBP messaging was discontinued in 2024)

### How to Do Local Keyword Research

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Local keyword research starts from services, not keywords: list everything the business offers, verify local intent in the SERPs, check volumes only to prioritize page creation, map keywords to dedicated service pages, and mine top-ranking competitors for "things to mention." ## Framework / Step-by-step

4. **Group and map keywords to URLs** — one page per service usually; decide on dedicated vs. combined pages by checking what the current top-ranking pages do (if #4 result is a page specifically about "oil boiler installation," make a dedicated page)
5. **Look for 'things to mention'** — reverse-engineer ranking pages for sub-topics: areas served, boiler types, fixed pricing, emergency availability, accreditations, brands; answer common questions (People Also Ask, question keywords)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Document every service offered (brainstorm + client interview)
- [ ] Run services through a keyword research tool's same-terms/related keyword ideas to find missed services
- [ ] Check competitors' top pages + navigation for service ideas
- [ ] Google each service; keep only keywords with map packs/local organic results
- [ ] Use a SERP preview tool (location set) when researching from elsewhere
- [ ] Get implicit-keyword volumes from a free keyword tool, with your service area as location
- [ ] Convert non-local-intent keywords to explicit (add location modifier)
- [ ] SERP-check each service: dedicated page needed only if top rankers use dedicated pages
- [ ] Rule of thumb: could you cover the sub-service fully on the broader page? Then no new page
- [ ] List a top-ranking local page's top-10 keywords in an SEO tool → note areas served, service variants, pricing/emergency terms to mention
- [ ] Eyeball top pages manually for trust markers (e.g., UK examples: Gas Safe, City & Guilds; use your country's equivalents; brands serviced)
- [ ] Use related-term ideas in a keyword research tool for supporting phrases
- [ ] Answer People Also Ask / question-keyword queries on the page
- [ ] Multiple locations: few = list on contact page; many = dedicated location landing pages
- [ ] Blog posts: mostly no local intent — write for visitors, not rankings
- [ ] Track local rankings in a rank tracking tool with locale (city/state/ZIP) set

## Data points & benchmarks
- Most people search implicitly ("plumber") not explicitly ("plumber in New York City") — Google personalizes by location
- Even the largest keyword databases (billions of keywords) miss many local keywords — they're too rare to register
- "Nine times out of ten," create the service page even if volume is near zero — it serves visitors arriving via broad queries
- Blog-post keywords rarely have local intent: ~99% of visitors from a ranking "how much does a plumber cost" post would be out of area

## Mistakes to avoid
- Guessing keywords instead of researching them — leads to targeting the wrong terms and neglecting profitable services
- Thinking in job titles rather than discrete services
- Worrying too much about search volume — low/no volume still justifies a page
- Keyword stuffing location + service variants into copy — unreadable; mention things naturally instead
- Creating dedicated pages for sub-services that top-ranking pages cover on one broader page
- Chasing blog-post keywords for a local business — traffic won't convert across geographies
- Skipping the local-intent check — brand names ("worcester boiler") can masquerade as locations

## Key principles
- Services first, keywords second — keyword lists fall out of service lists.
- SERPs are the source of truth: local intent, dedicated-page decisions, and "things to mention" all come from analyzing what already ranks.
- Search volume is a prioritization signal, not a go/no-go gate.
- "Things to mention" = topical relevance + long-tail rankings + visitor trust (pricing, areas, accreditations, brands).
- Keywords are a proxy for customer questions — answer them whether or not they drive rankings.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Local newspapers will link to almost any story showing how their area ranks on something — good or bad. The "tabloid technique" is a 3-step digital PR play: find a newsworthy local-comparison topic, pull local data (government databases, FOI requests, or your own research), and pitch it to local journalists in the areas your data flatters (or offends).

   - Brainstorm volume: get the whole team involved, write down every idea, even bad ones; use an AI assistant as an idea partner
2. **Pull local data**
   - Existing sources: government databases (usa.gov, Data.gov, UK Data Service, Eurostat, Statistics Canada, World Bank Open Data, UN Data, Pew Research, FBI Crime Data Explorer, CDC)
   - Merge multiple sources for bigger stories (e.g., lifetime tax by US state)
   - Or source your own via freedom of information (FOI) requests
   - Publish on a journalist-friendly page: all data, sources, methodology, and visual assets they can reuse
3. **Send it to local journalists**
   - Target journalists in the specific places your data highlights
   - Find them: search `[place name] newspaper`, check staff/journalist pages, get emails from profiles
   - If email not listed, use email lookup tools
   - Identify active journalists: check bylines for articles published in the last 30 days (a content research tool can list authors by recent articles)
