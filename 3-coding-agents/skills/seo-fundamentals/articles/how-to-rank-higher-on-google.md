# How to Rank Higher on Google (10 Steps)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Ranking improvements should be methodical: start with easy, controllable wins (intent matching, content completeness, on-page basics, internal links) and only then move to harder work like link building. The fastest gains usually come from pages already ranking on page one or two.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Choose a keyword** — find pages ranking in positions 2–10 (check your Search Console or a rank tracking tool); improving near-misses is easiest. Prefer the page's primary target keyword, not incidental long-tails. No page-one rankings? Work on page 2–3 keywords.
2. **Match search intent** — audit top-ranking pages for the 3 C's: content type, content format, content angle. Case study: a product-led page stuck at #6–10 for "backlink checker" jumped to #1 almost overnight after adding a free tool, matching what the SERP actually rewarded.
3. **Cover the topic in full** — find subtopics searchers expect: eyeball top-ranking pages' subheadings for commonalities, and find keywords competitors rank for that you don't (a content-gap report in any SEO tool).
4. **Update the page** — freshness is a query-dependent ranking factor. Check dates on top-ranking pages: "best headphones" results are all fresh; "best parks in london" results aren't. If your keyword rewards freshness, refresh content (rankings and traffic often improve after updates).
5. **Improve on-page SEO** — H1–H6 hierarchical structure; short descriptive URLs; compelling title tag and meta description; descriptive image filenames/alt text plus compression; polished, readable copy.
6. **Add internal links** — internal links flow PageRank and signal importance. Find mentions of keywords your target page ranks for on other pages of your site, and link them to the target (via a site audit tool or a `site:domain.com "[keyword]"` search).
7. **Merge similar pages** — multiple similar pages split backlinks and authority. Redirect weaker duplicates into the strongest page to consolidate link equity. Only merge pages serving the same intent.
8. **Build more backlinks** — the hardest, most powerful lever. Start by reviewing a competing page's backlinks in a backlink analysis tool and pursue replicable ones (e.g., pages listing "best X guides" that could include yours). Pitch on merit; paid or value-exchange links need rel="sponsored" or nofollow.
9. **Track rankings** — use a rank tracker (manual Google checks are unreliable due to personalization). Usually only the main keyword per page needs tracking.
10. **Repeat** — apply the process across more keywords to compound traffic.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List keywords ranking #2–10; pick the best underperformer
- [ ] Compare your page vs. top 3 results on type/format/angle; fix mismatches
- [ ] Add missing subtopics found in competitors' content
- [ ] Refresh dates/stats on pages where the SERP rewards freshness
- [ ] Fix titles, URLs, heading structure, image alt text
- [ ] Add 5–10 contextual internal links to the target page
- [ ] 301-redirect cannibalizing duplicates into one strong page
- [ ] Audit competitor backlinks; outreach for replicable links
- [ ] Track the main keyword weekly, not daily

## Data points & benchmarks
- Pages ranking #6–10 for years can jump to #1 quickly once intent is truly matched (the "backlink checker" case).
- A competing beginner's guide had links from 14K+ referring domains — benchmark for the scale of links on head terms.
- Backlinks confirmed by Google (2016) as one of the most important ranking factors.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Trying to improve rankings for long-tail keywords a page isn't primarily targeting.
- Skipping intent and going straight to link building.
- Keeping multiple similar pages competing for the same keyword (cannibalization).
- Merging pages that serve different intents.
- Judging progress via personalized, logged-in Google searches.

## Key principles
- "Start with the easy things within your control, then move to more challenging things."
- One strong page beats several weak pages on the same topic.
- Freshness matters only where searchers value it — check the SERP dates to decide.
