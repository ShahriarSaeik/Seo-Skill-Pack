# E-E-A-T Audit: Measuring Experience, Expertise, Authority, and Trust

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
E-E-A-T is a conceptual quality framework, not a ranking factor you can toggle — you can't "add E-E-A-T" to pages. But its qualities surface through 220+ measurable markers across three entities: your brand/website, the people behind it, and your pages. Audit those markers, score them like a quality rater, and turn weak signals into a fix roadmap.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Brand & website foundations** — brand-name consistency across site, socials, directories; clear About and contact info; visible legal pages (privacy, terms, disclaimers); professional design. Missing basics alone can justify a "Lowest" quality rating regardless of everything else.
2. **Brand authority in search** — branded search demand (number of branded keywords and total monthly volume); branded SERPs owned by your site and profiles; Knowledge Graph entity presence; non-branded topical rankings; SERP features (AI answers, PAA). This is the scoreboard for existing E-E-A-T.
3. **Brand authority in AI features** — AI citations (linked sources in AI answers), unlinked brand mentions, which topics the AI associates with you, competitor share for your core topics, and factual accuracy of what AI says about you.
4. **Off-site brand signals** — quality raters are told to trust independent sources over self-descriptions: audit press coverage, backlinks, review platforms, directory consistency, sponsorships/partnerships, and mention sentiment.
5. **Technical trust signals** — organization/author/content schema, HTTPS and secure checkout, site hygiene (broken links, crawl errors, spammy backlinks).
6. **Content & on-page elements** — demonstrated effort and originality, factual accuracy and citations, author bylines with bios and credentials, proof signals (case studies, awards, unique data, testimonials), freshness, and on-page trust markers (reviews, maps, seals).
7. **Key people** — bylines and professional profiles; verifiable credentials backed by independent sources; knowledge panels for individuals; name/role/expertise consistency across the web. Repeat brand-level checks per person; a key person's reputation feeds the brand's.
8. **Site-wide elements** — legal pages, real contact details with maps, consistent naming and voice, YMYL-grade accuracy and compliance where applicable, testimonials placed on key pages.
9. **Score and plan** — rate each marker on the quality-rater 5-point scale; convert to percentage scores for stakeholders; log concrete next actions per weak marker (improve bios, tighten schema, earn reviews) so the audit becomes a roadmap.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Verify brand name consistency across site, socials, and directories
- [ ] Publish/strengthen About, contact, and legal pages
- [ ] Measure branded search demand (keyword count + total volume) and track quarterly
- [ ] Check whether AI answers mention you, cite you, and describe you accurately
- [ ] Audit press mentions, reviews, and sentiment on third-party platforms
- [ ] Confirm organization/author schema and HTTPS everywhere
- [ ] Add bylines with credentialed bios to all substantive content
- [ ] Verify key people exist as consistent entities across the web
- [ ] Score all markers 1-5 and produce a prioritized fix list

## Data points & benchmarks
- The checklist operationalizes 220+ markers; prior researchers mapped 80+ candidate E-E-A-T signals from patents and official statements (e.g., site focus, content effort, site authority attributes).
- Quality raters explicitly weigh independent sources' reputation verdicts above the site's own claims.
- Foundational trust failures can cap a page at the "Lowest" rating even when other signals are strong.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Believing E-E-A-T is a plug-in: it's earned over time across the web, not sprinkled onto pages.
- Auditing only your own domain — most trust signals live off-site.
- Ignoring the people layer: authors and founders are audited as entities too.
- Treating the audit as a score-only exercise without the next-steps column.

## Key principles
- E-E-A-T is conceptual; its markers are measurable — audit entities (brand, people, pages), not abstractions.
- Trust is the umbrella: experience, expertise, and authority all feed it.
- Raters trust what others say about you over what you say about yourself.
- Strong E-E-A-T tends to go hand in hand with AI visibility: brands that are widely cited and trusted are more likely to be surfaced.
