# How to Do an SEO Competitor Analysis [With Template]

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Competitors are a goldmine of validated SEO data: what keywords, pages, and link strategies already work in your niche. An 11-step analysis covers identifying SEO competitors (not just business competitors), then mining their traffic structure, content gaps, snippets, backlink gaps, link bait, broken pages, page experience, and paid keywords for opportunities you can replicate.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Identify SEO competitors** — sites ranking top 10 for the same keywords as you (organic competitors report in any SEO tool). For new sites: enter seed keywords and see which domains capture the most traffic share. Filter out domains with far higher authority than yours — compete where the gap is realistic.
2. **Investigate how they get traffic** — study their site structure/subfolders to see where traffic concentrates (e.g., template libraries, blog categories). Those subfolders reveal proven topical opportunities.
3. **Find and cover content gaps** — keywords competitors rank for that you don't. Filter to keywords where ALL competitors rank top 10 for maximum relevance.
4. **Steal featured snippets** — find keywords where a competitor owns the snippet and you rank in the top 10; restructure your content (e.g., steps as H3s, definitions, lists) to win it.
5. **Map their traffic by country** — if competitors earn significant traffic from non-English countries, consider translating landing pages or launching multilingual blogs.
6. **Find backlink gaps** — sites linking to competitors but not you (link intersect analysis). Pursue easily replicable links (listicles, resource pages, relevant directories) only where the placement is editorially genuine; paid placements need rel="sponsored".
7. **Spot link bait opportunities** — check competitors' most-linked pages to learn which formats attract links in your niche (statistics posts, original research, templates). Build your own, then internally link from the link bait to money pages to pass authority.
8. **Find competitors' broken pages** — 404 pages on their site that still have backlinks. Rebuild the content (check archived versions) and ask the linking sites to point to your working version.
9. **Compare Core Web Vitals** — benchmark their page experience against yours; parity removes a competitive disadvantage.
10. **Check their paid keywords** — keywords competitors bid on are likely profitable; filter out brand terms to surface low-volume, high-converting keywords you missed.
11. **Learn from their PPC ad copy** — tested ad headlines/descriptions are click-winning language; borrow the phrasing for your title tags and meta descriptions.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List 3–5 realistic SEO competitors (similar authority, overlapping keywords)
- [ ] Map which subfolders drive their traffic; note replicable topics
- [ ] Run a content gap report; shortlist keywords all competitors rank for
- [ ] Identify stealable featured snippets where you're already top 10
- [ ] Check their traffic-by-country for translation opportunities
- [ ] Run a link intersect report; build a replicable-link outreach list
- [ ] Reverse-engineer their best link bait; plan your version
- [ ] Replicate + outreach for their broken pages with backlinks
- [ ] Benchmark Core Web Vitals vs. yours
- [ ] Mine their non-branded paid keywords for commercial keyword ideas
- [ ] Swipe proven ad phrasing into titles/meta descriptions

## Data points & benchmarks
- Example: a competitor's template subfolder alone drew ~260K monthly visits (9.9% of its total traffic) — subfolder-level analysis exposes these concentrations.
- One competitor's statistics post had earned nearly 4,000 backlinks — evidence that data-driven content is strong link bait in that niche.
- A competitor's dead post still had 33 linking sites — a ready-made broken-link-building target.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming business competitors = SEO competitors (media sites and publishers often outrank direct rivals).
- Benchmarking against domains with massively higher authority than yours — pick winnable fights.
- Ignoring SERP features when gap-analyzing (filter out SERP-feature rows for clean data).
- Only chasing links to money pages — they rarely attract links naturally; use link bait + internal links instead.
- Overlooking non-branded paid keywords — a cheap source of commercial intent data.

## Key principles
- Competitor data de-risks your SEO strategy: replicate strengths, exploit weaknesses.
- If all competitors rank top 10 for a keyword, it's proven demand — prioritize it.
- PPC ads are free copywriting research for organic titles and descriptions.
- Authority flows internally: earn links with bait, route it to pages that sell.
