# Small Business SEO: 7 Quick Wins

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Small business SEO is mostly local and mostly quick: claim and complete your business profile, get listed where customers actually browse (directories and niche sites), fix crawl errors, build service pages for what people search, answer the specific questions buyers have, earn a few links from existing relationships, and collect reviews relentlessly. If you do only one thing: claim and optimize the business profile.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Claim and optimize your business profile** — set the right category, keep hours current, add contact details, photos, and products/services. Profiles surface for broad searches like "pizza restaurant near me" via the map pack, not just brand searches.
2. **Get listed on relevant directories** — search "[what you do] in [location]" and list your business on every first-page directory/niche site (customers genuinely book through them); skip low-quality or paid-link directories. Find more via competitors' referring domains: sites linking to rivals but not you.
3. **Fix basic technical issues** — run a site audit (free tools exist), fix errors like 404s and broken internal links, and schedule recurring crawls for alerts.
4. **Create pages per product/service** — searchers query the service ("bathroom remodel near me"), not the trade ("plumber near me"), and Google ranks service pages, not homepages. Use keyword volumes to prioritize which pages to build first.
5. **Answer what buyers actually want to know** — cluster keyword variations around each service; terms reveal brands, types, and qualifiers customers care about (e.g., which boiler brands an engineer repairs). Mention them on the page.
6. **Build a few backlinks from relationships** — supplier testimonials, client spotlights (write up the project, ask them to feature it), stockist pages, and replicating competitors' easy links. Keep testimonials genuine; paid or traded links should be tagged rel="sponsored" or avoided.
7. **Ask for reviews and respond** — share your review link in thank-you emails, receipts, or QR codes; reply to every review. Never fake, buy, or incentivize reviews, and don't screen who you ask. Identify the niche review sites that matter for your category (search your business name and see what ranks).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Claim business profile: correct category, current hours, photos, services
- [ ] Search "[service] in [location]"; get listed on every ranking directory
- [ ] Run a link-intersect check on 2–3 local competitors for unlinked directories
- [ ] Crawl the site; fix errors; enable scheduled audits
- [ ] Brainstorm all services; check volumes; build pages for the top ones
- [ ] Add specifics to service pages: brands serviced, materials, types, FAQs
- [ ] Send testimonial blurbs to suppliers; pitch stockist/client-spotlight features
- [ ] Create a review-request process (link or QR at every touchpoint)
- [ ] Respond to all reviews, positive and negative

## Data points & benchmarks
- ~17% of SEO practitioners rated reviews as the single most important map-pack ranking factor (older survey).
- Even niche directories can close customers: one searcher booked a cat sitter entirely through a niche directory found via Google — the business's own site never mattered.
- Of a dozen local boiler-repair sites checked by a real buyer, only one listed the boiler brand they needed — and got the call (and ranked #3 for the brand-specific query).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Sending all traffic to the homepage instead of dedicated service pages.
- Leaving the business profile incomplete or with outdated hours.
- Ignoring niche directories and review sites because "Google is what matters."
- Asking for reviews once and stopping — consistency is the engine.
- Vague service pages: no brands, types, or specifics = lost calls and weaker rankings.

## Key principles
- Local SEO = profile + directories + reviews + service pages; each is a small lift with outsized visibility impact.
- Answer the buyer's unspoken specifics (brand, type, price range) — the business that names them wins the call.
- Existing relationships are the cheapest backlink source a small business has.
