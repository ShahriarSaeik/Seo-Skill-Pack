# Organic Traffic Benchmarks: What a Typical Website Actually Gets (Real Data)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Across 344,956 real websites, "average" organic traffic is a meaningless number — the median site earns a few hundred clicks a month while giants skew averages 40x+. Benchmark by your authority band and site size instead: traffic scales exponentially with both, and a sub-300-clicks/month young site is exactly where the data says it should be.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Pick the right comparison row.** Find your site's authority score band and indexed-page count in benchmark tables — not an industry "average."
2. **Use medians, never means.** Means are dragged up by a handful of megabrands; the median describes the typical site.
3. **Judge yourself against your own trend.** Month-over-month growth versus your baseline matters more than any global number.
4. **If below benchmark for your band, work the levers:** realistic keywords, topical depth, links to best pages, refresh/consolidate decay, non-AI-cannibalized queries.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Record monthly organic clicks (Search Console performance figure) as your baseline
- [ ] Look up the median for your authority band and page count; set that as the 12-month target
- [ ] Target keywords whose difficulty matches current authority (win easy now → win hard later)
- [ ] Build topical depth in one niche before scattering across many
- [ ] Point new links at the pages you most want to rank
- [ ] Refresh or merge decaying pages before publishing net-new
- [ ] Weight effort toward commercial/navigational queries AI answers rarely intercept

## Data points & benchmarks
- Median monthly organic clicks by authority score (0–100; a third-party metric, not a Google signal; one dataset): **9 (0–10)** → 60 (10–20) → 133 (20–30) → 308 (30–40) → 831 (40–50) → 1,594 (50–60) → 3,630 (60–70) → 10,988 (70–80) → 50,292 (80–90) → **81,530 (90–100)** — a four-orders-of-magnitude climb.
- By site size: **12 clicks** (<10 pages) → 97 → 400 → 945 → 2,463 → 5,754 → 16,567 → **132,889** (5,000+ pages).
- By industry (medians): news 102,910; reference 76,718; online communities 65,183; finance 20,940; health 11,168; real estate 10,194; law & government lowest at 4,417.
- Mean-vs-median gap: means run several times the median in every category — up to 40x+ (computers & electronics: mean 1,193,902 vs median 18,650).
- Industry spread is dwarfed by authority and size spread: a strong site in a "low-traffic" niche typically out-earns a weak site in a "high-traffic" one.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Setting traffic goals from industry averages (outlier math) or big-publisher comparisons.
- Publishing thousands of thin pages to fake "site size" — quality at scale earns traffic, page count alone doesn't.
- Treating early slowness as failure: the curves are exponential; the biggest gains come after the base is built.
- Assuming backlinks mechanically "summon" traffic — authority is a proxy for the content, links, and recognition that win rankings.

## Key principles
- Organic traffic follows a power law: few sites capture most clicks; the long tail earns little each.
- Authority compounds — rank for more keywords, higher, earning more links, widening the gap.
- Big brands harvest branded/navigational demand; new sites compete only on unbranded terms, the most contested traffic.
- Consistency beats intensity; as AI answers absorb bottom-of-funnel clicks, climbing means building authority plus getting cited inside AI answers.
