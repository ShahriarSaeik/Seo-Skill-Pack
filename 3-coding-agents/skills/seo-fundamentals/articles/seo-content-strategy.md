# How to Create an SEO Content Strategy

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A working SEO content strategy in one sentence: create and maintain high-quality, search-focused content about topics with business potential, search traffic potential, and ranking potential. Every word carries weight — drop any one filter and the strategy falls apart. Then maintain what you publish, because decay is guaranteed.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find topics with search traffic potential** — start from broad niche keywords, expand to related keyword ideas, and filter by traffic potential (estimated monthly traffic to the top-ranking page — more reliable than raw search volume, since pages rank for many keywords). Cross-check via competitors' top pages.
2. **Score business value** — how naturally can you pitch your product while covering the topic? Score 3 (product is the obvious answer) / 2 (relevant but soft tie-in) / 1 (no natural pitch). Prioritize 3s and 2s; a "3" for you may be a "1" for another business.
3. **Assess ranking potential** — define it as: viable to rank top 3 with your available resources. Check four things:
   - **Backlinks** — how many linking sites do current top rankers have? (e.g., one example keyword needed links from ~77 websites)
   - **Authority** — if the whole first page is far more authoritative than your site, deprioritize
   - **Search intent** — the three Cs: content type (post vs. product page), format (how-to, listicle, tool), angle (dominant selling point). If you can't fulfill the intent (e.g., SERP demands an interactive tool), you can't compete
   - **Quality** — can you realistically beat the incumbent content (e.g., a publisher that physically tested 47 products)?
4. **Create high-quality, search-focused content** — search-focused = matches intent for the main keyword. High-quality = R-E-A-C-H: Real (original), Experienced (written with genuine expertise), Accurate (sourced facts), Clear (no fluff), Helpful (actually solves the problem).
5. **Maintain it** — run a quarterly review where each writer flags posts to update or rewrite; refreshes produce visible traffic spikes. Reasons to maintain: first-page rank rarely comes on day one, rankings decay, intent shifts, facts go stale, and competitors one-up you.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a keyword list filtered by traffic potential, not just volume
- [ ] Score every candidate topic for business potential (3/2/1)
- [ ] Run the four ranking-potential checks: backlinks, authority, intent, quality
- [ ] Reject topics where you can't win on either intent fit or quality
- [ ] Write to match the SERP's dominant type/format/angle
- [ ] Hold content to R-E-A-C-H before publishing
- [ ] Schedule quarterly update/rewrite reviews per content owner
- [ ] Watch for ranking declines on owned keywords as refresh triggers

## Data points & benchmarks
- Example: "how to make kimchi" required backlinks from ~77 websites to compete — a numbers-backed go/no-go signal.
- Refreshing a single decaying post produced an immediate, sustained traffic spike in one large SEO blog's data.
- Traffic potential (top page's total traffic) > single-keyword volume as the estimator of a topic's worth.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing traffic on topics with zero business connection to your product.
- Targeting SERPs whose quality bar (original testing, proprietary data, tools) you can't match.
- Publishing and forgetting — unmaintained content reliably declines.
- Judging difficulty by keyword difficulty score alone without checking intent and incumbent quality.

## Key principles
- Three filters, all mandatory: business potential, traffic potential, ranking potential.
- "Ranking potential" means top-3 viability with available resources, not merely "possible someday."
- Content that ranks but can't pitch the product is a hobby; content that pitches but can't rank is a wish.
- Maintenance is part of the strategy, not an afterthought.
