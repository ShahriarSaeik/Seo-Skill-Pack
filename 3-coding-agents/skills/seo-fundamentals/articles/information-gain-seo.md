# What Is Information Gain in SEO?

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Information gain is the additional information your page contributes beyond what already ranks (and beyond what a searcher has already seen). Whether or not engines run a literal information-gain score, it is your differentiator: in an era of commodity AI content, the part that isn't consensus yet is what earns rankings, AI-answer citations, and brand memory.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Understand the mechanism (per a Google patent; a patent doesn't prove production use)** — the score is per-user, per-session (a sixth near-identical article scores ~zero for a user who read five); it compares meaning, not wording (paraphrasing a competitor adds nothing — the rewrite lands in the same semantic spot); it's dynamic (results re-rank mid-session as the user consumes pages); and it prunes duplicate facts, so assistants cite each fact once, from whoever states it best.
2. **Serve primary intent first** — covering what every ranking page says is the cost of entry. Information gain only works on top of it.
3. **Study intent mixes to find underserved angles** — most SERPs serve several intents at once, unevenly; the intent absorbing ~80% of ranking content is the consensus (cover it), and the underserved intent group is the gain.
4. **Map what the SERP already says** — run a content gap analysis against top-ranking competitors; read them as a searcher. Ask per section: "what can I say here that none of these pages does?"
5. **Fill gaps with unanswered questions** — mine question keywords that keep surfacing in PAA and forums but go unanswered on page one; sort by 3/6/12-month growth to catch questions without a definitive answer yet. Reddit ranking on page 1 = the SERP is failing intent; intercept those questions in searchers' own words.
6. **Include unique data** — original data is the most defensible gain: a customer survey, a poll, an analysis of your own numbers. Coverage-matching raises topical authority; novel information creates gain.
7. **Keep content fresh** — "new" is relative to what's been absorbed into consensus; refresh candidates = high pages with declining traffic on low-difficulty SERPs that haven't been updated recently.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] For each target keyword, list what every page-1 result says; identify the consensus core
- [ ] Identify the underserved intent slice and commit to covering it better than anyone
- [ ] Add at least one element no competitor has: proprietary data, first-hand test, contrarian-but-supported take
- [ ] Mine PAA/forum questions with growth trend; answer them properly
- [ ] Check where Reddit outranks the SERP — that's unmet demand in raw language
- [ ] Review decaying pages on low-KD SERPs for rewrite/republish
- [ ] Before publishing, answer: "what does this page contain that no other page on the topic does?" If unclear, don't publish

## Data points & benchmarks
- A study of 150 top-3 ranking pages found pages with 15+ unique data points averaged a tool-defined information-gain score (not a Google metric) of 62 vs. 40 for pages with 0-1 — original data was the strongest predictor, stronger than position itself (positions 1-3 all averaged ~50-55).
- An analysis of 400+ sites after a major core update found "proprietary assets" (original data, tools) were the third-strongest predictor of traffic gains.
- Google reported reducing "low-quality, unoriginal content" in results by 45% (March 2024).
- One rewrite/republish of a decayed post recovered traffic 50x (33 → 1,695 monthly visits) — a single case.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Rewording competitors' content — semantic comparison catches paraphrase; it counts as zero gain.
- Stat-stuffing: junk numbers add figures, not information; the score is measured on meaning.
- Chasing novelty while losing the core intent — balance gain with the consensus baseline.
- Publishing "comprehensive" AI-generated guides — comprehensiveness stopped being a differentiator when anyone can generate it.

## Key principles
- Information gain = "rewarding content for being different, not just better."
- Gain lives at the fringe of a topic — examples, data, and perspectives the SERPs haven't picked up yet.
- In a synthesized AI answer there is no position four: either you provide the unsaid information or your content gets cut.
- Even if no literal score exists, engines reward what information gain produces: content people actually want to read.
