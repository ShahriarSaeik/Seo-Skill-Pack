# How the Google Search Algorithm Works

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
The algorithm uses hundreds of signals (200+, changing ~6 times daily), and Google's public explainers describe signals around meaning, relevance, quality, usability and context. Practitioners translate these into five levers: backlinks, freshness, keyword mentions, user experience, and topical authority. Rankings are won by matching intent with quality content, keeping it fresh, nailing UX, and earning topically relevant links — not by gaming secret factors.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Five practical levers (a practitioner translation of Google's published signals) and how to act on each:

1. **Backlinks** — Google wants "prominent websites on the subject" linking to you. Compare your page's referring domains against the top-ranking pages for your keyword; if they have more, links are likely your gap. Find domains linking to multiple competitors but not you (link intersect) — those are your first outreach targets. Tactics: guest blogging, linkable assets, broken link building.
2. **Freshness** — query-dependent. News queries surface results <24 hours old; evergreen queries rank 2+ year-old pages. Analyze the SERP: if fresh content dominates, commit to frequent updates; if not, evergreen content wins.
3. **Keyword mentions** — include the exact keyword in the title, at least one subheading, the URL, and the intro paragraph. Beyond that, mentions occur naturally (a sample post mentioned its target phrase 18 times with zero deliberate effort). Focus on covering the full intent — Google explicitly checks for relevant content beyond the keyword (pictures, videos, lists of breeds for "dogs"). Content-gap analysis reveals missing subtopics.
4. **User experience** — page load speed (aim under ~2 seconds), no intrusive interstitials, intuitive navigation and internal linking, mobile-friendliness, solid design, and meta tags matching intent. Core Web Vitals act as the speed test.
5. **Topical authority** — "sites that many users seem to value for similar queries." Build clusters of related content (e.g., to rank for "best protein powder," also cover protein timing, safety, sourcing) plus topically relevant backlinks to those pages.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Benchmark referring domains: your page vs. top 3–5 SERP results
- [ ] Run link intersect vs. competitors; outreach to shared-but-missing domains
- [ ] Check SERP freshness signals; set an update cadence where needed
- [ ] Place keyword in title, one H2, URL slug, and intro paragraph
- [ ] Run content-gap analysis on top-ranking pages; add missing subtopics
- [ ] Pass Core Web Vitals; keep loads under ~2s
- [ ] Remove intrusive popups; verify mobile usability (Lighthouse, DevTools device mode)
- [ ] Build supporting content around money pages (topical clusters)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Billions of Google searches per day (older estimates ~5.6 billion; volume keeps growing).
- Google ships thousands of changes per year (older estimate ~6 per day); broad core updates arrive a few times a year.
- Believed to use 200+ ranking factors; only a handful are confirmed.
- Named major updates: Panda (thin content), Penguin (link spam), Hummingbird (semantic understanding), RankBrain (machine-learned relevance), Pigeon (local), Mobilegeddon (2015 mobile-friendly update), Intrusive Interstitials.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Keyword stuffing — Google assesses relevant content beyond the keyword.
- Chasing every micro-update; fundamentals rarely change.
- Building links from irrelevant sites — "prominent websites on the subject" is the bar.
- Publishing one isolated page per topic instead of building topical coverage.

## Key principles
- "Create high-quality, well-formatted content that matches search intent; keep it fresh; ensure good UX; build topically relevant links."
- Backlinks have been the biggest trust signal since 1996 — but topical relevance of the linking site matters as much as its authority.
- Freshness weight is decided per query, not per site.
