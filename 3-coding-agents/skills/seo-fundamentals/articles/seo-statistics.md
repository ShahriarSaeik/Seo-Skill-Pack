# SEO Statistics: Key Benchmarks for 2026

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A cross-industry snapshot of search behavior (figures come from different studies and years — check each source's methodology before quoting): Google still dominates referrals, but AI summaries are compressing clicks; nearly all traffic goes to page one; most published pages get nothing. Use these numbers to set expectations, benchmark performance, and win budget conversations.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Use stat categories as a benchmarking checklist when planning or reviewing strategy:
1. **Channel weight** — where search sits in the mix (justify investment).
2. **CTR by position** — model traffic upside of ranking improvements.
3. **Ranking/content realities** — timeline and success-rate expectations.
4. **Technical health** — compare your site against common failure rates.
5. **AI-era shifts** — recalibrate forecasts for AI Overviews and AI referrals.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Benchmark your pages' CTR against position averages in Search Console
- [ ] Model traffic projections from "page one or nothing" (96.6% of clicks)
- [ ] Set 6–12 month ranking timelines for new pages (only ~5.7% crack the top 10 within a year)
- [ ] Discount keyword-planner volume estimates (~54% overestimate in one study)
- [ ] Remember that a large share of Search Console clicks (~46% in one study) come from anonymized queries
- [ ] Factor a click penalty (studies range roughly 30–60%) into forecasts for keywords with AI Overviews
- [ ] Run a technical health check against the common-issue rates below

## Data points & benchmarks
**Search & channel:**
- 68% of online experiences begin with a search engine; search drives 1,000%+ more traffic than organic social (older studies).
- Google: ~90.4% global search share; ~63% of US web referrals; an index of ~400 billion documents (older estimate).
- 15% of Google searches have never been searched before.
- 61.5% of desktop and 34.4% of mobile searches ended without a click in one study.

**CTR & SERP behavior:**
- Top organic CTR: ~39.8% (one study) or ~9.28% (another, impressions-based) — position one always leads; positions 2–3 get 5.82% and 3.11%.
- 96.6% of clicks go to page-one results; the top 3 results take ~68.7% of all clicks (older study).
- With AI Overviews present, one study found organic CTR on affected queries fell from 1.76% to 0.61% in ~15 months (−61%); other studies report ~58% lower CTR for top-ranked pages and ~34.5% fewer clicks (methods differ, so ranges vary).

**Ranking & content:**
- 96.55% of pages got zero Google traffic; only 1.94% got 1–10 monthly visits (older study).
- The #1 page gets the most traffic only 49% of the time; the average top page ranks for ~1,000 keywords.
- 72.9% of top-10 pages were 3+ years old; only ~5.7% of new pages reached the top 10 within a year (older study).
- 94.74% of keywords get ≤10 monthly searches; 0.0008% exceed 100,000.
- Title tags: Google rewrites 33–76% (long titles 57% more likely rewritten; H1 used 50.76% of the time as replacement). 25% of top pages lack meta descriptions; meta descriptions shown only ~37% of the time; 62.78% rewritten.
- 30.9% of featured snippets belong to the #1 organic result; AI-cited content is 25.7% fresher on average than organic results.

**Backlinks:**
- Referring domains positively correlate with traffic; top pages gain new followed linking domains at +5–14.5%/month (ranking higher attracts links — a rich-get-richer loop).
- 73.6% of domains have some reciprocal links; 66.5% of links die within ~9 years; 10.6% of backlinks are nofollow.
- Pages with at least one exact-match internal anchor had ~5x more traffic (correlation, not proof); practitioners average 1–2 hours to build one link.

**Technical health (share of sites affected):**
- 95.2% have redirect issues; 88% have HTTP→HTTPS redirect issues; 72.3% have slow pages; 80.4% missing image alt text; 59.5% missing H1; 51.3% multiple H1s; 68.5% page/SERP title mismatch; 67% of hreflang implementations have errors; only 33% pass Core Web Vitals (older data; pass rates have improved).

**Local & mobile:**
- Complete business profiles are reported to be 70% more likely to be visited and 50% more likely to be purchased from; one expert survey attributes ~32% of local pack ranking to profile signals (treat as directional).
- 76% of nearby smartphone searches led to a store visit within a day; 28% ended in a purchase (older study).

**AI era:**
- 87% of marketers use AI in content creation; ~74% of newly published pages contained AI-generated material (April 2025 sample; likely higher now).
- 65% of people still rate human-written content higher quality.
- In one analysis, Google sent ~345x more traffic than the top AI assistants combined, and one chatbot accounted for ~78% of AI referral traffic (the ratio is shifting).
- YouTube (~23.3%) and Wikipedia (~18.4%) are the most-cited sources in AI Overviews (36M overviews analyzed).
- In one study, 48% of top news sites blocked a leading AI lab's crawler and 24% blocked the search giant's AI crawler.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Reading single-study CTR numbers as universal — methodology varies (panel vs. impressions data).
- Forecasting without discounting for AI Overviews on informational queries.
- Assuming title tags and meta descriptions appear as written — write them, but expect rewrites.
- Treating "zero traffic" as failure — most pages get zero; the bar is the top 3–5 results.

## Key principles
- Page one or nothing: the click distribution is a cliff, not a slope.
- Rankings attract links, which protect rankings — early link investment compounds.
- The AI layer redistributes, not destroys, attention: citations and brand mentions now matter alongside clicks.
- Freshness is quietly important: AI answers preferentially cite fresher content.
