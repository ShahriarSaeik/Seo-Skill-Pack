# E-E-A-T: How to Build Trust and Boost Web & AI Visibility

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is Google's content-quality framework from its Search Quality Rater Guidelines. It's not a direct ranking factor or a score, but Google's ranking systems are evaluated against raters' judgments and aim to reward content with strong E-E-A-T signals — and trust is the most important pillar. Demonstrate it with first-hand experience, verifiable credentials, off-site recognition, and transparent, accurate pages.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The four pillars:

1. **Experience (added Dec 2022)** — first-hand, real-world involvement. Demonstrate with: authentic stories/case studies, original photos and videos of the thing itself, "how this was created" notes, author about-pages with proof of involvement. Experience can be borrowed: invite guest authors who have it.
2. **Expertise** — verifiable knowledge/skill. Demonstrate with: author credential pages, in-depth comprehensive content, citations of authoritative sources and original research, expert review of YMYL pages. YMYL topics need formal credentials; non-YMYL topics accept "everyday expertise" (e.g., a gardening enthusiast on plant care).
3. **Authoritativeness** — being recognized as a go-to source. It's relative (a brand can be an authority on X but not Y). Build with: quality backlinks, positive reviews, original research/data studies, podcasts/webinars/interviews, PR, and an "as seen in" press page.
4. **Trustworthiness (the foundational pillar)** — accuracy, honesty, safety, reliability. Build with: clear About/contact info, transparent policies, fact-checking and citations, HTTPS, disclosures of sponsored content, genuine reviews. An untrustworthy page scores low E-E-A-T regardless of the other three.

YMYL ("Your Money or Your Life") topics — health, finance, safety, legal, news, groups of people — face heightened scrutiny; weak E-E-A-T there can hurt an entire site's visibility.

What changed in 2024: the Helpful Content System folded into core ranking, so quality signals (including site-wide ones) now feed core updates. Scaled content abuse and site reputation abuse are now spam policies. Large amounts of weak content can drag down a whole domain — pruning or improving it may help, though recovery isn't guaranteed.

Measuring E-E-A-T (proxies, since no official score exists):
- **Backlinks from unique, authoritative, topically relevant sites** ("largely based on links and mentions on authoritative sites" — Google).
- **Brand mentions** on authoritative sites (track monthly via searches excluding your domain, or mention-tracking tools, filtered by domain authority).

E-E-A-T and AI content: Google rewards quality regardless of production method — one study found 81.9% of top-20 content includes AI-generated material (AI use isn't penalized per se; low-value scaled content is). Requirements: human review, genuine added experience, rigorous fact-checking.

E-E-A-T and AI search: in one study, AI Overviews cited top-10 organic results 76% of the time, so they largely inherit traditional quality signals. The strongest AI-visibility correlations in that study were off-site authority signals: branded web mentions (0.664), branded anchors (0.527), branded search volume (0.392). Non-Google assistants apply their own similar trust filters.

## Actionable checklist
One-off:
- [ ] Link legal/policy pages (privacy, terms, returns) in the footer
- [ ] Publish clear company info and contact details
- [ ] Install HTTPS sitewide
- [ ] Add affiliate/sponsored disclosures
- [ ] Create author bios with credentials, linked to professional profiles
- [ ] Publish an editorial standards page (fact-checking, corrections process)
- [ ] Add an "as seen in" section for press/awards

Ongoing:
- [ ] Refresh key YMYL pages on a schedule; have experts review and credit them
- [ ] Audit and prune thin/outdated pages
- [ ] Add firsthand proof: stories, case studies, original research
- [ ] Back claims with authoritative sources and expert quotes
- [ ] Earn mentions and quality backlinks (PR, research, guest posts)
- [ ] Grow and interlink topical clusters
- [ ] Keep ads non-intrusive and clearly labeled

## Data points & benchmarks
- E-A-T launched 2014; "Experience" added December 2022.
- The Aug 2018 "Medic" update devastated health/finance sites lacking E-E-A-T — the canonical case for its ranking influence.
- 81.9% of content ranking top 20 contains some AI-generated material (one 600K-page study) — AI usage per se isn't penalized.
- One study: 76% of AI Overview citations came from top-10 results; AI visibility correlated with branded mentions (0.664), branded anchors (0.527), branded search volume (0.392).
- E-E-A-T improvements are typically reassessed at the next core update — expect months, not days (a common practitioner observation).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating E-E-A-T as a checkbox or a "score" to optimize — it's a proxy framework measured through links, mentions, and quality signals.
- Putting credentials on the page while the content itself shows no first-hand experience.
- Ignoring weak pages because "only good pages matter" — site-wide quality signals can suppress everything.
- Publishing AI content without human review, added experience, or fact-checking.

## Key principles
- "Why should someone trust this content, the author, and the site?" — if you can't answer easily, neither can Google.
- Trust is the roof of the temple: Experience, Expertise, and Authority are its supporting pillars.
- Authority is earned off-site (mentions, links, reviews) and demonstrated on-site (proof, transparency).
- There are no E-E-A-T hacks — consistency compounds into durable visibility, including in AI answers.
