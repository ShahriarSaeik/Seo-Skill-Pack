# Content Decay: What It Is and How to Fix It Before It Tanks Your Traffic

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Every article slowly loses traffic — decay is gradual ranking loss from staleness, competitor improvement, intent drift, or self-cannibalization, and it now has a second dimension: disappearing from AI answers. Run quarterly decay audits, then choose the right fix per page: update, consolidate, redirect, rewrite, or prune.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Detect:** top-pages report filtered to declining traffic over 12 months, difficulty <40 (filters out link-gap problems), sorted by biggest losses. In Search Console compare last 3 months vs. same period a year ago: impressions+CTR both down = decay; CTR-only drop = SERP feature problem.
2. **Diagnose the cause before fixing:** check the page's change timeline — declined with no changes = classic decay (refresh); declined right after an edit = restore what was changed. Look for competing internal pages swapping rankings (cannibalization) and SERP similarity/intent shift (new page types ranking).
3. **Prioritize the backlog** by business relevance, historical traffic peak, and remaining winnability; low on all three = prune candidates.
4. **Apply the decision framework:**
   - Keyword relevant, content outdated → **update/refresh**
   - Two pages, one keyword → **consolidate** weaker into stronger + 301 (merge link magnets too)
   - Keyword off-strategy, page has links → **redirect** to a genuinely relevant page (never homepage)
   - Minimal traffic/links/value → **prune** (noindex is reversible; deletion isn't)
   - Bad from the start, topic competitive → **rewrite**
5. **Refresh properly:** topical gap analysis vs. current top rankers; replace stale stats and examples; re-align with current intent (check page-type mix); refresh title/meta (searchers and AI assistants both read them); add internal links from authority pages; fix broken links; re-promote (email, social, contributors' networks).
6. **Check AI visibility separately:** rankings can recover while AI citations stay absent — study what AI-cited competitors do differently.
7. **Prevent:** quarterly audits (flag >20% YoY drops), keyword alerts for new competitors ranking, annual reviews for top pages regardless of metrics, clusters instead of silos.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Quarterly: pull declining top pages; flag >20% YoY traffic drops
- [ ] For each, verify cause: decay vs. own edits vs. cannibalization vs. SERP feature change
- [ ] Assign update / consolidate / redirect / prune / rewrite per the framework
- [ ] Refresh: close topical gaps, update every stale stat, refresh title + meta
- [ ] Add internal links from high-authority pages; repair broken links
- [ ] Update publish dates ONLY with genuine improvements
- [ ] Re-promote updates via email/social/expert contributors
- [ ] Track AI citations post-refresh as a separate KPI
- [ ] Calendar annual reviews for the top 10–20 revenue pages

## Data points & benchmarks
- AI systems favor fresh content: in one study, URLs cited by AI assistants were **25.7% "fresher"** than organic results on average; a separate experiment found artificially refreshed dates could move items up AI-generated rankings (a bias, not a tactic: faking dates is deceptive and risks trust and spam signals).
- In one older study, ~80% of meta titles were rewritten by search engines — write them well anyway; they are one input AI systems and searchers see.
- Intent-shift example: "how to start a blog" page one went from 34% forum threads / 27% guides to **60% forum threads** within a year; SERP similarity just 31/100.
- Pruning case (anecdotal): deleting 60%+ of articles on one real-estate site was followed by a notable click increase for the remaining pages; prune only genuinely low-value pages.
- Semi-automated refreshes (one 2,000+ article blog): 4x refresh pace and ~25% more articles at a fraction of the cost, with traffic upticks (keep a human editor accountable for accuracy).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Refreshing a page that actually needs consolidation, restoration, or pruning — the wrong fix wastes time and can worsen decay.
- Changing the publish date without meaningful content change — engines compare page versions across time.
- Redirecting pruned pages to the homepage (passes no relevance); discarding "link magnet" stats during merges.
- Treating a CTR-only drop (new AI Overview/snippet in the SERP) as content decay.
- Detecting decay at -80% instead of -20%.

## Key principles
- Content has a lifecycle: traction → growth → peak → plateau → decline; teams over-invest in the first three and ignore the last two.
- Decay has two layers now — classic rankings AND AI-answer citation; monitor both.
- The cheapest refresh beats the most expensive rescue.
- Clusters distribute authority; competing pages split it.
