# What Is a Good Website Authority Score? (Real Data, Three Benchmarks)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
There is no universal "good" authority score — it's logarithmic, relative, and only meaningful against three references: your direct competitors, your industry's distribution, and the sites already ranking for your target keywords. A score is "good" when it beats the sites you actually compete with. (Authority scores are third-party metrics, not Google ranking factors, and each tool's scale differs.)

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Benchmark vs. competitors:** compare your domain and direct rivals in a backlink tool's authority comparison. If competitors cluster at 40, your 50 is good; if they sit at 85, your 70 is behind.
2. **Benchmark vs. industry:** list 20–50 companies in your sector, export their scores, read the quartiles (median = average, top 25% = strong, bottom 25% = lagging).
3. **Benchmark vs. the SERP (the one that predicts rankings):** for each target keyword, check the average authority of the top 10 and the *lowest* authority site ranking. Then filter keyword lists to terms where sites at your level already rank top 5 — those are your winnable battles.
4. **Treat the score as a gauge, not a scoreboard** — use it to size difficulty and track backlink growth, not as a target to game.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a 5–10 domain competitor set; compare authority scores (and referring-domain counts)
- [ ] Build a 20–50 domain industry sample; record median and quartiles
- [ ] For each priority keyword, note avg + lowest authority of top 10 ranking sites
- [ ] Filter keyword research to "lowest ranking authority ≤ my score" opportunities
- [ ] Grow unique referring domains (distinct sites > raw link count)
- [ ] Create linkable assets: original data, free tools, definitive guides
- [ ] Reclaim unlinked brand mentions and fix broken inbound links

## Data points & benchmarks
- Example SaaS industry distribution (n=600): bottom quartile 38, **median 62**, top quartile 77, top 10% 86.
- Competitive commercial SERPs demand authority: "what is SEO" top 10 averages 91 (lowest 81); "best CRM software" 89 (73); "email marketing software" 85 (76).
- Low-authority sites still rank: "best running shoes" avg 76 but a **29** site ranks page one; "budgeting tips" avg 78 with a **37** site ranking.
- Authority score correlates only **0.131** with rankings — small positive, far from deterministic.
- The scale is logarithmic: most of the web sits at 0–10; 20→30 takes a handful of links, 70→80 can take thousands. One link from a huge site may barely move the needle, since its value is spread across a very large number of outbound links.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing a magic universal number ("70 is good") with no comparison context.
- Treating authority score as a ranking factor or a gate — it measures competitiveness of the climb, not permission to rank.
- Over-indexing on one big link instead of many distinct referring domains.
- Obsessing over point movements instead of the links behind them.

## Key principles
- "Good" is always relative: to competitors, to industry, to the specific SERP.
- Competitiveness correlation is loose — right query selection beats raw authority.
- Volume of unique linking domains builds the score; linkable assets earn them.
- The score follows the links, never the other way around.
