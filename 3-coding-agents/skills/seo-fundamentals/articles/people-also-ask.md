# People Also Ask Boxes: How to Rank and Whether It's Worth It

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
'People Also Ask' (PAA) boxes appeared for ~43% of queries in an older study (prevalence has shifted since AI Overviews), but clicks are thin: roughly 3% of searchers interact with the box and only a fraction of those click a source. Optimization is only worth it for high-traffic or high-value pages — the method is to find popular questions (high combined search volume across the keywords they appear for), confirm you're eligible (top-10 ranking for the question itself), then format the answer for extraction.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find pages that rank for many keywords** — your top pages by keyword count.
2. **Pull their keyword rankings** — filter to positions <20, monthly search volume ≥10, and keywords whose SERPs show a PAA box; export the list.
3. **Collect the PAA questions** for those keywords (via a SERP scrape or rank data export).
4. **Find popular questions** — pivot-table the questions by frequency and total combined search volume; a question appearing across 100+ keywords with high summed volume is worth targeting.
5. **Check you're not already the source** — search a triggering query, expand the question, and verify.
6. **Check eligibility** — most answers are pulled from pages ranking top 10 for the question itself; if you don't, pick another question or improve that ranking first.
7. **Optimize the page** — ensure the answer exists on the page, matches the expected format (paragraph/list/table/video), and sits under clean headings.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Add the answer to the page — exact question wording isn't required, Google matches meaning
- [ ] Match the current answer's format (list vs. paragraph vs. table)
- [ ] Use clear H2/H3 heading structure around the answer
- [ ] Don't rely on FAQ markup — it isn't required for extraction (FAQ rich results are now limited to well-known government/health sites); a visible, clearly structured answer matters more
- [ ] Never shoehorn irrelevant questions into a page
- [ ] Check PAA boxes on branded queries — fix misleading or competitor-sourced answers
- [ ] Mine PAA questions for content gaps and new seed keywords (products/niches you hadn't considered)

## Data points & benchmarks
- ~43% of search queries showed a PAA box in an older study; re-check for your niche.
- One user-behavior study found ~3% of searchers interact with PAA boxes on average, ranging up to 13.6% on some queries; interactions ≠ clicks.
- Working math: 3% interaction × ~40% click-through × ~25% split across four questions ≈ 0.3% of searches produce a PAA click — so a question needs roughly 33,000 combined monthly searches to yield ~100 extra clicks.
- The same question often sources its answer from the same page across the queries it appears in — one win can compound across many SERPs.
- Example: one question appeared in PAA boxes for 212 keywords a single page ranked for, with 61,800 combined monthly searches.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Optimizing for questions that only appear for a couple of low-volume keywords.
- Chasing PAA before you rank top 10 for the question itself — eligibility gates everything.
- Expecting meaningful traffic from PAA on low-value pages; prioritize pages with high conversion rates or value per visitor.
- Ignoring PAA boxes entirely — they're a free window into related searcher intent for content planning.

## Key principles
- PAA is a volume game: target questions, not keywords — the same question recycles across hundreds of SERPs, often with the same source.
- PAA boxes are a research asset first (intent mining, seed keywords, brand defense) and a traffic channel second.
- If a site gets little regular organic traffic, fix fundamentals before PAA optimization.
