# Discover Feed Optimization: Driving Traffic Beyond Query-Based Search

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
The Discover feed is a personalized, primarily mobile content feed built from users' activity, location, and followed topics — closer to a social network than search. You can't target it directly, but you can raise your odds: mobile-first UX, large quality images, honest metadata, entity authority in your niche, buzz-building distribution, refreshes, and embedded video. One large SEO blog pulled ~150K clicks from it in six months without chasing trends.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Get the basics eligible** — content must be indexed and comply with content policies. Then:
2. **Mobile experience first** — the feed is primarily mobile: responsive, fast, minimal ads/pop-ups/interstitials.
3. **Images** — unique, high-quality, ≥1200px wide, enabled via the `max-image-preview:large` robots setting.
4. **Aligned metadata** — titles/descriptions that summarize honestly; no clickbait (explicitly penalized). Check social-share (Open Graph) tags too — typos there surface in the feed even when the title tag is correct.
5. **Publish on topics with demand** — timely content dominates, but evergreen works because the feed shows content new *to the user*, not new to the web (e.g., investing guides surface when a user takes up investing).
6. **Build entity authority** — stay consistent in your category (mixed-topic publishing dilutes signals); analyze which entities drive your feed traffic (natural-language salience analysis); use image-search related-entity chips as content inspiration; aim to become a knowledge-graph entity (search your brand: a knowledge panel = you're in).
7. **Create distribution buzz** — feed performance correlates strongly with social engagement (0.91 correlation with one platform in the US); distribute on the platforms popular in each target market — identical content without local distribution gets almost no feed traction.
8. **Refresh periodically** — republished 2017-era articles have suddenly started pulling feed clicks; refresh for SEO reasons and accept Discover as a bonus.
9. **Embed video** — embedded videos can enter the feed independently and outperform their host articles (note: those clicks go to the video platform, not your site).
10. **Experiment with tappable story formats** where available.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Verify mobile UX: speed, layout stability, no intrusive interstitials
- [ ] Set `max-image-preview:large`; supply ≥1200px unique images
- [ ] Audit og:title/og:description for typos — the feed uses them
- [ ] Add clear dates, bylines, author bios, and contact info (E-E-A-T signals)
- [ ] Pick your entity lane; stop publishing far outside your category
- [ ] Check for a knowledge panel on your brand; if absent, strengthen entity signals
- [ ] Build a real distribution push for every important publish (locally relevant channels)
- [ ] Republish/refresh top evergreen pages on a schedule
- [ ] Embed relevant videos in high-potential articles
- [ ] Review Discover performance in Search Console (the only data source available)

## Data points & benchmarks
- one large SEO blog: ~150K feed clicks in six months, without publishing trending news.
- Correlation between feed performance and one social platform's engagement in the US: 0.91 (correlation, not proven causation).
- An older estimate put 60%+ of feed articles on accelerated-mobile-page formats, skewed heavily toward news sites (AMP is no longer required).
- Typical B2B pattern: a 3–5 day traffic spike per publish; evergreen pages can instead show constant trickle.
- Localized versions of identical articles earned almost no feed clicks where no local distribution effort existed.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Prioritizing the feed over fundamental SEO (except for news publishers, where it's often the top organic source).
- Clickbait titles/metadata — explicitly against feed guidelines.
- Expecting guarantees: content can do everything right and still never surface.
- Ignoring local platform dynamics when publishing in multiple languages.

## Key principles
- The feed rewards warm audiences: it shows your content to people already interested in your entities — authority in a niche is the entry ticket.
- Distribution creates the buzz signal that feed selection appears to ride on; publishing is only half the job.
- Everything that helps the feed (mobile UX, images, E-E-A-T, freshness, entities) is plain good SEO — treat feed traffic as a by-product.
- The feed can drive clicks for topics with zero keyword search demand — a reason to publish some content that isn't keyword-driven.
