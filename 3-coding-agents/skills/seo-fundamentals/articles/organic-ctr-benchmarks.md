# What Is a Good Organic CTR? Real Benchmarks by Industry, Authority, and Site Size

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Across 422,421 real websites, a "good" whole-site organic CTR is just 1–2% (median). The realistic target depends on your industry, your site's authority, and its size — and falling CTR is usually caused by AI answers and SERP features intercepting the click, not by your titles alone.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Benchmark whole-site CTR, not position-1 CTR.** Divide total clicks by total impressions for the month (the Search Console overview figure). Position-1 curves (~30–40%) describe single rankings, not sites.
2. **Find your industry's median** and compare against it, not against a global average (giant sites inflate means).
3. **Segment by authority score band and site size** to set a fair expectation for YOUR site (new sites sit below 1% — that's normal, not failure).
4. **Diagnose declines:** if impressions hold but CTR slides, an AI Overview is likely intercepting your queries. Check which keywords show AI answers.
5. **Fix the controllable levers** (titles, descriptions, SERP features, schema) and accept that the ceiling is lower than it used to be.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pull monthly clicks ÷ impressions for the whole site; compare to industry median
- [ ] Rewrite title tags to front-load value and match query intent
- [ ] Write meta descriptions that earn the click (even though they're often rewritten)
- [ ] Win SERP features: snippets, sitelinks, rich results — more pixels, more clicks
- [ ] Add structured data for rich results you are eligible for (review stars, product, recipe, etc.; FAQ rich results are now limited to well-known government/health sites)
- [ ] Shift effort toward commercial/navigational queries AI answers rarely cannibalize
- [ ] If CTR drops with flat impressions, audit which keywords now show an AI Overview

## Data points & benchmarks
- Overall good whole-site CTR: **1–2%** (median across 422,421 sites).
- Highest industries: adult 7.31%, online communities 5.37%, reference 2.48%, arts & entertainment 2.19%; most industries cluster at **1–2%**.
- Lowest industries: pets & animals 0.78%, law & government 0.82%, business & industrial 0.86%, home & garden 0.89%, health 1.15% — the niches where AI answers trigger most (e.g., health 43%, science 43.6% of queries).
- By authority score (0–100 backlink metric): rises near-linearly from **0.39% (score 0–10)** to **2.91% (score 90–100)** — a ~6x gap; every band beats the one below it.
- By site size: 0.58% (≤10 pages) → 1.74% (5,000+ pages).
- In one study, AI Overviews cut clicks to the top organic result by **~58%** when present (worsening from ~34.5% in earlier measurements).
- AI answers appeared on 57.9% of question queries in one study, and ~99% of keywords that trigger an AI Overview are informational — informational niches are most exposed.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Comparing your site-wide CTR to "position 1 gets 30–40%" figures — different metrics.
- Using averages instead of medians (megabrands distort averages 2–3x above typical).
- Chasing backlinks purely to raise an authority score "for CTR" — the score never appears in results; brand recognition wins the click, authority is only a proxy.
- Treating a sub-1% CTR on a young site as a failure — it's the norm below ~40 authority score.

## Key principles
- Google keeps clicks when it can answer the query; clean SERPs still pay, absorbed SERPs don't.
- CTR is becoming a less complete measure of SEO success — track citations in AI answers alongside clicks.
- Optimize the click you can win, then influence the sale in other ways: brand recognition, AI citations, high-intent capture.
