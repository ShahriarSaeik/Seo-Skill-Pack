# SERP Analysis: Judging If a Keyword Is Worth Chasing

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
SERP analysis is the 3-step vetting process that decides whether you can and should compete for a keyword: (1) size the effort-to-reward ratio with difficulty and traffic-potential metrics, (2) inspect who ranks — their authority, links, intent match, and content quality, (3) hunt for SERP-feature shortcuts. It prevents months wasted on unwinnable SERPs.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **High-level overview** — for each candidate keyword, look at keyword difficulty (KD, 0–100) and traffic potential (total search traffic to the top-ranking page). Plot keywords on an effort-vs-reward grid:
   - Low effort / high reward → golden opportunities (prioritize)
   - High effort / high reward → long-term plays
   - Low effort / low reward → maybe; usually skip
   - High effort / low reward → avoid unless strategically lucrative
2. **Investigate if (and how) you can rank** — open the SERP overview and check four things:
   - **Site authority of ranking domains**: look for at least one top-10 result at or below your site's authority level — that's the SERP cracking open.
   - **Referring domains to ranking pages**: count links per result; find positions winnable with a realistic link count (e.g., roughly 8–36 domains, not 500+).
   - **Dominant search intent**: identify the content type (blog post, category page, product page, landing page, video). If videos dominate, you need video; mixed intent → produce both formats.
   - **Content quality bar**: YMYL topics (health, finance, safety) may demand credentialed expert content; some SERPs are owned by high-resource review sites — skip if you can't match.
3. **Check for SERP-feature opportunities** — featured snippets (concise answers), video carousels (fresh, high-quality video can leapfrog stronger domains), image packs, top stories, People Also Ask. Winning a feature can out-size your organic position.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pull KD and traffic potential before committing to any keyword
- [ ] Scan the top 10 for authority outliers close to your level
- [ ] Compare referring-domain counts vs. your realistic link-building capacity
- [ ] Match the dominant content format — don't blog against product pages or videos
- [ ] Assess the expertise/resources needed to beat the current content quality
- [ ] Identify SERP features you could win with modest extra effort
- [ ] Final gut-check: can you build a better answer, do you have the resources, is the reward worth it?

## Data points & benchmarks
- In one tool's scale, KD 73 implied needing ~235 referring domains to crack the top 10 — a resource-mismatch signal (difficulty scales differ by tool).
- In one survey, ~65% of SEO practitioners checked domain-authority metrics when judging rankability.
- A realistic ceiling: if a site with a low authority score (~26) sits at position #6 with only 8 referring domains, positions 6–10 are the winnable zone for a mid-authority site.
- Traffic potential belongs to the #1 result: mid-SERP positions may only see ~800–900 monthly visits where the leader sees 24,000.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing high-difficulty, low-traffic-potential keywords (bottom-right quadrant).
- Judging difficulty by metrics alone without opening the actual SERP.
- Ignoring intent: a blog post cannot rank where free tools or product pages dominate.
- Competing on YMYL or expert-review SERPs without the credentials to match.
- Forgetting that a newer, better video can outrank aged results in video carousels.

## Key principles
- SERPs are crackable when at least one ranking page looks like you (similar authority, similar links, similar format).
- Read the SERP as Google's statement of intent — content type and format are non-negotiable.
- SERP features are the shortcut layer: a feature win can out-earn a lower organic position (a page shown in a featured snippet is not repeated as a normal organic result, so it replaces rather than doubles the listing).
