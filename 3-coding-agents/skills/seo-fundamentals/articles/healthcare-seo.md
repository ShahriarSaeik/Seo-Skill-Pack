# Healthcare SEO: 7 Strategies for Medical Websites

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Healthcare SEO lives or dies on trust: medical (YMYL) content needs expert review and visible credentials, low-competition long-tails must come before head terms dominated by 90+ authority giants, and providers' natural .gov/.edu relationships are an untapped link engine. Layer on condition-level topic clusters, local pages, and medical schema.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Medically review all content (E-E-A-T)**: treat every article as YMYL. Split production: ~70% common-knowledge topics written by non-expert writers (citing references, interviewing clinicians), ~30% cutting-edge topics by specialized medical writers. Source reviewers in-house or from freelancer networks; reviewers flag errors, add clinical context, and soften absolute claims ("best treatment", "guaranteed").
2. **Publish attribution prominently**: reviewer bio on every article (photo, name, qualifications, specialty), a dedicated reviewer credentials page, and author/reviewer schema.
3. **Target low-competition keywords first**: filter related terms to a low difficulty ceiling (under ~20 for brand-new sites; up to ~40 for established ones), then keep only keywords where low-authority sites (<30 authority score) already rank top 5 — proof the term is winnable. Expand toward competitive terms as authority grows.
4. **Build topic clusters per medical condition**: group content into pre-intervention (diagnosis, options, cost, images, outcomes), intra-intervention (anesthesia, duration), and post-intervention (recovery, complications, "can I do X") questions. Cluster thousands of raw keywords by parent topic into a few hundred article plans.
5. **Build links from research and relationships**: pitch what already exists — conference speaking (ask organizers for links), volunteer programs shared with local government, government directory listings, published research papers with company attribution, and university collaborations. One aged-care provider counts 33 referring .gov and 24 .edu domains from these plays.
6. **Local SEO**: fully filled Google Business Profile (specific business type, services, hours, photos), NAP citations in data aggregators plus vertical medical directories (find them by intersecting competitors' links), and location pages per service area with localized titles, /service/location URLs, contact forms above the fold, social proof, and interlinks. Check review-solicitation rules (e.g., patient privacy law) before asking for reviews, and never gate, incentivize or fabricate reviews.
7. **Medical schema and certification**: use Physician/Hospital/MedicalCondition/Drug/MedicalWebPage types where relevant (helpful, not mandatory — plenty of #1 pages skip it), and pursue independently assessed health-information certifications as video platforms start privileging credentialed sources.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Assign a qualified reviewer to every article, with byline and credentials page
- [ ] Separate content into common-knowledge vs. cutting-edge production tracks
- [ ] Build keyword lists under your difficulty ceiling with low-authority sites ranking top 5
- [ ] Map each major condition to pre/intra/post-intervention question clusters
- [ ] List every conference, directory, paper, and partnership that can yield .gov/.edu links
- [ ] Complete the Google Business Profile and submit NAP to aggregators + medical directories
- [ ] Launch one location page per service area, interlinked
- [ ] Add medical schema types; evaluate health-content certification schemes

## Data points & benchmarks
- Top medical SERPs are owned by ~92-authority brands (major clinics and health publishers).
- Expert content split: ~70% of articles = common-knowledge topics; ~30% = research-heavy topics needing specialists.
- Example long-tail math: a 121k-searches/month head term is "super hard", but filtering related terms to difficulty ≤40 surfaced 976 winnable keywords (use a lower ceiling for brand-new sites).
- Example cluster math: 5,163 raw condition keywords → 270 article-ready parent-topic clusters.
- Link baseline for relationship-driven healthcare link building: 33 .gov + 24 .edu referring domains in one case.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Attacking head terms ("headache"-level) before building authority with long-tails.
- Publishing medical content with no visible reviewer or credentials.
- Unqualified writers on cutting-edge topics (e.g., a novel drug's effects) without specialist oversight.
- Absolute treatment claims that reviewers should soften.
- Soliciting patient reviews without checking healthcare marketing/privacy regulations.
- Assuming schema alone will win rankings — it's an enhancer, not a requirement.

## Key principles
- Trust signals are the product in YMYL niches: expert review, attribution, and credentials are table stakes.
- Win the winnable: authority-based keyword filters beat volume-based picks in giant-dominated verticals.
- Healthcare organizations sit on natural link goldmines (research, events, government relationships) — link building can be as simple as publicizing what you already do.
- Every medical condition implies a repeatable question template — cluster, don't one-off.
