# 8 Most Important Types of Keywords for SEO (And How to Find Them)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A taxonomy of the 8 keyword types every SEO should know, each with its own discovery recipe: seeds, intent keywords, long-tails (topical vs supporting), low-competition, niche, branded/unbranded, competitor, and primary/secondary — plus special types (zero-volume, trending) and debunked ones (LSI, meta keywords).

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Seed keywords** — research INPUT that unlocks more keywords. Find via brainstorming, competitor keywords, nav menus, social language, AI (ChatGPT/Claude/a keyword tool's built-in AI). Ex: "vegan meatballs" seed → all its variations.
2. **Intent keywords** — informational ("who invented the mouse"), navigational ("facebook login"), commercial investigation ("wireless earbuds review"), transactional ("buy iphone 14"), local, plus "high-intent" = commercial + transactional combined. Find via modifier words or an intent filter on phrase-match keyword ideas. NOTE: intent is a feature of the SERP, not the keyword — general terms show mixed/fractured intent ("coffee" = shops + brands + articles).
3. **Long-tail keywords** — topical (a topic itself; TARGET these) vs supporting (variations; fold into the parent page). Find: phrase-match keyword ideas → Volume ≤300 AND traffic potential (TP) ≤300 → browse by parent topic.
4. **Low-competition keywords** — KD filter max ~20 (lower for brand-new sites); refine by checking whether sites of your size rank in the top 5/10. Special case — trending keywords: sort by volume growth rate (3–12 months), KD cap ~30, verify trajectory on the volume graph; growth modes: linear (stable growth — build on it), exponential (viral — catch early), S-curve (peaking), damped (fading — avoid).
5. **Niche keywords** — specific market segments, low volume but high intent ("vegan baby soap"). Find: seed + low Volume/TP filters + use-case modifiers ("alternative," "for," "substitute," "vegan," "free"). Verify the thing actually exists as a product (avoid misspellings posing as niches).
6. **Branded vs unbranded** — unbranded = new-audience acquisition; branded = people who know you. Find via an intent filter.
7. **Competitors' keywords** — target the same terms or use as seeds. Use a competitive-analysis tool's organic keywords, or a keyword-gap comparison for gaps. Find true organic competitors with an organic-competitors view — market competitors ≠ organic competitors.
8. **Primary and secondary keywords** — primary = page's main topic (needs TP, intent fit, business potential, rankability); secondary = subtopics under it. Find secondaries: keywords the top 10 pages also rank for.

**Also worth knowing:** zero-volume keywords (target if rising or high business value); LSI keywords (myth); meta keywords (Google ignores). Most tools offer filter presets: funnel stage, ecommerce, news, newly discovered, audience-specific, seasonal.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build seed list before any filtering
- [ ] Tag each keyword with intent via an intent filter; verify mixed SERPs manually
- [ ] Separate topical long-tails (Volume+TP ≤300) from supporting ones
- [ ] Filter KD under ~20 and check that sites of your size rank in the top 5/10 for the winnable list
- [ ] Run a trending-keyword pass (growth-rate sort, KD ≤30) quarterly
- [ ] Validate niche keywords denote real products/segments
- [ ] Assign ONE primary keyword per page; harvest secondaries from what the top 10 pages also rank for

## Data points & benchmarks
- Long-tail filter heuristic: Volume ≤300 + TP ≤300 (adjust upward in high-demand industries)
- Low-competition filter: KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites); trending filter: KD ≤30
- "vegan baby soap": low-volume, low-KD, high-intent niche example
- "shoes" seed generates millions of ideas

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating intent as a keyword property — it belongs to the SERP and can change
- Targeting supporting long-tails with dedicated pages
- Chasing low-KD keywords that are brand-dominated SERPs
- Falling for misspellings that look like niche keywords
- Worrying about LSI or meta keywords — debunked/ignored

## Key principles
- Only topical long-tail keywords deserve their own page.
- High-intent = commercial investigation + transactional keywords.
- Trend-shape literacy (linear/exponential/S-curve/damped) tells you whether to invest or avoid.
- Your organic competitors aren't always your market competitors — find them with data.
