# Topic-Based SEO: Optimize for Topics, Not Individual Keywords

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Keywords aren't dead — but optimizing them one at a time is like lighting a galaxy one star at a time. Search and AI systems map keywords, pages, and brands together in semantic spaces, so the goal is to become associated with an entire topic area. Match your approach to topic size using a three-tier model.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Start with the brand lens** (the step everyone skips): audit what you already rank for (organic keyword rankings), then define territory via: product/service categories, customer pain points, competitor gaps, and journey-stage questions. Pressure-test boundaries with six filters — topic meaning (one interpretation or several?), intent alignment, content-format capability, product/service scope, ICP relevance, business potential. This also reveals your tier.
2. **Choose the tier-appropriate approach**:
   - **Tier 1 — Clearly defined topics** (~1,000-keyword universes, consistent intent): build the full keyword list, cover it completely, reach the topic-leadership plateau, then hold it. Expand only if the business expands.
   - **Tier 2 — Ambiguous topics** (mixed intent, fuzzy boundaries): disambiguate BEFORE planning. "Product design" splits into UX/digital vs physical/industrial — same words lexically, different meanings semantically; associated brands are the tiebreaker. You can't own the whole topic; lead the slice that matches your brand.
   - **Tier 3 — Expansive topics** (hundreds of thousands to millions of keywords, growing via AI query fan-out): stop mapping keywords; find the structural patterns and build repeatable content architectures (pillars, templated page types) that earn authority across the universe — each page still needs unique, useful content; mass-produced near-duplicate pages are scaled-content spam.
3. **Build at the right scale**: Tier 1 via standard keyword lists; Tier 2/3 by exporting keyword data and clustering with an LLM by intent and context. Prompting tips that work: show example clusters for your niche, let the model decide cluster count (don't force a fixed number), exclude brand terms and years, prefer 3+ word keywords (short terms cluster too broadly), and expand one cluster at a time.
4. **Measure topic coverage, not single rankings**: query breadth (how many related queries + AI prompts you're visible for), share of voice (including AI share of voice), and coverage expansion over time.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Audit current organic keywords: what do search engines already associate you with?
- [ ] Run the six boundary filters before researching any keyword
- [ ] Classify each target topic as Tier 1/2/3 and commit to the matching approach
- [ ] Tier 1: cover everything relevant; stop at the ceiling and defend the plateau
- [ ] Tier 2: filter ambiguous keywords by semantic context and entity associations
- [ ] Tier 3: cluster with AI at scale; design repeatable page templates per subtopic, each with unique value
- [ ] Track query breadth + share of voice growth instead of single rankings
- [ ] Check what topics AI search systems already associate with your brand

## Data points & benchmarks
- Red wigglers niche: 1,070 total keywords → coverable in 12–15 blog posts plus ecommerce pages
- Solution-focused brief therapy: ~1,000 keywords → ~33 content pieces via clustering
- One B2B uniforms/scrubs brand: 114 ecommerce pages covering ~2,500 keywords held national topic leadership for 2 years (lost it to an SEO-blind site redesign)
- One large health publisher (point-in-time snapshot from one tool): visible for 4.2M keywords in web search, 1.1M AI Overview queries, and 395K/176K/50K/40K prompts across major AI assistants
- One ~1,000-word page from that publisher (magnesium glycinate): 2,500 web keywords + ~1,000 AI query/prompt appearances
- Counter-example: an AI-generated site published 208 pages (60+ with the same term in the URL) to capture just 266 keywords — ~200x the effort for ~1/10 the visibility
- LLM clustering works better on keywords of 3+ words

## Mistakes to avoid
- Expanding Tier 1 topics into adjacent spaces (SFBT → therapy; red wigglers → worm farming) — dilutes topical focus unless the business actually expands
- Treating Tier 2 ambiguity as a volume problem — keyword volume can't disambiguate meaning; entities and context can
- Running expansive topics like "Tier 1 but bigger" — page-per-keyword at scale produces massive output for minimal visibility
- Chasing traffic with no business potential (luxury brands ranking for budget queries, B2B SaaS explaining "what is a spreadsheet")
- Judging success by individual rankings when topic breadth and share of voice are the real scoreboard

## Key principles
- A keyword is a star; a topic is the galaxy. Own the galaxy your buyers live in.
- Search engines and AI systems place brands in semantic space via entities and knowledge graphs — visibility follows earned topical association, not the reverse.
- One well-structured, authoritative page per subtopic beats dozens of thin keyword-targeted pages.
- Topic size dictates strategy: complete coverage (T1), disambiguation (T2), structural pattern recognition (T3).
- Keyword research at tier-3 scale is an AI-clustering exercise, not a list-building one.
