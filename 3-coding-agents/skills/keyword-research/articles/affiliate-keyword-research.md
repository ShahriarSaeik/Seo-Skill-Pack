# Affiliate Keyword Research: 4 Keyword Types That Convert

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Most affiliate keyword research fails by chasing low-competition keywords and shoehorning in links — traffic without revenue. The fix is intent-first: target keywords where searchers are naturally in the market for products. Four types convert: general comparisons, branded comparisons, product reviews, and trust-and-authority keywords that earn the links commercial pages can't.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Filter by intent before difficulty** — "best protein powder" searchers weigh options and buy; "what is protein powder" searchers don't. Only target topics where product recommendations fit naturally.
2. **General comparisons ("best X")** — broad seeds → phrase match → include-filter "best" → optionally KD <10. Don't stop at low KD: cheap products make it a volume game. Balance with high-ticket items via the "best [product] for [user]" pattern (e.g., "best car seat for small car") — person/need-specific variants are less competitive. On mixed SERPs, narrowly focused posts from low-authority sites often outrank broader guides — stay specific.
3. **Branded comparisons ("brand A vs brand B")** — lower volume, excellent conversion (searcher has narrowed to two options). Seed a keyword tool with brand names — source lists from ecommerce "shop by brand" navigation, and use model names when a brand spans categories — then include-filter "vs". Read the SERP: where low-authority pages rank top 10, attack. Prioritize expensive products ($500–1,000+ items justify the content).
4. **Product reviews ("[product] review")** — the searcher is one read away from buying. Volumes are modest, so only review products people actually search for; include-filter "review" over brand/model seeds, then assess difficulty.
5. **T&A (trust & authority) keywords** — non-commercial informational topics in your niche. Two payoffs: audience trust (demonstrated expertise makes recommendations credible) and backlinks (people link to useful content, not affiliate pages). Find them in a list of question keywords filtered to high difficulty (~KD 40+) — a link-based difficulty score works as a rough link-magnet signal. Then funnel authority to money pages via internal links (the middleman technique: authoritative T&A pages link to "best X" pages).
6. **Blend the portfolio** — money pages (types 1–3) plus link-earning pages (type 4), interlinked. Disclose affiliate relationships clearly and mark affiliate links rel="sponsored" (or nofollow); review and comparison pages should reflect real evaluation, not just link lists.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Reject keywords whose intent can't naturally carry a product recommendation
- [ ] Build "best X" and "best X for Y" lists from broad and high-ticket seeds
- [ ] Harvest brand/model lists from relevant ecommerce stores; batch-generate "vs" keywords
- [ ] Check each "vs"/review SERP for beatable low-authority pages before committing
- [ ] Reserve review effort for products with real search demand and meaningful prices
- [ ] Add T&A content to the calendar; target link-heavy topics (high-KD questions)
- [ ] Internal-link every T&A page to its related money pages

## Data points & benchmarks
- Difficulty read example: "best car seat for 3 year old" at KD 22 — but specific posts from low-authority sites beat broad high-authority guides on the mixed SERP
- T&A filter: questions at KD 40+ (link-based metric) = likely link-attracting topics
- Economics example: $500–1,000 strollers compared in a "vs" post where low-authority pages ranked

## Mistakes to avoid
- Picking keywords for low KD alone, then shoehorning in affiliate links
- Reviewing products nobody searches for
- Targeting only cheap-product keywords and turning the site into a volume game
- Skipping T&A content, leaving commercial pages linkless and stuck
- Letting T&A pages accumulate authority without internal links to money pages
- Skipping affiliate disclosure or leaving affiliate links untagged

## Key principles
- Conversion is decided at keyword selection, not at link density.
- The closer a query sits to a purchase decision (vs, review, best), the fewer visitors you need to earn revenue.
- Someone has to earn the links; in affiliate SEO, that's your informational content.
