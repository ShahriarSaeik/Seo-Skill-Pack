# Buyer Intent Keywords Convert Better. Here's How to Find Them

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Buyer intent keywords signal readiness to purchase, converting better and shortening the journey. Find them by filtering for ad-driven SERP features (Google + advertisers have already validated the intent), sorting by CPC, and verifying with intent analysis — then choose SEO or PPC per keyword.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**5 types of buyer keywords:** transactional ("buy concert tickets"), commercial ("best running shoes"), navigational-to-brand ("target weekly ad"), location-based ("dentist near me"), long-tail ("best running shoes for cross country").

**Finding them (SERP-features method — most reliable):**
1. Enter broad seed terms (ask AI for suggestions) in a keyword research tool
2. Pull phrase-match keyword ideas and filter by SERP features: ads present (top ads, or shopping ads for ecommerce) — ads on the SERP = Google AND advertisers consider it high-intent; also catches keywords without obvious buyer modifiers
3. Sort by CPC descending — higher CPC = higher buying probability
4. Save relevant keywords to a list; verify intent with an intent-classification feature or by checking the SERPs (results should be product/service pages)
- Fallback for new niches (few advertisers): modifier words — buy, order, sale, best price, best, top, review, alternatives, near me, open now, in [city]

**Competitors' buyer keywords:** competitor domain → organic keywords → same SERP-features filter + CPC sort; paid keywords data to see what they actually bid on (check the right country — CPCs differ per market).

**Choosing the best (4 factors):** traffic potential (TP, the total search traffic a topic can bring to a page), conversion potential, keyword difficulty (KD), CPC. Strategies:
- Prioritize conversion potential even at moderate traffic ("buy stainless steel blender online" beats "blenders" for ROI)
- Score keywords with ChatGPT across the 4 factors
- Long-tails with high conversion + low competition ("affordable family vacation packages in Europe")
- Fill competitive gaps (a keyword-gap comparison against competitors)

**SEO vs PPC decision:** PPC when — limited-time offers, need immediate results, testing keywords/landing pages, keyword too expensive for SEO effort, traffic doesn't justify dedicated content. SEO when — keywords too expensive per click, limited budget, hyper-competitive SERPs would benefit from authority building, affiliate sites (free traffic = ROI), long-term independence from ad spend. Both simultaneously = occupy multiple SERP positions and block competitors' clicks (one large SaaS brand ranks AND pays for the same keywords).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Filter phrase-match keyword ideas by SERP features: top ads / shopping ads
- [ ] Sort by CPC (highest first) to surface strongest buying intent
- [ ] Verify intent: most ranking results should be product/service pages
- [ ] Cross-check competitor organic + paid keywords (right country)
- [ ] Score shortlist on TP / conversion potential / KD / CPC (a spreadsheet or an AI prompt works)
- [ ] Assign each keyword to SEO, PPC, or both using the criteria above

## Data points & benchmarks
- Modifier list for buyer intent: buy, order, sale, best price, best, top, review, alternatives, near me, open now, in [city]
- Some keywords = high CPC but low KD → better for SEO than PPC (and vice versa)
- One large SaaS brand ranks organically AND pays ads for the same keywords (occupying both slots)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Relying only on modifier words — they miss non-obvious buyer keywords
- Judging only by volume — conversion potential matters more for buyer keywords
- Checking competitor paid keywords in the wrong country
- Focusing SOLELY on buyer keywords — early-stage content builds awareness and feeds the funnel

## Key principles
- Ads on a SERP are a strong external signal of buying intent.
- High CPC + low KD = SEO arbitrage; low CPC + high KD = consider PPC.
- Blend buyer keywords with informational ones for a healthy funnel.
- Ranking + advertising on the same keyword compounds visibility and starves competitors of clicks.
