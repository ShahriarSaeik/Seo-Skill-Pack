# How to Find and Choose the Right Keywords for SEO

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
The right keyword has four traits: high traffic potential AND clicks, high business potential, content you can create that matches search intent, and a realistic chance of ranking. Follow the 9-step process to filter for those traits.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **List seed keywords** — broad topics important to your business; no tools needed. Seeds generate specific keyword ideas later.
2. **Use research tools** — free keyword tools (free, but volume ranges only), a full keyword research tool (precise volumes; a seed like "dog food" returns hundreds of thousands of ideas), Search Console (keywords you already rank for unintentionally), a PAA question tool (People Also Ask questions), a browser keyword extension (on-the-go data while searching), internal sources (sales/customer chats, site search, communities).
3. **Check traffic potential (TP)** — look at traffic to the top-ranking page, not just keyword volume. One page ranks for many keywords.
4. **Check search intent** — learn / buy / navigate. Verify by studying actual SERP results, not the keyword alone.
5. **Score business potential** — can you naturally pitch your product in the content? Use a 3–0 scale (3 = product is the perfect solution, 0 = no way to mention it).
6. **Assess difficulty (KD)** — one common KD metric = number of backlinks you likely need for page one. Filter by KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites) for easy wins.
7. **Weigh CPC/competition value** — high-CPC keywords = big ad cost savings when ranked organically.
8. **Steal competitor keywords** — a keyword-gap (content-gap) comparison shows keywords competitors rank for that you don't.
9. **Go granular with modifiers** — apply modifiers (industry, persona, business size, price, location, time, features, comparisons, intent, device, compliance) to short-tail keywords for specific, goal-oriented long-tails.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Brainstorm 5–10 seed keywords from your business' core topics
- [ ] Run seeds through a keyword research tool's phrase-match keyword ideas; note volume, KD, TP, and clicks per search (CPS)
- [ ] Mine Search Console for keywords already bringing impressions (pages often rank for many more keywords than the one you targeted)
- [ ] For each candidate keyword, study the live SERP and label intent (learn/buy/navigate)
- [ ] Score business potential 0–3; drop 0s unless strategically needed
- [ ] Filter KD to your site's realistic range (KD under ~20; lower for brand-new sites)
- [ ] Run a content-gap comparison vs 2–3 close competitors
- [ ] Build final list mixing short-tail (volume) and long-tail (conversion) keywords

## Data points & benchmarks
- "dog food" seed → hundreds of thousands of keyword ideas in a keyword research tool's matching terms
- "best dinner recipes": 7.4K monthly searches but top page gets ~36K monthly visits (traffic potential ≈ 5x head volume)
- "puppy food": 14K searches but top page only ~3.6K visits (searches split across intent variants)
- "does a frog have a backbone": 700 searches/mo, zero clicks per search (SERP feature/AI answer absorbs all clicks)
- "gemini horoscope daily": can exceed one click per search (people click several results) despite lower volume
- A page ranking at position 20 for one keyword has been found to rank top 10 for 200+ others on average (older industry study; directional)
- "vegan protein powder": 92% of top results are blog posts (informational) — don't target with a category page
- "dog kennel": 100% of top results are ecommerce category pages
- Business-potential scale (0–3): 3 = your product is the obvious solution; 0 = no natural way to mention product

## Mistakes to avoid
- Relying on search volume alone — check traffic potential of the top page and clicks per search
- Targeting keywords whose intent you can't satisfy (e.g., navigational keywords owned by a brand)
- Trusting KD scores blindly — they only model backlinks needed; other factors matter
- Skipping business potential and attracting traffic that can never convert
- Only chasing broad short-tail keywords when starting out

## Key principles
- "The keywords you choose can make or break your SEO campaign."
- Target keywords where traffic potential + business potential are high, intent aligns, and ranking is realistic.
- Choose high-business-potential keywords so product pitches are genuinely useful, not salesy.
- You need a mix of long-tail and short-tail keywords in your strategy.
