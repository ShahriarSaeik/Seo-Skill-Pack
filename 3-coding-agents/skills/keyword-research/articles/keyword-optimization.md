# Keyword Optimization in 3 Steps: Pick, Align, Execute

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Keyword optimization = increasing a page's relevance to a query. Do it in three steps: (1) confirm the keyword deserves the effort (traffic potential, business value, difficulty, intent, your expertise), (2) align with search intent via the three Cs, (3) execute on-page basics — cover required subtopics, optimize title/H1/meta/URL/images/links, and chase snippets. Goal: help search engines and searchers understand your content, not game the system.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Make sure it's the right keyword**:
   - **Traffic potential over volume** — some searches never click (ads, answers on the SERP) and pages rank for hundreds of keywords; one example keyword's traffic potential was ~10x its search volume.
   - **Value to your site** — score keywords on business potential (0–3: how naturally your product fits the content); prioritize high scores.
   - **Difficulty** — backlinks still dominate; use a KD metric (0–100). Stay under KD ~20 (lower for brand-new sites).
   - **Intent feasibility** — if the SERP is all product pages ("women's shoes"), non-ecommerce sites can't break in.
   - **Your expertise** — search systems favor first-hand expertise (E-E-A-T), especially in YMYL niches; a health blog should be written/reviewed by medically trained people.
2. **Align with the three Cs of search intent** (check top 3 results + SERP features):
   - **Content type**: blog post, product page, category page, landing page (e.g., "best all season car tires" → 9/10 results are articles).
   - **Content format**: how-to guide, step-by-step tutorial, list post, opinion, review, comparison ("home decor tips" → numbered listicles).
   - **Content angle**: the selling point ("how to become rich" → "before 30," "fast," "according to experts"; "how to peel a banana" → "the right way"). Mixed-intent SERPs (check PAA and "things to know" modules) can hide opportunities to rank a different type — riskier bet.
3. **Execute on-page best practices**:
   - **Cover what searchers expect**: shared subtopics across top-ranking pages are requirements. Run a content-gap comparison on top-ranking pages to map the topic, then add your URL to find your missing subtopics. Mention related terms and entities naturally — but ignore LSI/TF-IDF keyword-count myths.
   - **Be digestible**: simple words, skimmable structure, images.
   - **Title**: include the keyword, informative + attractive, correct length. **H1**: consistent with the title, keyword included (identical is fine for blog posts).
   - **Meta description**: no ranking effect but drives CTR — write newspaper-headline style, unique per page, take cues from search ads; expect the engine may rewrite it.
   - **H2–H6** for a skimmable hierarchy; **URLs** short and human-readable (`/how-to-peel-a-banana`, not keyword-stuffed dates).
   - **Images**: relevant/original, descriptive filenames + alt text ("house-on-a-hill.jpg" not "image1.jpg"), placed near related text — they feed image search and content feed surfaces.
   - **Links**: link out to trusted resources (context + E-E-A-T), use internal links tactically (they pass equity).
   - **Featured snippets**: answer the main question early, succinctly, in organized, jargon-free structure; filter keyword lists by "featured snippet" SERP feature.
   - **Rich results**: add schema markup where eligible (eyecatching, not a ranking boost) — check docs, deploy, test, request indexing.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Score candidate keywords on TP, business value (0–3), KD (under ~20; lower for brand-new sites), intent fit, expertise fit
- [ ] Identify the 3 Cs from the top 3 organic results before writing
- [ ] List subtopics common to top-ranking pages; cover them all
- [ ] Keyword in title + H1; consistent tags; compelling unique meta description
- [ ] Descriptive image filenames/alt text; images near related text
- [ ] Add internal links to and from the page; cite trusted external sources
- [ ] Structure one succinct, early answer for snippet capture
- [ ] Add schema for eligible formats; test and request indexing
- [ ] After publishing: build links and track rankings with a rank tracker

## Data points & benchmarks
- Example: "submit website to search engines" — traffic potential ~10x search volume
- Difficulty threshold: keywords under KD ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- Business potential scale: 0 (product irrelevant) to 3 (product essential to the topic)
- "best all season car tires": 9/10 top results were articles — format matching is mandatory

## Mistakes to avoid
- Choosing keywords on volume alone — clicks and value don't follow volume
- Competing for a SERP your page type can't win (blog post vs wall of product pages)
- Entering YMYL niches without demonstrable expertise
- Shoehorning LSI/TF-IDF keyword counts — related terms help only when natural
- Skipping common subtopics because you have a "unique take" — meet expectations first, then differentiate
- Ignoring snippets/schema when the format clearly supports them

## Key principles
- Relevance is the product: optimization helps engines and searchers understand content.
- The SERP defines the required type, format, angle, and subtopics — copy the pattern, then out-execute it.
- Traffic potential, business potential, and realistic difficulty beat raw volume every time.
- Expertise is central to how content quality is judged: the messenger matters as much as the message.
