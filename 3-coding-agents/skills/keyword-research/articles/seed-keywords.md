# Seed Keywords: What Are They & 7 Actionable Ways to Find Them

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Seed keywords are the building-block inputs of keyword research — the output of your research is only as good as the input. In one keyword tool, expanding a seed list from 1 to 6 related terms nearly doubled keyword ideas (264K → 519K). Use the 7 methods below to build a rich seed list before touching filters.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**7 methods to find seed keywords:**
1. **Brainstorm variations/synonyms** — include plurals and slang ("mountain bike", "mountain biking", "MTB", "hardtail"). Tools don't auto-match plurals. Find industry terms via a related-terms view in your keyword tool and by running the topic's Wikipedia page URL through a keyword-rankings tool (organic keywords).
2. **Reverse-engineer competitors** — a competitive-analysis tool → organic keywords + top pages. Filter OUT your head term (e.g., exclude "beard") to surface non-obvious terms like "mutton chops" you'd otherwise miss. When out of ideas, find more competitors and repeat.
3. **Study the SERPs** — People Also Ask boxes, Related Searches (also surfaces related brands), page titles, meta descriptions, and subheadings of ranking pages (blog posts beat ecommerce pages for keyword-rich subheadings).
4. **Mine communities/forums** — Reddit (search niche → pick subreddits → run the subreddit URL through a keyword-rankings tool), niche forums (Google `intitle:forum <keyword>` / `inurl:forum <keyword>`), blog comments, social groups, Quora, support tickets.
5. **List associated products/services/brands** — Google "[seed] + brands" listicles, browse Amazon facets/categories, read "best [seed]" affiliate posts. Example: 18 coffee-brand seeds → 65,177 keyword ideas.
6. **Website navigation menus** — large ecommerce stores' menus and top-level category pages list natural seeds (types of bikes, protein categories).
7. **Search Console** — Performance → Queries; find keywords you rank for but don't target. Search Console's interface shows up to 1,000 rows; a third-party rank database can show more.

**Then:** plug seeds into a keyword research tool → filter ideas down → evaluate on relevance, intent, volume, difficulty.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Brainstorm 5–10 core seeds + synonyms/slang/plurals
- [ ] Check related terms for industry jargon
- [ ] Pull 2–3 competitors' organic keywords; exclude your head term to find hidden seeds
- [ ] Search Google for each seed; harvest PAA, related searches, titles, subheadings
- [ ] Find 2–3 niche subreddits/forums; run their URLs through a keyword-rankings tool
- [ ] List associated brands/products from listicles and Amazon categories
- [ ] Pull Search Console/SEO tool queries for accidental rankings
- [ ] Run the full seed list through a keyword research tool's phrase-match keyword ideas

## Data points & benchmarks
- 1 seed ("mountain bike") = 264,608 keyword ideas (one tool's database; counts vary by tool)
- 6 seeds (bike, biker, biking, MTB, mountain bicycle, hardtail) = 519,830 ideas (+255,222, ~2x from 5 extra seeds)
- 18 coffee-brand seeds = 65,177 keywords (~30 extra seconds of work)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Starting research with a single seed — plural/synonym variants don't auto-appear
- Skipping competitor keywords when you don't know a niche
- Only using obvious head terms; missing slang/industry jargon (MTB, mutton chops)
- Forgetting your own Search Console data
- Using all 7 methods every time — treat it as a buffet; pick what fits the niche

## Key principles
- "The output is often only as good as the input."
- Seeds aren't a silver bullet — they multiply the usable terms your research surfaces.
- Competitors have already done the research; steal their seeds.
- Seeds also reveal subtopics for building topic clusters you weren't aware of.
