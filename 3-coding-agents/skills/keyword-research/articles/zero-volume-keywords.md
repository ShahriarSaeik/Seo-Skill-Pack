# Should You Target Zero-Volume Keywords? It Depends

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Zero-volume keywords are mostly overhyped: one experiment shows they usually get roughly the searches tools estimate, aren't automatically easier to rank for, and don't always convert. Target them only when they're genuinely a trending topic or a lucrative, high-intent niche.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Four claimed benefits, stress-tested:
1. **"They probably get some searches"** — TRUE but marginal. An experiment on one large SEO blog's keywords with volume ≤10 (ranking in the top 5): most drove the SAME or FEWER impressions than estimated; <1% drove >100 monthly impressions; average 11.3 impressions ≈ estimated volume. Viral "zero-volume success" stories usually conflate one keyword's traffic with the PAGE's total traffic (the page wins via hundreds of synonymous long-tails).
2. **"They're easier to rank for"** — SOMETIMES. "link building" = KD 87 vs "link building for roofers" = KD 0. BUT if the zero-volume keyword is just an unpopular phrasing of a popular topic ("submit domain to search engines" vs "submit website to search engines"), Google ranks nearly identical SERPs and difficulty is the SAME as the head term. Diagnose with: low KD = probably own topic; traffic potential (TP, the top page's total traffic) much higher than volume = it's a variation of something popular.
3. **"They're easier to create content for"** — MOSTLY TRUE. Specific queries ("how to structure an ecommerce site for SEO") beat broad ones ("ecommerce SEO") for answerable intent. But specificity isn't exclusive to zero-volume keywords — some high-volume keywords are perfectly specific too ("submit website to search engines", 450/mo).
4. **"They convert better"** — DEPENDS ON INTENT. "buy broken link building services" converts; "how to copy a picture on facebook without tagging" never will.

**When to actually target zero-volume keywords:**
1. Trending topic you believe has legs — get in first before volume registers.
2. Lucrative/high-intent topic — even a few visits can be worth real money.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Before targeting a 0-volume keyword, check KD: low KD suggests a genuine standalone topic
- [ ] Check TP: much higher than volume → it's a variation; target the head term on one page instead
- [ ] Confirm intent is bottom-funnel (buy/hire/pricing modifiers) before expecting conversions
- [ ] Reserve zero-volume targeting for trending bets or high-value niches; don't make it the strategy
- [ ] When you see a zero-volume success story, check the whole page's keyword portfolio

## Data points & benchmarks
- "hreflang tag seo": 10 est. searches vs 20 actual impressions (2x estimate, still tiny)
- Experiment: keywords ≤10 volume → avg 11.3 impressions; <1% exceeded 100 impressions
- "submit domain to search engines": 10 searches/mo, but the page targeting that topic gets ~3.8K monthly visits from synonymous variations
- "link building" KD 87 vs "link building for roofers" KD 0

## Mistakes to avoid
- Building a content strategy around zero-volume keywords
- Assuming zero volume = zero competition OR low competition (it can be either)
- Crediting one keyword for a page's total traffic
- Expecting conversions from informational zero-volume queries

## Key principles
- In this experiment, volume estimates for low/zero-volume keywords were, on average, accurate — outliers were rare.
- Use KD + TP together to tell "own topic" (targetable) from "unpopular variation" (fold into head-term page).
- Target zero-volume keywords opportunistically (trends, lucrative niches), not systematically.
