# How to Find Niche Keywords for SEO in 3 Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Niche keywords are clear, specific topics that appeal to small, specialized market segments (e.g., "sustainable jackets" inside the jacket market). Because competition is typically thin, they're a fast route to highly targeted traffic: build a raw list with low volume + low traffic potential (TP) filters, refine with difficulty/modifier/question filters, then validate every candidate against the SERP.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Create an initial keyword list** — enter broad seed terms into a keyword research tool, pull phrase-match keyword ideas, and set MAXIMUM volume and traffic potential filters low (e.g., 1,000). The volume cap finds limited-demand queries; the TP cap ensures they're genuinely specific topics, not just unpopular phrasings of popular things. Adjust thresholds to market size: ~500 in small markets, up to ~2,000 in huge ones; set a minimum too if zero-volume keywords don't interest you.
2. **Refine the list** (combine freely):
   - **Low difficulty + minimum demand**: KD max 10, volume 100–1,000, TP 100–1,000 → keywords with real demand and weak competition.
   - **Use cases, segments, features**: add modifiers like "for," "alternative," "substitute" with an include-any-word filter; harvest new modifiers you spot in results and re-filter.
   - **Questions**: filter to question keywords for informational opportunities where you can feature your product.
   - **Exclude** filters to strip off-topic results.
3. **Analyze the SERP** before writing anything:
   - **Is there a more popular counterpart query?** Rare queries often have better-known equivalents ("diy soap sheets" → "how to make soap paper"). A parent-topic indicator in your keyword tool surfaces these. Skip misspellings entirely.
   - **What's the dominant intent?** Learn (guides/tutorials), Buy (product/category pages), Go (brand/place names). E.g., "cupcake soap" is purely transactional — only a product page selling cupcake-shaped soap will rank.
   - **How hard is it really?** KD filters out strong-backlink competitors (e.g., one soap query needed links from ~123 sites), but also check: popular expected brands dominating the SERP (a 0-KD keyword can still be unwinnable against giant marketplaces), YMYL topics (health/finance need topical authority + genuine expertise), content quality (can you add anything original?), and whether top pages even target the keyword directly — pages that merely "seem relevant" from high-authority domains can be beaten by directly targeting it.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Enter market-level seed terms; cap volume and TP at a niche-appropriate level
- [ ] Run the KD ≤10 / volume 100–1,000 / TP 100–1,000 filter set first
- [ ] Filter by modifiers ("for," "alternative," "substitute") to find segment keywords
- [ ] Pull question keywords for informational content with product angles
- [ ] For every candidate: check the parent topic for a more popular equivalent
- [ ] Verify dominant intent and match your page type to it
- [ ] Eyeball the SERP for unbeatable brands and YMYL walls before committing
- [ ] Mine established niche sites: pull their organic keywords with the same filters (one keyboard niche site yielded ~5,000 keywords at volume 100–1,000, KD ≤10)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Suggested starting filters: max volume 1,000, KD ≤10, volume/TP window 100–1,000
- Example: ranking for "why is antibacterial soap banned" required backlinks from ~123 websites
- A single niche authority site yielded ~5,000 keywords with volume 100–1,000 and KD ≤10
- Niche volume ceiling varies by market: ~500 (small markets) to ~2,000 (very large ones)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Using only a volume cap — without a TP cap you collect unpopular phrasings of big topics
- Targeting misspellings or rare variants when a more popular equivalent exists
- Writing a blog post for a transactional SERP (or vice versa)
- Trusting KD = 0 blindly when famous brands/marketplaces hold every spot
- Ignoring YMYL difficulty: no topical authority or expertise = near-zero chance in health/finance
- Skipping the SERP check on "perfect-looking" low-KD keywords

## Key principles
- Niche = specific topic + limited demand + real searcher segment, not just low volume.
- The TP filter is what separates "niche topic" from "unpopular synonym."
- SERP composition (intent, brands, targeting) beats any single metric for difficulty.
- Niche sites themselves are keyword mines: reverse-engineer sites already winning your niche.
