# What Are Keywords? How to Use Them for SEO

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Keywords = the words/phrases people type into search engines. You appear for them via PPC (paid) or SEO (organic). Choose them on volume, search intent, value, and difficulty — and optimize primarily by matching intent, not by sprinkling keywords into tags.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Two ways to appear for keywords:** PPC (bid on keywords, pay per click, marked as ads) and SEO (rank organically; in one snapshot, one large SEO blog got ~383K organic visits/month from ranking #1 for terms like "SEO audit," "keyword research tools," "local SEO").

**Finding keywords:** seed keywords (e.g., "headphones," "earbuds," "beats") → keyword tool → ideas with volume, CPC, metrics (2M+ ideas from those seeds).

**Choosing keywords (4 criteria):**
1. **Search volume** — compare only RELEVANT keywords ("how to post on instagram" 35K vs "how to get backlinks" 1.2K — irrelevant volume is worthless). Volume ≠ traffic: "submit website to search engines" (lower volume) beats "SEO tips" (higher volume) in actual traffic to the top page.
2. **Search intent** — check the SERP. "robot vacuum" looks transactional but results are all review lists → searchers research, don't buy. Match the dominant content type.
3. **Value** — "pizza dough" has 43K searches but nobody wants a pizza oven mid-recipe. CPC gives a rough proxy for value (advertisers pay for valuable traffic), but value is personal: "link building service" has high CPC yet zero value for an SEO tool vendor.
4. **Keyword Difficulty** — "mortgage payment calculator" KD 84 = top pages have many links; hard without serious link acquisition. KD is a rough sense only.

**Optimizing for keywords:** the PRIMARY method is matching search intent, not title-tag stuffing. Match content type ("cast iron skillet" = product pages, not blog posts), match angle ("backlinks" results all explain "what are backlinks?"), and cover expected subtopics (every "backlinks" page answers: what / why important / types).

**Long-tail keywords (defined by volume, not length):**
- In one large keyword database, 350M+ one/two-word phrases have <10 monthly searches.
- **Supporting long-tails** = unpopular phrasings of popular topics ("how to lose weight" variants). Ranking for them is NO easier than the head term; optimize the page for the head term instead.
- **Topical long-tails** = the most popular phrasing of a niche topic ("keyword cannibalization," 250/mo; "keto and migraines"). Often easy to rank for and traffic compounds. Find via parent topic = the keyword itself.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List seed keywords; expand via a keyword tool
- [ ] Filter to business-relevant keywords before comparing volumes
- [ ] Check the live SERP for content type/angle before deciding to target
- [ ] Sanity-check value (would this traffic ever buy?) and CPC
- [ ] Check KD; align target difficulty with your link-building capacity
- [ ] Outline content from the SERP: dominant type + angle + subtopics covered by most top pages
- [ ] Distinguish topical vs supporting long-tails via parent topic before creating dedicated pages

## Data points & benchmarks
- "how to do SEO": 1,700 US searches/mo; 900+ visits in a month from position #1
- Headphones seeds → 2M+ keyword ideas
- "SEO tips" 2,200/mo vs "image seo" 350/mo — prioritize volume among RELEVANT terms
- "pizza dough": 43K searches, near-zero buyer value for a pizza-oven store
- "mortgage payment calculator": KD 84
- "how to lose weight": 89,000 US searches; all its <50/mo variants rank the SAME #1 page
- The average top-ranking page also ranks for ~1,000 other keywords in the top 10 (older study)
- 350M+ one/two-word phrases with <10 monthly searches

## Mistakes to avoid
- Judging keywords on volume alone (or comparing irrelevant keywords)
- Trying to rank the wrong content type for the intent
- Chasing high-CPC keywords that have no value to YOUR business
- Optimizing pages for supporting long-tails instead of the head term
- Believing long-tail = many words (it's about volume)

## Key principles
- "Keywords are the foundation of SEO."
- The primary way to optimize for a keyword is to match search intent.
- One page ranks for ~1,000 keywords — optimize for the most popular phrasing of the topic.
- Relevance first, then volume — irrelevant traffic is vanity traffic.
