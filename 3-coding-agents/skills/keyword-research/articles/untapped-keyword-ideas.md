# Untapped Keyword Ideas From Customer Language

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
When a niche is narrow or picked over, keyword tools recycle the same terms everyone targets. The untapped supply lives in how real customers talk: counterintuitive brainstorms, their own words in feedback forms and on-site search, community threads and product reviews, and social-network posts. Validate candidates by the top-ranking page's total traffic, not the keyword's own volume.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Brainstorm counterintuitive queries** — get inside the customer's head:
   - Pretend you know nothing about the industry; write queries a layperson would use.
   - Pretend your product doesn't exist; brainstorm problem-phrasings ("dry air home remedies" instead of "buy humidifier").
   - Ask how a friend with zero niche knowledge would describe the need ("best flowers to say sorry", "should I bring flowers on a first date").
   - Collect freely — ignore volume and difficulty at this stage.
2. **Mine customer feedback** — onboarding/goals forms ("what are you looking to do?") surface language you'd never target from the inside ("track links", "track outbound links"). Add on-site search tracking, especially queries that return no results — unmet demand in your visitors' exact words.
3. **Mine communities and user-generated content** — industry forums and large Q&A/discussion communities first; then go beyond them: product reviews (hundreds per product), marketplace listings, even blog comments. People ask where no good content exists — that's the untapped map.
4. **Mine social networks** — topic-focused groups (near-continuous question streams) and professional-profile text for B2B language. Extract topics and phrasings, not exact keywords. Skip platforms dominated by spam.
5. **Validate with traffic potential** — most finds show low or no volume. Open the SERP and read the top page's traffic: one N/A-volume query's top page earned ~280 visits/mo from dozens of low-difficulty synonyms ("I'm sorry flowers"). Caveat: if one fat head term drives the page's traffic (a "best protein powder" page fed by its head term), the long-tails inherit that difficulty — pass on the topic.
6. **Multiply the winners** — use your 10 best finds as seeds in a keyword tool, pull the full list of keyword ideas, and filter by low KD; one reseeded list surfaced a niche technical topic ("www vs non-www" SEO implications) whose top page had only 39 referring domains.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Run the two pretense exercises (layperson; product-doesn't-exist) on your niche
- [ ] Add a goals/struggles question to onboarding; review responses monthly for phrasing
- [ ] Turn on on-site search tracking; log no-result queries
- [ ] Schedule time in 2–3 communities; harvest questions and product reviews
- [ ] Extract topics from social groups and profiles, not just exact keywords
- [ ] Validate every candidate via top-page traffic and referring domains
- [ ] Reseed the tool with your 10 favorites; filter low KD for a second wave

## Data points & benchmarks
- About 15% of daily Google searches have never been searched before (per Google) — new demand appears constantly
- Validation example: N/A-volume query whose top page earns ~280 visits/mo from low-KD synonyms
- Multiplier example: reseeded list surfaced a topic with only 39 referring domains to the top page
- Head-term caveat: pages fed by fat head terms make their long-tails de facto hard

## Mistakes to avoid
- Brainstorming like an insider (industry jargon) instead of like a customer
- Dismissing ideas at collection time for low volume — validate later via top pages
- Stopping at the two or three best-known communities; reviews and comments are richer
- Treating social posts as exact keywords rather than topic material
- Chasing a low-volume gem whose SERP is locked by a head-term page

## Key principles
- Customers describe problems; vendors describe solutions — keywords live in the problem language.
- Volume tools index the past; customers' language hints at demand tools haven't mapped yet.
- A keyword's real potential equals the total traffic of the page ranking #1.
- Underserved queries cluster: one community thread usually reveals a whole topic family.
