# Keyword Gap Analysis: Find Your Competitors' Keywords

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Competitors who rank for keywords you don't have done your research for you. The process: find your true search competitors (not your business competitors), run a content gap, score topics by business value and top-page traffic, then assess difficulty beyond the KD number — intent fit, authority gap, and link quality. A page-level variant finds subtopics missing from your existing content.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find search competitors** (three methods):
   - `related:<your-domain>` search operator — quick but hit-and-miss on young sites; vet results manually.
   - A competing-domains view in your SEO tool — sites sharing top-10 keywords with you; requires that you already rank for something.
   - Traffic share by domain for seed keywords — best for new sites or topic-specific competitor hunting; enter the topic's key phrases and see which domains harvest the most traffic from them.
2. **Run a content gap** — enter your domain plus 2–3 competitors in a content-gap tool; when results are noisy, tighten the intersection to keywords ALL competitors rank for. Compare like with like: use a competitor's blog subfolder (e.g., /blog) when analyzing editorial content.
3. **Score the topics** on three axes:
   - Business value 0–3 — how likely is the searcher to buy what you sell?
   - Search volume — a rough demand signal only.
   - Traffic potential — open the SERP and read the top page's traffic, not the keyword's volume (example: a 7.2K-searches keyword whose top page earned ~25x that from thousands of long-tails).
4. **Assess difficulty beyond KD**:
   - Search intent — can you realistically publish the content type the SERP demands? A blog can't satisfy a product-page SERP.
   - Authority gap — if top results come from far stronger sites than yours, discount your odds even at moderate KD.
   - Link quality — KD counts link quantity, not quality; a page with many weak or nofollowed links may be beatable (a KD 60 query whose top pages' links were mostly low quality).
   - Don't discard hard keywords — tier them into long-, medium-, and short-term targets.
5. **Page-level variant (find content gaps)** — for an underperforming page: identify competing pages (search your target keyword, or use traffic share by page), run the content gap against those competing URLs (leave your page out of the comparison), and read the results as neglected subtopics — then add sections for them. Missing subtopics suppress both main-keyword rankings and long-tail coverage (a guide missing the topic's definition couldn't rank for its "what is" variants).
6. **Repeat periodically** — set alerts on competitors' new rankings to spot what they start targeting.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List search competitors via 1–2 methods; don't assume business rivals = SERP rivals
- [ ] Run a content gap vs 2–3 competitors; tighten with the all-competitors intersection
- [ ] Score every candidate: business value (0–3), volume, top-page traffic
- [ ] For shortlisted keywords, check intent match, authority gap, and link quality of top pages
- [ ] Split targets into short/medium/long-term difficulty tiers
- [ ] Run a URL-mode gap on important underperforming pages; add missing subtopics
- [ ] Quarterly: re-run the analysis or alert on competitors' new keywords

## Data points & benchmarks
- Traffic-potential example: keyword at 7.2K searches/mo; top page ≈ 25x that traffic via related long-tails
- Business value scale: 0 (searcher will never buy) to 3 (searcher ready to buy)
- Difficulty re-check: KD 60 query whose top pages relied on mostly low-quality/nofollowed links — winnable

## Mistakes to avoid
- Analyzing business competitors instead of SERP competitors
- Comparing your blog against competitors' entire domains (use their /blog path)
- Judging topics by keyword volume instead of top-page traffic
- Stopping at the KD score without checking intent, authority, and link quality
- Writing off hard keywords instead of tiering them by timeframe
- Spawning new pages from page-level gaps instead of enriching the existing page

## Key principles
- Competitors' rankings are pre-validated keyword research — harvest them.
- Traffic potential lives on the top page, not in the keyword's volume.
- Difficulty is three-dimensional: intent fit, authority, link quality — KD is only the preview.
- Domain-level gaps fill the calendar; page-level gaps fix existing content.
