# Related Keyword Optimization: Subtopic Coverage Case Study (+129.7% Traffic)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
In one case study, a guide that already ranked #10 for its main keyword was optimized for related keywords representing missing SUBTOPICS — traffic grew 129.7% (~2,300 extra monthly visits). The method: page-level keyword gap analysis against competitors who beat you with fewer links, then add or un-bury the subtopics searchers want.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Pick the right pages** — pages already ranking positions 2–10 for their main keyword. They're close to winning and just need a push.
2. **Find competitors beating you** — check the SERP for your main keyword; shortlist similar pages getting more traffic than yours with FEWER referring domains (beatable without new links).
3. **Run a page-level keyword gap** — keywords competitors rank for vs. your page. Include ALL keywords (even ones you rank for) because you can still optimize for terms you already rank for weakly; strip out sitelinks and other SERP-feature rows if your tool includes them.
4. **Identify subtopic keywords, not synonyms** — "what is local SEO" is a subtopic worth covering; "local SEO services" is just another phrasing of the main topic. Common sense decides.
5. **Cluster gap keywords by shared terms** — term clustering exposes themes: 17 keywords containing "month" (all asking monthly pricing), ~70 containing "much" (~19% of the entire gap report), 16 containing "local", 9 containing "small". Each theme = one section to add or improve.
6. **Also flag traffic losers** — keywords where every competitor gets far more traffic than you (e.g., one publisher pulling 300+ more monthly visits for one term) — that's better-optimized competitor content.
7. **Optimize**: add the missing section under a clear keyword-bearing H2/H3, un-bury buried answers, restructure into skimmable steps, and put direct answers immediately under the question heading.

### What was changed (and results)
- "what is local SEO": fluff removed, definition improved, moved from H3 to H2 → jumped multiple positions
- "local SEO strategy": term never appeared in the post; added a short strategy section + mentioned it in the definition → bottom of page two to page one (+7 positions)
- "how to do local SEO": restructured existing content into steps under a new "How to do local SEO" H2 → #7 → #4
- Pricing guide (second test, +23% only): added "How much does SEO cost?" H2 with direct answer → won featured snippets for long-tail variations; moved monthly pricing data up top with a descriptive subheading → won the snippet; added "How much does local SEO cost?" H3 with a snappy figure → modest gains; new "small business" H3 → #15 → #5; new "SEO pricing models" H2 (existing content nested under it) → #5 → #1

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List pages ranking #2–10 for their primary keyword (organic rankings data, position filter)
- [ ] For each, find similar pages outranking it with fewer referring domains
- [ ] Run a page-level content gap (all keywords, excluding SERP-feature rows)
- [ ] Export and cluster gap keywords by terms to spot subtopic themes
- [ ] For each theme: add a section, un-bury the answer, or restructure — under a keyword-bearing heading with a direct answer immediately below
- [ ] Check the SERP's "things to know"-style modules for subtopic validation
- [ ] Budget 2–3 hours per page; prioritize pages with the biggest traffic-loser keywords

## Data points & benchmarks
- Result 1: +129.7% traffic (~2,300 more monthly visits) after one optimization session
- Result 2: +23% on a second page — results vary; nothing in SEO is guaranteed
- Case-study page: #10, 909 referring domains, ~813 monthly visits; competitors got more traffic with fewer links
- Term clustering signal: ~70 of ~370 gap keywords (19%) contained "much" — one subtopic = ~1/5 of the gap
- Content scores from optimization tools have only a weak correlation with rankings

## Mistakes to avoid
- Asking an AI chatbot for "related keywords" and sprinkling them in — most aren't subtopics, so they won't move rankings
- Trusting content-optimization tools blindly (e.g., a recommendation to add "favorite features" six times); treat recommendations as hypotheses
- Chasing a higher content score instead of covering real subtopics
- Leaving the answer buried under fluffy intros or unclear headings — searchers (and search engines) skim
- Shoehorning keyword variations: search engines know "SEO" and "search engine optimization" are the same thing

## Key principles
- Only related keywords that represent SUBTOPICS are worth optimizing for.
- Competitors getting more traffic with fewer links expose content (not authority) gaps — that's the opportunity.
- Best ROI: pages already on page one or close to it.
- Un-burying > adding: often the answer exists but is hidden under fluff or a vague heading.
- Clear question-shaped H2/H3 + immediate direct answer wins rankings and snippets.
