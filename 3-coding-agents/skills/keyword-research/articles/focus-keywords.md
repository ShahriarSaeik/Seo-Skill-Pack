# Focus Keywords: How to Choose and Use the Right One

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A focus keyword is the single main search term a page targets — the answer to "if someone searches one thing and lands here, what should it be?" Pick it with a 4-step process (ideas → metrics → SERP validation → site fit), use it in the key on-page spots once, and track it over time. Search engines never see your focus keyword; it exists to focus YOUR optimization.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find keyword ideas** — brainstorm 5–10 phrasings; expand with a keyword tool (matching terms, questions filter); check what competitor pages rank for; mine autocomplete and "people also ask"; ask AI assistants for synonyms/variations.
2. **Check the numbers**:
   - **Search volume** — demand check only; a lower-volume, highly relevant term beats a high-volume magnet for the wrong audience.
   - **Keyword difficulty (0–100)** — target KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites); KD 50+ needs strong backlinks and content.
   - **Traffic potential (TP)** — total traffic the #1 page gets from ALL its keywords; usually more useful than raw volume.
   - **Intent** — informational/commercial/transactional/navigational must match your content type ("best running shoes" is commercial — a running-form blog post won't rank).
3. **Validate against the SERP** — actually search the keyword: Who ranks (all very high-authority giants = hard; a mix that includes mid-authority sites = opportunity)? What format ranks (listicles vs guides vs product pages)? Does real intent override your assumption? Is there a gap (outdated guides, uncovered subtopics) you can exploit?
4. **Confirm site fit** — relevant to your business/audience; not already targeted by another of your pages (if it is: refresh that page or differentiate intent — never cannibalize); and you can genuinely create top-quality content on it.

### Usage rules
- ONE focus keyword per page (title/H1 can only carry one main idea). Singular/plural and close variations count as one (same parent topic).
- Place it in: title tag (near the front), H1, URL slug, meta description, first 100 words, relevant H2/H3s, main image alt text, body copy where natural.
- Don't: stuff it into every heading, chase keyword density, or sacrifice readability for exact matches — modern search understands synonyms and context.

### Tracking cadence
- New content: check weekly for the first 3 months. Established pages: monthly. Full audit quarterly.
- Stuck below page two after 6 months → keyword too competitive or content needs strengthening. Ranking but no traffic → intent mismatch or weak CTR. Declining rankings → refresh or counter a competitor's newer, better piece.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Assign exactly one focus keyword per page before writing
- [ ] Score candidates on volume, KD, TP, and intent — reject intent mismatches immediately
- [ ] Search the keyword and confirm domains, format, and intent leave you an opening
- [ ] Check your own site for existing pages on the term (refresh or differentiate)
- [ ] Include the keyword in title, H1, slug, first 100 words, and meta description
- [ ] Set rank tracking for the focus keyword plus page-level traffic and total ranking keywords
- [ ] Revisit focus keywords periodically — intent and competition drift

## Data points & benchmarks
- An older study of 3 million queries: the average #1 page also ranks in the top 10 for ~1,000 other keywords — the focus keyword is the North Star, not the whole sky
- KD benchmarks: under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites); established sites can push higher; 50+ = strong backlinks required
- One study found AI Overviews cut click-through rate for top-ranking content by roughly 58% on average (varies by query and study) — position 1 pays less than it used to
- Review cadence: weekly (first 3 months) → monthly → quarterly audit; 6 months below page 2 = rethink

## Mistakes to avoid
- Choosing keywords that are too broad ("marketing" vs "content marketing strategy for B2B startups")
- Ignoring search intent and forcing your preferred content type onto the SERP
- Targeting the same keyword from multiple pages (cannibalization — both lose)
- Obsessing over exact-match phrasing in 2026-era semantic search
- Targeting terms far beyond your site's authority ("insurance" on a brand-new site)
- Setting and forgetting: a perfect focus keyword two years ago may be wrong today

## Key principles
- Focus keyword = target keyword = primary keyword: one main term, one page, one intent.
- The focus keyword is a mental model for you, not a signal for the engine.
- TP usually matters more than volume; intent matters more than both.
- Think topic-first, keyword-second in the AI search era: the focus keyword is the entry point, comprehensive coverage keeps you ranking.
