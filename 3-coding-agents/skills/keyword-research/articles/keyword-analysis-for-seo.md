# Keyword Analysis for SEO: Deciding if (And How) to Target a Keyword

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Before targeting any keyword, run it through a 4-question filter: Will it send enough traffic? Can you satisfy search intent? Does it have business value? Can you realistically rank? Choosing keywords on search volume alone is a mistake.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Will it send enough traffic?**
   - Use the TOP-RANKING PAGE's traffic as proxy, not keyword volume. Volume can exceed traffic (instant answers, SERP features absorb clicks) or undersell it (page ranks for many keywords).
   - Check the volume trend (a keyword research tool / Google Trends) — declining topics won't deliver current traffic by the time you rank.
2. **Can I give searchers what they want?**
   - Match the content type of page one (blog post, video, tool/calculator, product page). If SERP demands an interactive tool and you lack dev/design resources, skip.
3. **Does it have business value?**
   - Map to buyer's journey (earlier stage = less value) OR use a Business Potential score 0–3 (how easily you can pitch your product in the content).
4. **Can I realistically rank?** Three sub-questions:
   - **Backlinks needed:** a KD score hints at the rough number of links needed for the top 10 (not #1; ignores link quality). Audit top pages' backlinks with link-quality filters — in one example, filtering cut a "schema generator" page's links from 2.6K referring domains to 879 (~66% junk).
   - **SERP authority:** Eyeball the authority of ranking sites. Same or lower authority than yours in top results = good sign. High-authority sites benefit from internal-link juice and brand familiarity.
   - **Content quality / E-E-A-T:** Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. Top results written/reviewed by credentialed experts (e.g., doctors for "lump on neck") are near-impossible to beat; stock-photo list posts without firsthand experience are easy targets.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] For each candidate: check TP (Traffic Potential) of top page instead of raw volume
- [ ] Check search trend is flat or rising, not declining
- [ ] Open the SERP (incognito / VPN / a location-simulating browser extension) and note required content type + resources
- [ ] Score business potential 0–3; drop or deprioritize 0–1s
- [ ] Read the KD score as a hint for backlinks needed; audit top pages' links with quality filters
- [ ] Filter phrase-match keyword ideas for terms where at least one top-5 site has authority no higher than yours to find winnable keywords
- [ ] Assess whether you can out-E-E-A-T current top results (firsthand experience is the easiest differentiator)

## Data points & benchmarks
- "pheasant breast recipes": 1.9K searches/mo but top page gets far less traffic
- "backlink checker": 5.9K searches/mo but top page gets ~27K visits
- "schema generator" top page: ~2.6K referring domains raw → 879 after link-quality filters (−66%)
- ~2/3 of SEOs surveyed in one industry poll factor website authority into ranking chances
- Familiar brands (Target, Macy's) rank for "black cardigan" with few backlinks due to brand strength
- "days between dates": 100% of first page = interactive calculators (needs dev resources); "excel difference between two dates": blog posts (needs only a writer)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Choosing keywords by search volume alone
- Comparing raw backlink counts without filtering for link quality
- Analyzing personalized SERPs instead of incognito/location-adjusted results
- Targeting keywords where content type exceeds your production capability
- Underestimating brand strength of high-authority SERP competitors

## Key principles
- Traffic potential of the top page > keyword search volume.
- A keyword must make business sense AND be winnable — value without rankability (or vice versa) is worthless.
- Firsthand experience is the cheapest E-E-A-T edge you can add.
