# How To Do Keyword Clustering the Easy Way

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Keyword clustering = grouping keywords with the same/similar intent so you target them with ONE page instead of many. Cluster by whether Google ranks the same pages for them (parent topic), sanity-check edge cases with metrics, and use cluster members as search-intent intel for content briefs.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Build a keyword list** — enter 5–10 seed terms in a keyword research tool and pull phrase-match keyword ideas (a coffee seed list can return millions of keywords). Filter first if needed: low KD, low-authority sites in the top 5, or include-filters ("best", "review" for affiliate).
2. **Cluster** — use the tool's parent-topic grouping (or SERP-overlap clustering) to get instant clusters with total cluster volume + parent metrics (KD, volume, traffic potential or TP). Parent topic = the keyword sending the most traffic to the top-ranking page.
3. **Verify edge cases** — clustering is never perfect. "best chocolate cake recipe with coffee" got clustered under "chocolate cake," but the SERP metrics say otherwise: chocolate cake pages averaged authority score 74 / 318 linking domains vs pages for the coffee variant at 33 / 11 domains → targeting them separately is easier and smarter.
4. **Quick 2-keyword test** — compare the two keywords' SERPs with a SERP-similarity score out of 100 (high = same page; low = separate pages; middle = judgment call).

**Overlooked benefit:** cluster members reveal intent nuances — "best tasting coffee maker," "best coffee maker for home," "best coffee maker for the money" tell you searchers want HOME brewers that taste great at a good price. Feed these into the content brief.

**Term clustering (different):** term clustering groups keywords by shared words — use for TREND/niche discovery, not page planning. E.g., "hotels" clusters: near, beach, city, downtown, airport; low-KD "jacuzzi" cluster = 173K US monthly searches = affiliate niche idea.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Generate keyword list from seeds; pre-filter (KD, site authority, modifiers) before clustering
- [ ] Cluster by parent topic; note each cluster's combined volume and parent's KD/TP
- [ ] For borderline clusters, compare average authority + linking domains of the two intents' top pages
- [ ] Use a SERP-similarity score for pairwise decisions
- [ ] Mine cluster members for intent signals → subheadings and angle for the brief
- [ ] Use term (shared-word) clustering separately to spot trending modifiers and niche opportunities

## Data points & benchmarks
- Coffee seeds → millions of keywords in phrase-match keyword ideas
- One clustering-tool test: 4,703 keywords clustered in 51 minutes; more than half of the top 25 clusters matched a keyword research tool's own grouping
- Chocolate cake example: "chocolate cake recipe" avg authority 74 / 318 domains vs "with coffee" avg 33 / 11 domains
- "hotels with jacuzzi" term cluster: 173K combined US monthly searches

## Mistakes to avoid
- Blindly trusting cluster output — always sanity-check ambiguous groups
- Creating one page per keyword variation (the anti-pattern clustering solves)
- Ignoring cluster members' intent nuances when writing content
- Confusing term clustering (trend discovery) with intent clustering (page planning)

## Key principles
- If Google ranks the same pages for multiple keywords, those keywords belong on one page.
- Parent topic = keyword sending the most traffic to the top-ranking page.
- Clusters are decision support, not decisions — metrics of top-ranking pages break ties.
- Clustering doubles as search-intent research: the cluster IS the content brief seed.
