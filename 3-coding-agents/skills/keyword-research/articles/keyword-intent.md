# Keyword Intent: The First Filter in Keyword Research

- **Updated**: 2026-10-03

## TL;DR
Keyword intent is the "why" behind a query, applied as a filter DURING keyword research — before volume, difficulty, or content planning. If a keyword's intent isn't something your site can realistically serve and convert, cut it no matter how attractive the volume looks.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Understand the distinction**: "search intent" = optimizing content to match what the SERP rewards; "keyword intent" = the same concept applied one step earlier, deciding whether a keyword belongs in your strategy at all.
2. **Classify into the four main buckets** (see below) and decide target/skip per bucket.
3. **Check for the two buckets most models miss**: local intent and branded intent.
4. **Handle mixed-intent keywords**: pick one intent to optimize for, or create separate content for each intent.
5. **Verify at scale**: use SERP intent breakdowns (what % of ranking pages fit each intent) and monitor for intent drift on priority keywords over time. AI assistants connected to keyword data can group whole lists by intent and flag "micro-intents."

### The four main buckets
- **Informational** ("how to grow tomatoes") — majority of search volume in most niches; the backbone of content strategies. Target when traffic potential is high, topical authority matters, and there's a natural product angle. Skip when the intent is too broad ("what is gardening"), traffic potential is low, or there's no credible product fit.
- **Commercial investigation** ("best garden hose," "raised bed vs in-ground") — where most strategic value lives for ecommerce/affiliate. Assess with a business potential score (0–3): how naturally can you present your product as the solution?
- **Transactional** ("buy garden hose online") — convert well but are the most competitive and expensive to win. Target organically if you can rank; bid in paid search if you can't.
- **Navigational** ("a brand's website") — the only navigational terms worth owning are your own brand terms; that's about protecting your presence.

### The two missed buckets
- **Local intent** ("dentist near me") — triggers map packs, business profiles, and local listings, not standard organic content. Needs a local SEO response (profile optimization, citations, reviews), not a blog post.
- **Branded intent** — includes brand names with commercial/transactional signals: "{brand} alternative" (commercial), "{brand} pricing" (transactional), "{brand} vs {brand}" (comparative commercial). Often requires a competitive approach; paid search can be a valid play.

### Mixed-intent keywords
Examples: "project management software" (informational + commercial), "best deals on running shoes" (informational/commercial/transactional depending on buyer proximity). Options:
- Optimize one page for one chosen intent, or
- Cover the same keyword with separate pages per intent — e.g., an ecommerce page for the commercial/transactional side plus blog posts (DIY ideas, couples' costume ideas) linking to products, covering the full buying journey.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Before scoring volume/KD, ask: can my site serve AND convert this intent?
- [ ] Sort the keyword list into informational / commercial / transactional / navigational
- [ ] Flag local-intent keywords and route them to a local SEO workflow, not content
- [ ] Mine branded queries ("alternative," "pricing," "vs") for commercial-intent opportunities
- [ ] For mixed-intent keywords, decide: one intent per page, or multiple pages per intent
- [ ] Check the SERP's intent mix (percentage of results per intent) when unsure
- [ ] Re-check intent on priority keywords periodically — intent drifts

## Data points & benchmarks
- Informational keywords make up the majority of search volume in most niches
- Business potential scored on a 0–3 scale (how naturally your product fits the content)
- SERP intent breakdowns show what fraction of top results fit each intent — a mixed split (e.g., ~50/50 informational/commercial) signals mixed intent

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating intent as the last check instead of the first filter — high volume never rescues mismatched intent
- Chasing broad informational terms ("what is X") that attract everyone and convert no one
- Publishing informational content with no credible product angle just to fill a calendar
- Applying a standard blog strategy to local-intent keywords
- Assuming all branded queries are navigational — many carry commercial or transactional intent

## Key principles
- Keyword intent is your first filter, not your last: it shapes everything downstream.
- Intent must match something your site can serve AND convert — traffic potential alone is never enough.
- Local and branded intent behave fundamentally differently and need their own playbooks.
- SERP composition is ground truth for ambiguous intent; re-verify it over time.
