# How to Find and Use Competitor Keywords

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Competitor keyword research is the easiest form of keyword research: if rivals rank for (or pay for) a keyword, it may be worth targeting too. Pull their organic and paid keywords, exclude brand terms, filter for popular/low-KD terms, then cluster and either create new pages or optimize existing ones.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Finding competitor keywords:**
1. **All their organic keywords** — a competitive-analysis tool → competitor domain → organic keywords (sorted by traffic). Exclude brand terms with a "doesn't contain" filter (the competitor's brand name + misspellings). For smaller sites: Volume ≥500 + KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites).
2. **Keywords they rank for, you don't** — run a keyword-gap (content-gap) comparison: your domain vs the competitor, showing keywords they rank for that you don't. Add Volume/KD filters.
3. **Keywords MULTIPLE competitors rank for, you don't** — same comparison with several competitors; filter to keywords where ALL of them rank (strongest signal).
4. **Their paid keywords** — the same tool's paid keywords data. If they pay repeatedly for a keyword, it's likely profitable. Review their ad copy and landing pages (ad history or a manual SERP check).

**Using them (3 options):**
1. **Create pages** — but first cluster by parent topic: export competitor keywords → group them (by parent topic or SERP overlap) → one page per cluster (e.g., "what is digital marketing" + "digital marketing definition" → single "digital marketing" page).
2. **Optimize existing pages** — look for parent topics where you already have a page; add missing subtopic sections (e.g., add "press release example" and "press release format" sections to your existing press-release-template post).
3. **Bid on their paid keywords** — target the same keyword in Google Ads if relevant; inspect their ad copy and landing pages first.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Identify 3–5 real SEO competitors
- [ ] Pull each one's organic keywords; exclude their brand + misspellings
- [ ] Run a content-gap comparison vs your domain; repeat with multiple competitors; tighten to keywords all of them rank for
- [ ] Filter everything to Volume ≥500, KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- [ ] Cluster the exported keywords by parent topic
- [ ] For each cluster: no page → create; page exists → add missing subtopic sections
- [ ] Review their paid keywords; note repeatedly-bid keywords, ad copy, and landing pages

## Data points & benchmarks
- Smaller-site filter heuristic: Volume ≥500 + KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- One email-platform example: most organic traffic from its own brand term — always filter brand terms first
- Keywords every competitor ranks for = validated demand

## Mistakes to avoid
- Forgetting to exclude competitor brand terms (unwinnable navigational keywords)
- Creating a page per competitor keyword instead of per cluster
- Ignoring existing pages that just need subtopic sections
- Dismissing paid keywords — repeated bidding signals profitability

## Key principles
- Competitors already validated the keywords — your job is filtering for fit and rankability.
- Keywords ALL competitors rank for are the highest-confidence opportunities.
- Cluster before you build: one page per intent, not per keyword.
