# How to Find Low-Competition Keywords for SEO

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Find low-competition keywords with a 3-step filter (brainstorm seeds → phrase-match keyword ideas → KD 0–10 filter), but ALWAYS verify manually: KD only models backlink quantity, so also check search intent, SERP authority/brand equity, and link QUALITY before committing.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Quick 3-step discovery:**
1. **Brainstorm topics** — 5–10 broad themes your audience searches (no exact phrases needed).
2. **Expand with a tool** — pull phrase-match keyword ideas from a keyword research tool (volumes + metrics).
3. **Filter KD 0–10** — add a minimum volume filter to narrow. Five seeds for "computers" niche → 167,000+ low-KD keywords.

**Verify true competition (3 checks beyond KD):**
1. **Search intent** — use modifier cheat sheet ("buy/cheap" = transactional; "how/what/where" = informational; "best" = commercial investigation; brand names = navigational). Trap: "best buy coffee makers" has KD 1 but is navigational — Best Buy holds all 10 positions; unwinnable. Use an include-any-word filter for modifier-based buckets; filter to question keywords for informational.
2. **Website authority / brand equity** — "black sandals" has KD 1 but the first page averages a domain authority score of 86 (lowest 76). Big brands dominate because that's where people want to shop. Ranking difficulty is RELATIVE: same keyword is low-competition for Target, high-competition for a small store.
3. **Backlink quality** — KD counts domains, not quality. Compare: page with 14 referring domains (RDs; 94% followed, relevant, authoritative, relevant anchors) vs page with 92 RDs (96% nofollow, coupon-site links, naked URLs) — the 92-RD page is actually EASIER to outrank.

**Bonus: don't avoid high-KD keywords entirely** — high-KD = pages with many backlinks = great LINK BAIT topics. Filter question keywords for KD 50+, create the best resource, then do outreach to sites linking to existing pages (earn links on merit; no paid or swapped links without rel="sponsored"/nofollow). Two benefits even without ranking: build site authority, and pass it via internal links to money pages (the "middleman" technique).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List 5–10 seed topics
- [ ] Pull phrase-match keyword ideas with a KD ≤10 filter + min volume filter
- [ ] Check intent bucket via modifiers AND actual SERP (avoid navigational traps)
- [ ] Eyeball the authority of the top 10; skip if all far above yours and brand-dominated
- [ ] Manually audit top pages' backlinks: follow ratio, relevance, authority, anchors
- [ ] For blogs: filter question keywords by KD for easy informational wins
- [ ] Separately: build a link-bait list from KD 50+ questions in your niche

## Data points & benchmarks
- 5 computer-part seeds → 167K+ keywords with KD 0–10
- "black sandals": KD 1, average first-page domain authority 86, minimum 76
- "best buy coffee makers": KD 1, navigational — Best Buy ranks #1–10
- "best coffee for cold brew" (competitor: 14 strong RDs) is HARDER than a keyword whose competitor has 92 weak RDs (96% nofollow from coupon sites)
- One common KD formulation: a trimmed average of referring domains pointing at the top 10 pages

## Mistakes to avoid
- Trusting KD scores blindly — they ignore intent, brand equity, and link quality
- Assuming modifier words always signal intent ("best buy" trap)
- Comparing raw RD counts without checking follow status and link relevance
- Never attacking high-KD keywords — they make ideal link bait
- Treating keyword difficulty as absolute rather than relative to your site

## Key principles
- "Ranking difficulty is relative" — same keyword, different difficulty per site.
- Low KD means you probably won't need many backlinks — nothing more.
- High-competition keywords attract links; use them to build authority that flows to low-competition money pages (the "middleman" technique).
- Always manually review the top-ranking pages before creating content.
