# How to Do Keyword Optimization for SEO (3 Steps)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Keyword optimization = making a page maximally relevant to a query. Three steps: pick the right keyword (traffic potential, business value, difficulty, intent, your expertise), align with search intent (Three Cs), then execute on-page best practices — without shoehorning keywords.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Step 1 — Optimize for the RIGHT keyword.** Evaluate:
- **Traffic potential** (TP metric): total traffic of the #1 ranking page across ALL its keywords — better than raw search volume (TP for one example keyword was ~10X its search volume)
- **Business value**: score keywords 0-3 by how well attracting those searchers serves your goals (product-led content scale)
- **Keyword Difficulty** (KD 0-100, backlink-based): new/weak sites should target KD under ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- **Search intent**: can you realistically match the dominant intent? (non-ecommerce sites can't win "women's shoes" — all product pages)
- **Your expertise**: E-E-A-T matters, especially YMYL; write from first-hand experience

**Step 2 — Align with search intent (Three Cs):** Content type (blog post/product/category/landing), Content format (how-to, list, review, comparison), Content angle (USP like "before 30", "fast", "according to experts"). Mixed-intent SERPs: check PAA boxes and "Things to know" for minority-intent opportunities.

**Step 3 — On-page execution:**
- Cover subtopics from top-ranking pages (a content-gap comparison in any SEO tool)
- Make content digestible: simple words, skimmable, images
- Title: keyword included, informative, attractive, right length
- H1 consistent with title tag (identical is fine for blog posts; product pages can differ)
- Meta description: newspaper-headline style, unique per page, take cues from search ads
- H2-H6 for subheadings creating skimmable hierarchy
- Clean URL: `/how-to-peel-a-banana` not `/how-to-peel-a-banana-like-a-monkey-the-right-way-10-2022`
- Images: relevant/original, descriptive filenames + alt text, near relevant text
- Internal + external links for context and E-E-A-T; internal links pass equity tactically
- Featured snippets: answer early, succinctly, structured, jargon-free
- Rich results: schema markup → test with a rich results testing tool → Search Console URL Inspection → request indexing (FAQ rich results are now limited to well-known government/health sites and HowTo rich results were removed)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Check TP of target keyword (traffic to top page, not just volume)
- [ ] Score keyword's business value before optimizing
- [ ] Check KD; if site is weak, stay under KD ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- [ ] Confirm you can match the dominant intent type
- [ ] Run a content-gap comparison on top pages (leave your own page out) → extract subtopic keywords; add your page to find gaps in existing content
- [ ] Target keyword in title + H1; unique compelling meta description
- [ ] Featured snippet hunt: filter keywords in a keyword research tool by the "featured snippet" SERP feature
- [ ] Crawl and review old content for thin or outdated sections before optimizing it
- [ ] Take Google's helpful content self-assessment when done

## Data points & benchmarks
- KD under ~20 = realistic target for sites without strong backlink profiles (lower for brand-new sites)
- TP vs volume: "submit website to search engines" TP ≈ 10X its search volume
- Google helpful content questions: does content show first-hand expertise? did you enter a niche only for search traffic?
- Google's SEO starter guide: use simple, descriptive URLs

## Mistakes to avoid
- Shoehorning keywords into every second sentence ("optimized" text you can smell)
- Believing LSI/TF-IDF myths — no need to sprinkle or count related keywords; DO mention related terms/entities naturally for coverage
- Judging keywords by search volume alone
- Entering niches without real expertise just for traffic
- Describing a page as a recipe but only listing ingredients (Google: provide the complete amount of content)

## Key principles
- "The aim of keyword optimization is not to game the system… The goal is to help Google and searchers find and understand your content."
- Pages rank for hundreds of keywords while optimized for one — traffic potential > search volume.
- The messenger matters as much as the message (E-E-A-T).
- Cover common subtopics of top pages, then add unique value — don't just copy.
