# How Many SEO Keywords Should a Page Really Target?

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Target ONE primary keyword per page plus enough secondary keywords (as subtopics) to cover the topic in full. Google ranks a well-optimized single-topic page for hundreds of variations automatically — targeting multiple primary topics on one page splits relevance and fails.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Definitions:** Primary keyword = the page's main topic (one only). Secondary keywords = closely related synonyms/subtopics/long-tail variations, best used as subtopics.

**Why one primary keyword (3 reasons):**
1. Pages need one clearly defined topic — Google shows the most specific, relevant result
2. Google catches close variations, synonyms, and misspellings automatically (SERP comparison shows near-identical results for paraphrased queries)
3. One optimized page often ranks for ~1,000 keywords (older study): one "seo basics" article targeted one 1,400/mo keyword yet ranks for 463 keywords (156 in top 10) and gets ~8,600 visits/mo

**Finding primary keywords:**
- A keyword research tool → matching terms → browse parent topics → weigh KD + traffic potential (TP) + fit
- Content-gap comparison of top-ranking pages (leave your own site out), keeping keywords several of them share; min volume 100, max KD ~20 (lower for brand-new sites)
- Competitor organic keywords (any SEO tool)

**Finding secondary keywords:**
- A keyword research tool → keywords the top 10 pages also rank for → pick subtopic-sounding keywords
- Content-gap comparison WITH your page included → missing subtopics vs. competing pages

**Placing keywords:**
- Primary keyword → determines search intent; goes in title, H1 (can be identical), URL
- Secondary keywords → H2-H6 headings where natural
- Don't stuff; don't force LSI/synonyms; no density targets

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pick exactly one primary keyword (check KD/TP)
- [ ] Determine intent from SERPs: content type, format, angle
- [ ] Extract secondary keywords (related terms; what the top 10 also rank for)
- [ ] Turn secondary keywords into H2-H6 subtopic headings
- [ ] Check top-ranking pages' heading structure (a browser SEO extension shows it) for subtopics keyword tools miss
- [ ] Add unique value beyond competitors (own research, fresher data)
- [ ] For existing pages: run a content-gap comparison including your URL to find missing subtopics

## Data points & benchmarks
- Average #1-ranking page also ranks top 10 for ~1,000 other keywords (older industry study)
- Case: "seo basics" (1,400 US searches/mo) → 463 keywords, 156 top-10, ~8,600 monthly visits — 6X the primary keyword's volume
- Google auto-consolidates variations: "seo basics," "how to use seo," "beginner's guide to seo," "getting started with seo" all served by one page

## Mistakes to avoid
- Targeting multiple primary topics on one page
- Manually targeting every synonym/variation (Google handles it)
- Keyword stuffing or chasing keyword density / LSI scores
- Copying competitor subtopics without adding anything new

## Key principles
- "Pick just one primary keyword and enough secondary keywords to cover a given topic in full."
- Primary = topic of the book; secondaries = chapters/subtopics.
- Rankings for hundreds of keywords come free from topical completeness, not from targeting each keyword.
- Google tends not to reward content that "brings nothing new to the table" — match subtopics AND add unique value.
