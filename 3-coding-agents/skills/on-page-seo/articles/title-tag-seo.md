# What Are Title Tags? How To Write Them for SEO

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Title tags are a tiny ranking factor but a major CTR lever. Build them in 3 steps: verify the best keyword via parent topic, add natural secondary keywords, then apply the ABC formula (Adjective + Benefit + Confidence Booster) — and keep under ~60 characters / 600px width.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Step 1 — Target the best keyword.** Check the parent topic in a keyword research tool (keyword sending most traffic to the top-ranking page). If a parent keyword has more searches (e.g. "best video doorbell" 19K/mo vs "best smart doorbell" 1.5K/mo), target that instead.
**Step 2 — Add secondary keywords naturally.** Look up the top-ranking page's organic keywords in an SEO tool; incorporate popular co-occurring terms (e.g. "camera") only if it makes sense, reads well, and keeps meaning.
**Step 3 — Make it clickable with the ABC formula:**
- **A**djective (best, proven, effortless…)
- **B**enefit ("To Keep You Safe")
- **C**onfidence Booster ("in 2026 (Tried & Tested)")
Take inspiration from current top results, don't copy verbatim. Check length; reword if over.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Include target keyword in the title tag
- [ ] Check parent topic before committing to a keyword
- [ ] Add 1 popular secondary keyword where natural (3-question test: sense? reads well? meaning kept?)
- [ ] Apply ABC formula for clickability
- [ ] Keep between 50-60 characters; prioritize pixel width (<600px) over character count
- [ ] Consistent casing, correct punctuation, front-loaded keywords
- [ ] Review/refresh titles post-publication, especially after traffic drops (may signal intent shift)
- [ ] Prioritize "opportunity" pages ranking #4-10 for title rewrites
- [ ] Use templates for scale: `Product | Category`, `Subcategory | Category`, `Service | Location`
- [ ] Give top-traffic templated pages a custom VIP title
- [ ] Don't just duplicate the H1 — differentiate for keywords/CTR/brand, but keep topic and intent aligned

## Data points & benchmarks
- Google truncates/rewrites titles exceeding ~600 pixels width (~60 characters)
- Common title tag issues across 1M+ sites (one older site-audit crawler dataset):
  - Mismatched title tag vs SERP title: 68.54% of sites
  - Title too long: 63.19%
  - Title too short: 32.76%
  - Missing/empty title tag: 5.55%
  - Multiple title tags: 3.04%
- Google has said it may rewrite titles that are too long, stuffed, or don't match the page
- Example title in article: 46 chars = 440px — safe from truncation

## Mistakes to avoid
- Keyword stuffing titles (listed in Google's spam policies; penalty risk)
- Clickbait: overpromising in title, under-delivering in content (helpful content system generates sitewide signal)
- Shoehorning secondary keywords that make the title weird
- Confusing title tag with H1 (title tag = `<head>`, SERP display; H1 = on-page heading)
- Worrying excessively about benign rewrites (e.g. Google trimming brand name)

## Key principles
- Title tags matter for two reasons: tiny ranking factor (Mueller-confirmed) and big CTR impact.
- An accurate, well-matched title is more likely to be kept than rewritten; Google tests and adjusts title links over time.
- Optimize even though Google rewrites: users may prefer your version, and original tags persist in the HTML where AI systems may read them.
- Drops in traffic tied to titles often indicate a shift in search intent — rewrite the title to match the new intent.
