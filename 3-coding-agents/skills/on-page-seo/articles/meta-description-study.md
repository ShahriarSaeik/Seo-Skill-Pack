# How Often Does Google Rewrite Meta Descriptions? (Study Summary)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One older study compared hardcoded meta descriptions vs. Google snippets across 20,000 keywords: Google rewrites 62.78% of the time, so your description shows only ~37% of the time. Rewrite rates vary over time and by query, so treat the figures as directional. Still worth writing — but prioritize pages that get traffic, were built to rank, or get shared socially.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Study methodology: compared hardcoded meta descriptions against actual Google desktop snippets for 20K keywords (192,656 unique pages).

Key findings and implications:
1. **25.02% of top-ranking pages have NO meta description at all** — missing descriptions are common even among winners (weak 0.28 Spearman correlation with position)
2. **Google rewrites 62.78% of descriptions overall** — hardcoded versions appear only ~37% of the time
3. **Rewrites: 59.65% for fat-head keywords vs. 65.62% for long-tails** — hardcoded descriptions are written around the head keyword, so they're less relevant to long-tail queries; expect the gap to grow as intent understanding improves
4. **Truncation barely matters** — Google rewrote 61.46% of too-long descriptions vs. 63.69% of properly-sized ones. (40.61% of studied pages had too-long descriptions anyway)

**Prioritization framework (for large sites):** write/refresh meta descriptions only for pages that:
- Already get organic traffic
- Were created to rank in Google
- Are likely to be shared on social media (meta description = social snippet fallback without OG tags)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Set expectations: ~63% of the time Google picks its own snippet
- [ ] Write descriptions around the primary/head keyword (shown most for those queries)
- [ ] Don't stress about length-vs-rewrite; keep under limits mainly for the 37% + social
- [ ] Prioritize: traffic pages → ranking-focused pages → socially-shared pages
- [ ] On huge sites, skip zero-traffic pages entirely

## Data points & benchmarks
- 62.78% rewrite rate overall (desktop, 20K keywords)
- Fat-head rewrite: 59.65% | Long-tail rewrite: 65.62%
- Truncated descriptions rewritten 61.46% vs. 63.69% for others (near-equal)
- 25.02% of top-ranking pages lack a meta description entirely
- 40.61% of 192,656 pages had descriptions that were too long
- Hardcoded descriptions displayed ~37% of the time on average

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming your crafted description always shows (it shows ~1/3 of the time)
- Rewriting metas sitewide on large sites instead of prioritizing
- Believing exact length compliance prevents rewrites (data says it doesn't)
- Forgetting the meta description doubles as the social snippet fallback

## Key principles
- Meta descriptions are a CTR asset, not a ranking factor — worth writing despite the rewrite rate.
- Google rewrites more for long-tails because hardcoded text targets head terms — write for your primary keyword.
- Prioritization beats perfection: traffic pages first, everything else rarely.
