# What Are Sitelinks, Their Benefits, & How To Influence Them

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Sitelinks are algorithmically generated links to your pages (or on-page sections) shown beneath search results — usually branded queries, up to six for the top result, or up to four one-line links (including #fragment jumps) on many query types. You can't edit them, but you can influence them through site structure, internal linking, headings, and relevance signals. They add SERP real estate and speed up user navigation.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Types:**
- Paid sitelink assets (formerly "extensions") on ads — fully controllable text/URLs.
- Organic sitelinks — up to six links under the #1 result, mostly branded queries.
- One-line sitelinks — up to four links (carousel variants show more) on many query types; can jump to in-page fragments.
- Sitelinks search box — deprecated November 2024 (was a site search box within results).

**Why they matter:** more visible space, stands out from competitors, and sends users straight to what they want — on one brand's query, 12.9% of clicks went to sitelinks instead of the homepage.

**How to influence (no direct controls; the demote tool was removed):**
1. **Site structure** — clear (not overly flat) architecture helps the algorithm pick meaningful sections.
2. **Internal linking** — which pages you link to, anchor text, link placement, and breadcrumbs shape candidates.
3. **Noindex** — removes a page from sitelink eligibility (blunt instrument; rarely worth it).
4. **Legacy search-box controls** — the sitelinks search box and its `nositelinkssearchbox` opt-out no longer apply (retired November 2024).
5. **Usefulness & relevance:**
   - Hreflang helps show locale-appropriate sitelinks.
   - Headings (and a table of contents) frequently become one-line sitelinks.
   - Popular search demand around your brand tends to correlate with the pages chosen (unconfirmed).
   - Click/user signals may play a role per a competing engine's patent (unconfirmed).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Structure the site with clear, logically nested sections
- [ ] Link to important pages prominently (nav, breadcrumbs, in-content) with descriptive anchors
- [ ] Use descriptive H2/H3 headings; add a table of contents on long posts
- [ ] Implement hreflang for multi-language sitelink targeting
- [ ] Noindex pages you never want appearing as sitelinks (rare cases)
- [ ] Audit which keywords trigger your sitelinks and which pages appear; adjust links/structure accordingly
- [ ] Track sitelink CTR share on branded queries as a navigation-health metric

## Data points & benchmarks
- Sitelinks appeared in ~1.8% of SERPs in one large US keyword database (older figure); nearly every branded query has them.
- ~67% of a major encyclopedia site's organic keywords show sitelinks.
- 12.9% of clicks on one brand's query went to sitelink URLs.
- One-line sitelinks often mirror page headings verbatim.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Expecting to choose or edit organic sitelinks — fully algorithmic.
- Flat architecture with weak internal linking — leaves the algorithm guessing.
- Noindexing pages just to swap sitelinks — usually costs more than it gains.
- Ignoring heading quality — headings are a direct one-line sitelink source.

## Key principles
- Sitelinks are earned by structural clarity + internal link prominence + user relevance.
- Influence, don't control: improve architecture and linking, then observe what changes.
- Branded SERP real estate is free visibility — audit it like any other asset.
