# AI Assistants Prefer to Cite "Fresher" Content (17M Citations Analyzed)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Across ~17 million citations in one study, AI assistants cite content that is 25.7% "fresher" than what organic Google results surface (avg 1,064 days old vs. 1,432). ChatGPT has the strongest recency preference (~458 days newer than organic); Google's AI Overviews mirror classic search and favor long-lived pages. Regular updates can help AI visibility — but the average cited page is still ~3 years old, so freshness is a tiebreaker, not a substitute for quality.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The measurement protocol:
1. **Extract cited URLs** per platform (chatbot citations, in-text references, AI Overview top-3 links, organic SERP results).
2. **Compute two ages per URL** — days since first publication (first crawl date) and days since last update (last publish date).
3. **Compare platforms against the organic SERP baseline** to isolate each assistant's freshness tilt.
4. **Check citation ordering** — correlate citation position with content age to detect deliberate age-based ordering.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a refresh cadence for pages that earn AI citations (update substance, not just dates)
- [ ] Prioritize freshness plays for chatbot surfaces (strongest recency preference), not Google AIOs
- [ ] Keep evergreen pages alive: ~3-year-old content still dominates citations — don't delete old assets
- [ ] Publish genuinely new data/updates rather than cosmetic date bumps (search engines warn against it)
- [ ] Weigh opportunity cost: new high-quality content often beats hyper-frequent updating

## Data points & benchmarks
- Avg cited URL age: 1,064 days (AI assistants) vs. 1,432 days (organic SERP) = 25.7% fresher
- Avg time since last update: 909 days (AI) vs. 1,047 days (organic) = 13.1% fresher
- By platform (days since publication): AI Overviews top-3 1,432 (oldest; +16 vs. organic) | Perplexity 1,166 | Gemini 1,118 | Copilot 1,056 | ChatGPT references 1,023 | ChatGPT citations 958 (freshest)
- ChatGPT's recency tilt vs. organic: -393 days (references), -458 days (citations)
- Organic SERP: older pages tend to rank higher; AI platforms show flat age-vs-position curves
- Perplexity and ChatGPT order in-text references newest → oldest (positive age-ordering correlation)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Reading "fresher preferred" as "only new content wins" — cited content averages ~2.9 years old
- Updating publish dates without changing content (explicit search-engine guidance against it)
- Ignoring that Google (least freshness-sensitive) still carries the majority of searches
- Sacrificing new content creation for constant micro-updates

## Key principles
- Freshness is a meaningful but secondary factor in which pages AI assistants cite.
- These are one study's averages; assistants change behavior, so re-check periodically.
- Each platform weights recency differently — tune refresh cadence per surface.
- Longevity still wins by default; freshness differentiates among qualified candidates.
