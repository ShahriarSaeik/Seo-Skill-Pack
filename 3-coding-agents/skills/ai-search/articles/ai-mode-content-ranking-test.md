# Raw AI Mode Content Didn't Rank in Google (Publishing Test)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A small test (three pages, so not conclusive) published verbatim AI Mode output on a high-authority blog (domain authority score ~91) under ideal conditions — none ranked for their target topics. Google happily shows AI-generated answers atop the SERP but appears to suppress that same class of content from organic rankings, likely via AI detection, information-gain, or related systems. Adding human content to the mix restored performance in follow-up tests.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The test setup (reusable protocol):
1. **Generate content entirely from an AI Mode response** for a known topic (no human edits).
2. **Publish on a strong domain** with topical history — remove authority and relevance as excuses.
3. **Include one brand-new topic** where the site has no competing page (removes cannibalization).
4. **Track rankings for 1+ month** — the pages never ranked beyond branded terms.
5. **Compare against control pages** — a less relevant human page outranked the more useful generated page.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Never publish raw AI Mode/assistant output as-is — it didn't rank even on a ~91-authority domain
- [ ] Blend in human value: original examples, first-hand data, expert review, information gain
- [ ] Re-test with human-augmented versions of AI drafts (early tests show recovery)
- [ ] Treat "good summary of the topic" as insufficient — usefulness to readers ≠ ranking system approval
- [ ] Watch for factual errors in generated drafts (the test pages contained wrong sections)

## Data points & benchmarks
- 3/3 verbatim-AI pages failed to rank for target terms despite a ~91-authority host + topical history
- A more relevant generated page lost to a human page ranked 41st that didn't even mention the keyword
- Expected outcome was modest (positions 10–30); actual: zero meaningful rankings
- Follow-up: adding human content to generated pages showed positive results

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming high domain authority lets low-effort AI content rank
- Interpreting "decent overview" quality as rankable quality
- Blaming crawl/indexing when the failure pattern is content-value systems

## Key principles
- Google displays AI answers, yet in this test raw AI-generated pages did not rank — generation alone is not a ranking asset.
- Information gain (or similar systems) appears to penalize content that only restates existing coverage.
- The working formula: AI drafts + human insight, not AI alone.
