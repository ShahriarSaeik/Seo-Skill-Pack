# LLMs Don't Reward Originality, They Flatten It

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
LLMs reward consensus, not firsts: a genuinely new idea can rank #1 in Google for its own coined term, appear in AI Overviews — and still get its definition hallucinated, its creators uncredited, and its concept absorbed into the generic topic pool. "LLM flattening" happens at micro level (new ideas smoothed into consensus summaries) and macro level (innovation gets less exposure over time). Counterplay: label ideas clearly, brand them, define them explicitly, and seed repetition through distribution and networks.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Understand the experiment** — a team coined a named framework, published it, ranked #1 for the exact term, and got the image cited in an AI Overview. But when prompting assistants directly in that experiment, every model tested: had access to the article, repeated the exact coined term, hallucinated a definition from generic sources, never named the creators, and on re-prompt often gave the originators zero visibility.
2. **Why it happens** — LLMs match meaning, not word strings; meaning is inferred from repetition. A teacher knows the subject but can't recall which source taught each fact. New ideas without repetition have no pattern to anchor to.
3. **Name the two levels** — micro flattening (per-topic: nuance compressed into the most statistically reinforced version) and macro flattening (over time: fewer new ideas surface, so fewer get repeated, so fewer surface — a discovery catch-22).
4. **Run the counterplay (7 moves):**
   1. **Label ideas clearly** — give concepts repeatable names
   2. **Add your brand to the label** — brand + idea co-occurrence is what eventually earns credit
   3. **Define ideas explicitly** — include a "What is [concept]?" section in plain language
   4. **Self-reference with purpose** — use the term in body copy, headings, and internal links (not just captions/alt text)
   5. **Distribute widely** — LinkedIn, podcasts, newsletters; an idea needs many homes
   6. **Invite others in** — ask collaborators and community to use the term; visibility takes a network
   7. **Play the long game** — originality is a seed for future consensus, not a citation shortcut

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Name every proprietary concept/framework you publish
- [ ] Add a plain-language "What is [term]?" definition block on the canonical page
- [ ] Use the branded term in headings, body copy, and internal link anchors
- [ ] Promote the idea on 3+ channels beyond the blog (adapt, don't copy the full article everywhere)
- [ ] Recruit colleagues and community members to reference the term in their own work
- [ ] Re-prompt major assistants periodically to track whether the idea-brand association is forming
- [ ] Set expectations: a coined term may take quarters, not weeks, to attach to your brand

## Data points & benchmarks
- Case: coined term ranked #1 in Google and appeared in an AI Overview — yet assistants repeated the exact term while citing paraphrasing third parties and never the creator
- Re-prompting frequently returned zero visibility for the originator even with the article accessible
- The original post's own flaw: no explicit definition section — LLMs couldn't latch onto a canonical meaning
- Contrast case: the same flattening article drew negligible search clicks but massive social resonance — unsearchable ideas can still spread person-to-person
- Pre-LLM baseline: lexical search reliably surfaced unique phrases verbatim; semantic models absorb them

## Mistakes to avoid
- Coining a concept and expecting the citation to follow from ranking #1
- Dropping the term only in images, captions, or alt text where models can't anchor it
- Publishing a new idea without a canonical, quotable definition
- Relying on a single blog post as the idea's only home
- Treating zero early LLM visibility as failure — flattening delays recognition, it doesn't kill the idea

## Key principles
- LLMs don't reward firsts; they reward consensus — originality needs a crowd before it earns a citation
- Meaning is inferred from repetition: no pattern, no placement
- Flattening rounds off originality, plateaus discoverability, and makes innovation invisible — unless you engineer repetition
- Confident ≠ correct: models that can't attribute origins also can't self-detect their misinterpretations
- Originality is how consensus begins — create the idea anyway, then make it repeatable
