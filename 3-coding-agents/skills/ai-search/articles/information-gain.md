# What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Information gain = the additional information your page contributes beyond what already ranks (per Google's 2018/2022 patent). Whether Google runs a literal score is unconfirmed — irrelevant, because in AI-era search there is no "position four": either you provide information not yet said, or you're cut from synthesized answers. Add gain via underserved intent angles, content gap analysis, unanswered/trending questions, unique data (strongest single predictor), and freshness.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Study intent for underserved angles** — read the SERP as a searcher and group the ranking pages by intent (manually or with a SERP-analysis tool) to find the intent split. Intent absorbing ~80% of content = consensus (cover it — cost of entry); the underserved intent group = the gain opportunity.
2. **Content gap analysis vs. top rankers** — use a content optimization/grading tool (or a manual topic checklist) to compare topic-by-topic coverage against ranking pages; start with the weakest-covered topics and ask "what can I say none of these pages does?" Matching competitors raises coverage; content no competitor has creates information gain. Sequence: max coverage first, then novel additions.
3. **Fill unanswered questions** — pull question keywords from keyword research data, sorted by 3/6/12-month growth; filter to questions where your site doesn't rank top 10 to find ones page-one competitors fail to answer. Reddit interception: filter questions where reddit.com ranks top 10 + sort by growth — Reddit ranking = SERP not satisfying intent; searchers state in their own words what content didn't tell them.
4. **Include unique data** — strongest defensible gain. Sources: customer surveys, LinkedIn polls, analysis of your own industry data (example: one agency analyzed 20.9M shopping SERPs from keyword research data). Don't stat-stuff — gain is measured on meaning.
5. **Keep content fresh** — "new" is relative to what's been said; long-circulated facts are absorbed into consensus. Refresh workflow: list your top pages in an SEO tool → filter to declining traffic → keep lower-difficulty keywords → compare over a 1-year range → sort by biggest drop → check change history to skip pages already updated.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Group the target SERP's results by intent; note the underserved intent cluster
- [ ] Score draft vs. competitors in a content optimization/grading tool; fix coverage gaps first
- [ ] For each low-score topic, add something no ranking page says
- [ ] Mine trending unanswered questions (question keywords + growth sort + not-ranking filter)
- [ ] Find Reddit-interceptable questions (reddit.com top 10 + question filter + growth sort)
- [ ] Add ≥1 original data element per flagship page (survey, poll, internal data analysis)
- [ ] Run declining-pages refresh workflow quarterly
- [ ] Pre-publish test: "What does this page contain that no other page does?" — no answer, don't publish

## Data points & benchmarks
- Patent: "Contextual estimation of link information gain" (filed 2018, granted 2022); explicitly extends to chatbots/voice assistants
- Gain score is per-user, per-session, semantic (paraphrase ≠ new info), dynamic (re-ranked as users consume pages)
- Patent's dedup mechanic: repeated facts get excluded from assistant answers — source used only for its NEW facts
- One study of 150 top-3 pages: positions 1-3 average info-gain score ~50-55 (originality barely separates top 3); pages with 15+ unique data points average 62 vs. 40 for 0-1 — unique data is the strongest single predictor
- Google reported that its March 2024 update cut "low-quality, unoriginal content" in results by 45%
- One consultant's analysis (400+ sites, Dec 2025 core update): "proprietary assets" (original data, tools) = 3rd strongest predictor of traffic gains
- One single-site republish case study reported 50x traffic recovery after a rewrite (anecdotal)
- 7 competing credit-score articles shared identical info with zero differentiation — typical finite-topic saturation

## Mistakes to avoid
- Rewording competitors' content — semantically identical, lands in the same "semantic spot"
- Stat-stuffing with junk numbers — measured on meaning, not figure count
- Skipping consensus coverage — expected topics are the entry ticket; gain layers on top
- Straying so far from core intent that relevance is lost
- Publishing without answering the differentiation question

## Key principles
- "Information gain is Google rewarding content for being different, not just better."
- "Gain lives on the edges of the topic" — examples, data, perspectives the SERP hasn't picked up
- In a synthesized AI answer there is no position four: provide unsaid information or get cut
- LLMs generate the most probable content → consensus content gets absorbed without credit; information gain is the part that isn't consensus yet — less susceptible to click-loss and more memorable
- Speculative: Google may treat gain as an output rather than an input — once your claims prove reliable, they may feed future AI answers and entity understanding
