# Google's AI Overviews Track Big-Brand Mentions More Than ChatGPT or Perplexity

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
In one study, branded web mentions (pages mentioning your brand anywhere on the web) correlate strongly with AI Overview visibility (ρ = 0.65) but only weakly with Perplexity (0.30) and very weakly with ChatGPT (0.15). Google's long-standing preference for established brands (plausibly a trust/anti-misinformation mechanism) appears to carry into its AI surfaces; independent assistants reward it far less, which may be an opening for emerging brands. These are correlations, not proven causes.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Analysis design:
1. **Take the top 50 mentioned websites** per platform (same month: ~77M AI Overviews, ~1M prompts each for the chatbot and answer engine).
2. **Count each brand's web mentions** (any page mentioning the entity at least once, linked or not).
3. **Correlate mention counts with AI visibility** per platform.
4. **Cross-check against the 75K-brand study** — results align (branded mentions = strongest AIO factor, 0.664).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] For AI Overview visibility: treat branded web mentions as the #1 growth lever — systematic PR, reviews, expert commentary
- [ ] For chatbot/answer-engine visibility: mention volume matters less; diversify into their actual source diets (partnerships, UGC, communities)
- [ ] If you're an emerging brand: prioritize the less brand-biased assistants first — the barrier looked lower in this study
- [ ] Track branded mention growth as a leading indicator of AIO visibility
- [ ] Don't assume Google-scale authority is required for every AI surface

## Data points & benchmarks
- Branded web mentions ↔ AI visibility (Spearman):
  - AI Overviews: 0.65 (strong)
  - Perplexity: 0.30 (weak)
  - ChatGPT: 0.15 (very weak)
- Consistent with the 75K-brand study: branded mentions ranked #1 factor (0.664), ahead of branded anchors and branded search volume

## Mistakes to avoid
- Applying one brand-building playbook uniformly across assistants
- Expecting mention volume alone to move chatbot visibility
- Ignoring Google's institutional brand bias when forecasting AIO share

## Key principles
- Google has long been said to favor established brands, and in this study that appears to carry into its AI surfaces.
- Independent assistants select sources differently — less brand gravity, more retrieval/partnership logic.
- Where brand bias is weakest, scrappy brands win disproportionate visibility.
