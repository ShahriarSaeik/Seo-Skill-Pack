# Self-Promotional "Best" Lists and ChatGPT Visibility (26,283 Source URLs Studied)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An analysis of 750 top-of-funnel prompts (software, products, agencies) found "best X" blog lists are the dominant cited page type in ChatGPT responses (43.8% of sources), that brands positioned higher on cited lists are more likely to be recommended, and that cited lists skew heavily fresh (79% updated in 2025, the study year). Self-promotional lists do get cited — but they carry trust risk if undisclosed, and the bigger wins are landing pages, third-party list positions, and pairing mentions with award/update commentary.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Categorize sources** for each response: blog list, non-blog list (review platforms), landing page, homepage, docs, product page, social, general post.
2. **Measure brand position** on every cited "best" list (top/middle/bottom third, normalized for list length).
3. **Check freshness** — publish/last-updated dates of cited lists.
4. **Check domain quality** of cited lists — a large share come from low-authority sites.
5. **Split first-party vs third-party** — what of *your* pages get cited when you're recommended.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] If you publish a self-promotional "best X" list: no ranking or citation downside was observed in this study, but keep such lists a small fraction (<1%) of total content and follow the transparency rules below
- [ ] Prioritize climbing positions on third-party lists — higher placement correlates with being recommended
- [ ] Keep comparison lists genuinely fresh (79% of cited lists were updated in 2025; 26% within two months)
- [ ] Build strong first-party landing pages per market/use case — they out-cited homepages and even self-lists when the brand was recommended
- [ ] Be transparent it's your own list (disclosed, first person), link out to real alternatives, rank honestly, and don't always put yourself #1
- [ ] Publish wins (awards, analyst placements, milestones) near product mentions — assistants quote the commentary
- [ ] Product brands: invest in product pages and editorial posts (87% of first-party product citations were product pages)
- [ ] Keep earning third-party list spots via product updates, original data, and audience activity — that remains the primary driver

## Data points & benchmarks
- Blog lists = 43.8% of all cited page types; slightly MORE prominent in Google AI Overviews than ChatGPT
- First-party page types when recommended — software: landing pages 37.2%, own blog list 34%, homepage 15.6%; agency: landing 30.4%, blog list 17.2%; products: product pages 87.2%
- Self-promotional list appeared in >⅓ of software responses where its publisher was recommended
- 1,100 cited lists: 79.1% updated in 2025, 26% in the past 2 months; 57.1% updated post-publication vs left as-is
- ~35% of 3,000 cited lists sat on low-authority domains; 28% of one assistant's most-cited pages had zero organic visibility
- In classic Google: 67.6% of 250 "best X software" SERPs featured a list whose publisher ranked itself #1
- ChatGPT referral traffic runs ~8–9x the next AI-first platform across 60K+ monitored sites

## Mistakes to avoid
- Publishing a self-list with no outbound links to alternatives — poor user signals may catch up
- Writing self-promotions in fake third-person voice — transparency costs nothing and builds trust
- Chasing every "best X" variation — cover markets where you genuinely compete
- Ignoring low-quality cited competitors: treat them as citation gaps you can earn honestly with better, more useful pages, and don't count on weak sources staying cited as trust scoring tightens

## Key principles
- Lists are the currency of recommendation queries; position on the list matters as much as presence.
- Freshness is a citation filter — stale lists fall out of the source pool fast.
- The durable play is third-party validation; self-lists are a supplement, not a strategy.
