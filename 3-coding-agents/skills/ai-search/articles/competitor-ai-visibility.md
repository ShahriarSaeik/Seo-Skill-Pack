# AI Visibility Competitor Analysis: Benchmark, Diagnose, Close Gaps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Competitor analysis for AI search answers three questions: who dominates AI answers in your category, where they earn visibility that you don't, and which topics/pages/sources drive the advantage. Combine quantitative benchmarks (mentions, citations, impressions, share of voice) with qualitative framing analysis (how AI describes each brand), then close gaps with a Fix/Build/Influence plan. Repeat monthly or quarterly.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Identify three competitor types:** brands AI mentions alongside you, sources AI cites instead of you, and brands your audience compares you to. Find them via AI-suggested similar brands, "[brand] vs" query mining, and cited-domains reports (ignore mega-sources like encyclopedias and forums — focus on real rivals).
2. **Define entities precisely.** Group each brand with its domain; for deeper analysis add sub-brands, product names, and regional domains. Save the setup so you can reuse it each cycle.
3. **Benchmark per platform:** mentions (named in responses), citations (your site cited as source), impressions (estimated exposure), and AI share of voice (mentions vs competitors) — tracked per assistant and over time.
4. **Analyze framing, not just presence.** Compare responses in three views: your brand only, with competitors, competitors only. Note mention volume, placement order, depth of coverage, positioning (leader/alternative/afterthought), sentiment, and credibility cues.
5. **Measure topic-level share of voice.** For each category you want to own, compare SOV per topic; find unbranded subtopics where a competitor leads (e.g., "best X" vs "compact X") and attribute-level queries to target.
6. **Diff top-cited pages.** Pull each brand's most-cited pages per AI platform. Classify gaps: visibility (cited less), topic (they cover it, you don't), format (AI favors a content type you lack), freshness (theirs is newer).
7. **Compare third-party web mentions.** Export pages mentioning each competitor (excluding their own domains) into one sheet; empty cells = pages mentioning several competitors but not you — the fastest outreach wins. Reverse-engineer the winning mentions: content types, publications, campaign triggers, distribution tactics.
8. **Report with a Fix/Build/Influence plan:** Fix inaccurate descriptions and missing brand data; Build content where competitors lead; Influence the publications and creators feeding AI answers in your category.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a 3-6 competitor set from "vs" queries and cited-domain overlap
- [ ] Record baseline metrics per AI platform for every brand
- [ ] Write a one-line "AI positioning statement" per brand from real responses
- [ ] Map topic ownership; flag subtopics/attributes where you're invisible
- [ ] List competitors' top-cited pages and identify format/freshness gaps
- [ ] Build a web-mention comparison sheet; shortlist pages mentioning multiple rivals but not you
- [ ] Deliver a stakeholder report: SWOT, topic gap matrix, prioritized actions
- [ ] Re-run the full analysis on a monthly/quarterly cadence

## Data points & benchmarks
- Third-party web mentions are among the strongest correlates of AI answer visibility — AI usually cites someone else's page even for queries about your brand
- Example asymmetry pattern: one brand owns "reliability/SUV" framing, the rival owns "affordability/compact" — SOV follows the framing
- Fastest wins: pages already mentioning several competitors (they clearly cover the category; one addition is an easy ask; any paid inclusion must be disclosed and tagged rel="sponsored")

## Mistakes to avoid
- Benchmarking only aggregate visibility — topic-level and framing-level gaps hide in averages
- Comparing against encyclopedic mega-domains instead of real competitors
- Skipping entity setup (wrong variants = noisy data)
- Treating the analysis as one-off — AI answers shift with model updates; benchmark recurrently

## Key principles
- Quantitative metrics tell you who wins; qualitative framing tells you why.
- Share of voice should be measured per topic you intend to own, not blended.
- Web mentions feed AI answers — the mention-gap sheet is a PR pipeline, not trivia.
- Fix (accuracy) → Build (coverage) → Influence (sources) is the action hierarchy.
