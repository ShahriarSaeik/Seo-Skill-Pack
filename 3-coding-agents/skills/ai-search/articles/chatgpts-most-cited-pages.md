# ChatGPT's Top 1,000 Cited Pages: What's Influenceable (Study)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An industry study (a snapshot; ChatGPT's sources shift often) of ChatGPT's 1,000 most-cited pages found ~67% are "dead" citations — Wikipedia, homepages, app-store listings — that outreach can't influence. Only ~32% (educational content, reviews, news, blogs) are pitch-worthy. Cited pages skew fresh (76% updated within 1-6 months), and 28% have zero organic visibility, meaning traditional rankings don't gate AI citation.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Separate dead vs. influenceable citations** — dead: Wikipedia (29.7%), homepages (23.8%), app listings (6.6%), dictionaries/reference (6.2%), corporate pages (0.5%). Influenceable: educational/how-to content (19.4%), reviews (5.8%), news/media (5.2%), blog articles (1.9%) = 32.3%.
2. **Build outreach lists from the influenceable set** — pull your niche's most-cited pages (an AI visibility tracker's cited-pages report), filter to blogs, publications, and review sites, and pitch those.
3. **Target fan-out niches where pages rank for nothing** — 28.3% of top-cited pages have zero organic keywords; content answering narrow sub-questions gets cited without ever ranking. Publish for specificity, not volume.
4. **Refresh aggressively** — cited pages are overwhelmingly updated within the last 6 months; real content updates (new examples, data, sections) drive citation retention.
5. **Think domain authority, not page authority** — ChatGPT cites pages on strong domains regardless of the specific page's links (median domain authority score ~90, but most cited pages have near-zero page-level authority).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Export your niche's top 100 ChatGPT-cited pages; classify dead vs. influenceable
- [ ] Pitch the educational/review/news pages on that list (product inclusion, expert quotes, data); no undisclosed paid inclusion
- [ ] Maintain Wikipedia presence only where genuinely eligible and per its notability and conflict-of-interest rules (citation-rich but hard to influence)
- [ ] Keep homepage + app-store listings accurate and information-complete (they get cited directly)
- [ ] Publish deep pages for narrow fan-out sub-questions, even with no search volume
- [ ] Refresh priority pages every 1-6 months with substantive updates
- [ ] Don't judge AI-citation potential by Google rankings alone

## Data points & benchmarks
- Content-type mix of top 1,000 cited pages: Wikipedia 29.7%, homepage/landing 23.8%, educational 19.4%, app store 6.6%, reviews 5.8%, news/media 5.2%, language/grammar 4.0%, dictionary/reference 2.2%, blog/article 1.9%, Q&A/forum 0.9%, corporate 0.5%
- 67% off-limits to outreach vs 32.3% influenceable
- 28.3% of top-cited pages have zero organic keywords (no search visibility at all)
- Pages that do rank: median domain authority score 90 (65.3% scoring 81+), median 279 keywords, median 70 referring domains, 52.1% rank top-3, 11.7% are low-authority (0-20) exceptions
- Page authority barely matters: 67.3% have a page authority score of 0-10 (median 6)
- Freshness (of pages with detectable dates): 60.5% published within 2 years; 76.4% updated within 1-6 months; ~90% updated in the study year; excluding Wikipedia still ~82%
- One external researcher documented a URL freshness score in ChatGPT's pipeline; artificially refreshed dates moved AI ranking positions by up to 95 places (not a tactic to copy: cosmetic date changes without real updates are manipulation and can backfire)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Pitching Wikipedia editors or app-store pages as if they were publications
- Assuming pages need Google rankings to earn AI citations
- Chasing only high-domain-authority targets — 11.7% of cited pages sit on low-authority domains
- Letting key pages go 6+ months without substantive updates
- Ignoring homepage/app-listing accuracy because "they're not content pages" — they're 30% of top citations

## Key principles
- ChatGPT citation ≠ outreach opportunity: classify before pitching
- Reference-style, predictably structured content (Wikipedia-style) is the model's default source shape
- Recency bias is structural (freshness scoring), so refresh cadence is a citation tactic
- Niche specificity can substitute for search authority in AI discovery
