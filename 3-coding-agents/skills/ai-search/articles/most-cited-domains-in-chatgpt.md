# ChatGPT's Most-Cited Websites: Citation Source Study (Sept 2026)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An industry study tracking every domain ChatGPT cites across broad US queries found a heavily concentrated citation economy: Reddit alone takes 16.8% of top-source citations (2.4x Wikipedia's 7%), followed by consumer publications, health/government institutions, and big-box retail domains. Use the source mix to reverse-engineer where ChatGPT "shops" for answers — and which platforms to earn placement on.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Map the citation economy by category** — ChatGPT's top sources cluster into: community/UGC (Reddit, YouTube), encyclopedias/dictionaries (Wikipedia, Merriam-Webster, Cambridge, Dictionary.com), consumer press (Forbes, Tom's Guide, TechRadar, Wired, Good Housekeeping), medical/gov authority (Healthline, Cleveland Clinic, Mayo Clinic, PubMed, FDA, CDC), retail/product catalogs (Walmart, Target, Home Depot, Lowe's), and review/aggregator sites (Tripadvisor, Trustpilot, BBB, Edmunds, Cars.com, Car and Driver).
2. **Match category to your niche** — each niche has 2-4 dominant cited domains; those are the placements to earn (mentions, reviews, listings, contributor slots).
3. **Track monthly** — the source mix shifts (new entrants like FDA, CDC, and product-test sites appeared recently; dictionary sites fell); re-prioritize outreach targets quarterly.
4. **Prioritize UGC presence** — Reddit's #1 position suggests community threads are a default evidence layer for opinions, comparisons, and recommendations.
5. **Feed structured product data to retail/aggregator citations** — commerce queries cite retailer and review-aggregator pages, so listings, feeds, and review profiles are citation assets.

## Data points & benchmarks
- Reddit: 16.8% mention share (810,887 distinct cited pages) — more than 2x the #2 source
- Wikipedia: 7.0% (129,667 pages cited)
- Consumer Reports 3.7%, Forbes 3.1%, Healthline 2.5%, YouTube 2.5%, PubMed Central 2.4%, Walmart 2.3%
- Long tail (0.8-2.1%): Tom's Guide, Merriam-Webster, TechRadar, Edmunds, Home Depot, Good Housekeeping, Target, Cambridge Dictionary, Lowe's, Tripadvisor, Trustpilot, Angi, Car and Driver, Kelley Blue Book, Facebook, NerdWallet, FDA, ScienceDirect, BBB, Cleveland Clinic, Mayo Clinic, Wired, RTINGS, LinkedIn, Vogue, MedlinePlus, J.D. Power, Apple App Store, This Old House, Collins, CDC, Allure, Who What Wear, MotorTrend
- Notable: high-authority domains dominate, but at least two mid-authority niche sites cracked the top 10 with huge cited-page counts — depth of useful pages can outweigh raw domain authority
- Citation counts concentrate: top 2 domains hold ~24% of top-source citations; everything outside the top 50 fights for scraps
- Mention share = a domain's citations as % of summed citations among top sources; measured over broad US queries

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating all AI platforms as one pool — ChatGPT's source mix differs from AI Overviews/AI Mode (track each separately)
- Chasing only high-authority publications when niche sites with deep content libraries also get cited at scale
- Ignoring Reddit because it's "just a forum" — it is the single most-cited source
- Letting review-platform profiles go stale (rating/review recency may feed AI recommendations)
- Building presence on sites that never appear in your niche's cited set

## Key principles
- ChatGPT's answers come from a relatively small, stable set of trusted sources
- Community consensus (Reddit), encyclopedic baselines (Wikipedia/dictionaries), institutional authority (health/gov), and commerce catalogs form the four pillars
- Being citable = being present on the platforms ChatGPT already trusts in your category
- The citation economy is measurable monthly — treat source tracking as a routine monitoring task, not a one-off study
