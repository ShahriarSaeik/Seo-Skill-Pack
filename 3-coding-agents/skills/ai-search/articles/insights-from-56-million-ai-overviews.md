# Insights From 55.8M AI Overviews Across 590M Searches

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One large public AI Overview dataset (55.8M AIOs among 590M desktop keywords; a point-in-time snapshot, so re-measure) shows AIOs on 9.46% of all keywords (16% in the US) and ~12.8% of searches by volume — likely an undercount. The top 50 domains take 28.9% of all mentions (community Q&A, encyclopedia, and health domains dominate). AIOs skew hard toward informational, non-branded, non-local, longer, higher-volume queries — and 71.67% of AIO searches carry no ad CPC data, meaning they mostly appear on non-monetized demand.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Size the surface** — AIO keywords ÷ total keywords in a country index (9.46% overall; country rollouts vary: ~16% US, 16.5% India, 15.5% Brazil, 12.5% UK).
2. **Weight by volume** — AIO keywords' combined search volume ÷ total (~23B of ~180B monthly searches = 12.8%+); counts undercount because low-volume and logged-in queries aren't sampled.
3. **Find who gets mentioned** — pull the most-mentioned domains and their impression totals; compute top-50 concentration (28.9%).
4. **Profile query triggers** — compare AIO distribution vs. baseline across intent, branding, locality, word count, difficulty, volume, and CPC.
5. **Reproduce it yourself** — filter any keyword dataset by the AIO SERP feature to refresh every number on demand.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Prioritize informational, question-style, longer (5+ word) queries for AIO-targeted content
- [ ] Don't deprioritize high-volume head terms — AIOs over-index on them (counterintuitively)
- [ ] Expect low AIO presence on branded and local queries — defend those with classic SEO/local profiles
- [ ] Benchmark your niche's most-mentioned AIO domains; target the same source categories (community Q&A, reference, health, video)
- [ ] Re-run the distribution filters quarterly; AIO prevalence moves fast (huge presence jump in early-year rollout months)
- [ ] For monetization planning: assume AIO queries mostly sit outside ad auctions (71.67% no CPC)

## Data points & benchmarks
- 55.8M AIOs / 590M desktop keywords = 9.46%; US: ~24.9M of 156M = 16%
- By volume: ~12.8% of ~180B monthly searches; the author speculates the true share could be far higher because anonymous and logged-in queries are undercounted (unverified)
- Scale context: Google has reported 1.5B monthly AIO users (≈18.3% of all people, 26.6% of internet users)
- Top 50 domains = 28.9% of all AIO mentions; top mentions: a community forum ~2.97M, an encyclopedia ~2.92M, a Q&A site ~2.28M, a video platform ~2.06M; health/medical systems fill much of the rest
- Top-50 category shares: Q&A & community 5.9%, health & medical 5.8%, knowledge & reference 4.4%, video & social 3.4%
- Intent of AIO keywords (multi-intent allowed): informational 97.7%, commercial 12.86%, transactional 2.85%, navigational 1.23%
- Branded 19.97% vs non-branded 80.03% (AIOs under-index on branded); local 6.85% vs non-local 93.15%
- Longer queries → higher AIO likelihood; higher search volume → higher AIO likelihood (surprising); lower difficulty skews only because low-KD terms dominate the base
- 71.67% of AIO keywords have no CPC data — mostly unmonetized searches

## Mistakes to avoid
- Extrapolating keyword-share (9.46%) as traffic impact — volume weighting raises the share (~12.8%) and still undercounts
- Assuming AIOs only hit long-tail queries — the data shows over-representation on high-volume terms
- Comparing raw index counts to the search engine's own trillions-of-searches PR numbers without adjusting for anonymous queries (~46% hidden in one study)
- Treating the top-50 list as permanent — concentration is high but per-category long tails exist for niche players
- Ignoring country rollout stagger — many countries show zero AIOs for logged-out users, skewing global averages

## Key principles
- AIOs are an informational, non-branded, non-local, longer-query phenomenon
- Mentions concentrate: ~29% of all visibility goes to 50 domains — but category filters reveal niche winners
- Where AIOs show, ad monetization mostly doesn't — commercial queries are relatively protected today
- Any team can re-derive these numbers from a keyword database with a SERP-feature filter; don't wait for the next study
- Companies investing most in informational content are also the most exposed to AIO click loss
