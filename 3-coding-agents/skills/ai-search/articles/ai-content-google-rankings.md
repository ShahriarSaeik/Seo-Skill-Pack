# AI-Generated Content Does Not Hurt Google Rankings (600K Pages)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
In one study of 600K top-20 ranking pages, 86.5% contained at least some AI-generated content and the correlation between AI share and ranking position was 0.011 — effectively zero. Google neither punishes nor rewards AI content as such; it ranks helpfulness (though mass-produced low-value pages can violate its scaled content abuse policy however they are made). The only nuance: position-#1 pages skew slightly toward minimal AI use.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Study protocol:
1. **Sample 100K random keywords**, extract top-20 URLs (~600K pages with extractable content).
2. **Run AI-content detection** per page — classify pure AI / pure human / mixed with AI-share bands.
3. **Correlate AI-content percentage with ranking position** across the dataset.
4. **Segment by position band** to find edge effects (e.g., #1 behavior).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Drop the "will AI content get me penalized?" debate — production method isn't the ranking input; but don't mass-produce thin pages, which is penalized whoever or whatever writes them
- [ ] Use AI across the workflow (drafts, edits, grammar, titles) without ranking fear
- [ ] Concentrate human effort on what AI can't do: original data, experience, unique perspective
- [ ] For pages targeting #1: bias toward heavier human contribution (slight edge at the top)
- [ ] Audit quality signals (helpfulness, uniqueness) instead of AI percentage

## Data points & benchmarks
- Top-20 pages: pure AI 4.6% | pure human 13.5% | mixed 81.9%
- Mixed-page AI share: minimal (1–10%) 13.8% | moderate (11–40%) 40% | substantial (41–70%) 20.3% | dominant (71–99%) 7.8%
- AI % ↔ ranking correlation: 0.011 (zero)
- Minimal-AI group (0–30%) shows a very slight association with better rankings; pure AI rarely reaches #1
- AI detectors are imperfect, so the bands are approximate and the study shows correlation, not causation
- Context (survey): 87% of marketers already use AI in content creation

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating "penalized for AI" as the reason a low-effort page failed — the failure is low value
- Publishing raw output at scale expecting parity — pure AI rarely wins top spots
- Avoiding AI tools entirely and losing the speed/coverage advantage competitors gain

## Key principles
- Google ranks helpfulness, not provenance.
- The winning default is hybrid: AI for leverage, humans for value.
- The only measurable AI effect is at the very top — and it favors a lighter AI touch.
