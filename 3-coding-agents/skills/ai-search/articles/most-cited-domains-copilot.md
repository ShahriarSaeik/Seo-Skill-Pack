# The Most-Cited Websites in Copilot (Industry Study)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An industry study of 3M+ US queries found Copilot's citations are the most commerce-skewed of any assistant: Amazon (16.8%) and Walmart (12.6%) lead, e-commerce and retail domains fill the top 10, and — unusually — small niche content sites with very low authority scores earn top-50 citation share. Copilot's reach through office software makes its sources work-relevant, and its in-chat shopping makes them commercially decisive.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Mention share = each domain's slice of top-source citations. Copilot's source mix:
1. **Marketplaces & retail (~40%+ of top-10 share)** — Amazon 16.8%, Walmart 12.6% (+ a second Walmart subdomain at 3.0%), Target 2.8%, Etsy 2.4%, Best Buy, Home Depot, Lowe's, Macy's, Wayfair, Nordstrom, Kohl's, eBay, Instacart, Alibaba
2. **Reference** — Wikipedia 7.6%; dictionaries (Merriam-Webster, Collins, Cambridge) for definitional queries; Britannica
3. **Niche long-tail content sites** — science/engineering explainers (3.5%, 3.3%, 2.5%) and health micro-sites with authority scores as low as 5–35 out of 100
4. **AI-generated wiki** — an AI-built encyclopedia holds 3.1% share (Copilot cites AI-generated reference content)
5. **Health authority** — Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Healthline, WebMD
6. **Automotive research** — KBB, Edmunds (both ~1.6%)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] For product brands: treat Amazon and Walmart listings as Copilot ranking surfaces, not just sales channels
- [ ] Audit whether your product pages are structurally extractable (specs, prices, availability) since Copilot pulls shopping answers
- [ ] Small publishers: niche depth can compete here even without strong domain authority; thin or mass-produced content is not a safe bet
- [ ] Add definition boxes/terms sections to capture dictionary-style citations
- [ ] For B2B: Copilot lives in office software, so prioritize product and comparison content that surfaces during work tasks
- [ ] Re-check monthly; small sites enter and exit the top 50 quickly (one site jumped +23 spots in a month)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Copilot apps: 100M+ monthly active users; 800M+ people touch Copilot features across the ecosystem (company-reported figures)
- Top shares: Amazon 16.8%, Walmart 12.6%, Wikipedia 7.6%; retail chains hold roughly a dozen of the top 50 slots
- Wikipedia cited across ~118K distinct pages
- Low-authority sites in the top 50: authority scores of 5, 6, 9, 14, 35 (suggests niche content can earn citations over stronger domains on Copilot)
- An AI-generated encyclopedia holds 3.1% of top-source citations
- Definitions matter: three dictionary domains in the top 35

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming big-domain authority is required — Copilot's long tail includes very-low-authority sites
- Ignoring marketplace data quality (titles, specs, reviews) as an AI visibility factor
- Writing only journalistic content when the cited formats are product pages, explainers, and definitions
- Forgetting the audience context: Copilot answers arrive inside work tools, not on a search page

## Key principles
- Copilot is the most commercially skewed citation economy studied — optimize product data, not just content.
- Niche depth can substitute for domain strength on Copilot.
- As assistants add in-chat checkout, their cited retail sources become direct sales channels.
