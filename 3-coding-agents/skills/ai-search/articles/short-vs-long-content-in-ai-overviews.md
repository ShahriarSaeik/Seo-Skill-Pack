# Short vs. Long Content in AI Overviews: Length Doesn't Predict Citations (174K Pages)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An industry study of 174,048 pages cited in AI Overviews found a near-zero correlation (0.04 Spearman) between word count and getting cited. Over half of cited pages are under 1,000 words, short pages win top citation positions as often as long ones, and no 10,000-word mega-guide pattern exists. Write as much as the topic needs — no more.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The analysis protocol:
1. **Extract cited pages** — 560K AI Overviews → 1.68M cited URLs → 174K pages with extractable content.
2. **Strip HTML/boilerplate** and measure raw text word count.
3. **Track citation position** (1–10) per page to test whether length affects placement.
4. **Segment by page type** (articles, listings, tools, UGC, video, docs) — medians differ sharply by format.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Delete arbitrary word-count targets from content briefs; set length by topic and format
- [ ] Answer the query directly and early — don't bury the lede
- [ ] Use headings, declarative sentences, and skimmable structure
- [ ] Keep ecommerce listing/category pages concise (300-550 words is the cited median) instead of bolting on 1,500-word guides
- [ ] Let comprehensive guides run long only when the intent genuinely demands depth
- [ ] Audit existing short pages (under 1,000 words) for citation opportunities — they compete on equal footing

## Data points & benchmarks
- Average cited page: 1,282 words (vs. 1,188 for pages ranking organically)
- Distribution: under 350 words 16.6% | 350–1,000 36.8% | 1,000–2,000 30.6% | 2,000+ 16.0%
- Word count ↔ citation correlation: 0.04 (essentially zero); same for citation position
- Pages under 350 words: 34% cited at position 1, 32% at position 2, 31% at position 3 — >95% of short-content citations land in the top 3
- Median word count by type: listings 315 | core pages 317 | UGC 387 | video 407 | interactive tools 507 | category collections 534 | documents 676 | articles 1,166 | audio/podcast 1,226
- Longest cited blog post: ~3,500 words

## Mistakes to avoid
- Commissioning 10,000+ word mega-guides "because AI prefers long content" — no evidence in this study (one dataset; correlation only)
- Assuming short content can't win premium citation positions — it does, constantly
- Applying one word-count rule across formats (a product page is not a guide)
- Padding with fluff that weakens engagement signals

## Key principles
- AI cites what answers the query, in whatever length that takes.
- Content gets cited because it's relevant and in the right format — not because of raw length.
- Match format and length to intent; clarity and structure are the real levers.
