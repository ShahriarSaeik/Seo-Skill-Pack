# On-Page AEO: 4 Writing Frameworks for Better AI Visibility

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
AI-friendly writing = human-friendly writing. Four pre-LLM frameworks from military/consulting/journalism boost AI citation odds: BLUF (conclusion first), declarative statements (confidence), entity density (specificity), and strategic repetition. Prerequisite: content must rank first — 88% of ChatGPT-cited URLs come from its general search index.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)** — state conclusion/recommendation/key finding in the first 1-2 sentences of any section, then support. Apply to intros, first sentence of each H2, and even titles (ChatGPT prefers titles with strong semantic similarity to fan-out queries). Test: read only first sentences of sections — full argument should be reconstructable.
2. **Declarative statements** — definitive assertions that stand alone as answers ("X is defined as", "X refers to", "X means"). Use for definitions, established facts, core concepts, recommendations. Reserve hedged language for emerging research/contested claims.
3. **Entity density** — ratio of named entities (brands, tools, people, places) to total words. Rule: name the tool, cite the source, specify the metric. Replace every vague phrase ("use a tool", "according to research") with a concrete reference.
4. **Strategic repetition** — place key ideas at multiple points (intro, mid-article context, conclusion summary), rephrased each time. Creates multiple extraction opportunities and matches different query fan-out formulations.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Move the key takeaway into the first sentence of the intro and every H2
- [ ] Rewrite needlessly hedged sentences ("might", "could potentially") into definitive ones — only where the facts are solid
- [ ] Audit for vague references; replace with named entities and numbers
- [ ] Repeat the core insight ≥3 times (intro/middle/conclusion) with different phrasing
- [ ] Make titles directly answer the target question
- [ ] Verify content ranks in regular search before optimizing for citation
- [ ] Measure citations: use an AI visibility tracker (or manual prompt checks) to see which of your pages are cited, and track over time

## Data points & benchmarks
- 44.2% of AI citations come from the first 30% of a page ("ski ramp" pattern — one analysis of 1.2M citations)
- Citation winners ~2x more likely to use definitive language: 36.2% vs 20.2%
- Cited text entity density: ~20.6% vs 5-8% in standard English prose (3-4x higher)
- 88% of ChatGPT-cited URLs come from its general search index (1.4M prompt study)
- ChatGPT cites only ~half the URLs it retrieves — writing quality closes that gap
- AI often retrieves snippets, not whole pages (per one independent analysis) — unseen paragraphs may be invisible

## Mistakes to avoid
- "Burying the lede" — context before conclusion
- Academic hedging in AI-facing copy (signals uncertainty to models choosing between sources)
- Vague, jargon-heavy writing with low entity density
- Stating a key insight only once (most readers and all retrieval systems will miss it)
- Treating AEO writing as a substitute for ranking — on-page writing is moot without search visibility

## Key principles
- "Neither AI models nor people actually read content — we both skim."
- Frameworks predate LLMs by decades: busy humans and AI have the same job (extract meaning fast)
- AI models look to validate claims, so they bias toward confident, self-contained sentences
- The more directly your title answers the fan-out query, the higher the citation chance
