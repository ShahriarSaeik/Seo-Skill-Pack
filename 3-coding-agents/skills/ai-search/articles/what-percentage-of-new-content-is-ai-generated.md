# What Percentage of New Content Is AI-Generated? (900K-Page Study)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An industry study ran 900,000 newly created English pages (April 2025, one page per domain) through an AI-content detector: 74.2% contained AI-generated material — 2.5% pure AI, 25.8% pure human, 71.7% hybrid. AI-assisted creation was already the default mode of web content production in that sample, pushed by generative features embedded directly in docs, email, and social platforms, and it spreads automatically as new content references older AI content. Detectors are imperfect but still decision-useful.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Measure the web baseline** — sample newly discovered pages across many domains; classify each by AI-share bands (pure AI / hybrid / pure human).
2. **Explain the mechanism** — free, native "generate" buttons in everyday productivity tools make AI involvement nearly unavoidable; content that references AI-assisted sources inherits AI-derived material (contamination spreads forward).
3. **Cross-check with marketer self-reports** — surveys of content marketers confirm adoption rates consistent with the page-level detection data.
4. **Use detection data responsibly** — benchmark competitors' AI share, estimate a SERP's AI saturation before investing to rank, and correlate AI share with performance metrics; never use a single detector score for career-affecting verdicts.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Benchmark the AI share of newly published pages in your niche (the April 2025 baseline was ~70%+ hybrid; expect it to be higher now and re-measure)
- [ ] Don't treat AI involvement as a differentiator by itself — nearly everyone uses it
- [ ] Estimate AI saturation of target SERPs when sizing the effort needed to outrank incumbents
- [ ] Track competitor AI-share trends over time as a (weak) signal of their content investment strategy
- [ ] Use detectors as directional data, corroborated with multiple signals — not as proof of authorship
- [ ] Focus differentiation on what AI can't supply: original data, first-hand experience, verified facts

## Data points & benchmarks
- 900K new pages, April 2025, one per domain: 74.2% included AI content
- Breakdown: pure AI 2.5% | pure human 25.8% | hybrid 71.7%
- Hybrid AI-share bands (of all pages): moderate (11–40% AI) 25.86% | substantial (41–70%) 20.50% | dominant (71–99%) 15.51% — these sum to ~61.9%, so the remaining ~9.8% of the 71.7% hybrid share sits in a lower-AI band not listed here
- 87% of 879 surveyed content marketers use AI to create or help create content; blogs are the most common AI-assisted format
- Rank-neutrality context: in one study AI share correlated ~0.011 with Google position across 600K pages — production method alone doesn't appear to decide rankings (quality and usefulness still do)
- Detection caveats: models train on narrow datasets, struggle with partial AI use, and are vulnerable to humanizing tools

## Mistakes to avoid
- Reading "74% AI" as "74% spam" — most AI content is hybrid, human-supervised work
- Assuming your niche is exempt — generative features are embedded in the default tools everyone uses
- Using detector scores punitively (firing clients, accusing writers) — no detector is 100% accurate
- Ignoring the contamination loop: citing AI-assisted sources makes your page partially AI-derived too
- Expecting "100% human" to be a scalable positioning claim while the whole web converges on hybrid

## Key principles
- AI-assisted is the default content production mode; the web's content unit is now "human + AI hybrid"
- Pure AI remains rare at the extremes; pure human is shrinking (~1 in 4 new pages)
- AI content presence spreads passively through citation chains — the share has likely risen since April 2025, so re-measure rather than rely on this snapshot
- Detectors are like search-volume estimates: imperfect but useful for strategy, dangerous for verdicts
- Competitive advantage shifts from production method to information gain and verification
