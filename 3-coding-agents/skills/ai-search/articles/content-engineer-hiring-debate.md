# Don't Hire a Content Engineer (Argument): Experimentation Over Scale

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One side of a debate (see the content-engineering article for the other): a counter-position to the "content engineer" role (AI-native operators who build automated content systems): scaled content automation is the most accessible it has ever been and therefore the least effective. Hire marketers and writers who experiment; AI fluency is a baseline expectation for everyone, not a dedicated job.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The three-part argument:
1. **Experimentation > scale** — Scaled content's golden era is over: indexing is harder, "scaled content abuse" penalties are routine, programmatic plays are trivially copied, and AI answers gut the unit economics. Anything fully automatable is destined to become worthless because every competitor can do it identically.
2. **Quality > quality assurance** — AI raised the floor of content but not the ceiling. A systems/QA role optimizes for consistency (errors, uniformity, brand guidelines) when the actual need is remarkable, irregular, human-voiced work.
3. **Marketing experts > AI experts** — Model providers are relentlessly lowering the barrier (one-line prompts, deep research, auto model selection, plain-text API access). Prompt-adjacent roles are stopgaps being solved by the tools themselves.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Index hiring on marketing/writing skill; treat AI fluency as a train-on-the-job baseline for every role
- [ ] Audit any "content at scale" strategy for its useful half-life — assume months, trending to weeks
- [ ] Shift budget toward strategies needing effort and experimentation: original research/data, platform-native thought leadership, short- and long-form video
- [ ] Use AI to amplify craftspeople (research synthesis, iteration, editing), not to productize widgets
- [ ] Move risky programmatic strategies off the main domain (this isolates risk; it doesn't make thin or scaled pages acceptable)
- [ ] When evaluating candidates, weight great writer with no AI skills over middling writer with automation fluency

## Data points & benchmarks
- Case: 2,161 articles across 6+ languages, programmatic peak ~34,000 auto-generated pages and millions of pageviews — now a fraction of peak Google clicks; best programmatic strategy moved to a separate domain
- AI-adjacent spam follows the same decay curve as automated AI SDR outreach: novelty wears off fast
- Pattern: the easier a tactic is to automate, the faster competitors replicate it and the faster it dies

## Mistakes to avoid
- Hiring for skills the tools are actively absorbing (prompt engineering, workflow plumbing)
- Spending limited resources scaling unremarkable strategies instead of finding new outsized-return plays
- Confusing consistency (QA) with quality (remarkable work)
- Treating AI as a specialist function instead of a universal capability

## Key principles
- Automatability and worth are inversely linked in content.
- AI raised the floor; humans still define the ceiling.
- The author's view: great marketers can learn AI more easily than AI experts can learn marketing.
