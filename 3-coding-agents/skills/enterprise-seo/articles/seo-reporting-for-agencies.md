# SEO Reporting for Agencies (With Real Report Examples)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Agency reporting splits into SMB (standardized: dashboard + monthly report + call) and Enterprise (fully tailored, "no rules," SEO as a chapter of a bigger performance report). Reporting quality is existential: done well, clients forgive underperforming campaigns; done badly, you lose the contract at renewal.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**SMB reporting stack** (3 components):
1. **Dashboard** — automated KPI view, usually a free BI/dashboard tool. Options: build your own (medium effort), use existing templates, or use your SEO platform's dashboard connectors if it offers them — easiest, no API skills needed.
2. **Monthly report** — commentary + insights document. Start from a free SEO report template (many exist); real structures include: visibility split by sector + links acquired; or delivery-focused report with an "impact analysis" section quantifying the outcome of specific optimizations.
3. **Presentation** — standard rhythm: weekly status call + monthly report call after delivering the report.

**Enterprise reporting** — first rule: no rules. Client dictates format (their ugly in-house template, old agency's format, real-time dashboards, integration with their tools/PPC, or part of a combined performance report). Expect:
- Dashboards in BI tools (custom or off-the-shelf)
- More personalization, more tools (agencies hold licenses across the market), more reports for different teams
- Report chapters: organic performance (often a dashboard screenshot + commentary), technical SEO, competitor performance, industry updates (buy-in education), and talk clients out of self-sabotage
- Presentation cadence (real examples): daily KPI reports (from the client's enterprise analytics platform, e.g., GA4 or an equivalent), weekly commentary + weekly call, bi-weekly dev-team call with shared notes + tickets in the client's tracker, monthly multi-channel slides, quarterly business reviews

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Match reporting depth to budget: no 60-page reports for $1K/month clients; no 10-second PDFs for $XXK/month clients
- [ ] SMB: wire up data connectors (Search Console, analytics, SEO tool) to an automated dashboard
- [ ] SMB: adapt a template report; always include an impact analysis section tied to your delivered work
- [ ] Lock the weekly status + monthly report call rhythm
- [ ] Enterprise: inventory the client's existing reporting stack (BI and dashboard tools) and integrate, don't replace
- [ ] Enterprise: agree deliverables + cadence in onboarding (their template? dashboards? PPC integration?)
- [ ] Use your SEO tools' API or scheduled exports to pipe data into client dashboards
- [ ] Log call outcomes where the client works (their wiki and ticketing tools)
- [ ] Prepare separate content/technical reports for different client teams

## Data points & benchmarks
- Time math: 5 clients × 2 hrs/month reporting = 1+ full day/month — standardize to survive
- SMB vs Enterprise table: scope (1 domain vs multi-domain/territory), audience (site owner vs multiple teams), KPIs (ROI/traffic/leads vs team-specific), recommendations (high-impact quick wins vs incremental long-term growth)
- Enterprise example: client operating in ~20 international markets needing market-level movement summaries
- Report only verified numbers; label estimates (third-party traffic, traffic value) as estimates

## Mistakes to avoid
- One-size-fits-all reporting across budget tiers
- Ignoring the client's existing reporting infrastructure
- Skipping the "why" — enterprise reports double as internal education/buy-in tools
- No impact analysis linking agency work to growth numbers
- Letting reporting slip — it's the renewal decision's biggest input

## Key principles
- "Do it well, and clients will give even poor-performing SEO campaigns a second chance. Do it badly, and you'll almost certainly get the chop at renewal."
- Higher budget = more tailored reporting
- Enterprise SEO reporting is usually one chapter of a performance marketing report — zero fluff
- Notes in the client's own systems (their wiki and ticketing tools) make SEO part of their workflow
