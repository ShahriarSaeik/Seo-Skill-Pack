# The Realities of In-House SEO: What to Expect and How to Succeed

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
In-house SEO trades agency chaos for organizational politics: relationships and soft skills matter more than SEO skill, and success = executing basics repeatedly, aligned to company priorities. Companies mature along a predictable model (siloed → centralized → SOPs → proactive training). Effectiveness formula: prioritize on impact/effort, train evangelists, collaborate cross-departmentally, prove impact in money.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Calibrate to company stage**: mid-size = hat-wearing generalist; enterprise = evolving program along the SEO maturity model (ad-hoc → siloed → centralizing → standards/SOPs → proactive buy-in/training); SEO-native companies = buy-in preexists, focus shifts to strategy depth (e.g., programmatic SEO projects).
2. **Choose a track**: manager (coordinate, grow team/resources) vs. individual contributor (execute, own strategy/automation). Both are valid; preferences change over time.
3. **If transitioning from agency**: (a) apply broadly, (b) skill up — deep or wide, plus communication soft skills, (c) network via meetups/conferences for the "in".
4. **Be effective in-role**:
   - Prioritize: align to company/team goals (bonus-tied projects); use impact/effort matrix; draft effort estimates before handing to devs
   - Educate: SOPs, best-practice knowledge base, card-sorting with writers, office hours, lunch-and-learns, internal newsletter
   - Collaborate: show teams the bigger picture (paid retargets organic visitors; social amplifies content; leads get nurtured)
   - Prove impact: report money metrics, competitor comparisons, status/project reports; ballpark ROI with a simple revenue-based model (projected organic revenue vs. SEO cost)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Map your company's position on the SEO maturity model; plan the next stage
- [ ] List priority products/projects where someone's bonus is tied — align SEO work there first
- [ ] Build an impact/effort matrix of your backlog; take a first pass at effort estimates
- [ ] Write SOPs for recurring SEO decisions (URLs, redirects, on-page standards)
- [ ] Run a card-sorting exercise with content writers per major topic
- [ ] Schedule recurring training: office hours + one lunch-and-learn per month
- [ ] Start an internal SEO newsletter
- [ ] Pitch cross-team plays to execs (paid retargeting on organic audiences, social amplification)
- [ ] Report wins in revenue terms; use a simple ROI model for budget asks

## Data points & benchmarks
- In-house vs agency (practitioner view, varies by company and market): in-house often pays more, with better work-life balance and benefits, but with more internal politics
- Companies mature: siloed → centralized → SOPs → proactive (the recurring maturity model)
- Even at SEO-native companies, buy-in fights persist for novel bets like programmatic SEO

## Mistakes to avoid
- Expecting in-house to be "easier" — it's often more frustrating (politics, buy-in)
- Relying on SEO skill alone — "relationships and soft skills are arguably more important"
- Getting pulled in every direction — capability attracts workload; guard prioritization
- Chasing advanced strategies — basics executed repeatedly win; "boring projects = $$$"

## Key principles
- "In-house SEO is about getting things done... get the basics right over and over and execute."
- Enablement scales: one SEO trains evangelists who multiply impact
- Be opportunistic: work with teams when they're ready
- Great people managers who fight for the team are a super power — promote your devs' wins
