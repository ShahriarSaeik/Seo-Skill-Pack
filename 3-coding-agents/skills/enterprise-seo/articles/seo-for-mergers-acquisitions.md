# The Role of SEO in Mergers and Acquisitions

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
SEOs add measurable value across the whole M&A lifecycle: finding targets via competitive search data, pricing risk during due diligence (traffic declines, update hits, link quality), deciding merge-vs-keep, guarding redirects/content during migration, and auditing old acquisitions for value left on the table. Acquiring a genuine business brings its customers, content, and earned links; buying expired or lapsed domains just to pass ranking signals is a spam-policy violation and not part of this playbook.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Identify targets**: an organic-competitors view in your SEO tool (with date comparison for market evolution); if market players are unknown, look up the domains ranking for your market terms and compare their estimated organic share of voice. Check fit with your content strategy — acquiring a property that already ranks in a target market is the fastest way to enter it.
2. **Pre-screen risks**: declining traffic, history of search-update hits (traffic drop diagnosis), forecast future traffic, scan the link profile (good links = asset; paid, PBN, or other manipulative links = risk/liability).
3. **Due diligence & valuation** (during exclusivity): deeper pass on traffic, rankings, backlinks, forecasts + an audit/quick-wins pass to size upside potential. Targets often own multiple domains — check them all.
4. **Merge or not**: trade-off = more SERP listings (keep separate; common for news sites in Google News/affiliate) vs. one stronger listing (merge). Typical 3-stage merge: (1) "an X company" tag → (2) migrate domain under main brand → (3) full rebrand/integration.
5. **Migrate safely**: the two big traffic killers are missing redirects and killed content. Preserve legacy branded-term traffic (people search old names for years). Verify TLS certificates work across domains or users hit errors despite redirects.
6. **Post-acquisition monitoring**: group the old domain + new path in one tracking view; watch for traffic drops; compare organic keywords before/after to localize losses. Crawl the most-linked old URLs (top pages by links → custom URL list in a crawler) and check redirect status codes (301/308 good; 302/307 tell Google the move is temporary). Keep renewing old domains forever so competitors can't grab them.
7. **Train and transfer** best practices to acquired teams.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Screen targets: organic competitors + estimated share of voice for market terms
- [ ] Diagnose any traffic declines and update-history hits before valuation
- [ ] Run a quick-wins audit on target domains to quantify upside
- [ ] Decide merge vs. keep with the listings-vs-strength trade-off
- [ ] If merging: map redirects for every linked URL; migrate all content
- [ ] Keep ranking for the acquired brand's name (dedicated page or documentation)
- [ ] Verify TLS/HTTPS across both domains before cutover
- [ ] Set up a tracking group (old domain + new pages) and monitor for drops
- [ ] Crawl old top-linked URLs; confirm 301/308 (not 302/307) redirects
- [ ] Renew old domains indefinitely (and keep redirects in place for at least a year, longer while the old URLs still earn links or traffic)
- [ ] Audit past acquisitions for missed redirects/content — cheap recovered value

## Data points & benchmarks
- M&A is routine at enterprise scale, so migration and consolidation playbooks pay off repeatedly
- Practitioner war stories report fast ranking gains after redirecting an acquired site; those were acquisitions of real businesses. Redirecting an unrelated expired domain to borrow its rankings is a spam-policy violation, and Google may simply ignore or demote it
- Permanent moves need 301/308; 302/307 signal temporary and keep old URLs indexed
- Legacy brand terms keep search volume "for many years" — support them indefinitely

## Mistakes to avoid
- Letting the acquired domain expire — competitors or worse can register it
- Killing content during migration — the #1 avoidable traffic loss with missing redirects
- Using 302/307 redirects for permanent moves
- Ignoring legacy branded search demand
- Forgetting TLS across domains — redirects break with certificate errors
- Never auditing old acquisitions — value is routinely left on the table

## Key principles
- A genuine acquisition inherits earned links and brand equity that can be consolidated into stronger pages (this is a side effect of buying a real business, not a link-buying tactic)
- "More listings vs. one stronger listing" is the core merge decision
- Support old brand names for years — that traffic is free and converting
- Post-mortem audits of past acquisitions are among the cheapest wins in enterprise SEO
