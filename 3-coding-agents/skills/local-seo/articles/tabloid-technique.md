# The Tabloid Technique: How to Easily Land Local Links With Digital PR

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Local newspapers will link to almost any story showing how their area ranks on something — good or bad. The "tabloid technique" is a 3-step digital PR play: find a newsworthy local-comparison topic, pull local data (government databases, FOI requests, or your own research), and pitch it to local journalists in the areas your data flatters (or offends).

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find a newsworthy topic** — fits one of two buckets:
   - *Evergreen topics*: taxes, potholes — things locals always care about and can get riled up about
   - *Trending topics*: local elections, seasonal events — things journalists need to cover right now
   - Brainstorm volume: get the whole team involved, write down every idea, even bad ones; use an AI assistant as an idea partner
2. **Pull local data**
   - Existing sources: government databases (usa.gov, Data.gov, UK Data Service, Eurostat, Statistics Canada, World Bank Open Data, UN Data, Pew Research, FBI Crime Data Explorer, CDC)
   - Merge multiple sources for bigger stories (e.g., lifetime tax by US state)
   - Or source your own via freedom of information (FOI) requests
   - Publish on a journalist-friendly page: all data, sources, methodology, and visual assets they can reuse
3. **Send it to local journalists**
   - Target journalists in the specific places your data highlights
   - Find them: search `[place name] newspaper`, check staff/journalist pages, get emails from profiles
   - If email not listed, use email lookup tools
   - Identify active journalists: check bylines for articles published in the last 30 days (a content research tool can list authors by recent articles)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List evergreen local gripes (tax, potholes, traffic, crime, schools) and trending seasonal topics
- [ ] Use an AI assistant to expand topic + campaign ideas quickly
- [ ] Mine a content research tool for proven campaigns (search the topic, filter for pages with many referring domains) and adapt the angle
- [ ] Use Google Trends / Google News / a keyword research tool's growth or trend data to spot rising topics
- [ ] Pull data from official government/statistical sources; document methodology
- [ ] If no data exists, file FOI requests to create unique data
- [ ] Build a journalist-friendly page: full dataset, sources, methodology, embeddable visuals/maps
- [ ] Search "[place name] newspaper" for each area featured in your data
- [ ] Find named journalists; grab emails from byline pages or via email lookup tools
- [ ] Verify journalists are still active (recent bylines in the last 30 days)
- [ ] Send short, personalized pitches: the local angle + the key stat + link to assets

## Data points & benchmarks
- UK driving test wait-times campaign: links from 58-59 referring domains, mostly local newspapers
- US lifetime tax by state campaign: links from 188 websites (merged multiple data sources)
- UK illegal vape hotspots campaign (FOI-request data): links from 72 referring domains
- Even weak data points get coverage (e.g., "37th best city for millennials")
- A useful idea filter: look for similar campaigns whose pages earned links from 100+ referring domains

## Mistakes to avoid
- Waiting for the "perfect" idea — volume of ideas beats perfection; even mediocre rankings get covered
- Pitching journalists in areas with no relevance to your data — target the specific towns/cities your data features
- Wasting time on inactive journalists — always check recent author activity first
- Publishing raw data without a journalist-friendly page (methodology, sources, visuals) — make coverage frictionless
- Giving up after a flop — no technique works every time; luck plays a role
- Publishing sloppy or misleading numbers, or paying/offering freebies for coverage links — keep data accurate and sourced; paid placements need rel="sponsored"/nofollow

## Key principles
- "Local newspapers will link to anything" — local journalists are desperate for locally relevant content.
- Local media love comparisons: how their area stacks up against others on literally anything.
- Simple data can still work — the local angle is the story — but accuracy and a clear methodology are non-negotiable.
- Highlighting societal issues (e.g., food bank reliance) is legitimate journalism fodder, not just fluff.
