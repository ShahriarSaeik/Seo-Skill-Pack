# How to Build Local Citations (Complete Guide)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A local citation is any online mention of your business's name, address, and phone number (NAP). Their ranking impact has declined over time, but listings on relevant, trusted sites still matter — both as a (secondary) ranking signal and because directories dominate local search results where customers actually look. Build a small set of high-quality structured citations (aggregators, core sites, industry/local dirs) plus unstructured mentions, and keep NAP consistent everywhere.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Citation types:
- **Structured** — NAP in a structured directory listing or social profile (US examples: Yelp, Yellow Pages, lawyers.com; use your country's equivalents)
- **Unstructured** — contextual mentions in blog posts, press, forums, "best of" lists (often include followed backlinks — part of why they're powerful)

Build order (4 steps):
1. **Get listed with the major data aggregators (US example; use your country's equivalents)** — aggregators distribute your NAP to hundreds of sites. Several are free to claim and manage; skip paid access unless your data is wrong. Aggregator names and ownership change often, so check the current list.
2. **Submit to core sites directly** — Apple Maps, Facebook, Yelp, Bing Places, YellowPages, BBB, Foursquare (US examples). Only bother with sites you've heard of or that rank in the top 100 for "[industry] in [location]" searches.
3. **Submit to industry + local sites** — e.g., TripAdvisor (hotels), findlaw.com (attorneys), realtor.com (realtors) in the US, plus your country's equivalents; Chamber of Commerce, local business associations. Find via Google searches, a top-citations-by-category list (easily found online), or a link-intersect analysis vs. competitors.
4. **Pursue unstructured citations** — supplier/stockists pages (site:yoursupplier.com intitle:"stockists"), HARO-style journalist-request services, competitors' dofollow backlink profiles (e.g., "best brunch in London" lists → invite bloggers to visit; disclose any free meals or products, and expect rel="sponsored"/nofollow where there's a value exchange).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Search + claim existing listings on the main data aggregators; fix duplicates/inaccuracies before submitting new
- [ ] Submit/claim on core sites: Apple Maps, Facebook, Yelp, Bing Places, YellowPages, BBB, Foursquare (US examples; use local equivalents)
- [ ] Quality-test a directory: only submit if you've heard of it OR it ranks top 100 for "[industry] in [location]"
- [ ] Get industry-specific listings (e.g., TripAdvisor, Avvo, findlaw, realtor.com — US examples; use your country's equivalents)
- [ ] Join local Chamber of Commerce + business associations
- [ ] Run a link-intersect analysis with 3+ competitor homepages vs. your homepage to find directories competitors have that you don't
- [ ] Check referring-page context + estimated organic traffic to prioritize
- [ ] Audit suppliers' sites for stockists/clients pages and request inclusion
- [ ] Sign up for a HARO-style journalist-request service; answer relevant queries for press citations
- [ ] Analyze competitors' dofollow backlinks for "best of" list opportunities; outreach or invite relevant bloggers
- [ ] Keep NAP identical across all listings (address, phone, website); audit periodically
- [ ] Skip paid citation-submission services unless justified — manual submission to the handful of sites that matter is usually enough

## Data points & benchmarks
- Citations ranked as the **5th most important local ranking signal** in a 2018 industry survey — and their importance has likely declined since
- **80% of consumers lose trust** in local businesses with incorrect/inconsistent contact details online (older consumer study)
- Business directories (not business websites) dominate local SERPs — e.g., "plumber nottingham" returns mostly directories
- Minor name variations ("Beanies Coffee Shop" vs "Beanies") are OK if address/phone/website match — don't obsess

## Mistakes to avoid
- Submitting to every directory you find — quality trumps quantity; most low-tier directories see no real traffic
- Paying for citation management services by default — money usually better spent elsewhere
- Creating duplicate listings — always claim/correct existing ones before submitting new
- Inconsistent NAP across sites — erodes consumer trust (80% stat) and confuses verification
- Using directory homepages (instead of competitor business homepages) in a link-intersect analysis
- Expecting citations alone to move rankings — they're one signal among many (GBP, keywords, on-page, links)

## Key principles
- Citations help Google "verify the existence, legitimacy, and trustworthiness" of a business.
- Google's John Mueller: see citations "more as advertising" — valuable if people see them and seek out your business.
- Unstructured citations often come with followed backlinks — the most powerful kind for SEO.
- Directories rank in local SERPs themselves, so being listed there = visibility where customers search.
- If competitors have no press/blogger citations (small towns), nailing structured citations is usually enough to win.
