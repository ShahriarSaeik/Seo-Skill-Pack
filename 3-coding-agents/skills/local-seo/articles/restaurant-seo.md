# 5 Restaurant SEO Tips Backed by Diners & Data

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Diners search by dish, cuisine, and proximity — not restaurant name. Win by keeping a complete Google Business Profile (menu, photos, booking), mining reviews relentlessly, optimizing for "restaurants near [attraction]" searches, building blogger/supplier backlinks, and fixing a diner-friendly (non-PDF) website.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Make your Business Profile shine** — upload the full menu (dish names match dish searches like "carbonara near me"); add food/drink photos (3+ minimum per Google); add reservations (US examples: Resy, OpenTable; use your country's equivalents) and direct online ordering (40% of consumers prefer ordering direct vs. third-party apps)
2. **Get more reviews** — reviews were the 3rd most important local pack ranking factor in one industry survey; Google reviews matter most. Tactics: QR/NFC review cards on tables, post-meal reminder emails via reservation apps, review links printed on receipts/takeout boxes, email signatures, website + social prompts
3. **Optimize for proximity searches** — rank for "restaurants near [attraction]":
   a. List nearby attractions (or ask an AI assistant: "give me a list of 10 popular attractions near: [address]...")
   b. Check popularity: in a keyword research tool, review phrase-match keyword ideas containing "restaurants near"
   c. Mention attractions naturally: feature reviews mentioning them, booking advice for pre-event diners, or a genuine local area guide (not AI fluff)
4. **Build backlinks** — more backlinks correlate with more keyword rankings (14B-page study). Tactics: invite food bloggers for reviews (find via a link-intersect analysis vs. competitors; they should disclose free meals, and such links may be tagged rel="sponsored"/nofollow), request supplier "stockists" links (search `[supplier] partners|stockists`), pitch supplier interview/spotlight pieces
5. **Optimize the website for diners** — menu as HTML page not PDF (mobile pinch-zoom kills conversion; PDFs cause indexing weirdness); fix technical issues with a free site-audit tool, e.g., orphan pages, accidental deindexing

## Data points & benchmarks
Survey-based figures below are indicative and may be dated.
- 76% of 18-24 year olds search by cuisine/dish, not restaurant name
- 62% of consumers discover restaurants on Google; 67% search for reviews
- Review readership: Google 43%, Yelp 31%, TripAdvisor 16%
- 85% of diners use menus to pick new restaurants
- 40% prefer ordering directly from restaurant websites over third-party apps
- 80% of diners check a restaurant's website before visiting
- 69% of consumers recall leaving a review after being asked by the brand in the past year
- Photos: +42% direction requests, +35% clicks (older Google-reported figures); 100+ photos → +520% calls, +2,717% direction requests, +1,065% clicks (industry study; correlation)
- Example proximity volume: "restaurants near bryant park" = ~3,100 monthly searches
- One industry study (~14B pages): clear correlation between backlinks and number of keyword rankings
- Real rankings achieved: pub #5 for "pubs near wembley stadium"; #8-9 examples for proximity queries via simple on-page mentions

## Mistakes to avoid
- Thin GBP listings (few photos, no menu) — diners skip them outright
- Sending website visitors to third-party ordering apps — loses margin to commissions and hands the customer relationship to the app
- PDF menus — unusable on mobile, cause blank-page/orphan ranking problems
- Spammy AI-written "location guide" pages — may have worked briefly, but reads as fluff and risks scaled-content spam treatment; feature genuine recommendations instead
- Cluttered, misaligned websites (random merch, scattered buttons) — diners choose convenience over slightly better food
- Ignoring technical SEO — one wrong backend click can deindex the whole site

## Key principles
- Diners search by dish/cuisine/proximity, not brand — optimize for those queries.
- The menu on your GBP is a keyword asset: every dish matches "[dish] near me" searches.
- Reviews drive both rankings and diner confidence — asking works (69% stat).
- A great website with third-party-only ordering = leaking revenue and customer relationships.
- Food bloggers deliver links + social reach — invite them, don't just email pitch.
