# How to Hire a Link Building Agency

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Vet link building agencies on eight criteria: communication, realistic timelines, accountability, progress reporting, transparent methods, verifiable reputation, sustainable pricing, and capacity to scale. Unrealistic promises or bargain prices signal black-hat risk. Agencies fit short-term speed; in-house teams fit long-term cost efficiency.

## Framework / Step-by-step
First decision — **agency vs. in-house team:**
- Agency pros: years of experience, coordinated team (writers, SEOs, PR), pre-built journalist relationships, systems already running. Cons: premium pricing eats SEO budget; poor long-term economics for scale.
- In-house pros: cheaper long term, trainable to your SOPs, full control. Best when you have link building experience yourself and just need extra hands.

Then vet candidates on eight traits:

1. **Great communication.** Responses within ~24 working hours; a documented onboarding process; transparency about methods and timelines. Reluctance to explain how they work = walk away.
2. **Realistic timelines and goals.** Anyone promising "762 links in 30 days" is selling snake oil. Digital PR and journalist-request services take months (relationships, publishing cycles). Overpromising on counts or speed predicts black-hat methods or disappointment.
3. **Accountability.** Good strategies are client-specific (niche, business type) and involve trial and error. The agency must own mistakes and show how it will course-correct.
4. **Progress updates.** Client dashboards for campaign tracking; at minimum, pre-arranged regular check-ins. Essential for coordinating link building with the rest of your SEO.
5. **Methods and tools.** Should share a process overview at onboarding and name the professional tools used (competitor analysis, prospecting, outreach, tracking). Secrecy often means a static list of guest-post vendors, PBNs, or bought links — penalty territory, and you (not the agency) bear the consequences. Ask directly whether any placements are paid and how they are tagged (rel="sponsored"/nofollow).
6. **Reputation.** Testimonials from recognizable industry professionals (not anonymous nobodies), case studies with known brands, awards. Go beyond their site: check social, community forums, and ex-employee reviews.
7. **Pricing.** Expensive ≠ better; geography legitimately shifts rates. But real agencies carry unavoidable costs (tools, outreach staff, writers, managers, overhead) — so ~$20/link is a red flag regardless of where they're based. Expect pricing by link type (illustratively, roughly $1 for local citations up to ~$700 for journalist-request placements at established agencies; rates vary by market and year).
8. **Ability to scale.** A four-person shop can't grow with you. Prefer established teams you can build a lasting relationship with, so doubling down doesn't mean switching providers.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Decide agency vs. in-house based on budget horizon and internal expertise
- [ ] Shortlist 3-5 agencies; send identical scoping questions
- [ ] Test response times and onboarding transparency
- [ ] Ask for expected link counts + timeline; reject anything fantastical
- [ ] Request process overview and tool stack
- [ ] Verify testimonials with named people; find case studies and third-party reviews
- [ ] Benchmark per-link pricing against link type (citations vs. guest posts vs. PR placements)
- [ ] Confirm reporting cadence or dashboard access
- [ ] Ask how strategies adapt per client and how mistakes get handled
- [ ] Check team size for scaling headroom

## Data points & benchmarks
- Standard agency response expectation: within 24 working hours
- Per-link market ranges (illustrative, established agencies; vary by market and year): ~$1 (local citations) → ~$700 (journalist-request placements)
- ~$20/link for editorial placements = below any sustainable cost structure = red flag
- Digital PR / journalist-request campaigns realistically take months, not weeks

## Mistakes to avoid
- Buying from freelance marketplaces or LinkedIn cold-outreach "experts" without vetting
- Believing huge link-count promises on short timelines
- Ignoring secrecy about methods — it usually hides penalty-prone tactics
- Assuming the highest price buys the best links
- Committing long term to an agency too small to scale with you
- Skipping third-party and ex-employee reviews

## Key principles
- Quality link building is slow, client-specific, and relationship-driven — price and promises should reflect that
- Transparency (methods, timelines, reporting) is the single best quality signal
- Testimonials from verifiable industry names beat anonymous praise
- Match the engagement model to your horizon: agency for speed, in-house for scale economics
- A good agency partnership compounds: they learn your site and goals, so you never re-onboard
