# How To Get Backlinks: Add, Ask, Earn, Buy

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Every link building tactic falls into exactly four buckets: Add, Earn, Ask, Buy. Adding links yourself is worthless; earning them via remarkable assets is best; asking works only with a genuine exchange of value; buying is risky and mostly discouraged. Start by reverse-engineering competitors' links (homepage links, most-linked pages, and links to pages you want to outrank).

## Framework / Step-by-step
The 4-bucket model (from interviews with dozens of practitioners):
1. **Add** — place links yourself on sites that allow user-generated content (forums, comments). Easy but near-worthless; usually nofollow'd; auto-plugin backlinks are pure spam.
2. **Earn** — create something so notable people link without being asked: industry surveys (ego bait), free online tools, original research, thorough tutorials, news. Best links, hardest to get.
3. **Ask** — outreach. Works only as an exchange of value: guest articles (fair trade: content for link), link exchanges (risky at scale, "link spam" per Google guidelines), or a truly outstanding resource.
4. **Buy** — risky; this library advises against it. If you do pay for anything link-related: never buy from spam email sellers; the least risky "bought" links come as side benefits of real business interactions (e.g., event sponsorship), and any paid link should be rel="sponsored" or nofollow. Safer alternative: pay agencies/freelancers to execute legit tactics.

3-step competitive research process to start:
1. **Study competitors' homepage links** — in a backlink analysis tool, list the backlinks to the exact homepage URL. Find patterns (e.g., integration listings) you can replicate.
2. **Study competitors' most linked pages** — view their top pages by links. Free tools and research studies top the list; if competitors have linkable assets and you don't, they earn links "on autopilot" while you hustle.
3. **Study pages you want to outrank** — check the backlinks of the top-ranking pages for your target keyword; infer the tactic that earned links (e.g., ego-bait awards) and replicate it.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Classify every planned tactic as Add / Earn / Ask / Buy; drop pure "Add" tactics
- [ ] Build at least one linkable asset (free tool, original-data study, or thorough tutorial)
- [ ] Run competitor homepage backlink analysis for integration/listing opportunities
- [ ] Run "top-pages-by-links" on 3-5 competitors; identify asset gaps
- [ ] Reverse-engineer backlinks of pages outranking yours for target keywords
- [ ] If outreach fails, ask linkers of competitor pages directly why they linked
- [ ] If outsourcing, demand past work examples + client references

## Data points & benchmarks
- 111 personalized outreach emails (strong content, strong brand) → 19 links = **17% success rate**; anything above **10%** is considered amazing
- Expect roughly **5 links per 100 emails** for an unknown site using "my page is awesome" pitches (a rough figure from one practitioner's experience)
- Average price of a decent bought backlink: **$200–300** (older industry poll; prices drift); $3/link = spam neighborhood → penalty risk
- Ego-bait example: one industry survey report earned links from **571 websites** including several big-brand homepages

## Mistakes to avoid
- Buying links from "quality high-authority backlinks" spam emails
- Scaling guest posts with cheap/AI-written content — nobody will publish it
- Scaling link exchanges (even three-way/indirect) — Google link spam penalty risk
- Believing your outreach success is all skill — brand affinity does heavy lifting (a no-name site gets 2-3x lower rates)
- Buying 1,000 links/month at $3 each — a likely penalty (one reported case: traffic collapsed)

## Key principles
- "The strategies are secondary. What comes first is your understanding of why people link, and how to persuade them to link to you."
- Link building is dealing with humans, not hacking a technical process — deserve the link.
- A link request must be an exchange of value; "my content is awesome" alone is asking for a favor.
- If a site easily sells you a followed link (price list ready), that's a red flag — editorial sites don't sell untagged links.
