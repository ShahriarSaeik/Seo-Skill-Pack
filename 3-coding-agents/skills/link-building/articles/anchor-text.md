# Anchor Text: A Data-Driven Guide (384,614 Web Pages Studied)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Anchor text is the clickable text of a link, and search engines have used it since day one to understand what a page is about — but an industry study of 384,614 pages found only a *weak* correlation between keyword-rich anchors and rankings. Most ranking pages have zero exact-match anchored backlinks, so don't engineer anchor ratios; keep anchors natural and branded, and spend the effort on topics and content instead.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Know the anchor types** (for a page targeting "backlink checker"):
1. **Exact match** — anchor is exactly the keyword ("backlink checker")
2. **Phrase match** — anchor contains the keyword phrase ("best backlink checker")
3. **Partial match** — anchor has all the words, unordered ("checker for backlinks")
4. **Branded** — your brand name
5. **Naked URL** — the raw URL as the anchor
6. **Random** — "click here", "this article"
7. **Image link** — the image's alt text acts as the anchor

**History that shapes today's practice:**
- Original search algorithm treated link text as a ranking signal — anchors could describe pages better than the pages themselves (and rank uncrawlable files like images).
- Heavy reliance enabled "Google bombing" — spammy exact-anchor campaigns ranking irrelevant pages.
- The 2012 Penguin update specifically targeted over-optimized anchor text; affected ~3.1% of queries and tanked aggressive exact-match sites overnight.

**Study design (why to trust it):** top 20 results for 19,840 keywords (2-5K monthly searches, 2-4 words) = 384,614 pages; keywords chosen so top-10 results had similar page-authority scores, isolating the anchor variable; reported medians as well as averages because extreme values skew averages.

**Results (Spearman correlation with position):**
- Exact-match anchor share: 0.14-0.19 — weak positive; median share of exact-match anchors was 0% at every position
- Phrase-match: ~0.11-0.14 — very weak
- Partial-match: ~0.11-0.14 — very weak
- Random anchors: ~0.01 — none
- Surrounding link text (per a second study of 16K pages with only random anchors): 0.02-0.06 — no effect, even though 20-25% of links contained at least one keyword word nearby

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Audit your profile for exact-match anchor clusters (clusters of one keyword across many sites = manipulation pattern)
- [ ] Use branded or natural anchors wherever you control them (e.g., guest-post author bios)
- [ ] Write descriptive image alt text for linked images (it becomes the anchor)
- [ ] Let organic anchors be whatever linkers naturally write — usually branded or random
- [ ] If copying competitor anchor ratios: stop — you can't control anchors with legitimate tactics anyway
- [ ] Optimize pages for topics and semantic variants (on-page) rather than engineering anchors per keyword

## Data points & benchmarks
- 384,614 pages / 19,840 keywords analyzed; exact-match anchor correlation only 0.14-0.19 (weak)
- Median exact-match anchor share at every ranking position: 0%
- Penguin (2012) hit anchor over-optimization; ~3.1% of queries affected
- Top 10 ranking pages also rank for 400-1,300 other queries on average (3M-query study) — one anchor can't (and shouldn't) target them all
- On one large SEO blog, only ~22% of a page's traffic comes from its main target keyword (one example: 17%)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Building exact-match anchor links to rank for one keyword — ignores the long tail and invites Penguin-style risk
- Assuming high average anchor percentages matter — averages hide that most winners have zero
- Using PBNs or paid links to place keyword anchors — rare, unnatural, and a spam-policy violation (paid links must be rel="sponsored" or nofollow)
- Copying the anchor distribution of currently ranking pages — no legitimate tactic gives you that control

## Key principles
- Anchor text gives search engines context; it is a weak ranking signal, not a dial to turn.
- Natural anchor profiles are mostly branded, random, and naked URLs — because that's what real linkers write.
- Topics beat keywords: optimize for the cluster of queries a page deserves, not the one keyword an anchor could hit.
- The main legitimate place you choose the anchor (an editorial guest post author bio, never a paid placement) is exactly where you should choose branding over keywords.
