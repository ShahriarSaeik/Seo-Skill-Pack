# 6 Proven Ways To Find Anyone's Email Address

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Finding a real contact email is the bottleneck of every outreach campaign. Six methods cover it: email lookup tools (the best resolved ~92% of addresses in one older head-to-head test), pattern guessing with verification, searching social posts for obfuscated addresses, newsletter replies, simply asking, and routing through generic inboxes. For scale, pair an author database with a bulk email finder.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Email lookup services** — feed in name + domain, get the address. Best-in-class tools resolved 92% of a test contact list in one older head-to-head; a free browser extension also performed strongly (no bulk mode). Many tools offer a small free monthly allowance, with paid tiers priced per valid result (pricing changes often; check current rates).
2. **Guess and test** — most business emails follow a handful of formats (one older sample put it near 70%): first@domain, firstlast@, first.last@, flast@, firstl@, initials@. Use a permutation tool to generate all variants, then check which resolves to a profile (compose windows and spreadsheet add-ons show profile matches); or search the exact guess on a search engine to find public mentions.
3. **Search their social posts** — people mask emails in posts as "name AT domain DOT com". Use advanced search on the person's posts for "at", "dot", plus "email", "contact", "reach". Many don't even obfuscate.
4. **Subscribe to their newsletter** — campaigns usually send from (or reveal) the personal address. Bonus: replying to a newsletter with a genuine question is a natural relationship opener.
5. **Just ask (on social)** — message the person and request their email; most reply happily if your profile clearly shows who you are.
6. **Ask for a connection via generic channels** — email press@/contact forms and request a routing to the right person; a clear signature (or a VA whose signature names you) makes this work.

**Last resorts:** check your own contact list/history, export your professional network connections (~7% expose emails), scan About pages and social bios.

**At scale (outreach campaigns):** search a content database for your topic → export articles WITH author names → extract domains from URLs with a regex formula (`=REGEXEXTRACT(...)`) → upload name+domain pairs to a bulk email finder → verify before sending.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Start with an email lookup tool (name + domain) before manual work
- [ ] For high-value prospects, verify guesses via profile-population or exact-match search
- [ ] Run the "at/dot" social search for prospects who publish openly
- [ ] Subscribe + reply to newsletters of key targets (dual purpose: address + rapport)
- [ ] Ask directly on social when the address is simply unavailable
- [ ] For campaigns: build the author+domain sheet first, then bulk-resolve and verify
- [ ] Never send to unverified addresses — bounces damage deliverability

## Data points & benchmarks
- Best lookup tools: ~92% success rate on a controlled test list
- Roughly 70% of contact-list emails used first-name-based formats in one older sample
- Free search allowances are small and paid tiers charge per valid address (verify current pricing)
- Professional-network exports: only ~7% of connections exposed their email in one test
- Compliance: use business contact details for relevant, personalised outreach only; follow anti-spam and privacy rules in your region (e.g., CAN-SPAM, GDPR) and honor opt-outs

## Mistakes to avoid
- Blasting unverified guessed addresses (bounce rate kills sender reputation)
- Sending to info@ when the author's address is findable — signals laziness to the recipient
- Using scraped lists without checking whether the person is still at the company
- Relying on one tool — hit rates vary; cross-check critical prospects

## Key principles
- The right person beats the right template: contact accuracy is outreach step zero.
- Verification is part of finding — an address you haven't tested is a liability.
- Newsletter replies and direct asks double as relationship-building, not just data collection.
- For volume, automate resolution (author + domain + bulk finder) but keep sends human.
