# Stats Page Link Building Case Study (36 Links, Ranked #1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
In one case study, a curated statistics page was built around journalists' most-cited stats, then everyone citing the outdated versions was pitched: 515 emails → 36 links from 32 websites and the #1 ranking for the target stats keyword. Stats pages are ideal skyscraper targets because they attract tons of links, go stale, and get sloppily updated (links survive even after stats are deleted).

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find a winning topic.** Seed your industry terms in a keyword tool → keyword ideas containing "statistics, stats, facts, figures" → favor topics with high keyword difficulty (a rough proxy for how many referring domains top pages have) → confirm the SERP is mostly curated stats pages.
2. **Find link prospects by STATISTIC, not by page.** Take the most-linked competing stats page; in its backlink anchor text, find which specific statistics earn the most links. For each stat, check: (a) enough sites link because of it (one famous stat had 700+ linking sites), and (b) you have a reason to contact them — the stat is missing from the page, or outdated (trace the original source; e.g., one viral stat dated from 2006). Log in a spreadsheet: stat, linking-domain count, original source + age, newer stat + source, pitch angle.
3. **Narrow the prospect list.** From ~1,986 raw URLs: deduplicate domains, drop nofollow/UGC/sponsored linkers, remove sites without meaningful traffic (batch traffic lookups) → 902 vetted prospects.
4. **Create the page.** Include the popular stats (updated where stale — these are proven citation magnets), add other interesting verified stats, group by category with jump links, and put the most-cited stats in a "top statistics" section up front.
5. **Find contacts and vet.** Scrape author names (~741 from 902 URLs), run them through an email-finder tool (~60% hit rate → 452), verify deliverability (168 valid / 92 catch-all / 178 dead), manually vet pages a few hours/week → 515 sites ready to pitch.
6. **Send segmented personalized outreach.** One template personalized by statistic: "I saw you mentioned [stat] on your page about [topic]. [Custom pitch: that stat is X years old; newer research says Y]. I published this and other fresh stats here: [link]. Not sure if you're actively editing posts, but might be worth an update if you are? No pressure." Write one custom pitch paragraph per statistic (8 in this campaign) and merge.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Validate the stats keyword: high keyword difficulty + curated-pages SERP
- [ ] Mine top stats pages' anchors for link-winning statistics
- [ ] Build the stat-by-stat sheet (link counts, ages, replacements, pitch angle)
- [ ] Clean prospect list: dedupe, followed-links-only, traffic floor
- [ ] Publish page: proven stats updated, categorized, top-stats section
- [ ] Automate contacts: scrape → email finder → verifier → manual catch-alls
- [ ] Write one pitch paragraph per statistic; merge and send
- [ ] Reply to every response; follow up once with promising non-responders; decline payment demands and link swaps

## Data points & benchmarks
- 515 emails sent, 473 delivered → 27 linkers contacted + 5 unsolicited = 36 links from 32 domains
- Conversion: 5.71% (of delivered); reply rate 17.55% (83 replies: 8 link-exchange asks, 6 other asks, 3 payment demands)
- Projected if all replies were converted: ~41 domains (8.7%); with 3 follow-ups (an older 600K-email study found follow-ups at least double results): 11.4%+
- Link quality: 9/32 domains authority 70+, 12 in 40-69; one linker >1M monthly search visits
- Email pipeline: ~60% finder hit rate; ~1/3 of found emails deliverable-clean

## Mistakes to avoid
- Blasting every linker of a stats page with "ours is better, swap the link" — the win is the specific outdated/missing statistic
- Including stats without tracing original sources — stale numbers kill credibility
- Pitching nofollow/UGC/sponsored linkers and duplicate domains
- Taking payment or link-swap offers: 14 of 83 repliers asked for exchanges or other favors, but paid and swapped links are spam-policy risks (tag as rel="sponsored"/nofollow if you ever pay)
- Skipping follow-ups entirely (this campaign chose zero automation to stay polite, at the cost of some links)

## Key principles
- Stats pages are link magnets: journalists cite them constantly and rarely re-check
- Outdated + missing statistics on competitor pages = legitimate, welcome outreach angles
- Segment by statistic, personalize the pitch paragraph, keep the ask soft
- Reply rate >> link rate: thoughtful replies (not payment or swaps) are where the marginal links live
- Once set up (with a vetting helper), a campaign like this is ~one working day to launch
