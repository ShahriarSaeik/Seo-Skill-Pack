# 4 Reasons to Build More Links From the Same Website

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Unique referring domains matter most, but repeat links from the same website are natural (79% of referring domains linked to the same site multiple times in one older study) and valuable. Don't skip a prospect just because they linked once — additional links add page authority, hit more target pages, drive referral traffic, and are far easier to earn from existing fans.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The four reasons to keep working a domain that already links to you:
1. **Increase page authority** — PageRank flows page to page, so links from multiple pages on the same site stack authority on the linked pages (page-level authority correlates with organic traffic, though correlation is not proof of causation).
2. **Get links to more of your pages** — the referring-domains/traffic correlation is at PAGE level, and link magnets (studies, tools) are rarely the pages you need to rank. If a site that linked to your study could also link to your money page, pitch it — internal links distribute some authority, but direct links are better.
3. **Get more referral traffic** — one study drew 1,200+ referring domains in 36 months (~34/month) while getting almost no organic traffic: linkers discovered it from OTHER linking pages with traffic (4 of the 6 highest-traffic referring pages came from a single domain). Traffic compounds links. Workflow: find your best referral sources in analytics (sort by conversions/users) → check their top-traffic pages → pitch a contextual link where your resource fits.
4. **Build links more easily** — repeat linkers are warm. Find them: export your backlinks, group by referring domain, followed only, and sort by link count (e.g., a fan site with 10 links to your posts). When you publish something relevant, share it with them directly — or use `site:theirsite.com [topic]` searches to find contextual placement opportunities and pitch those.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Stop filtering prospects by "already has one link from us"
- [ ] Segment referring domains by link count; build a "serial linkers" list
- [ ] For each serial linker, match a NEW target page (not the one they already linked)
- [ ] Pull top referral sources from analytics; pitch contextual links on their top-traffic pages
- [ ] Note the context of each previous link in your CRM/spreadsheet and reference it in pitches
- [ ] Give serial linkers special, non-generic outreach — they're fans, treat them like partners

## Data points & benchmarks
- 79.23% of referring domains linked to the same website more than once in one older study; most linked 2-5 times
- Caution: repeated links should be earned editorially; recurring paid or swapped links from one site are a link-scheme risk
- Case: a single data study earned 1,200+ referring domains over 36 months (~34/month) with near-zero organic traffic of its own — discovery happened via referring pages' traffic
- Page-level authority metric correlates with organic search traffic (public PageRank scores discontinued in 2016)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming multiple links from one domain is unnatural or wasteful — the data says it's the norm
- Always pointing new prospects at the same linkbait page instead of pages you want to rank
- Sending your standard mass-pitch template to serial linkers — they've earned personal outreach
- Ignoring referral traffic value because a link is "only" from an already-linking domain

## Key principles
- Referring domains are the KPI; repeat links from quality domains are the compounding interest.
- Links do three jobs at once — authority, distribution to the right pages, and discovery by future linkers.
- Your warmest audience is people who already linked to you; mine them before cold prospects.
- Traffic on the linking page is a link multiplier: eyeballs today, backlinks tomorrow.
