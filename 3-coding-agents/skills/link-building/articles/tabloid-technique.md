# The Tabloid Technique: Landing Local Links With Digital PR

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Local newspapers link to almost any story that compares how their area stacks up on something — taxes, potholes, driving-test pass rates. The tabloid technique: pick a newsworthy topic, pull LOCAL data for it, and pitch the ranking/statistic to local journalists in the areas that come out best (or worst).

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find a newsworthy topic.** The hardest part. Two buckets:
   - **Evergreen topics** — taxes, potholes, commute times: things locals always care about (bonus if slightly aggravating)
   - **Trending topics** — local elections, seasonal events: things journalists need to cover right now
   - Mine ideas from a keyword tool's trending metrics, news search, and AI-assisted brainstorming; study past hit campaigns with a content research tool (e.g., pages about "tax" with links from 100+ domains). Quantity beats quality here — brainstorm broadly with the whole team; even bad ideas get written down.
2. **Pull local data.** Sources:
   - Government/statistical databases (national statistics portals, data.gov-style catalogs, EU/canada/world statistics, central bank and UN data)
   - Topic specialists: crime stats, health data, public-opinion research centers
   - **Freedom of information (FOI) requests** — cheap, underused, and produce exclusive numbers
   - Merge multiple sources when no single dataset suffices
   - Publish findings on a journalist-friendly page: full data, sources, methodology, and ready-to-use visual assets.
3. **Send it to local journalists.**
   - Match data to place: journalists in the areas that top (or bottom) the ranking are your targets
   - Find outlets via `[place name] newspaper` searches; identify journalist names on staff pages; emails are usually listed on profiles — otherwise use an email lookup tool
   - Prefer currently active writers (sort a publication's authors by recent output) so you don't pitch people who've left
   - Email pattern that works: personalized, leads with the local hook ("Your city ranked X for Y"), includes the key stat and a link to the full data page

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List 10+ evergreen local topics (tax, traffic, crime, schools, weather) + current trending hooks
- [ ] Validate topics against past campaigns that earned 50+ referring domains
- [ ] Identify the dataset(s); file FOI requests if public data doesn't exist
- [ ] Build the local ranking/statistic; document methodology and sources
- [ ] Create embeddable maps/charts journalists can reuse
- [ ] Build a target list: one journalist per ranked area, prioritizing top/bottom areas
- [ ] Verify each journalist is still active at the outlet
- [ ] Send localized pitches (area-specific stat in the subject/first line)
- [ ] Track coverage and referring domains earned per campaign

## Data points & benchmarks
- UK driving-test wait-times index: links from ~58-59 websites, mostly local newspapers
- Lifetime tax paid per US state (multi-source merge): links from 188 websites
- Illegal vape hotspots investigation (built from FOI requests): 72 referring domains, heavy newspaper pickup
- Even mediocre rankings (e.g., a city placing 37th "best for millennials") got local coverage — the bar is low
- These are one practitioner's best examples; results vary widely by topic and market

## Mistakes to avoid
- Waiting for a brilliant idea: hit campaigns are usually simple local comparisons executed well
- Pitching national outlets when the data is local — local papers are the link source
- Sending journalists raw data without a page containing methodology and visuals
- Publishing unverified or cherry-picked numbers — check data quality and be transparent about limitations before pitching
- Contacting inactive journalists or generic newsroom inboxes
- One idea per campaign: run several — no technique works every time

## Key principles
- Local journalists need constant copy about their area; give them a pre-packaged story
- The more areas your data ranks, the more outlets you can legitimately pitch
- Exclusive data (FOI, proprietary surveys) beats recycled public stats
- Journalist-friendly = data + sources + methodology + visuals, all citable
- Volume of ideas and pitches drives hit rate, not one perfect campaign
