# Easy Off-Page SEO Checklist (5 Steps)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Off-page SEO is more than link building: replicate/replace competitor links, systematize review generation, get listed on the niche search platforms your buyers actually use, promote every piece of content, and do genuinely interesting real-world things that earn links and signals conventional campaigns can't.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The three Rs for competitor links, plus four more checklist pillars:

1. **Get as many competitor backlinks as you can (the three Rs):**
   - **Replicate** — same link, same place. Use a link-intersect comparison (domains linking to multiple competitors but not you): sign up for the directories; pitch inclusion for listicles featuring several competitors but not you (open by asking why you weren't featured, not with a hard pitch).
   - **Reproduce** — same tactic, new instance. Spot journalist-request links (filter a competitor's backlinks for repeated expert names in anchors); spot digital PR campaigns in their top pages by links and run your own variant, pitching the same journalists.
   - **Replace** — find competitors' dead pages with backlinks (top pages by links filtered to 404s), rebuild the content better, then ask everyone linking to the dead page to swap to yours.
2. **Put systems in place to get more reviews.** Buyers check review scores before purchasing; reviews are also a local map-pack ranking factor. Systems: QR codes with a review link on tables/receipts; automated post-delivery emails with a review link a few days after arrival. Ask every customer (no review gating), never offer incentives that violate platform rules, and never post or buy fake reviews.
3. **Get listed where people actually search in your niche.** Many buyers start on niche directories or industry search engines, not Google. Find them via a link-intersect comparison (high-traffic directories linking to competitors) or by checking which domains rank for keywords like "lawyer in NY". These platforms usually also rank in Google themselves — double exposure.
4. **Promote your content, always.** More eyeballs = more links. Tactics: tell quoted/mentioned people about the piece; pitch industry newsletters and resource pages (findable via a content research tool); post a stripped-down, non-promotional TL;DR version to relevant community forums (disclose your affiliation and follow each community's self-promotion rules).
5. **Do genuinely interesting stuff in the real world.** Sponsor a local team (sponsor links may be tagged sponsored/nofollow, which is fine), launch a quirky low-cost product stunt, host an event. These earn links and positive signals that competitors can't replicate through conventional SEO.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Run a link-intersect comparison vs. 3-5 competitors; claim replicable directory listings
- [ ] Pitch listicles that include multiple competitors but not you (conversation-first opener)
- [ ] Check competitors' backlinks for repeated expert-quote anchors → join journalist request services
- [ ] Mine competitors' top pages by links for PR campaign formats to imitate
- [ ] Find competitors' 404 pages with backlinks; rebuild and pitch linkers as replacements
- [ ] Set up review-generation systems (QR codes, post-purchase email sequence)
- [ ] Identify 3-5 niche directories/industry search engines with real traffic; get listed
- [ ] Build a promotion checklist for every new post: notify mentioned people, pitch 5 newsletters/resource pages, post a TL;DR to communities
- [ ] Plan one low-cost, interesting real-world stunt per quarter

## Data points & benchmarks
- One UK vape retailer's local-data PR campaign: 110 referring domains from newspapers
- A competitor's dead "SEO tips" page still held links from 124 referring domains — prime replacement target
- One legal directory surfaced this way drew ~2.8M monthly organic visits (an older figure) — being listed there can rival a Google presence
- A stripped-down forum TL;DR of one guide earned 80+ upvotes and extra links
- Real-world stunts can cost almost nothing: a 10-pence novelty doughnut launch earned a local newspaper feature

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating off-page SEO as only link building — reviews and niche listings convert and rank
- Pitching listicle authors cold and hard; open with a question instead
- Promotional posts in community forums — strip the marketing or get removed
- Assuming small brands need Wikipedia pages or heavy social activity — marginal at best
- Ignoring real-world activity because it's "not SEO" — it produces the hardest-to-replicate signals

## Key principles
- Competitor backlinks are a ready-made link prospecting list (replicate, reproduce, replace)
- Reviews are an off-page ranking AND conversion asset; systems beat asking ad hoc
- Meet buyers on the niche platforms where searches actually start
- Promotion is a permanent habit, not a post-publish afterthought
- Interesting real-world actions generate links and trust that pure SEO plays can't fake
