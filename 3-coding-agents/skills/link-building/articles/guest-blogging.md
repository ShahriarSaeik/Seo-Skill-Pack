# Guest Blogging for SEO: Everything You Need to Know

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Guest blogging (write for another site, get an author-byline link) was the #3 most popular link building tactic in one industry survey (47% of SEOs; an older survey). Google treats large-scale guest posting done mainly for links as a link scheme, so keep it editorial. The winning playbook: skip the crowded "write for us" pool, find sites via a content research tool, pitch ideas backed by data (content gaps, declining posts), and write genuinely great posts with 1-2 links back to your site.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find opportunities** — beyond `write for us` operators: a content research tool → broad niche keyword → title search → filters: authority roughly 30-60, one page per domain, exclude homepages/subdomains → the resulting list of sites is your pitch list.
2. **Develop ideas** — five techniques:
   - **Copycat**: a content-gap comparison between the target blog and its 3 competitors → pitch keywords competitors rank for but the target doesn't (every blog wants more search traffic)
   - **Refresh**: in a traffic-estimation tool, view the target's top pages compared with the previous year, sort by traffic change ascending → pitch updates of their declining posts
   - **Robin Hood**: take topics that work for high-authority blogs, spin them for lesser-known blogs
   - **Splintering**: break your own big pillar guide into standalone chapter-posts
   - **Perspective**: re-cover the same topic for different audiences (e.g., "best remote jobs for students" → for moms → for developers)
3. **Pitch** — read their guidelines and follow to a T; identify the managing editor/head of content via team page or LinkedIn/Twitter; find their email with an email-finder tool; write an outreach email based on principles, not templates.
4. **Write** — build the outline first: review the top-ranking pages for the topic and find the subtopics/keywords they share → those become H2s. Include 1-2 relevant links to your own content, no more, with natural (ideally branded) anchors. If you paid or received anything for the placement, the link must be rel="sponsored" or nofollow.
5. **Promote** — thank the editor, reply to comments, share on social, submit to relevant communities.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a prospect list: a content research tool, authority roughly 30-60, exclude homepages/subdomains
- [ ] Prioritize sites that have never linked to you (check your backlink list)
- [ ] Prepare 2-3 data-backed pitch ideas per target (gap keywords or declining posts)
- [ ] Find the exact editor + verified email before pitching
- [ ] Pitch the same topic to multiple blogs; give it to first/best replyer, tell others the truth
- [ ] Outline from the shared subtopics of top-ranking pages before writing
- [ ] Re-check editorial guidelines before submitting draft
- [ ] Post-publication: thank, answer comments, promote in communities

## Data points & benchmarks
- One older industry survey: **47% of SEOs** used guest blogging; **#3** most popular tactic
- Suggested prospect authority band: roughly **30-60** on the tool's scale
- Average referral traffic per guest post (older survey of 500+ bloggers, 239 articles): only **~50 visits** — do it for brand and credibility, not traffic
- An older industry study of 1 billion pages found a strong correlation between organic traffic and number of referring domains (correlation, not proof of causation)
- Self-serving links in post: **1-2 max**

## Mistakes to avoid
- Only pitching blogs with public "write for us" pages — everyone does; competition is rife and pitches get ignored
- Copy-pasting outreach templates — editors get flooded; follow principles instead
- Stuffing your own links into the post, or using keyword-rich anchors in mass-produced guest posts — a spam-policy risk
- Paying for guest-post placement without rel="sponsored"/nofollow and disclosure
- Disappearing after publication — engagement (comments, communities) multiplies results
- Expecting meaningful referral traffic — 50 visits is the average

## Key principles
- Many blogs will consider a genuinely great, well-targeted guest post even without an advertised program.
- Pitch value to the blog (their traffic gaps, their decaying posts), not to yourself.
- Matt Cutts' 2014 "guest blogging is dead" was about spammy low-quality guest posts on low-quality sites — quality guest posting on established, relevant sites still works.
