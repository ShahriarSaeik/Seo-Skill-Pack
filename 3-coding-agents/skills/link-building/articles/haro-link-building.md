# How to Use HARO (And Alternatives) to Get Killer Mentions and Backlinks

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
HARO-style journalist-request services (the original service declined and was later relaunched) let you answer journalist callouts for quotes — earning mentions and backlinks from authoritative publications. In the AI-search era, even UNLINKED brand mentions can matter (brand and E-E-A-T signals, and how AI answers describe you). Winning pitches are Helpful, Authentic, Relevant, On time — and in one practitioner's test, 68% of responses failed by telling journalists what they already knew instead of showing evidence.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Model: journalist posts callout (tight deadline) → you reply with useful answer → if chosen: mention + often a link.

Before pitching, vet the publication: check its authority score and organic traffic in an SEO tool, scan its backlink profile for spam links, and read recent posts.

The 8 rules of a winning pitch:
1. **Show, don't tell** — offer evidence (how/why), not explanations of obvious concepts findable in AI Overviews in 30 seconds
2. **No "trust me, bro" language** — "I've personally seen..." only with proof; journalists need fact-checkable sources; "everyone's witnessed this" perspectives get ignored
3. **No opinions unless asked** — 80%+ of responses contained unsolicited opinions; journalists requesting data ignore them (opinions are hard to validate)
4. **Don't force follow-ups** — never reply "book a meeting"; deliver everything upfront; THEN respond fast if they engage (speed + offering follow-up answers = big boost)
5. **Avoid sweeping generalizations** — "most people think X" without evidence is ignorable fluff
6. **Give a punchy quotable soundbite** — "AI changed search from question-and-answer to prediction-and-summary" beats a description of AI in search; create a curiosity gap
7. **Give the journalist what they want** — understand intent: fresh examples, stats AI can't generate, new angles, valid source links; only 4% of responses did this; keep it skimmable (a 900-word wall of text dies on mobile)
8. **Establish credibility up front** — 1-2 sentences: relevant job title, known company, or years of experience (or a clever bridge connecting your niche to the topic); lead with verifiable links (LinkedIn, company, media mentions)

Perfect pitch anatomy: credibility opener → short skimmable insights answering exactly what was asked → quotable one-liners → brief bio + headshot → contact details.
Prep trick: search the publication + topic and read how experts were quoted in its past articles → mirror that format. Only claim credentials you actually hold, and never fabricate quotes or data.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Sign up for HARO + 2-3 alternatives (other journalist-request platforms)
- [ ] Vet publications (authority, traffic, backlink profile) before investing time
- [ ] Answer only callouts where your credibility is provable in one sentence
- [ ] Lead with data/evidence; cut all "as you know" explanations
- [ ] Include one quotable soundbite per answer
- [ ] Keep replies short and mobile-skimmable; bio + headshot + links at the end

## Data points & benchmarks
- one practitioner's callout test: **68% of responses were a waste of time**; only **4%** understood what was needed; **80%+** included unsolicited opinion; just **2 of 47** responses stood out
- Response relevance stats: 61.7% on-topic, 46.8% relevant experts, 36% included requested data
- 690 journalist callouts received in 3 weeks of testing (post-revival)
- An informal survey (101 votes) on alternatives: no single platform dominates — the top three each had ~18–22% preference, and pricing ranges from free to paid tiers. Try 2–3 (including niche B2B and regional ones) and keep the ones that produce placements.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Explaining basics to journalists who cover the beat (over-explaining = instant discard)
- AI-generated generic pitches — journalists and platforms detect and flag them
- Responding to callouts where you have no genuine credential ("it can often be a popularity contest" — practitioner advice)
- Missing deadlines — early responses win
- Sending unsolicited attachments, meeting requests, or 900-word essays

## Key principles
- "It's not about you. It's about helping a reporter out." (long-standing advice for journalist-request services)
- Helpful, Authentic, Relevant, On time — the HARO acronym is the formula.
- Brand mentions can matter even without links: they support brand and E-E-A-T signals and can influence how AI answers describe you — but earn them legitimately, never by fabricating expertise.
