# How to Use Podcasts for Link Building

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Guest on industry podcasts and collect the link from each episode page — very likely if you vet the show first (pay-to-appear slots are advertising, so any link from them should be tagged rel="sponsored"/nofollow). Each appearance costs ~60 minutes, builds homepage authority, and exposes your brand to a new audience. Find shows, vet that they link to guests, pitch the three Ws, record.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find industry podcasts** (millions of shows exist; every niche has dozens):
   - Google "best [industry] podcasts" — harvest listicles and the podcast carousel
   - Search podcast directory apps for your topic, "see all" shows
   - Reverse-engineer prolific guests: pull a frequent guest's homepage backlinks, filter referring page titles for "episode" — every hit is a podcast that links to guests
   - Log name + website in a spreadsheet as you go
2. **Vet the shows:**
   - Batch-check authority scores for all sites; sort high to low (links from higher-authority sites pass more equity; don't delete low scorers, just deprioritize)
   - Verify the show (a) links to guests and (b) is still running — remove if either fails
   - Exception: huge-audience shows that don't link may still be worth it purely for exposure
3. **Pitch:**
   - If the show has an application process, use it exactly — hosts ignore people who can't follow instructions
   - Otherwise email the host (use email-finding techniques)
   - Cover the three Ws: **Who** you are, **Why** you're a good guest, **What** you'll talk about
   - The "what" must appeal to their audience, be uncovered in their episode history, and match your expertise — ideally a topic you've already written about (your article becomes your episode notes)
   - Pitch in batches of 5-10/week; most hosts won't reply, so volume sustains the pipeline
4. **Record:** decent microphone, stable connection, quiet room. The host does the heavy lifting; episodes usually go live a few weeks later.

**Hosting your own podcast?** Episodes attract links when listeners reference them — one marketing show's episode pages each earned links. But hosting is a lot of work; treat links as a bonus, never the reason to start.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a podcast list from Google listicles + directory apps + guest backlink mining
- [ ] Batch-analyze authority; sort descending
- [ ] Confirm each show links to guests and is active
- [ ] Study target shows' episode histories for topic gaps
- [ ] Send 5-10 pitches weekly covering Who/Why/What
- [ ] Use application forms where they exist
- [ ] Prep from your own published content; show up with good audio setup

## Data points & benchmarks
- Millions of podcasts exist (older estimate: 2M+) — supply is not the constraint
- ~60 minutes per episode = one of the cheapest link + exposure plays available
- Pitch cadence: 5-10/week sustains a consistent flow of appearances
- Episode pages can earn their own backlinks over time when listeners cite them

## Mistakes to avoid
- Pitching shows that don't link to guests or stopped publishing
- Ignoring application processes and emailing hosts directly
- Pitching topics the show already covered or that don't fit its audience
- One-off pitching: without weekly volume, the pipeline dries up
- Starting your own podcast solely for links

## Key principles
- Podcast links are highly likely: many shows' formats include linking to guests (verify first)
- Vet for the link, prioritize by authority, but don't ignore audience value
- The best episode topic = your existing content (preparation is already done)
- Exposure compounds: mentioning your content on-air can trigger listener links
