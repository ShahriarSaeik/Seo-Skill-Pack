# 6 Simple Blog Post Templates

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Most posts aren't that different — six templates cover nearly everything: the list post, competitor comparison (+ your brand), step-by-step guide, contrarian thought leadership, expanded definition, and beginner's guide. Each has a fixed skeleton: title formula → intro job → subheading structure → conclusion move. Match the template to whether order matters and where the reader sits in the funnel.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **The list post** — for non-chronological lists (items reorderable). Numbered title ("XX Ways to [Outcome]," "XX [Topic] Tips," "XX Reasons Why [Problem]"); short intro (establish trust fast + linked table of contents — readers skim and jump); one benefit-focused H2 per item ("Eat spicy foods to boost calorie-burning" beats "Eat spicy foods"); conclude with one or two final tips
2. **The competitor comparison + your brand** — BOFU conversion play; also target "competitor vs competitor" keywords and insert your brand into peripheral conversations (disclose that the page is published by one of the products compared, and compare honestly). Title: "X vs Y: Which Is Better? [Year]." Structure: unbiased intro (plant your brand as a possible alternative) → category subheadings (features, pricing, UX, support, use cases) → comparison tables (readers scroll straight to these; optionally add your brand as a third column) → key differences (what's each one's USP? what are BOTH missing? — setup for your callback) → fair feature assessments ("Competitor 1 / Competitor 2 / Your take" + reviews, screenshots, embeds) → recommendations by user scenario, then a SHORT brand pitch (most word count stays with the two competitors)
3. **The step-by-step guide** — for ordered processes (can't draft before outlining). Title: "How to [Outcome] (XX Steps / Even If [Obstruction] / + Benefit)." Intro: PSP formula (Problem → Solution → Proof of your experience). Numbered H2 steps starting with present-tense verbs; conclude with a quick summary
4. **The contrarian thought leadership post** — challenges conventional wisdom; balance provocative with credible. Title: "Why [Common Practice] Is Totally Illogical (And What to Do Instead)." Structure: hook (acknowledge the common belief, state your contrarian view) → the set-up (describe prevailing wisdom; jargon OK here for shared experience) → the unequivocal problem (logic + data + examples) → carrot + proof (your counterintuitive solution, backed by data/cases/experts) → preempt objections directly → conclusion that pushes readers to test the idea (and take it to social)
5. **The expanded definition** — for concepts readers must understand first. Title: "What is [Concept]? Everything You Need to Know." Answer the question directly and define the concept in the first sentences (this also suits featured snippets and AI answers); answer follow-up questions as H2s (source: People Also Ask + question keyword reports); conclude with summary + further resources
6. **The beginner's guide** — comprehensive educational introduction vs. actionable steps. Chapter-style subheadings building from basics; suited to topics needing full context (e.g., canonical tags for beginners)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Classify every planned post into one of the six templates before outlining
- [ ] List post: reorderable items only; add jump-link TOC
- [ ] Comparison: fair assessments build trust; keep your pitch subtle and short
- [ ] How-to: PSP intro; present-tense numbered steps
- [ ] Contrarian: evidence-backed, objection-preempting — never inflammatory for engagement
- [ ] Definition: direct-answer first sentences; PAA-driven sections
- [ ] Save each template as a shared doc your whole team can reuse

## Data points & benchmarks
- Comparison keywords sit at the bottom of the funnel with strong conversion intent
- Definition posts: answer the query directly in the opening sentences, then expand

## Mistakes to avoid
- Using a list post for ordered steps (that's a how-to) or vice versa
- Biased comparisons — readers detect it and distrust everything
- Contrarian takes without evidence — provocation without credibility backfires
- Long intros on list posts (people skim and jump)

## Key principles
- Templates kill blank-page paralysis; originality lives in the content, not the structure
- The SERP already tells you which template wins for a keyword
- Structure by reader position: curiosity (contrarian) → learning (definition/guide) → buying (comparison)
