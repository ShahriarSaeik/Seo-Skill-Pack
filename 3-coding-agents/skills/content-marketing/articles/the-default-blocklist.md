# The Default Blocklist: Why Google Trusts Big Brands (But Not You)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One argument (the author's interpretation of observed SERP shifts, not a stated Google policy): AI slop made per-article trust evaluation impractical, so Google hedged — boosting a small set of big trusted brands and user-voted platforms and default-distrusting everyone else. The claim is that search has moved from "trust until lost" to "distrust until earned": smaller publishers start on a mental "blocklist" and must earn allowlist status by demonstrating credibility, transparency, and real people behind the content. Google says it aims to reward helpful content regardless of site size, so treat this as a useful model, not a rule.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The mechanism:
1. **The problem** — AI content is hard to detect and bad content looks like good content; evaluating billions of pages case-by-case is too taxing
2. **Google's hedge** — elevate big accountable brands (legacy media, health, consumer-tech publishers) into many more SERPs (+85M estimated organic pageviews in a year for a handful of brands, per third-party estimates); elevate UGC platforms with built-in voting quality control (+579M estimated pageviews for two forum-style sites in a year) — policing a few giants is easier than policing thousands of small publishers
3. **Side effects** — slow, selective indexing for new sites ("crawled, currently not indexed" purgatory); big-brand slop tolerated as the lesser evil; five core updates + three spam updates + heavy E-E-A-T emphasis in a single year
4. **The broader shift** — searchers do the same: default-blocklist everything, allowlist chosen communities and individuals. Pre-AI: brands trusted until they violated trust. Now: guilty of slop until proven otherwise

How to earn trust from a trust deficit:
1. **Be selective** — publish only where you have credibility; measure yourself by what you don't publish
2. **Align content with your business model** — affiliate/coupon spam subdirectories destroy trust
3. **Avoid being a "content site"** — content is credible when it supports a real, tangible product
4. **Make motivations crystal clear** — who you are, why and how you created it, how you benefit
5. **Add unique/proprietary value** — simple experiments, more effort than competitors, first-hand experience anchors
6. **Real authors** — credentials, photos, anecdotes, author bios; build personal brands over faceless company accounts
7. **Use Google's gatekeepers** — if forums are trusted, distribute ideas there genuinely (follow community rules and disclose your affiliation; undisclosed promotion backfires)
8. **Become a gatekeeper yourself** — curate tightly, vouch for everything you share

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Cut publishing to topics where you have genuine standing
- [ ] Add author pages with credentials and real photos to every article
- [ ] Disclose how each piece was made and what you gain from it
- [ ] Attach proprietary data or first-hand experiments to tentpole content
- [ ] Remove monetization-only content sections
- [ ] Share your best ideas on platforms Google already trusts, openly and in line with each community's rules
- [ ] Curate ruthlessly: vouch for only what you'd defend

## Data points & benchmarks
- A handful of big brands gained an estimated ~85M organic pageviews in a year (third-party estimate; causation not proven)
- Two major UGC/forum platforms gained an estimated ~579M pageviews in the same span
- One year saw 5 core updates + 3 dedicated spam updates + E-E-A-T emphasis

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming helpful content alone still earns trust — it's table stakes now
- Jack-of-all-trades publishing across topics you have no standing in
- Copying the legacy-brand playbook that built their moat — it doesn't transfer
- Hiding commercial motivation instead of disclosing it

## Key principles
- The blocklist is a mental model, not a literal list: everything new lives under suspicion
- Trust now flows through gatekeepers — brands, communities, individuals — become one
- Public accountability (real people, real stakes, willingness to suffer consequences) is the trust currency
