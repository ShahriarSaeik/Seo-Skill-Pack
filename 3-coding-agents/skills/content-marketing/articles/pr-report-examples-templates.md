# PR Report Examples & Templates (5 Report Types)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A PR report proves the impact of your work — and good ones answer one specific question, match their audience (exec, client, or internal team), and follow a simple narrative arc: summary → top wins → expectation vs. reality → next steps. Less is more: over-reporting kills retention of insights.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Five real-world report archetypes:
1. **Exec-friendly one-pager** — high-level results (traffic, sales generated) told as a narrative arc: the challenge, the idea, the results. Aligns PR to business objectives, not just links/coverage counts
2. **PR supplement** — a short coverage snapshot (links, authority) attached to a broader SEO/marketing report; for clients already up to speed
3. **Monthly deep-dive** — exec summary and next steps on page one, then campaign activity, organic visibility, media coverage, competitor analysis; reports across channels and the full funnel
4. **Campaign wrap-up** — logical flow: campaign overview → results → wider impact → next steps; designed visually to guide the eye; ideal for mid-level managers/clients
5. **Live coverage dashboard** — cross-channel metrics (coverage authority, social shares) for internal teams tweaking campaigns in real time; minimal context, minimal hand-holding

How to create a PR report (6 steps):
1. **Answer a single specific question** — treat it like a study with a hypothesis: did we drive traffic? awareness? sales?
2. **Keep the audience front of mind** — do they need this? what do they care about? how do they consume info?
3. **Choose the right KPIs** — granular for practitioners (e.g., dofollow link counts); top-level for execs (share of voice uplift on campaign terms, site-level SOV vs. competitors)
4. **Pick a fitting format** — spreadsheets for internal; designed reports (curated stats, headlines) for execs; white-labeled with context for clients; live dashboards for internal iteration
5. **Set the right cadence** — live/ongoing for teams; monthly, quarterly, or sales-cycle-length for senior execs
6. **Follow a simple structure** — exec summary (goal + overview), top wins, expectation vs. reality charts, recommendations/next steps

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Start each report by writing the one question it answers
- [ ] Put next steps on page one, not in an appendix
- [ ] Curate 2–3 charts that tell the story; skip the data dump
- [ ] Split goals into primary (link volume, quality, relevance, authority, trust) and secondary (rankings, traffic, conversions, revenue)
- [ ] Ask clients directly how they want reporting structured
- [ ] Build a reusable template library across clients
- [ ] Match cadence to audience: live → internal; monthly/quarterly → execs

## Data points & benchmarks
- Quarterly template structure: Primary Owned Goals (volume/quality/relevance of links, authority, trust) + Secondary Shared Goals (keywords/rankings, sessions, conversions, revenue growth)
- Link earned → keyword/ranking change → traffic → conversion is the causal chain reports should trace

## Mistakes to avoid
- Over-reporting — volume of slides ≠ value
- Reporting links/coverage only, detached from business objectives
- One-format-fits-all audiences
- Leaving recommendations out — reports should end in action

## Key principles
- A PR report is a retention and budget tool: good ones bring repeat business, bigger budgets, more creative control
- PR value extends past coverage: rankings, traffic, sales, and currency (£/$) driven
- If unsure how to report for a client, ask
