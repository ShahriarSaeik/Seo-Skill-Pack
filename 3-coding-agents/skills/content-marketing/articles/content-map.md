# How to Create a Content Map (Two Ways + a Hybrid)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A content map aligns content with the buyer's journey for each persona (traditional way) or with a business-potential score that measures how essential your product is to solving the topic's problem (an alternative approach). Mapping gives you a strategic inventory: you spot funnel gaps and repurposing opportunities instead of publishing blindly.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Traditional content map (3 steps):**
1. **Create buyer personas** — via customer interviews (scientific) or competitor/industry data enrichment (educated guess). One persona per customer type; group similar personas into categories if there are too many.
2. **Map existing content to journey stages** — the four stages: (a) increase brand awareness (problem-aware, product-optional), (b) create interest and desire (show how the product solves challenges), (c) nurture interest and entice purchase (why it's the best solution), (d) retain customers and build loyalty (get more from the product + topics customers care about). Rules: content fitting multiple stages goes to its dominant use case; content fitting nowhere gets updated or triggers new stage definitions.
3. **Fill gaps with new ideas** — four methods: keyword research (seed keywords + modifiers like "for/how/guide/ideas/tips", volume and difficulty filters), competitor analysis (content-gap keywords, competitors' top pages by traffic, links, or social shares), niche study (communities, magazines, blogs — hidden gems like "next big thing with no comprehensive guide"; one top-performing article was inspired by a print magazine), and audience surveys ("What would you like to learn? Biggest challenge? Biggest priority? How did you find us?").

**The alternative — business-potential mapping:**
- Premise: one article can serve the whole funnel — a how-to guide can take a stranger from problem to purchase in one read; buyer-journey models are simplifications ("the map is not the territory").
- Method: score each topic 0–3 for how crucial your product is to solving the problem:
  - **0** — no practical product use case (e.g., creative strategies behind famous startups).
  - **1** — product helps with only part of the topic.
  - **2** — product is one of multiple viable solutions.
  - **3** — solving the problem practically requires the product.
- Map = topic + BP score (+ traffic potential if search matters). One large SEO blog credits this product-led approach for much of its organic traffic (a source-time figure of 273K+ monthly search visits; traffic numbers change).
- Personas still matter — for calibrating style and depth (jargon or not), not for bucketing topics.

**Hybrid:** insert BP scores into journey stages — require BP 2–3 for purchase-stage content; allow BP 0–1 in interest/desire/retention stages.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build personas via at least a handful of real customer conversations
- [ ] Inventory existing content into the 4 journey stages per persona
- [ ] Score every mapped topic 0–3 for business potential
- [ ] Run all four gap-filling methods; log ideas into the map
- [ ] Decide a minimum BP threshold per stage (hybrid model)
- [ ] Revisit the map quarterly — it doubles as a content audit view

## Data points & benchmarks
- Product-led (BP-mapped) content: one large SEO blog reported 273K+ monthly organic visits at the time of the source (a dated, single-site figure).
- Example mapping rows: BP0/300 TP, BP1/1,200 TP, BP2/800 TP, BP3/900 TP — high BP isn't always high traffic; the map makes the trade-off explicit.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Forcing every piece into exactly one funnel stage (real journeys aren't linear).
- Over-granular personas that multiply work without customizing value.
- Zero-BP content everywhere — or BP-3 content only — both starve some part of the funnel.
- Constant heavy product placement in owned channels → reader fatigue.

## Key principles
- A content map is a strategic inventory, not just a calendar: it exposes gaps and repurposing stock.
- Business potential predicts content's commercial power better than funnel position.
- One strong how-to can compress the entire funnel into a single session.
- Spotting an underserved topic beat early (via niche study) outperforms pure keyword data.
