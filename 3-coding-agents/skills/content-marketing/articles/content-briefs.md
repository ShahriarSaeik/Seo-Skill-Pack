# How to Create Content Briefs (with 6 Templates)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A content brief tells a writer what to write and why. It should be simple and brief — covering title, goal, audience, subtopics, product fit, uniqueness, and practical details. Most quality problems and endless rewrites boil down to miscommunication, which briefs eliminate at the source.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Write a working title** — Simple, not clickbait; communicates the core idea. For SEO content, it must reflect the primary intent of the target keyword (check the SERP; if all top results share a format like "how to", follow suit).
2. **Explain the goal** — Why this article exists (e.g., "Explain how to create an effective content brief", "Teach existing users how to use a new product feature"). Writers who know the why hit the goal more often.
3. **Describe who you're writing for** — Just experience level (beginner vs. expert) and role (engineer vs. solopreneur, junior marketer vs. CMO). No complex persona documents needed. SERPs hint at audience ("for beginners" in top titles → write for beginners).
4. **Share sub-topics to include** — Use common sense + data: a keyword research tool's related-keyword data shows keywords top articles commonly rank for (e.g., if top "content brief" articles also rank for "templates" and "examples", include those sections).
5. **Explain how your product fits in** — Suggest natural product mentions only; never shoehorn. If the writer isn't a product expert, explain the fit and name helpful features.
6. **Suggest ways to make the article unique** — Experts to interview, quotes, data/stats to reference, personal experiences/stories competitors don't have.
7. **Share practical details** — Deadline, style guide, editors to contact, internal links to include.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Create a 7-part brief for every article: title, goal, audience, subtopics, product fit, uniqueness, practicalities
- [ ] Verify keyword intent by reviewing the SERP before finalizing the working title
- [ ] Mine related keywords for required subtopics
- [ ] List 2-3 unique elements (data, quotes, stories) per brief
- [ ] Keep briefs brief — if you're writing full headers and examples, you're writing the article

## Data points & benchmarks
- Up to 7 core sections per brief — enough for A-grade drafts without drowning writers in SEO jargon
- Besides the base 7-part template, five expert variants exist for special cases (six templates in total): technical-audience, product-focused, ABCD (audience/brand/context/details), marketing-strategy-linked, and audience/SEO/brand/conversion

## Mistakes to avoid
- Writing briefs so detailed you may as well have written the article (they are NOT content outlines)
- Drowning writers in SEO jargon or removing their stylistic freedom
- Shoehorning products into articles where they don't belong
- Skipping the goal/audience — the root cause of inconsistent quality and endless rewrites

## Key principles
- "Briefs should also be brief."
- Share the goal — writers who understand why write better-fitting content.
- Content marketing exists to grow a business; every brief should clarify the product connection.
- Uniqueness suggestions are what lift an article above the identical SERP competition.
