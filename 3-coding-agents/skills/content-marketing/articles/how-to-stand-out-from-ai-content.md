# How To Stand Out in an Ocean of AI Content

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Most content is arbitrage — moving existing information between websites — and AI is the ultimate arbitrage machine. Humans can't win that game, so stop playing it. Three things AI can't replicate: experimentation (new data), experience (dirty hands), and effort (going further than anyone will bother). Let AI handle the low-end of content; spend human energy on the high-end.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Experimentation: create proprietary data**
   - LLMs know everything common — they cannot know information that doesn't exist yet. Experiments create it
   - Forms: industry surveys; analysis of your own product/company data (e.g., a benchmark from 150M pageviews of analytics data); technical tests (blocking pages to observe effects); collecting receipts for widely-believed-but-unproven ideas
   - Use real data only — never fabricate or cherry-pick numbers; disclose methodology
   - Doubles as one of the best link-building tactics (people cite original data)
2. **Experience: dirty your hands**
   - AI content is trapped in theory; the same advice from a proven practitioner wins
   - With 500 sites offering the answer, readers choose the source — the advisor who's done it, the reviewer who bought and tested the cameras (original photos, documented methodology) over scraped product descriptions
   - How: write only about things you've done; interview people who've done them; show concrete evidence (screenshots, filmed interviews, photos); share anecdotes and context; take on public challenges with skin in the game
   - Inverse rule: avoid topics where you lack experience and can't justify acquiring it
3. **Effort: go further than others**
   - Most AI scaling is cost-motivated (sacrifice quality for speed/headcount) — so visible effort is a differentiator
   - Forms: webcomic guides, documentary series, illustrated books, free tools, unique on-page experiences (e.g., a themed interactive guide with custom illustrations)
   - Difficulty is the moat: if it's hard to make, it can't be pumped out by anyone with an AI tool

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Add one original-data element to every tentpole piece (survey, test, product-data analysis)
- [ ] Attach first-person proof to experience claims: screenshots, photos, methodology notes
- [ ] Replace "topics we can write" with "topics we've done" as the editorial filter
- [ ] Interview practitioners where the team lacks direct experience
- [ ] Budget for one high-effort, memorable content project per year (tool, book, series, experience)
- [ ] Delegate definitional/summary/listicle content to AI workflows; reserve humans for the high-end

## Data points & benchmarks
- Marginal cost of content ≈ zero; outsized results from being first with a basic how-to are numbered
- Original-data reports attract links indefinitely (industry reports keep earning links passively)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Competing on well-written, fairly accurate common information — AI does that infinitely cheaper
- Publishing experience-less content under faceless "Content Team" bylines
- Judging effort-based projects purely on short-term ROI

## Key principles
- Bifurcate content: AI for the low-end (definitions, summaries, listicles), humans for the high-end
- Prove the provenance of your advice
- Create information, don't just share it
