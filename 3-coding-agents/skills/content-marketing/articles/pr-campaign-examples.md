# 9 Successful PR Campaign Examples, According to the Data

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Post-mortems of nine data-backed PR campaigns (newsjacking, surveys, exclusive reports, rankings, brand collabs) with the links, mentions, and search growth each earned (figures as reported at the time of each campaign; percentage growth from tiny bases can look dramatic). Winning patterns: time launches to conversation peaks, exploit unique data, rank/compare audiences or locations, and track results holistically (links + mentions + search volume + social).

## Framework / Step-by-step
The nine campaigns and what each teaches:
1. **Heinz × Mattel "Barbiecue"** (62 links) — newsjack a cultural moment; tease a mockup months early to gauge demand; coordinate launch with peak discussion. Result: 118 top-tier mentions, 38M organic social impressions, zero paid spend, +200% YoY search growth
2. **Lush "Saltburn" bath bomb** (142 links) — parody a trending film scene 3 months post-release; meme-able product photography amplified social reach; landing page still pulls 500–600 monthly visits after archival. Lesson: scan for affiliate-link coverage that hides from backlink reports by tracking campaign keywords
3. **Pinterest "Eclectic Grandpa" trend report** (98 links) — mine internal search data; coin novel trend names (memorable, trackable); ~150 mentions on high-authority sites were unlinked — claimable. Lesson: track mentions, not just links
4. **A property-portal rate-locked homeowners survey** (235 links) — survey 1,815 homeowners on an industry-pain question; extract multiple hard-hitting stats so outlets cite varied angles
5. **LinkedIn Future of Work report** (383 links) — open proprietary platform data (AI conversation growth) as exclusive research
6. **WalletHub most/least educated cities** (604 links) — interactive study ranking 150 metros across 11 metrics; superlatives + rankings + location slices win national AND local syndication
7. **Krispy Kreme Olympic doughnuts** (95 links; 45K monthly searches) — recurring-event newsjacking; track domain-level mentions with keyword filters because product campaigns rarely have one landing page
8. **McDonald's × Crocs collab** (516 links) — the "side" brand can win more links (416 vs 260 in this case); social posts themselves attract links (one UGC post earned 24); set backlink alerts on every asset
9. **Duolingo Super Bowl "No buts"** (130 links) — upcycle owned content that already proved itself (a semi-viral widget); coordinate channels (ad + push notification simultaneously); 5M YouTube views, 24M TikTok plays

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Time launches to conversation peaks (monitor discussion volume before dropping news)
- [ ] Run teaser PR to validate demand before committing to product/R&D
- [ ] Mine internal data (site search, platform queries) for exclusive trend reports
- [ ] Name your trends/concepts so uptake is trackable
- [ ] Design surveys around one industry pain; pull several quotable stats
- [ ] Rank locations or audience tribes; slice data for local + national appeal
- [ ] Track: links, unlinked mentions, campaign keyword search volume, domain-level co-citations, social posts' backlinks
- [ ] Repurpose proven owned content into bigger campaign moments

## Data points & benchmarks
- Link counts by campaign type: brand collab stunt 62–516; trend report 98; survey 235; exclusive report 383; interactive ranking study 604
- Search growth achievable via newsjacking: +200% (barbie ketchup), +37K% (Saltburn bath bomb), +4.4M% (Olympic doughnuts), +838% (brand collab)
- One collab drove 37K average monthly brand-related searches
- ~150 high-authority unlinked mentions from a single report — a claimable link source

## Mistakes to avoid
- Tracking only the campaign landing page — fragment across releases, social posts, and affiliate links hides most impact
- Ignoring unlinked mentions (free links left on the table)
- Assuming the lead brand in a partnership earns the most links
- Launching after the conversation peak (or before demand exists)

## Key principles
- Best PR campaigns create conversations, not just links: measure awareness via search volume and mentions too
- Unique data is the common denominator — internal, surveyed, or recombined third-party
- Localization and ranking multiply coverage outlets
- Upcycle winners: what worked once owned (widget, post, format) can anchor a bigger campaign
