# Copywriting vs. Content Writing

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Content writing informs and entertains (blogs, newsletters, social); copywriting persuades and sells (ads, landing pages, product descriptions). The lines blur in practice — the most valuable (and best-paid) writers combine both plus SEO skills.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**How they differ:**
1. **Purpose** — content writing: inform/entertain, usually to rank in organic search. Copywriting: drive immediate action (buy, sign up).
2. **Length** — content writers: long-form (1,000+ words — articles, ebooks, newsletters). Copywriters: short and snappy (a few hundred words — ads, product descriptions). Generalities, not rules: copywriters write long landing pages; content writers write short social posts.
3. **Deliverables** — content: blog articles, social posts, email newsletters. Copy: PPC ads, landing pages, product descriptions. Hybrid discipline: SEO copywriting — content that ranks AND converts.

**What makes both great (3 shared pillars):**
1. **Enticing** — know the audience, be concise, conversational tone, edit ruthlessly, master transitions and emotional wording.
2. **Solves a need** — not the superficial problem (nobody buys a T-shirt to stay warm; they buy identity, belonging, confidence). Uncover and address the underlying problem. One clothing brand sells socks by naming the exact problems they solve.
3. **Search-optimized** — keyword research to pick a target keyword, read the SERP to decode intent, implement on-page basics (e.g., answer People-Also-Ask questions in the article).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Classify every writing task as content (trust/traffic) or copy (conversion) before briefing
- [ ] For copy tasks, write down the underlying emotional need, not the functional one
- [ ] For content tasks, document target keyword + SERP intent before drafting
- [ ] Blend both: add conversion elements (CTAs, product tie-ins) to top-of-funnel content
- [ ] When hiring: copywriter for product pages/ads/landing pages; content writer for blogs/ebooks/newsletters; ideally a writer with SEO + copy skills
- [ ] Answer People-Also-Ask questions inside content for on-page lift
- [ ] Price/charge by proximity to revenue, not by word count

## Data points & benchmarks
- Average US salaries: content writer ~$56K/yr vs. copywriter ~$58.5K/yr (older job-market data; check current figures) — copy pays slightly more because its impact on revenue is immediate and measurable (e.g., a 2% conversion lift = directly attributable dollars).
- Writers who combine both skills plus SEO out-earn either specialty.
- One experienced writer's first freelance gig paid $5 for a ~3,000-word article — skill premiums take years to unlock.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Hiring on title alone — skill and industry expertise matter more than the "copywriter" vs. "content writer" label.
- Content that informs but never sells; copy that sells but never ranks.
- Addressing surface-level problems instead of underlying motivations.

## Key principles
- Both crafts share one job: words that inform, entertain, and ultimately make people find and want to buy from your brand.
- SEO + copywriting is the premium combination.
- Measurability drives pay: the closer your writing sits to revenue, the more you can charge.
- Titles are shorthand, not skill guarantees: a skilled writer who knows the industry beats either label.
