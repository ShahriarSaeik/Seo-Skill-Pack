# Content Writing: The Complete Process + 8 Tips

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Content writing = researching, planning, writing, and editing web content for marketing purposes. Follow a 5-step process (strategy → topic planning → outline → draft → feedback/edit), then layer on 8 craft tips: templates for similar topics, PAS intros, plain language, examples, stats, demonstrated expertise, FAQs, and expert quotes.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**The process (5 steps):**
1. **Define your content strategy first** — answer 4 questions before writing anything: Why create content? Who is it for? Where will it be published? What format? (e.g., "influence buying decisions by showing how our product solves problems, for people who want more search traffic, on the blog, as posts")
2. **Plan topics** — every niche has thousands of possible topics; use keyword research as a proxy for what people want to know even if you're not chasing rankings; weigh volume against how naturally the topic lets you pitch your product
3. **Outline with the "What–How–Wow" formula:**
   - **What** — subtopics. Don't guess: check what top-performing pages on the topic all cover (definitions, getting-started steps, etc.); a content-gap analysis on top-ranking pages reveals common subtopics
   - **How** — organization. Use the inverted pyramid: "need to know" before "nice to know"; plan narrative and transitions in bullets
   - **Wow** — your USP. NOT length ("nobody wants 10,000 words for something sayable in 500"). Instead: draw on experience/expertise, and make it entertaining
4. **Write the draft** — outline open beside a fresh doc; match tone to format (video scripts more conversational than posts)
5. **Get feedback and edit** — never just self-edit; you're too close to the work. Ask a colleague to flag logical loopholes, choppy flow, unclear points

**The 8 craft tips:**
1. **Reuse one template for similar topics** — e.g., glossary posts all follow "What is X? / Why does X matter? / Best practices for X" — one outline can yield many pages (find template-worthy clusters via keywords sharing a modifier like "best" or "how to"); each page still needs its own unique, useful content — near-duplicate templated pages risk scaled content abuse
2. **PAS intro formula** — Problem → Agitate → Solution; hooks impatient readers immediately
3. **Cut fluff and jargon** — about half of US adults read below a sixth-grade level (older survey); "Cooking steak can be hard" beats "can be extremely challenging"; use a readability checker, but don't obsess over scores
4. **Use examples liberally** — ~520 "for example" mentions across one large SEO blog; examples make abstract points click
5. **Back claims with stats** — "90.63% of content gets no traffic from Google" (older study) is more credible than "most content gets no traffic"; stats also earn backlinks (one stat earned a guide 23 backlinks)
6. **Show expertise/experience** — "why you should trust me" author blurbs, or explain how you researched/tested; subtle or explicit both work
7. **Answer FAQs** — end-of-post FAQ sections catch questions that don't fit the main body; mine question-form keywords for them
8. **Quote industry experts** — if your industry is divided on something, be transparent: ask 2–3 experts for opinions and include them; find experts via author searches on your topic

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write down the 4 strategy answers (why/who/where/what format) before any drafting
- [ ] Build outlines with What–How–Wow; verify subtopics against top-ranking pages
- [ ] Differentiate with experience and entertainment, never word count
- [ ] Draft in a fresh document with the outline visible alongside
- [ ] Get at least one non-author review before publishing
- [ ] Identify template-able keyword clusters (shared modifiers) and standardize
- [ ] Run PAS on every intro
- [ ] Add one concrete example or screenshot per major claim
- [ ] Add a stat or source for every bold claim
- [ ] Close posts with an FAQ section from question queries
- [ ] Solicit expert quotes where the industry disagrees

## Data points & benchmarks
- About half of US adults read below a sixth-grade reading level (older survey)
- 90.63% of content gets no organic traffic (older study; stat framing > vague framing)
- ~520 "for example" mentions across one large SEO blog (one outline template produced 200+ glossary pages, each with unique content)
- One quoted statistic earned a single guide 23 backlinks
- 319,019 keyword variations from a single broad seed ("smoked") — topic universes are huge; prioritization is mandatory

## Mistakes to avoid
- Writing without a strategy ("pointless drivel with no real purpose")
- Choosing subtopics randomly instead of from competing top performers
- Competing on length — nobody wants 10,000 words for a 500-word idea
- Skipping external feedback and publishing self-edited drafts
- Shoehorning stats in for the sake of it (an annoying trend)
- Using big words and jargon that exclude average readers

## Key principles
- Good content writing is a process, not an act of inspiration.
- The outline does half the work: What (subtopics) + How (order) + Wow (USP).
- Credibility compounds: stats, sources, expertise, and expert quotes.
- Templates are a superpower for scale — one structure, hundreds of pages.
- "Need to know" before "nice to know" (inverted pyramid).
