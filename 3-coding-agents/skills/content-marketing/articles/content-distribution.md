# Content Distribution: How to Do It Right

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Publish-and-pray doesn't work — your audience lives on other platforms, not your blog. Distribute via owned, earned, and paid channels using two strategies: PULL (drive people from platforms to your content) and PUSH (repurpose content natively where they already are). Build the strategy from audience research, not guesswork.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Channel types:**
- **Owned** — blog, social accounts, email list: full control, no permission needed
- **Earned** — others' podcasts, Reddit, forums, groups, blog comments: coverage must be earned
- **Paid** — search ads, social ads, native ad networks, sponsorships
- Organic search = owned + earned: publish on your owned channel, earn distribution via SEO

**Two distribution strategies:**
- **Pull** — use other channels to drive traffic to content on your owned channel (tweet pitching a blog post, search ads to a download)
- **Push** — repurpose/republish natively on the platforms where people already hang out (a blog post condensed into a social thread; a short version posted natively on Reddit; content posted into groups — follow each community's rules and disclose your affiliation)

**Building the strategy (4 steps):**
1. **Research the audience** — where do they hang out? who do they trust? what formats do they like? Three methods:
   - Gut + team consensus (yours alone risks bias)
   - Ask them directly (a quick multiple-choice survey to subscribers — few marketers do)
   - Research popular niche blogs: find high-traffic niche sites via a content research tool, then reverse-engineer where their founders actively publish (one grilling blogger ignores a near-dead social account but is highly active on visual platforms, YouTube ~170K subs, runs a paid private community + free newsletter — that's where that audience lives)
2. **Pull** — share links to your content on every owned channel that fits (socials, newsletter); make organic search the backbone (passive, consistent traffic — one guide pulled ~16K–60K monthly visits for years)
3. **Push** — republish condensed/native versions: social threads, Reddit posts, group posts, guest posts on larger industry blogs (pitch sites that already published adjacent topics; paid placements need rel="sponsored")
4. **Paid** — promote links or native content; search ads target in-demand keywords (spy competitors' paid keywords for ideas); consider sponsoring industry podcasts/newsletters (clearly labeled as sponsored; one brand moved its ENTIRE ad budget from big ad platforms to podcast/newsletter sponsorships); native ad networks place your content on major media sites (one coffee brand converted 10K customers in 6 months)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List every owned channel (blog, email, socials) — you control these today
- [ ] Survey your audience: where they hang out, who they trust, preferred formats
- [ ] Pick 4–6 niche blogs; trace their active channels — that's your channel shortlist
- [ ] For each new post: schedule pull shares (link) on all owned channels
- [ ] Create one push version per post (thread, Reddit summary, group post)
- [ ] Pitch 1 guest post to a site that published adjacent content
- [ ] Test one paid channel; consider newsletter/podcast sponsorships over big ad platforms
- [ ] Measure per channel (analytics per network) and cut what underperforms

## Data points & benchmarks
- One brand distributes each post to a 200K+ subscriber newsletter, ~141K LinkedIn followers, ~136K X (Twitter) followers, plus a social thread (audience sizes are a snapshot)
- A single evergreen guide sustained ~16K–60K monthly organic visits for ~3 years — search compounds; social doesn't
- One brand reallocated its whole paid budget to industry podcast/newsletter sponsorships
- Native ads case: 10K new customers in 6 months for one coffee brand
- Push examples take little time: threads and Reddit posts are condensed versions of existing content

## Mistakes to avoid
- Publishing and praying ("if it's good, they'll find it" — no)
- Assuming your audience is everywhere you have an account — match channels to THEM
- Relying on one person's gut (biases → dumb conclusions); use team consensus + surveys
- Only ever pulling — many people never click through; push native versions too
- Distributing on platforms your audience doesn't use (a B2B SEO audience isn't on TikTok)
- Ignoring organic search as a distribution channel — it's the passive, compounding one

## Key principles
- Don't wait for people to come to you — go to where they already are.
- Pull for the click; push for native reach. You need both.
- Audience research (survey + niche-blog tracing) beats channel guesswork.
- Organic search is one of the few distribution channels that compounds passively.
