# How to Create a Content Plan in 6 Easy Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Content planning = deciding what to publish and when. Since you can't write everything (one large SEO publication has ~15,000 posts), the plan finds and prioritizes topics by traffic potential, search intent, business potential, and keyword difficulty — then schedules and assigns them. Strategy must exist first.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Have a clear content strategy** — Why/who/where/what in a few simple answers (no SMART-goal jargon). Plan without strategy = cooking without a recipe.
2. **Find topic ideas** — Use Google-based keyword research even for TikTok/YouTube (search behavior is broadly similar across platforms, and Google data is a reasonable proxy). Workflow: popular industry blog → any SEO tool's top-pages data (see traffic + top keyword per post) → spot trends → use them as "seed" keywords in a keyword research tool's keyword ideas.
3. **Identify the best topics** — Four filters:
   - Traffic Potential (TP): top-ranking page's estimated monthly traffic — more reliable than search volume
   - Search intent: "smoked brisket" = recipes (blog-friendly); "wagyu brisket" = e-commerce pages (not blog-friendly)
   - Business potential (0-3): ease of pitching your product. RELATIVE to your business: "best meats to smoke" = high for a meat delivery service, low for a BBQ seller
   - Keyword Difficulty (KD): higher = more backlinks needed ("smoked turkey" KD 45 vs "smoked chicken legs" KD 9)
4. **Prioritize topics** — Color-coded spreadsheet: topic + TP + KD + BP, greener = better; eyeball for the greenest rows. Weight columns by goal: brand awareness → prioritize TP; retention → prioritize BP (e.g., "low competition keywords": low TP, high BP for an SEO software company). Cluster similar topics and tackle together.
5. **Schedule on your content calendar** — Pull from spreadsheet into PM tool or Google Calendar.
6. **Assign topics to writers** — Assign by nearest due date (don't assign months ahead — overwhelming). Give writers instructions for handing off downstream tasks (editing, images, uploading) via a simple Google doc; the plan falls apart without it.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write the 4-question strategy (why/who/where/what) before planning
- [ ] Mine competitors' top pages for proven topics, then expand via seed keywords
- [ ] Score every topic: TP, intent match, BP (0-3), KD
- [ ] Build a color-coded prioritization sheet; weight columns by current goal
- [ ] Move greenest rows to the calendar; assign by nearest deadline
- [ ] Document the handoff workflow (edit → images → upload) for writers

## Data points & benchmarks
- One large SEO publication: ~15,000 published pages — nobody can cover everything; prioritize
- Example KD spread (snapshot): "smoked turkey" = 45 (hard) vs. "smoked chicken legs" = 9 (easy)
- One BBQ blog's brisket post: 60,000+ estimated monthly visits, mostly from one keyword

## Mistakes to avoid
- Planning before strategy exists
- Using platform popularity (views may come from ads) instead of keyword data
- Ignoring search intent (targeting e-commerce keywords with blog posts)
- Assigning topics months in advance
- Treating BP as absolute — it's relative to YOUR business model

## Key principles
- "You need to pick your battles" — prioritization is the heart of planning.
- TP > search volume as the traffic signal.
- The same topic sheet serves every goal — just re-weight the columns.
- Writers can't just write; they must trigger the downstream workflow.
