# How to Create an SEO Content Strategy (A Proven Framework)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A complete SEO content strategy in one sentence: "Create and maintain high-quality, search-focused content about topics with business potential, search traffic potential, and ranking potential." Every word matters — remove any and it falls apart.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find topics with search traffic potential** — use a keyword research tool: enter broad niche keywords, pull phrase-match keyword ideas, and filter by traffic potential (TP). TP = estimated monthly organic traffic to the top-ranking page (more reliable than search volume since pages rank for many keywords). Shortcut: plug a competitor into a backlink or traffic analysis tool and open its top-pages report to see their biggest traffic pages.
2. **Check business value** — Score how easy it is to pitch your product while covering the keyword (3 = easy pitch, 1 = hard). "Best coffee machines" = high for a coffee store; "why does coffee make me sleepy" = low. Prioritize high scores.
3. **Analyze ranking potential** — Defined as: viable to rank top 3 with available resources. Assess four things:
   - **Backlinks**: some tools' keyword difficulty scores estimate roughly how many linking websites you need for the top 10 (e.g., ~77 for "how to make kimchi"; tool-specific estimates). Check the referring domains of the top-ranking pages; review link quality, not just quantity.
   - **Authority**: If top-ranking pages' domain authority is much higher than yours, prioritize other keywords.
   - **Search intent**: Analyze SERPs for the 3 Cs — content type (post vs. product page), content format (listicle, how-to, tool), content angle. If you can't fulfill intent (e.g., SERP requires a free tool you can't build), you can't compete.
   - **Quality**: If the #1 result tested 47 air purifiers, beating them requires matching that effort or doing something unique.
4. **Create high-quality, search-focused content** — Search-focused = matches search intent. High-quality = R-E-A-C-H: Real (authentic/original), Experienced (topical expertise), Accurate (trustworthy sources), Clear (no fluff, illustrations), Helpful (actually solves the problem).
5. **Maintain high-quality, search-focused content** — Update or rewrite regularly because: posts don't always rank on first try, rankings decline, search intent changes, info gets stale, competitors one-up you.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a keyword list filtered by traffic potential, not just search volume
- [ ] Score every topic 1-3 for business potential before writing
- [ ] Check keyword difficulty, referring domains, domain authority of top pages, and the 3 Cs of intent for each target keyword
- [ ] Apply R-E-A-C-H quality criteria to every draft
- [ ] Run a quarterly content review: each writer flags posts to update or rewrite, then discusses with the content lead
- [ ] Republish/refresh decaying posts (watch for traffic spikes after refresh)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Difficulty example: "how to make kimchi" needed backlinks from ~77 websites for the top 10 (one tool's estimate)
- Refreshing one "free tools" post produced a visible traffic spike
- One team runs maintenance quarterly — every writer reviews their own content each quarter

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing traffic without business potential — "there's no point in generating a ton of organic traffic if people don't buy"
- Ignoring ranking difficulty (backlinks, authority, intent, quality) before committing to a keyword
- Publish-and-forget: assuming content keeps rankings without maintenance
- Judging backlink competition by count alone instead of quality

## Key principles
- Prioritize keywords by three potentials: business, traffic, ranking.
- Traffic potential > search volume for topic selection.
- "Ranking potential" = top 3 viable with available resources, not just top 10.
- Simplicity of the framework is deliberate — easy to follow consistently, which explains its success.
