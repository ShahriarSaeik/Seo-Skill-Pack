# How to Write a Blog Post (That People Actually Want to Read) in 9 Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Writing a blog post people read = proven topic + novel angle + outline + fast messy draft + ruthless editing + great headline + basic on-page SEO + active promotion. The magic is in the editing, not the writing.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find a proven topic** — use a keyword research tool to pull phrase-match keyword ideas, then filter for questions. Pick 5-10 topics with high traffic potential (the estimated total organic traffic the #1 page gets, not just one keyword's volume).
2. **Decide on the angle** — With millions of posts daily, novelty wins. Five novelty categories (Julian Shapiro): counter-intuitive, counter-narrative, shock & awe, elegant articulation, make someone feel seen. Angle sources: personal experience, expert interviews, crowdsourced opinions, data/science, informed contrarianism. For SEO: match the 3 Cs of search intent (content type, format, angle) — guideline, not rule.
3. **Create an outline** — Use templates (one content team uses a single listicle template for almost all list posts). Fill H2s/H3s from (a) your own experience/expertise, (b) a content-gap analysis of top-ranking competitors to find subtopics most top pages cover. Use top pages as inspiration, not copy targets — correct their misconceptions where possible.
4. **Write the first draft** — "Get it out": no self-censoring, no rearranging, no rewriting sentences yet. Use Pomodoro (25 min on / 5 min off) if stuck. Use PAS formula for intros: Problem → Agitate → Solution.
5. **Polish and edit** — Wait 1-2 days after drafting for fresh eyes. Use a grammar checker, read aloud, break long sentences, add formatting (images, bullets, bold), add transition words/cliffhangers. Get an impartial third party's feedback; iterate until happy. Don't adopt the editor's voice.
6. **Create an amazing headline** — Draft several (one viral publisher wrote 25 per article). Use power words; add parentheses. Five times as many people read the headline as the body copy (Ogilvy).
7. **Sprinkle on on-page SEO** — Topic in title, compelling meta description, short descriptive URL slug, alt text on images, internal + external links.
8. **Publish** — Use a doc-to-CMS export tool to avoid formatting errors.
9. **Promote** — Share with your audience (friends/family/social when starting), email people you mentioned, share in relevant communities (no spam).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Build a 5-10 topic list from keyword questions with high traffic potential
- [ ] Choose a novelty category before writing
- [ ] Outline with a reusable template + content gap analysis
- [ ] Draft fast without editing; use PAS intro if stuck
- [ ] Self-edit after 1-2 days, then get external feedback
- [ ] Write 5+ headline options before picking
- [ ] Apply basic on-page SEO (title, meta, slug, alt text, links)
- [ ] Execute at least 3 promotion tactics per post

## Data points & benchmarks
- Millions of new blog posts are published daily (one older estimate: 4.4M+) — novelty is mandatory to stand out
- "On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." — David Ogilvy
- One viral publisher created 25 headlines per article before publishing
- Pomodoro: 25-minute writing sprints, 5-minute breaks

## Mistakes to avoid
- Writing from scratch without an outline (causes writer's block)
- Editing while drafting (kills momentum)
- Publishing without promotion — good content is not discovered by itself
- Copying/rephrasing top-ranking pages (cookie-cutter content)
- Skipping the editing phase or trusting your own fresh-draft judgment

## Key principles
- "The magic is not in the writing. It's in the editing phase where the true blog post appears."
- Shannon Hale: "I'm simply shoveling sand into a box so that later I can build castles."
- Outlines mean you're filling gaps, not writing from scratch.
- Search intent matching is a guideline — a better, more novel article can still win.
