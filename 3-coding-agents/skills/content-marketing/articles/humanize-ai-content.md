# So You've Been Asked To "Humanize" AI Content

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
"Humanizing" 100% AI-generated long-form content — having writers retrofit humanity and pass AI-detector checks — is flawed, inefficient, and demoralizing. AI belongs at the start (ideation, outlines, drafting with humans) or on low-touch formats (glossaries, FAQs), not as a ghostwriter you later disguise. If stuck with the task, train custom models on your style to lighten the load, and push back with data.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Why the process is broken:
1. **It defeats the value proposition** — AI's point is speed/scale; humans rewriting AI prose costs as much as writing from scratch once the "rich human element" is researched and added
2. **Detector checks are nonsensical** — an AI validating humanity: writers report editing for hours only to be flagged as AI by the tool; one consultant only "passed" when writing in first person (a style clients rarely approve)
3. **It wastes talent and burns people out** — batches arrive so off-mark they get rewritten under unrealistic deadlines; shoehorned examples compromise flow; "humanize this" is a talent-haemorrhaging strategy

If you must do it, reduce the pain:
- **Train a custom model on your content first** — brand guidelines + best-writing examples as training docs (project-style bots accept large document sets); few-shot prompt with exemplars; iterate ("digital intern" framing)
- Expect it to get you only ~40–50% of the way; training time itself can exceed the benefit; without a clear vision you'll loop on prompts

How to push back:
- Feed back issues regularly in meetings
- Track humanizing time in a shared spreadsheet to prove the time-sink
- Present the risk with real examples of sites hit by spam-policy actions (scaled content abuse) for mass-produced, low-value AI content; the risk is low value at scale, not AI use itself
- Redirect stakeholders to AI content needing little human touch: glossaries, product descriptions, FAQs, wikis (still fact-checked and reviewed)
- Propose proven non-AI traffic plays: free tools, programmatic pages, content localization (in one analysis, the top bootstrapped SaaS traffic growers used these three); programmatic pages need unique data on each page, not near-duplicate templates

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Never start from 100% AI drafts for editorial content — use AI for ideation, structure, outlines, repurposing, and pacing edits
- [ ] If forced: build a style-trained custom bot before manual editing
- [ ] Log hours spent humanizing; report the true cost
- [ ] Show leadership examples of sites hit by scaled-content-abuse actions
- [ ] Migrate low-touch formats to AI where human voice matters least
- [ ] Pitch one scalable alternative (free tool, programmatic pages, localization)

## Data points & benchmarks
- Searches for "how to humanize AI content" grew 943% in 12 months (older figure)
- Custom-trained bots get content ~40–50% of the way to publishable
- Best-performing bootstrapped SaaS SEO growers relied on: free tools, programmatic content, localization — not humanized AI

## Mistakes to avoid
- Using AI detectors as proof of quality or engaging writing
- Keyword-stuffing to game SEO traffic-light tools
- Signing off first drafts that are 100% AI then animating them later
- Treating writers as AI-polishers — it's the fastest way to lose them

## Key principles
- The problem isn't AI content per se; it's content so bland readers can tell
- AI as guide (ideas, structure, cleanup) not ghostwriter
- Trust humans to write content for humans; use AI where humanity isn't the value
