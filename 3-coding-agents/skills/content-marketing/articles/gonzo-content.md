# Gonzo Content: What Marketers Need to Learn From Hunter S. Thompson

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Generative AI is a world-class armchair commentator — it outperforms humans at remixing other people's ideas, making the "curator" content marketer obsolete. The response is gonzo content: becoming part of the story, building real (even short, intense) firsthand experience, and doing things worth writing about instead of just writing.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The argument, in stages:
1. **The old model** — a decade of content marketing rewarded zero subject-matter expertise: read widely, write quickly, shuffle ideas, add keyword-targeted H2s. By the author's rough estimate, ~80% of returns came from regurgitation.
2. **The break** — AI is the best regurgitator ever built. Humans cannot out-copycat it. Firsthand experience is now the only durable differentiator.
3. **The gonzo precedent** — Thompson's journalism abandoned objectivity and made the reporter part of the story: he joined the motorcycle club he covered, campaigned openly for his candidate, and made the Kentucky Derby about himself — a story that outlasted every factual account.
4. **The restructure** — radically reduce content output; dedicate up to half the team's time to research and experimentation; make skin-in-the-game a hiring prerequisite; treat "writing" as a secondary skill to a decade of hands-on experience.
5. **Early signals already visible** — companies hiring bona fide SMEs (expensive, scarce, worth every cent); marketers documenting experiments on social where the meta-content outperforms the "real" content; brands sharing subjective stories instead of competing on objective facts; companies promoting in-house creators' personal brands while corporate accounts lay dormant.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Audit your recent output: what percentage required genuine firsthand experience? Raise it
- [ ] Cut output volume and reallocate that time to experiments and original research
- [ ] Attach every assignment to a lived experience: interviews, tests, teardowns, field work
- [ ] Hire practitioners first, writers second
- [ ] Document experiments publicly as they happen (meta-content)
- [ ] Let subjectivity, opinion, and personality into brand content

## Data points & benchmarks
- Rough rule of thumb (an estimate, not measured data): 80% of returns came from curating others' ideas — that share is now captured by AI
- Investment thesis: SME-marketers are expensive and hard to find, but the only moat in the AI era

## Mistakes to avoid
- Continuing to compete on objective information — information abundance makes it worthless
- Assuming writers can never become experts — short, intense immersion yields real insight
- Keeping corporate-brand voice at the expense of human creators

## Key principles
- Become your story: first-person, skin-in-the-game content can't be synthesized
- In information abundance, hunger is for entertainment, personal interaction, and pattern-interrupting weirdness
- Doing things worth writing about > writing
