# How to Create a Winning Blog Content Strategy in 6 Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A blog strategy aligned to business goals helps you avoid the fate of the ~90% of pages that get no organic traffic (one older study put it at 90.63%). Build it in six moves: define your "why" (help-to-by sentence), research business AND content competitors, do question-focused keyword research, plan with traffic/difficulty/business-value scoring plus clustering, codify editorial identity, and track a maturing KPI.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Define your "why"** — one sentence: "The purpose of my blog is to help [audience with specific problem/JTBD] to [desired outcome] by [means]." Example: "Help marketers get better at SEO and drive more traffic by publishing in-depth tutorials." Every later decision hangs off this
2. **Research two kinds of competitors:**
   - *Business competitors* — same product/market; pull their top pages for pre-proven topics and keywords
   - *Content competitors* — rank for your keywords but sell something else; find them via traffic-share-by-domain reports, then repeat the top-pages analysis for more ideas
3. **Do thorough keyword research** — list seed keywords that define your business → expand them into related terms → filter for questions (often hundreds of question-keyword candidates, most bloggable)
4. **Build the content plan** — score each topic on: traffic potential (TP: the top-ranking page's traffic, not just search volume — a top page can out-draw its head term's volume), ranking difficulty (keyword difficulty score + manual SERP checks), business value (0–3 product relevance). Then **cluster** related topics ("how to make iced/cold brew/french press/whipped coffee") and tackle clusters in batches — same template, shared interlinking into hubs
5. **Create your blog's identity** — editorial guidelines covering visual style (distinctive, recognizable header illustrations), voice & tone (describe your blog as a person; define jargon, formality, humor), and writing rules (spelling variant, formatting, paragraph length, headline formats). Write it all into one onboarding-proof document
6. **Track your success** — start with traffic as the KPI (write, distribute, gain traction); mature to organic share of voice/visibility vs. competitors once established

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write and circulate your one-sentence help-to-by purpose
- [ ] Analyze 3 business + 3 content competitors' top pages
- [ ] Generate the question-style keyword list for your 5–10 seeds
- [ ] Score the shortlist on TP / difficulty / business value; publish only topics with a business-value score of 2 or 3
- [ ] Group topics into clusters; template each cluster; interlink into a hub
- [ ] Write the editorial guidelines doc (visuals, voice, rules)
- [ ] Track traffic now; set a date to graduate to share-of-voice

## Data points & benchmarks
- 7M+ blog posts published daily and ~90% of pages get zero organic traffic (older estimates) — strategy is the filter
- A coffee example: "how to make iced coffee" — top page ~46K monthly visits, KD 28, small sites ranking (moderate difficulty; aim lower, around KD 20 or less, for a brand-new site)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Creating content "for the sake of content" with no strategy
- Judging topics by search volume alone instead of top-page traffic
- Skipping clustering — unclustered topics waste template and interlinking efficiencies
- Letting every writer improvise voice (no guidelines doc)

## Key principles
- Business goals drive the blog, not the reverse
- Competitors (both kinds) hand you a validated topic list before you write a word
- Clusters + hubs + templates convert research into compounding efficiency
- Blogging isn't dead while people still search for information
