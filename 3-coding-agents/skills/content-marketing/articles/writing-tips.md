# 21 Writing Tips to Become a Better Writer Fast

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Good non-fiction writing is easy to understand, well formatted, grammatically clean, and holds attention — and it comes from good editing, not first drafts. The 21 tips group into preparation (goal, outline, focus), style (simple words, short sentences, active voice, no adverbs, no fluff), and practice habits (write daily, read aloud, journal, read great writers, sleep on it).

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Preparation (before you write):**
1. Start with the end goal — know exactly what the reader should take away or do
2. Make an outline — structure logically, finish research up front so writing and research don't interleave
3. Focus — silence notifications, headphones, instrumental music (or silence); whatever gets you into flow

**Style rules (while writing):**
4. Ditch fancy words — "fancy words only confuse people"; simplest word that still explains the idea wins
5. Short sentences with varied lengths — keep sentences under 16–25 words, 1–2 ideas each; but vary lengths to create rhythm (all-same-length paragraphs feel robotic)
6. Conversational tone — write like you talk, within reason; "buttoned up" professional register reads boring
7. Master transitions — a paragraph's job is to get the next paragraph read; smooth abrupt jumps
11. Active voice — "You will write better after reading this" beats "Your writing will be made better"
13. Ditch adverbs — delete "really/very", reread; 9 times out of 10 removing it makes writing punchier
14. Master punctuation — commas/dashes/colons are tools; new writers overuse long dashes and commas (many commas can simply be deleted)
15. Ruthlessly cut fluff — if removing a word or sentence loses nothing, it was fluff; say the most with the fewest words

**Editing and feedback:**
9. Read your writing out loud — the single highest-impact tip; awkward phrasing and bad transitions become glaring
12. Use first drafts — you cannot edit a blank page; expect to edit, edit, edit
16. Have someone else read your draft — fresh eyes catch what your sunk-cost brain can't

**Practice habits (getting better over time):**
8. Write every day — any writing counts (emails, posts, journaling)
10. Keep a journal — pen-to-paper practice was the author's second-biggest improvement
17. Know your audience — research who they are and the search intent behind their questions
18. Develop curiosity — uncurious writing is bad writing; find an interesting angle first
19. Read great writers — find the best writers in your field and study them
20. Write somewhere new — fresh environments shake loose better words
21. Sleep on it — stuck drafts often resolve themselves overnight

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write your one-sentence goal before starting any piece
- [ ] Outline and complete research before drafting
- [ ] Set a distraction-free writing block (notifications off)
- [ ] Simplify any word a sixth grader wouldn't know
- [ ] Keep sentences <25 words; vary lengths deliberately
- [ ] Convert passive constructions to active
- [ ] Delete adverbs and test whether they're missed (they won't be)
- [ ] Read every draft out loud before publishing
- [ ] Get one external reader's feedback
- [ ] Write something daily, even small
- [ ] Study 2–3 great writers in your niche
- [ ] When stuck, change location or sleep on it instead of forcing

## Data points & benchmarks
- Sentences: keep under 16–25 words, 1–2 ideas each
- Rule of thumb: 9 out of 10 adverbs can be deleted with no loss (only ~1 in 10 is load-bearing)
- Rule of thumb: nearly all first drafts need editing before they're publishable
- Good writing = understandable + well-formatted + grammatical + concise + attention-holding

## Mistakes to avoid
- Trying to sound "professional" instead of natural
- Long run-on sentences packing multiple ideas
- Uniform sentence lengths (boring rhythm)
- Adverb stacking for false emphasis
- Beefing up word count with fluff to sound sophisticated
- Never reading work aloud (misses the worst problems)
- Writing about topics you can't get curious about

## Key principles
- "Good writing comes from good editing."
- The paragraph's job is to make you read the next paragraph.
- Say the most with the fewest words.
- If you use only one tip: read your writing out loud.
- Writing is a daily practice, not a talent.
