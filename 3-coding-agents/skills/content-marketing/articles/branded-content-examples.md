# 7 Examples of Great Branded Content

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Branded content is entertainment-first, values-led media (sponsored, commissioned, or produced by a brand) that builds affinity instead of selling. It stands out because it makes the audience — not the product — the subject, and it's measurable through mentions, topic co-mentions, traffic, keyword growth, and links.

## Framework / Step-by-step
What branded content is (and isn't):
- Mass-appeal media/entertainment tied to a brand; the product may appear but never overshadows the entertainment
- Not content marketing (sparks brand interest), not advertising (direct persuasion), not product placement (subliminal insertion)
- Classic proof: a tire company's restaurant guide — useful to drivers, brand-aligned, zero selling

Why create it:
1. Stand out among "pick-me" brands ("content marketing is a first date — don't talk only about yourself")
2. Build positive associations
3. Show your human side
4. Reach new audiences via new formats/channels (chat show → podcast audiences; zine → design subcultures; interactive game → gamers)
5. Justify premium pricing through storytelling (thrift-store items with writer-penned stories sold for 6,400x original value)

The 7 examples (patterns to copy):
1. **B2B "business thriller" podcast** — an infosec firm's scripted crime drama voiced by known actors: makes an obscure topic accessible, quietly sets up the problem the product solves
2. **Surreal fashion short film** — comedy actors, decades-spanning spelling-bee gag; defer creative control to signal entertainment over selling
3. **Cross-brand collaboration** — a holiday-movie channel × a sports league: each gains the other's audience and values alignment (64% of Gen Z/millennial women viewed the league favorably post-pop-culture moment)
4. **SaaS Netflix-style studio** — payment infrastructure brand produces documentaries and web series on founders; long-game marketing that makes a faceless category human
5. **Authentic grassroots campaign** — laundry brand celebrates messy game-day food traditions with NFL legends at tailgates; unscripted, regional, distributed natively per platform
6. **Pop-up TV channel for a cause** — a pet charity + broadcasters run a calming movie/music channel for dogs on fireworks night; pure audience-empathy alignment
7. **Quirky physical artifacts** — a children's book about SEO for practitioners to read with their kids, plus a sponsored board game; icebreakers and recall devices that humanize a cookie-cutter category

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Mine customer conversations and trending topics to find what your audience loves outside your product
- [ ] Pick a format your audience already consumes for fun (podcast drama, short film, games, events)
- [ ] Defer creative control to actual creators; keep product mentions subordinate
- [ ] Plan multi-platform distribution from day one
- [ ] Measure: brand mentions (including news coverage), topic co-mentions as % of brand mentions (topical authority), campaign-page traffic, related keyword growth, links earned by the campaign name

## Data points & benchmarks
- Viewers were 62% more likely to react positively to branded content vs. 30-second TV ads (older study)
- Content featuring even one human was 81% more effective (Kantar/Meta/CreativeX study, older)
- Branded content improved brand recall by 81% (older study; vendor-reported figures vary)
- Significant Objects study: $1.25 thrift items sold for ~6,400x original value after adding purpose-written stories
- Example topical authority: 3.2% of one outdoor brand's mentions co-mention "sustainability"

## Mistakes to avoid
- Inauthenticity — audiences see through values you don't actually hold
- Ambiguous messaging that reads the wrong way (a tech brand's ad showing art crushed by a press outraged creatives)
- Emotional content cuts both ways: negative reactions will be equally emotional
- Expecting immediate leads — this is long-game brand building
- Undisclosed sponsorship — paid or commissioned content must be labeled (ad-disclosure rules apply), and sponsored links need rel="sponsored"

## Key principles
- Values first, product later: the message is "we get you"
- Emotional response drives interaction signals, which drive search and AI visibility
- In a world of dirt-cheap information, gut-feeling content is one of the few remaining moats — faceless, emotionless brands lose
- Brands aligned with audience interests win mentions, links, and branded search volume
