# 5 Types of Blogs That Make Money

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Five blog models reliably generate income: niche blogs, affiliate/review blogs, personal blogs, personal brand blogs, and business/corporate blogs. Pick the model first — it dictates topic selection, content formats, and which of the nine monetization methods fit.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Type 1 — Niche blogs.** Deep coverage of one theme. Topics chosen by traffic potential, monetizability, reader requests, and trends. Proven money niches (older, self-reported income; treat as outliers): food ($95K/mo on 4.2M pv), health/wellness ($9.6K/mo), parenting ($11K/mo), news (one blog sold for $315M), tech ($5.2K/mo), personal development ($6.6K/mo), pets ($7.7K/mo), entrepreneurship ($166K/mo), finance ($33K/mo), fashion ($11K/mo), lifestyle ($41K/mo), travel ($41K/mo), DIY/crafts ($15K/mo). Profitability check: search "niche + affiliate program".
**Type 2 — Affiliate/review blogs.** Reviews-only sites. Demand is huge: one "best headphones" page pulls 12K+ organic visits/month ranking for hundreds of keywords. One entrepreneurship blog earned 63.4% of $166K/mo from affiliate deals. Follow search-engine product-review guidelines — review pages get extra scrutiny — and disclose affiliate relationships (tag affiliate links rel="sponsored").
**Type 3 — Personal blogs.** Monetize "something" unique: a teachable skill, a teachable success story, an inspiring philosophy, or community access. No format rules; typically monetized via content upgrades, books, courses.
**Type 4 — Personal brand blogs.** Content tightly focused on your industry: guides, tutorials, original research, case studies, opinions, free resources. One designer's brand blog built a 250K+ audience, monetized via speaking, mentoring, audits, and products.
**Type 5 — Business/corporate blogs.** Educational content around the product to attract/retain customers. One large SEO company's blog generated ~641K monthly organic visits (older figure) — equivalent ad traffic would have cost ~$838K/month. Big-corporate blogs often just capture branded search and PR.

**Finding proven topics (3 methods):**
1. Keyword research — filters for quick wins: low difficulty, low-authority sites in top 10, traffic potential ≥ 100 (one seed set yielded ~3,000 ideas).
2. Competitor keyword mining — pull keywords a competitor ranks for that you don't (content gap).
3. High-performing content — find pages with high traffic and few backlinks (≤10 referring domains, 500+ monthly visits), or link-magnet pages (200+ referring domains).

**Nine monetization methods** — ads (typically ~$5–$40 per 1,000 pageviews), affiliate (3–90% commissions), sponsored content, selling products (own or affiliate storefront), courses (one blogger: $258K/mo from a course vs $26K/mo from affiliate), paid memberships/subscriptions ($44–$99/mo tiers), speaking (up to $100K/engagement), coaching, selling/flipping the blog (marketplaces list 1,100+ blogs, up to $4.7M).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Choose your blog type before choosing topics
- [ ] Validate the niche: affiliate program supply + competitor income reports
- [ ] Read 2–3 public blog income reports in your niche for realistic benchmarks
- [ ] Build a keyword pipeline with low-difficulty filters
- [ ] Match monetization to blog type (reviews → affiliate; brand → speaking/coaching; corporate → product education)
- [ ] Consider building to sell: blogs trade at multiples of monthly profit

## Data points & benchmarks
- In one older, self-reported survey: ~15% of bloggers earned full-time income ($30K+/yr); 31% earned ~$6K/yr side income; 6–10% earned $10K+/mo.
- Some guides cite $25K–$50K as a first-year target; treat that as a best case — most blogs earn far less in year one.
- One food blog earned 55% of income ($52K) from ads in a single month; one wellness blog earned 39% ($3.8K) from six sponsored campaigns that came inbound via brand networks.
- One niche food ebook lists 16 distinct ways to monetize a food blog (incl. recipe licensing).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating a personal blog like a diary with no monetizable "something."
- Review content that ignores product-review quality guidelines.
- Choosing topics with no demand evidence.
- Waiting for fame before monetizing — coaching requires skill proof, not fame.

## Key principles
- Blog type determines strategy end-to-end; pick deliberately.
- Reviews monetize best when built on first-hand experience.
- Corporate blogs compound into ad-equivalent traffic worth six figures monthly.
- Selling the blog is a legitimate exit — even built-to-flip is a valid model.
