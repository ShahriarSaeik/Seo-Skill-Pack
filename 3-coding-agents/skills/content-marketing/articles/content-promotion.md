# Content Promotion: There are Only 3 Ways to Distribute Content

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Stop asking "how do I promote my article?" — that's spam-thinking. Distribute IDEAS, not articles: an article is just a vessel for an idea (e.g., "we make the best SEO tool"). There are only three ways to get an idea in front of people: build an audience, borrow an audience, or buy an audience.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Reframe first:** "How do I promote my content?" really means "how and where can I spam my article?" Instead: (1) your idea must be unique/original — nobody shares or links to mediocre; (2) promotion isn't just link clicks — podcasts, YouTube, conferences, social sharing all count. One brand spreads its ideas via blog, YouTube, podcast appearances, conferences, workshops/webinars, creator sponsorships, community, newsletter.

**The 3 ways:**
1. **Build an audience** — People who deliberately follow you (newsletter, podcast, X, LinkedIn). Best channel: EMAIL — you own it; social platforms can ban you or throttle reach. Give people a reason to subscribe: a carrot (ebook, PDF, email course) or simply trust ("see more good ideas" — the approach one solo creator used to pass 200K subscribers). Convert social followers to email constantly.
2. **Borrow an audience** — Go where your audience already hangs out and play by their rules. Best channel: GOOGLE — search traffic is predictable, compounding, cheap, targeted. The SEO trio: (a) find what people search for (keyword research); (b) give them what they want (match search intent — top results + featured snippet format + People Also Ask); (c) prove you deserve to rank (backlinks = votes; every legitimate link building tactic is a variation of "a reason to link": editorial guest blogging, broken links, podcasts, original assets, unlinked mentions; never buy or trade links without rel="sponsored"). Unique data/insights/stories make links come naturally.
3. **Buy an audience** — Google/social/display ads, influencer + creator sponsorships (clearly labeled as sponsored), even Quora ads (cheaper). PPC is fast to set up and gives near-immediate feedback — great for experimenting. Downside: not infinitely scalable, ROI on content promo is rarely immediate, spend never stops. Route paid traffic into email lists and SEO assets.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Define the core idea every piece of content serves
- [ ] Start building an email list now (offer a carrot or earn trust)
- [ ] Convert social audiences to email subscribers
- [ ] Do keyword research → match intent → build links for Google traffic
- [ ] Create genuinely link-worthy content (original data, insights, stories)
- [ ] Test cheap ad platforms (e.g., Quora) for fast feedback
- [ ] Reinforce every paid/borrowed touchpoint with owned-channel capture

## Data points & benchmarks
- Only 18% of SEOs still used the Skyscraper Technique in one older industry link-building survey — saturated "reasons" get ignored
- One large SEO blog's traffic would have cost an estimated $796,000/month if bought via Google Ads (snapshot estimate), versus a small content team
- One solo creator: >200K newsletter subscribers with a trust-based CTA
- Featured snippets + People Also Ask reveal required format and sub-questions

## Mistakes to avoid
- Promoting articles instead of ideas (leads to spam tactics)
- Relying solely on rented social audiences (algorithm/ban risk)
- Using weak link building "reasons" (Skyscraper is beaten to death)
- Expecting ads to be an infinitely scalable content-promo channel
- Publishing nothing original and expecting shares/links anyway

## Key principles
- "An article is simply a vessel" — the idea is what spreads.
- Build (email), borrow (Google), buy (ads) — every tactic fits one of three buckets.
- "What works—and will always work—is the idea behind your content."
- Even paid traffic should end up feeding owned assets (email + SEO).
