# How to Write a Great Listicle Post in 10 Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Listicles work because they're skimmable, digestible, and measurable (the number tells readers how much is left). A good listicle = searched-for topic that suits list format + length matched to the SERP + basic-vs-detailed format choice + a winning angle + original points expanded from your own brainstorm, illustrated, and properly concluded.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Choose a topic** — people must be searching for it; use keyword research (phrase-match reports); pick something you genuinely know
2. **Ensure a listicle makes sense** — check top-ranking results: if they're mostly step-by-step guides (e.g., "how to tie a tie"), a listicle is the wrong format. Find candidates with modifier filters: ideas, tips, best, tools, things, strategies, tactics, list
3. **Decide on length** — item count should roughly match what's ranking (all triple-digit lists for "date ideas"? don't write 5; no top result over 30 for "blogging tips"? don't write 100). Deviate only when the SERP norm genuinely fails searchers
4. **Choose a format:**
   - **Basic** (1–2 sentences per item) — for long lists or simple topics (50+ date ideas need no essays)
   - **Detailed** (paragraphs per item) — for short lists or complicated topics (SEO tips need step-by-step execution advice)
5. **Choose a winning angle** — personal experience ("we use at our company"), best, expert-backed, for beginners, specific outcome ("to increase organic traffic"), tried & tested. Take cues from top results (e.g., "travel tips" results = personal experience; "dinner ideas" = ease). Never a clickbait angle you can't deliver; for self-promotional "best X" lists, disclose your interest and rank honestly
6. **Brainstorm your own points FIRST** — before looking at existing listicles; carbon copies attract neither readers nor backlinks
7. **Expand with research** — then supplement with: a content-gap or topic-coverage report (terms frequently mentioned by top-ranking pages) and competitors' subheadings (use a tool that extracts only the subheadings to avoid absorbing their full content)
8. **Flesh out each point** — match depth to format; keep the angle in mind (beginner lists: no jargon; experience lists: add your thoughts/opinions)
9. **Illustrate** — images exploit the picture superiority effect (pictures are remembered more than words); one example list has 39 images because the topic is complicated; don't force them where unneeded
10. **Wrap things up** — listicles tend to end abruptly; write a short conclusion: don't repeat points, drive home one main takeaway, link to related posts

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Confirm the topic's top results are actually listicles
- [ ] Use modifier filters (ideas/tips/best/tools/things) to find listicle candidates
- [ ] Count items in top 5 results; set your target length in that range
- [ ] Pick basic vs. detailed format by list length + topic complexity
- [ ] Choose an angle evidenced in the SERP
- [ ] Brainstorm your own points in a blank doc BEFORE reading competitors
- [ ] Extract competitor subheadings (not full content) for gap-filling
- [ ] Add images to points that need visual explanation
- [ ] End with a short, non-repetitive conclusion + internal links

## Data points & benchmarks
- Listicles in the wild drive serious traffic (figures from the time of the analysis): one brand's alternative-search-engines list gets 11K+ monthly search visits; an email-finding list 8K+; an affiliate-sites roundup 3.5K+; a quick-SEO-tips list 3K+; even a blog-templates list does 1.2K+/mo
- "Date ideas" SERPs: lists of hundreds of items; "blogging tips" SERPs: none over ~30 — match your niche's norm
- Picture superiority effect: images are remembered over words in repeated experiments

## Mistakes to avoid
- Forcing listicle format on step-by-step topics
- Item counts wildly out of line with the SERP norm
- Copying existing listicles point-for-point (no unique value = no links = no rankings)
- Reading competitors in full before brainstorming (you'll absorb their list)
- Essays on each of 50 simple items; one-liners on each of 7 complex ones
- Clickbait angles the content can't deliver
- Self-promotional "best X" lists that hide the bias (disclose it; rank honestly)
- No conclusion ("things just stop")

## Key principles
- The SERP defines format, length, and angle — your job is originality within those rails.
- Own ideas first, competitor inspiration second.
- Skimmability is the listicle's superpower — preserve it with subheads and images.
- Listicles can be serious, high-traffic assets — not just clickbait fodder.
