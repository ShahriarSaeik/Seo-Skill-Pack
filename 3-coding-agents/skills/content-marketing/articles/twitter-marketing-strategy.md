# A Data-Driven Twitter (X) Marketing Strategy: How One B2B Team Does It

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One in-house social manager's playbook for a B2B Twitter (X) account: publish in multiple formats per content asset, experiment constantly instead of repeating one formula, plan weekly from internal pipelines + external sources, review engagement monthly — and deliberately DON'T track CTRs or conversions, focusing on quality content and steady audience growth instead.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Why Twitter/X for a B2B brand:
- It's where the industry's marketers and influencers congregate
- It humanizes the company (casual, close interaction)
- Brand-building: on one account, 47% of profile visitors also clicked through to the linked website (~113 link clicks/day across its tweets; older, single-account data)
- Versatility: one content asset (post, video, course, tool) amplifies in many formats

Working with the algorithm (no formula exists — experiment):
- Publish each blog post in ≥2 tweet formats, staggered to reduce fatigue (numbered lists reliably perform)
- Keep finding NEW formats; repeating one winner makes you look uninventive
- Don't pander: memes and snark weren't this brand's voice; tone can evolve deliberately (the team later tested a more casual voice)

Three operating lessons:
1. **Develop a thick skin** — responding to experts and trolls is daunting; ask questions openly, keep a tight feedback loop (thread drafts reviewed by the original authors; manager reviews published tweets)
2. **Normalize mistakes** — more posts = more mistakes; a divisive tweet taught the value of context (never divisive for its own sake)
3. **Talk outside your circle** — lurk communities and hashtag top-tweets; patterns people love: recommended reads, "X years, X lessons" formats, clean infographics/charts, Google/industry updates, and content that either affirms or strongly challenges views (add value when engaging)

Weekly planning process:
- Inputs: upcoming blog/video schedules, product updates (internal chat), latest newsletter, brand mentions, own top-performing tweets (search operators on your account), older posts worth resharing
- Share external content too (trending industry threads, newsletters) — win-win amplification
- Calendar: not mandatory — write/schedule directly in a scheduling tool; adapt per platform. (Only skip the calendar AFTER you've fixed frequency and timing from analytics — e.g., one account's impressions peaked Wed/Thu, so 5+ pieces were queued each of those days)
- Build an SOP: plan → write → schedule → archive copy → promote → track

Measurement (the contrarian part):
- This team deliberately does NOT track click-through rates or conversions — it frees them to focus on quality content that resonates (a choice that suits one team; most teams should still track clicks and conversions, e.g., with UTM-tagged links)
- Success signals: >100 likes or above-average RTs/replies/quotes; monthly review (every 4 weeks with the CMO) of what formats/topics worked
- Vanity metrics (likes, followers, impressions) are indicators, not truth — not everything resonates with everyone; don't obsess
- Ads: amplification only, reviewed ~every 3 weeks, not relied on for growth

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Repurpose every major asset into 2+ tweet formats, staggered by days
- [ ] Study your analytics for peak days; queue 5+ posts on those days
- [ ] Run account search operators monthly to catalogue your top formats
- [ ] Open DMs/questions to the community; ask, don't posture
- [ ] Review last month's tweets in 4 categories: repurposed posts, content picks, product/video updates, tips/trivia (+ external guest picks)
- [ ] Try everything once; kill formats that stop performing

## Data points & benchmarks
- 47% of profile visitors click the linked website; ~113 organic link clicks/day
- Success threshold: >100 likes or above-average engagement per post
- Cadence: weekly planning; 5+ queued posts per peak day; monthly performance review; ad review every ~3 weeks

## Mistakes to avoid
- Riding one winning format into the ground
- Divisive tweets without context — engagement isn't worth the trust hit
- Maintaining process theater (heavy calendars) that hampers actual output
- Judging every post by vanity metrics

## Key principles
- Experimentation + feedback beats any "algorithm hack"
- Sharing others' content builds community, not just your reach
- Not measuring conversions was a deliberate choice for this team — quality content and audience growth lead; conversions follow
