# How to Write a Blog Post Outline in 7 Simple Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An outline kills writer's block, organizes thinking, prevents missed points, and aligns you with editors before drafting. Build it in 7 steps: topic → format → angle → template → subheadings (from expertise, content gaps, and PAA questions) → supporting bullets → planned intro (PAS) and conclusion. Then get it reviewed before drafting.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Choose your topic** — brainstorm burning niche issues; or mine question-form keywords with a keyword research tool
2. **Select your content format** — listicle, how-to, opinion, review, news. Often obvious from the query ("how to make kefir" = how-to); if ambiguous ("best productivity app"), check what's ranking — if the SERP is all listicles, write a listicle
3. **Decide your angle** — the topic is the street, the angle is the spotlight. "How to make ramen" can be fast / restaurant-quality / vegan / Korean-style — you can't cover all. Angle sources:
   - Personal experience or expertise (unique recipe you actually developed)
   - Expert interviews
   - Crowdsourced opinions (community polls)
   - Data or science
   - Contrarian (only with evidence, not for its own sake)
   - Or read the dominant angles in top-ranking pages ("date ideas": quirky / fun / location-specific / creative)
4. **Use a template** — most posts are variations of a few templates; don't reinvent structure (e.g., a standard skeleton for step-by-step guides used across an entire blog)
5. **Figure out subheadings (H2/H3/H4)** from three sources:
   - **Your expertise first** — the proper way to do things as you know it
   - **Content gap analysis** — paste 3–4 top-ranking URLs (similar angle only) into a content-gap tool, intersect their keyword rankings → common subtopics they all cover (e.g., "keto diet" pages all cover: what is it / rules / is it healthy / what to eat)
   - **People Also Ask boxes** — expand PAA questions, or pull all question keywords grouped under your parent topic
6. **Add bullets under each subheading** — NOT full prose. Bullets = brief arguments, examples, potential expert quotes, data to cite. Detail level is up to you and your editor
7. **Plan intro and conclusion** — intro: a few bullets on the Problem–Agitate–Solve hook; conclusion: one-line takeaway, links to further resources, or a final consideration

**Before drafting:** send the outline to a colleague/editor for feedback (common practice on in-house teams; flaws caught at outline stage are far cheaper to fix).

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] State topic, format, and angle in one line each before outlining
- [ ] Check the SERP if format or angle is ambiguous
- [ ] Start from a proven template for your format
- [ ] List your own expert subtopics BEFORE competitor research
- [ ] Run a content gap on 3–4 same-angle top pages; convert shared subtopics into H2s
- [ ] Pull PAA/question keywords for FAQ-worthy subtopics
- [ ] Bullet each section: argument + example + data + quotes
- [ ] Draft the PAS intro plan and one-line conclusion
- [ ] Get outline reviewed before writing a single paragraph

## Data points & benchmarks
- Outlines align writers with editors/managers/clients before expensive drafting
- Content gap intersection of 3–4 top pages usually yields the subtopics searchers expect (keto example: what/why/rules/health/food = the expected H2 set)
- At one large blog, every in-house writer submits outlines for review — standard practice, not training wheels

## Mistakes to avoid
- Starting a draft on an empty document
- Writing the full article inside the outline (it's an outline — bullets only)
- Skipping the angle decision (trying to cover every angle at once)
- Competitor-gap research before capturing your own expertise (you'll absorb their structure)
- Including pages with different angles in your gap analysis
- Drafting without anyone reviewing the outline
- No planned hook for the intro

## Key principles
- The outline is a contract between writer and editor — review it before drafting.
- Topic = street; angle = spotlight. Choose what to illuminate.
- Your expertise first, SERP evidence second.
- PAS for intros; one-line takeaway for conclusions.
