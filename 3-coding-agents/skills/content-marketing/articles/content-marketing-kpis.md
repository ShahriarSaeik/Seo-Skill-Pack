# 2 Types of Content Marketing KPIs to Track Separately

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Split KPIs into inputs (resources you control: quantity, content mix, distribution power) and outputs (value created: share of voice, traffic, leads, engagement, backlinks). Never tie input KPIs to cost efficiency or output KPIs to sales — both cause bad decisions.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Input KPIs (your resources):**
1. **Quantity** — how much you publish. For beginners, quantity = number of experiments; for teams that know what works, quantity is the ultimate growth KPI. Right question: not "how much is enough?" but "can we make more content?" Benchmark against competitors' publishing rate (new + republished).
2. **Proportion** — the mix of content types: education (solve problems, feature product), inspiration (motivate), entertainment (evoke experience). Example target: 70% educational / 20% inspirational / 10% entertainment, or filter by business-potential score (publish low-score content max once a month). Change the mix only to experiment.
3. **Distribution power** — link profile strength, social followers, email subscribers, depending on your channels. Quality of content grows it; over-mailing or neglect shrinks it — track it separately.

**Output KPIs (value created directly):**
1. **Organic share of voice** — % of all clicks on your tracked keywords landing on you vs. competitors; instant read on discoverability.
2. **Organic traffic** — qualified clicks via targeted keywords; measure from Search Console and exclude branded queries to isolate content performance. Estimate ceilings with traffic-potential metrics (traffic of the #1 page).
3. **Leads** — only meaningful with gated content; normalize as views→form-completion rate to remove promotion effects.
4. **Engagement** — comments, engagement rate, shares. Imperfect proxies; use to compare pieces and test formats, not to obsess over. Shares weigh more than likes (reputation stake).
5. **Product usage** — usage frequency, time-on-task, feature flow for features mentioned in content. Watch causation (measure workflows the content recommends) and privacy (aggregate, compliant analytics).
6. **Feedback** — comment sentiment, audience surveys ("want more of this topic?" "easy to follow?"), NPS-style "would you recommend?"
7. **Backlinks** — people link to useful content; track mainly for link-bait pieces. Links are rare — don't expect them from every format.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pick 2–3 input KPIs (quantity, mix %, distribution power) and set targets
- [ ] Benchmark competitor publishing volume quarterly
- [ ] Define your content-type proportions explicitly
- [ ] Track organic SOV across your keyword set, not just positions
- [ ] Filter organic traffic reports to exclude branded queries
- [ ] Normalize lead KPIs to conversion rate per view
- [ ] Survey your audience at least twice a year
- [ ] Reserve backlink targets for link-bait content

## Data points & benchmarks
- Example mix target: 70/20/10 education/inspiration/entertainment.
- Business-potential scoring: prioritize topics scoring ≥2 of 3.
- Shares are always lower than likes — a noticeable spike in shares signals something special.

## Mistakes to avoid
- **Input side:** modeling content decisions on cost per piece; cutting costs for its own sake; assuming "more with less" indicates performance.
- **Output side:** tracking sales or ROI as a content KPI — purchases depend on price, product-market fit, support, competitors. Sales are an *outcome*, not an output (birthday cake = output; happy kid = outcome).
- Treating engagement as proof of value rather than a weak proxy.

## Key principles
- Inputs are what you control; outputs are what you create. Match inputs to desired outputs before setting KPIs.
- Every metric can be a vanity metric without context — even sales.
- Distribution power is an asset that must be maintained, not a given.
