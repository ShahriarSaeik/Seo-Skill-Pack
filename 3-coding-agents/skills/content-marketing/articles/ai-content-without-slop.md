# Using AI for Every Article Without Making AI Slop

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Slop is not defined by how much AI you use or by stylistic tells (em dashes, banned words). Slop is content published without enough human understanding, judgment, evidence, or original contribution to justify the reader's attention — it transfers effort from the creator to the reader. Use AI for execution, but keep the thinking, verification, and ownership human.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Do the human work before you draft.** Writing is now cheap; move effort upstream. Before prompting for prose, define:
   - Reader: who is this for, what are they trying to accomplish?
   - Promise: what will they understand or be able to do afterward?
   - Point of view: what are you actually saying, and where might someone disagree?
   - Evidence: which claims need sources, data, demos, or expert input?
   - Information gain: what can you add that isn't already in the search results?
2. **Feed the model raw material it could not produce itself**: interviews, internal knowledge, proprietary data, real demonstrations, failed experiments, specific examples. A "Source of Truth" library (facts/stats, explanations, product details, how-to guides) gives you and the AI a shared, verified knowledge base.
3. **Offload substance by talking, not typing.** Dictation preserves the mess (uncertainty, caveats, half-formed opinions) that contains the value. Or have the AI interview you and extract claims before outlining.
4. **Create places to stop and pass judgment.** Never one-shot an article; break work into stages with a human decision gate between each:
   - Idea gate: do we have something useful to add, or would this repeat what's ranking?
   - Outline gate: does every section serve the article's promise?
   - Evidence gate: can we support the important claims?
   - Draft gate: has AI smuggled in certainty, filler, or examples we didn't earn?
5. **Spend the time AI saves on making better content, not just more content.** AI lowers the cost of data analysis, small tools, interactive elements, and data studies — work that used to need specialists. Ask what you can now put inside an article that used to be impossible.
6. **Give every article an owner and a second human.** The owner must understand the topic well enough to validate claims and put their name on it; an editor must read every word with the authority to say no.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write a one-paragraph premise (reader, promise, POV, evidence, information gain) before any drafting
- [ ] Collect proprietary raw material: interviews, internal data, experiments, examples
- [ ] Build a searchable Source of Truth (facts, explanations, product details, how-tos)
- [ ] Dictate or get interviewed by the AI instead of typing tidy summaries
- [ ] Run idea → outline → evidence → draft gates, with a real kill option at each
- [ ] Reinvest saved hours in original data, tools, or interactive elements
- [ ] Assign a named owner plus a second human reviewer who can reject the draft

## Data points & benchmarks
- One reported automated pipeline can research, outline, draft, and fact-check a near-publish-ready article in 6–12 minutes — capable of tens of thousands of articles, which is exactly why restraint is the strategy (mass-producing them risks scaled content abuse)
- One reported data-refresh automation handling a dozen-plus datasets saves 20+ hours of manual work per month, turning quarterly (or never) updates into monthly ones
- Heavily AI-generated pages can and do rank in positions 1–3 (see one large AI content study) — the differentiator is quality, not AI usage

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating stylistic scrubbing (removing em dashes, "delve", "it's not X, it's Y") as a fix — you get slop with cleaner punctuation
- Letting one polished 2,000-word draft create sunk cost: cheap output regenerates the sunk cost every few minutes
- Using AI only to produce the same article for less and spending the savings on volume
- Rubber-stamping: a human in the loop means nothing if they lack the knowledge or willingness to reject the output

## Key principles
- Slop transfers effort from creator to reader; the publisher skips investigation, verification, and opinion-making
- The question is not how many or which words AI wrote — it's whether a human understood, judged, verified, and stood behind what went live
- If nobody truly owns the result, it's slop
- Use AI to remove the work behind each article, or to attempt work you previously couldn't afford — never to lower the bar for what deserves to publish
