# 23 Actual Content Metrics From Actual Marketers

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A crowd-sourced list of the metrics practitioners actually track, grouped into eight categories: operational, keywords, backlinks, leads, traffic, audience growth, engagement, and conversions. Not every metric fits every strategy — pick the ones that match your funnel role.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The 8 categories and their metrics:
1. **Operational (team efficiency)** — publishing frequency (results compound with output; traffic and referring domains grow in proportion to consistent publishing); deadline hit-rate (validates estimates; critical for agencies)
2. **Keywords** — impressions; rankings; organic keyword growth (count of keywords a page ranks for — best early signal for new content); rate of keyword acquisition ("when Google picks up lots of keywords quickly, it's going to rank well"); share of voice (% of possible organic clicks for tracked keywords)
3. **Backlinks** — growth of linking domains (among the strongest correlates of rankings; equity flows site-wide via internal links). Track only for link-bait assets or active link building; skip in a site's early stages; quality over quantity (track "best links" only)
4. **Leads** — MQLs (left contact info, may buy later); SQLs (sales-reviewed, pitch-ready); high-intent leads matching your ICP
5. **Traffic** — measure growth, not absolute clicks: (this period − last) ÷ last × 100%. Pro tip: first-7-day traffic indicates launch/promotion quality and topic resonance. Compare content groups (e.g., by site section or folder)
6. **Audience growth** — newsletter subscribers, LinkedIn, YouTube follower growth (native platform metrics). Caveats: viewer fatigue, algorithm changes, topic drift; beginner content attracts the most subscribers
7. **Engagement** — likes/comments; email open + click rates + unsubscribe spikes; time on page; scroll depth; content mentioned in sales conversations (pure word-of-mouth); pro move: track engagement only for ideal customer profiles (removes the "vanity" from vanity metrics)
8. **Conversions** — traffic↔revenue/signup correlation (escapes per-piece attribution problems); conversion growth from BOFU content; "first page seen → paying customer"; content downloads

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Track publishing frequency and deadline adherence as first-class metrics
- [ ] Watch keyword acquisition speed on every new post (fast pickup = future winner)
- [ ] Report traffic as growth %, weekly for launch signals, monthly/quarterly otherwise
- [ ] Restrict backlink tracking to deliberate link assets
- [ ] Filter engagement metrics by ICP where possible
- [ ] Use traffic↔conversion correlation instead of impossible per-article attribution
- [ ] Prune the list to metrics that match YOUR strategy — nobody tracks all 23

## Data points & benchmarks
- One site's content portfolio: traffic and referring domains grew proportionally with cumulative articles published (a single-site observation)
- First-week traffic = launch quality + topic-interest signal
- Beginner-level content drives the most channel subscriptions

## Mistakes to avoid
- Tracking backlinks too early (they lag; expect none at first)
- Reading engagement absolutes without audience-size/algorithm context
- Expecting short-window traffic ROIs — content compounds over months
- Forcing every metric into every report

## Key principles
- Different strategies justify different metrics; mismatched metrics create false failures
- Correlation beats attribution for bottom-line proof
- Operational metrics predict future output; keyword metrics predict future traffic
