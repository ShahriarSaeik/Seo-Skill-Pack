# How to Write an Irresistible Headline in 3 Easy Steps

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A headline must catch attention, spark curiosity, and accurately describe the content — without clickbait. Build it in 3 steps: pick the format searchers want (from the SERP), add a winning angle (the differentiator after the "for..."), and make it human (readers' own language, specific and concrete).

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Ground rules:**
- Write headlines for searchers (in one older study, ~2/3 of the average site's traffic came from search)
- Write the headline BEFORE the post (writing it after invites overpromising = clickbait)
- The headline is usually the ONLY factor people use to choose your article

**Step 1: Pick a format** — candidate formats for any topic:
- Listicle ("10 Best X to Y")
- How-to ("How to Choose the Best X for You")
- Tutorial ("Best X: Choosing in 6 Steps")
- Opinion ("Is There Really Such a Thing as the Best X? Science Says No")
- Review ("X Review: The Best X")
- Comparison ("X vs. Y: Which Is Best?")
- Choose by the DOMINANT format among top-ranking pages, not personal taste (use a private/incognito search or a SERP preview to de-personalize results)
- Existing post mismatched? Small edits can switch formats: "Write Your Best Headline: A Six-Step System" → "6 Ways to Write a Good Headline"

**Step 2: Add a winning angle** — scan top titles for the differentiator (usually the words after "for"):
- For men / for women; for every diet; for weight loss; for building muscle
- Balance what searchers want vs. what you can genuinely deliver; if you're the expert, take the angle that makes more sense
- No dominant angle? Use a clear benefit or an under-served angle readers actually want

**Step 3: Make it human** — use adjectives/figures of speech you'd use describing the article to a friend:
- "10 best protein powders" → "10 best protein powders for effortless weight loss" / "...to get shredded"
- Mine readers' language: industry jargon, slang, community glossaries (subreddit wikis, social threads)

**4 power tips:**
1. **Add concreteness** — tangible, real-world specifics: "15 Tasty Protein Powders to Help You Gain 1lb Per Week in Muscle"
2. **Surprise and delight** — for social traffic, an unexpected angle beats intent-matching
3. **Create curiosity gaps** — "Everyone's doing X but you" — then deliver in the article
4. **Test variants** — run different titles for the same article on social; promote the winner as the main title

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Check the dominant headline format in the top 5–10 results before writing
- [ ] Write the headline before the article (prevents clickbait drift)
- [ ] Extract the common angle from top titles; adopt or deliberately improve it
- [ ] Replace generic phrasing with community language from niche forums/glossaries
- [ ] Add one concrete, tangible detail (number, timeframe, outcome)
- [ ] Verify the article delivers everything the headline promises
- [ ] A/B test title variants on social before settling on the final

## Data points & benchmarks
- Office workers receive on the order of 100+ emails a day (older figure: 121) — headlines compete in brutal attention markets
- ~2/3 of the average website's traffic came from search (older study) — optimize headlines for searchers first
- Copywriting classics remain the deepest source of tested headline formulas (direct-response advertising books from the 1920s–60s still describe formulas getting clicks a century later)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Picking a headline format you like instead of the one searchers reward
- Writing the headline last (leads to overpromising)
- Clickbait angles the content can't deliver
- Robotic titles with no personality
- Vague headlines with no concrete specifics

## Key principles
- Catch attention + create curiosity + describe accurately = a headline that wins without clickbait.
- The SERP is your headline format consultant.
- Specificity is a click magnet: tangible outcomes beat abstractions.
- Readers' own words (from communities they inhabit) are the best headline vocabulary.
