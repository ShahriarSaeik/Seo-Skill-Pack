# How to Build an SEO Topical Map (Spreadsheet Layout Included)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
An SEO topical map organizes your content into a hierarchy of topics and sub-topics, signaling comprehensive coverage to search engines. A 7-step process filters every candidate topic by brand relevance, business potential, and traffic potential — so you never create content nobody cares about or that doesn't grow the business.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Identify your site's main topic** — Questions: What's your core focus (as precisely as possible)? What do you want to be an expert in? What themes tie content together? What limits your product/service scope?
2. **Determine supporting and sub-topics** — Idea sources: AI keyword suggestions (any SEO tool), ChatGPT (can generate hundreds of ideas quickly; verify them), Google features (autocomplete, People Also Ask, related searches), Wikipedia tables of contents, competitor content plans. Spreadsheet layout: supporting topics on top, up to ~20 sub-topics under each.
3. **Assess brand relevance + business value** — The step most people skip. Not every SEO-adjacent topic fits every business model (locations vs. services vs. courses vs. tools vs. SEO types all suit different models — even big brands can't target them all). Score brand relevance 0-3 and business potential 0-3 (3 = product is an irreplaceable solution).
4. **Verify traffic potential** — Check topics one-by-one in a keyword research tool or bulk-import your keyword list. Auto-group keywords by the topic of the top-ranking page. Paste the metrics into your spreadsheet (drives total volume + traffic potential calculations).
5. **Finalize topics** — Keep topics scoring well on brand relevance, business potential, total volume, traffic potential. Remove/replace any topic low on at least TWO metrics (e.g., "bulbs" for a flower site: high volume, zero brand relevance/business potential → cut).
6. **Map existing pages and new URLs** — Tag each URL "Existing" or "New" (proposed slugs) against topics; exposes coverage gaps.
7. **Plan follow-up tasks** — Content tasks: create pillar pages, create sub-topic resources, update existing pages, consolidate/separate multi-topic pages, internal link the hub. Prioritize via a priority score you calculate in the spreadsheet (relevance + business potential + traffic), bucketed into priority bands — work the top band first.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Set up a spreadsheet with columns for topic, sub-topics, scores, metrics, URL status, and tasks
- [ ] Define one precise main topic
- [ ] Brainstorm sub-topics from 5+ data sources
- [ ] Score every topic 0-3 for brand relevance AND business potential
- [ ] Bulk-validate traffic potential; cluster keywords by topic
- [ ] Cut topics scoring low on 2+ of the 4 metrics
- [ ] Map existing vs. new URLs; identify gaps
- [ ] Export prioritized task list (priority bands) into project management

## Data points & benchmarks
- Suggested layout: up to ~20 sub-topics per supporting topic
- Priority score formula combines brand relevance + business potential + traffic potential
- Business potential scale: 3 = irreplaceable solution, 0 = no way to mention product

## Mistakes to avoid
- Building a massive topic list without verifying business relevance ("be selective")
- Targeting every topic adjacent to your niche — different intents suit different business models
- Skipping traffic potential validation → content nobody searches for
- Not mapping existing pages → duplicate work and missed gaps
- Treating the map as a one-off — it feeds ongoing prioritized content planning

## Key principles
- The map is "the roadmap for your website's content strategy."
- Selective beats exhaustive: fewer, on-brand topics build authority; off-topic content dilutes it.
- Every topic must pass brand relevance, business potential, AND traffic potential.
- Topic-level performance data (including competitors') builds the business case for non-SEO stakeholders.
