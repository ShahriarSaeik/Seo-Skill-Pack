# Topical Authority: What It Is, How Google Measures It, and How to Build It

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Topical authority = search engines recognizing your site as THE expert source on a subject (not just single keywords). Build it via topic-based keyword research → topic clusters with pillar pages → authority content → relevant links → freshness/gap-closing → measurement. It's niche-specific: a low-authority specialist can outrank Amazon for its niche.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Do topic-based keyword research** — Pick seed keywords specific enough to focus, broad enough for subtopics ("coffee roasting" not "coffee"). Expand via phrase-match keyword ideas. Find competitor topic gaps with a competitor-comparison (content gap) report that lists keywords competitors rank for and you don't. Check which topics AI assistants associate with your brand in an AI visibility tracker (with and without your brand name in the prompt).
2. **Map topic clusters and pillar pages** — Group your keywords in bulk by the topic of the top-ranking page (most keyword research tools can cluster this way). Each cluster gets a pillar page (guide / what-is / how-to format, based on what ranks) linking to deeper cluster pages. Filter every subtopic: meaningful demand? business relevance? within topical focus? Combine thin subtopics rather than publishing weak standalone pages.
3. **Write authority content** — Definitive resources built on firsthand experience and original insight. Match search intent, cover subtopics thoroughly (an AI content grader check), demonstrate E-E-A-T, internally link the cluster. Depth > length. Do NOT fill gaps with thin AI/programmatic content at scale — it risks scaled content abuse problems and can drag down the site's overall quality.
4. **Build relevant links** — External: topically relevant links beat high-volume irrelevant ones (coffee blog link > finance blog link). Tactics: editorial guest blogging, linkable assets, ego bait, HARO-style journalist-request services, digital PR (no paid links without rel="sponsored"). Internal: pillar = hub linking to every cluster page and back; cross-link related cluster pages; fix orphaned pages first (site audit + an internal link opportunities report).
5. **Keep content fresh and clusters complete** — Refresh triggers: traffic drop from peak, superseded stats, changed tools, competitor published substantially better version, dropped out of an AI Overview. Run content gap analysis vs competitors every few months — AI query fan-out means more subtopic coverage = more sub-query visibility.
6. **Track over time** — Branded topic searches ("yourbrand keyword research") in Search Console/a backlink analysis tool; unbranded keyword coverage (total keywords, positions 1-3, new top-10 entries, share of voice); AI Share of Voice in an AI visibility tracker.

**Be selective:** topics you DON'T cover matter as much as those you do. Pre-commission test: "Would a first-time visitor to this page immediately understand what your site is about?"

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Choose focused seed keywords; build full keyword list (research + competitor gaps + AI topics)
- [ ] Auto-cluster keywords by the topic of the top-ranking page; assign pillar + cluster structure into a content map
- [ ] Filter subtopics through demand/relevance/focus checks

## Data points & benchmarks
- One specialist e-bike retailer with low domain authority outranked Amazon (very high domain authority) for e-bike keywords and earned AI Overview appearances — topical focus can beat raw authority
- One health publisher's magnesium glycinate page (snapshot at the time of the analysis): 2,500 Google keywords, 473 AI Overview queries, 279 ChatGPT prompts, 200 Perplexity, 28 Gemini, 86 Copilot
- Timeline: 6-12 months to significant movement (months 1-3 coverage, 4-6 initial gains, 6-12 compounding)
- Article count: ~15-20 well-connected pieces for a specialized niche; 50+ for broader subjects
- Leaked 2024 Google API documentation mentions siteFocusScore and siteRadius attributes, which hint at measuring topical concentration; Google hasn't confirmed how (or whether) they're used in ranking, so treat "off-topic content dilutes authority" as a hypothesis
- Algorithm milestones: Hummingbird (2013) → RankBrain (2015) → Medic (2018) → BERT (2019) → MUM (2021) → Helpful Content (2022) → SGE preview (2023) → AI Overviews launched (2024) → API documentation leak (2024) → query fan-out (2025-26)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing every keyword with volume — off-topic content may dilute site focus (hypothesized site focus score/site radius)
- Publishing thin AI-generated/programmatic content to fill cluster gaps — risks scaled content abuse problems and lowers overall quality
- Combining subtopics that deserve standalone pages, or splitting ones that don't
- Building irrelevant high-authority links instead of niche-relevant ones
- Forgetting internal links (orphaned pages = cluster gaps)

## Key principles
- "Google used to rank individual pages that targeted the right keywords. Now it increasingly features sites that own the right topics."
- Topical authority ≠ domain authority metrics: subject-matter depth in a niche vs. backlink strength.
- Variety of branded topic queries matters more than volume of one branded term.
- Authority compounds: each new cluster page benefits from every page around it.
