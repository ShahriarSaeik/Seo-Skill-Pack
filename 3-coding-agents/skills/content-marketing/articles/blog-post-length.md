# How Long Should Blog Posts Be? The 'Truth' About Content Length

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Word count is not a ranking factor (John Mueller, 2019). One industry analysis of ~900M pages shows the correlation (not causation) between length and SEO benefits REVERSES past a point: after ~1,000 words backlinks decline, after ~2,000 words organic traffic declines. Write as long as necessary and as short as possible.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Debunking "longer is better":**
1. **"Longer content gets more backlinks"** — True up to ~1,000 words; strong NEGATIVE correlation beyond. Theory: long posts bury link-worthy points in "nice to know" fluff; fewer people finish 3-hour reads, so fewer encounter (and link to) the link magnets.
   - Fix: include all link-worthy points, succinctly. Find them: look up a top-ranking competitor URL's backlinks in any backlink tool and scan the anchor text and linking context for trends (e.g., one post's links disproportionately cited its "APP intro method" section — include an equivalent, not a copy).
2. **"Longer content gets more traffic"** — Moderate positive correlation up to ~2,000 words; negative beyond (avg 10K-word post gets LESS traffic than avg 2K-word post). Real causes: more backlinks (up to 1K words) and thoroughness → ranks for more long-tail keywords (one search operators post: ~7,200 keywords; a competing post with only 12 operators ranks for far fewer).
   - Fix: research what's important FIRST, then cover relevant subtopics succinctly. Method: eyeball top-ranking pages for commonalities (both keto pages cover "what is" + "foods"), or run a content-gap comparison on the top URLs to extract required subtopics ("what is the keto diet", "keto meals", "keto breakfast" → instant outline).

**Why succinct wins:** "Nobody likes to read. They just want the information." — a veteran SEO. Older platform data (Medium): engagement drops past 7 minutes reading time; at ~250 wpm (Univ. of Chicago) that's ~1,750 words. But length follows the topic: one YouTube SEO guide runs 7,000+ words; Chrome extensions list just 1,275.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Never target a word count; target complete-but-succinct subtopic coverage
- [ ] Mine competitor backlink anchors to find must-include link-worthy points
- [ ] Build outlines from content-gap report/commonalities of top-ranking pages
- [ ] Cut 10%+ of fluff with a readability editor
- [ ] Get a colleague to flag points that only complicate the post
- [ ] Treat ~1,750 words (7 minutes' reading) as a reference point for engagement, not a target or a rule

## Data points & benchmarks
- ~900M pages analyzed (one SEO data provider's index; correlations, not causal effects)
- Backlinks correlate positively with word count up to ~1,000 words, then decline
- Organic traffic correlates positively up to ~2,000 words, then declines
- Engagement drops after 7 minutes reading (~1,750 words at 250 wpm)
- Search operators post: ~7,200 keyword rankings from thoroughness
- Real examples: 7,000+ words (YouTube SEO) vs 1,275 words (Chrome extensions)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Setting word-count targets or "1,900-word minimums"
- Padding posts with "nice to know" info that buries link magnets
- Assuming Google penalizes length or rewards it — it's neutral; thoroughness is what earns long-tail rankings
- Skipping external review of unnecessary points

## Key principles
- "Don't focus on length. Focus on including all link-worthy points as succinctly as possible."
- "Don't focus on length. Focus on covering all relevant subtopics as succinctly as possible."
- Thoroughness ≠ word count: a 12-item list can be less thorough than a complete one.
- Minimum viable length: ~300 words — shorter means you probably have nothing to say.
