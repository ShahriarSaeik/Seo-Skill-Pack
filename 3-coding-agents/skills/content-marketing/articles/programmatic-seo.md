# Programmatic SEO: A Beginner's Framework

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Programmatic SEO creates keyword-targeted pages automatically from a template plus a dataset — one template can yield thousands of ranking pages. It works only when every page is fed by unique, relevant data; templated pages with nothing to say risk being classified as spam under Google's scaled content abuse policy.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Find keywords that scale** — look for a repeating phrase where only one part changes ("fastest growing [X] companies", "[job title] salaries in [city]"). Group keyword matches by the repeating part; require ≥50 variations, check combined volume, median difficulty, and the strength of currently ranking sites. One real project found 169 variations across 122 categories at median difficulty 9.
2. **Decide on your page template** — audit what top-ranking pages for these keywords share (sections, data points, page elements). Include what's common; skip what's rare; note genuine gaps. Typical must-haves: jump-link table of contents, stated methodology, visible "last updated" date, fixed data block per item, item name + key figure in headings, a chart, a short written profile.
3. **Find relevant data** — three options: proprietary data (yours alone — strongest), licensed public data, or scraped data (watch copyright and terms of service). Check which sources competitors use and avoid saturated ones (e.g., most lists rank by public funding data; ranking by proprietary growth data instead differentiates).
4. **Build your pages** — fixed template + per-row data + freshly written per-page copy. Separate instructions: what stays identical (layout/headings), what's fetched from data (numbers, charts), what's written fresh per row. Test on 10 rows, fix template issues, then scale. A no-code route: spreadsheet formulas that join fixed text with cell values, optionally with AI functions writing per-row summaries (fact-check and review the output) — drag down to generate a small pilot batch of pages.
5. **Publish and automate** — push to your CMS programmatically; schedule recurring rebuilds from fresh data (e.g., monthly) so numbers never go stale.
6. **Track performance** — keep all programmatic pages in one URL folder so the project can be monitored as a unit. After 3 months, review: project traffic trend, dead pages, per-page keyword rankings, impressions/clicks/CTR. Monthly: improve declining pages, redirect overlapping ones, prune pages that earn nothing after a fair test.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Identify keyword patterns with 50+ variations and manageable difficulty
- [ ] Reverse-engineer the shared sections of top-10 pages before designing your template
- [ ] Secure a data source competitors don't all share
- [ ] Pilot 10 pages; verify quality before generating the rest
- [ ] Automate data refresh on a fixed cadence
- [ ] Isolate pages in one folder and review monthly
- [ ] Prune or redirect zero-traffic pages instead of letting them accumulate

## Data points & benchmarks
(Figures are from the time of each analysis and may have changed since.)
- A nutrition directory: ~4K pages → ~182K monthly organic visits (grown from ~100 pages in 9 months).
- A stock-news directory: ~72K pages → ~1.74M monthly visits.
- A design-tool brand's feature pages: ~310 pages → ~13M monthly visits (from ~20K in 2022).
- A productivity platform's template categories: ~600 pages → ~204K monthly visits within months of launch.
- A writing-tool brand's tool library: ~130 pages → ~464K monthly visits, built once then compounding with no new pages.
- One practitioner tracked 70+ companies hit by search-engine updates after scaling content; another agency scaled 10,500 AI-assisted pages with consistent multi-year ranking gains — the difference is data value, not page count.
- An industry study of 331K pages: publishing at scale is only a risk when there's nothing worth publishing at scale.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Publishing thousands of near-identical thin pages — "programmatic SEO is often a fancy banner for spam" (search-engine spokesperson).
- Reusing the same saturated public data as every competitor.
- Scaling before testing a small batch.
- Letting stale data sit in live pages.
- Ignoring the long tail of dead pages that quietly accumulate.

## Key principles
- One template + unique data row = one page; the data is the product.
- Searchers want to do something, not just read — interactive tools/calculators resist being summarized away by AI answers.
- Scaling is only risky without something worth scaling.
- Free tools and utilities are programmatic assets that hold traffic even as AI answers reduce clicks on articles.
