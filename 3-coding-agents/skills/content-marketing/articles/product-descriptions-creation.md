# How to Write Product Descriptions That Convert

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A product description is a story about who the product is for and the life it helps them get — not a list of features. Know the audience, tell a story, optimize for search, test with ads, format beautifully, and ruthlessly cut fluff.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Know your audience above all** — answer two questions per product: Who exactly is the buyer (age, gender, interests, life situation)? What desired outcome does the product deliver (not "a boot" but "looks good, holds up, doesn't break the bank")? Research via forums, customer feedback, and actual buyer calls — worth giving free products or discounts for.
2. **Tell a story** — make the reader part of your mission (fair-priced, ethically made, quality materials). Borrow angles: one boot brand sells "highest quality, priced fairly"; one fire-pit brand leads with the universal pain of smoky fires and teary eyes; one chocolate brand describes the tasting experience so vividly you can imagine it — visualization drives purchase. Humor works even for unglamorous products (one bathroom-gadget brand built its whole page on an evolutionary "story").
3. **Write with SEO in mind** — pick the target keyword via keyword research (e.g., "purple bikini" ~3,000 searches/mo beat "lavender bikini"; 38 related terms found; example figures). Weave related variants ("purple swimsuit", "purple bikini set") into headers, descriptions, and alt text — naturally, as word variation, not stuffing. Write unique descriptions rather than pasting manufacturer copy used by every other retailer.
4. **Use ads to test copy** — run search ads and port proven winning phrases into descriptions. No ads? Study competitor ads for recurring wording (e.g., "Designer", combo pricing) — heavily-run ads are already optimized and tested; borrow the patterns that convert, but write your own wording (never copy text verbatim).
5. **Make it look good** — scannable formatting, short paragraphs, high-quality photos and video, page broken into visual sections. Treat the product page as a landing page, not a photo + buy button. Use benefit bullets: feature + benefit + power word ("pre-shrunk, so it will fit the same forever").
6. **Cut the fluff** — sleep on it, read aloud the next day. Fix "that" overuse, passive voice, weak verbs. Example rewrite: "made by hand in a factory in Indiana by people who put a lot of care into every stitch" → "hand-crafted with love in every stitch, sewn right here in Indiana."

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write a one-sentence desired-outcome statement for each product
- [ ] Lead the description with the audience's pain, not the product's specs
- [ ] Check that the top fold states what the product is in ≤2 sentences, zero fluff
- [ ] Choose the target keyword by comparing traffic potential of synonyms
- [ ] Add 3–5 natural keyword variants across headers, copy, and alt text
- [ ] Mine competitor search ads for proven phrases before drafting
- [ ] Convert features into feature→benefit→power-word bullets
- [ ] Do a next-day read-aloud edit pass

## Data points & benchmarks
- One chocolate brand donates 10% of profits to conservation — a story element that doubles as copy.
- "Purple bikini" is searched ~3,000 times/month; a single product keyword can expose 30–40 viable related terms.
- Best-performing example descriptions use 1–2 sentences above the fold, then detail sections below imagery and specs.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Describing features instead of the customer's imagined life.
- Boring copy — "the worst fault a salesman can commit is to be a bore."
- Keyword stuffing instead of natural variation.
- Weak verbs, passive voice, filler words.
- Treating the product page as a static spec sheet instead of a conversion landing page.

## Key principles
- Sell the outcome and the identity, not the object.
- If customers can visualize owning/using/consuming the product, they're one step from buying.
- Proven ad copy is free conversion research.
- The product page is your 24/7 sales machine; every improvement compounds.
