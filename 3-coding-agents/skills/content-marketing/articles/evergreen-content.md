# Evergreen Content in the Age of AI Search

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Evergreen is a TOPIC-selection strategy, not a publish-once-and-forget strategy. Even the most stable topics (compound interest has been stable for 4,000 years) have top-ranking pages updated within months, because competition and algorithms demand freshness — and AI search is even more recency-biased than Google. Pick semantically stable topics, then commit to a measured refresh cadence.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Core distinction:**
- **Evergreen topic** — the semantic core (fundamental answer/mechanism) is stable ("compound interest" = stable; "best AI writing tools" = changes every few months)
- **Evergreen content** — what you build on the topic; requires active maintenance not because the concept changes, but because the competitive landscape does

**7-step process:**
1. **Find stable demand** — use search volume trend graphs: flat lines over long periods = good; ad hoc spikes = news/hype-driven; slow declines = avoid. Target the "plateau" phase of the search demand lifecycle
2. **Apply the semantic stability test** — ask: Will the fundamental answer be the same in 5 years? Has it been the same for 5+ years? Is it independent of specific tools/platforms/people/trends? Quick test: ask an AI assistant WITHOUT web search — a confident, accurate answer from training knowledge signals a stable topic ("how to use [specific tool]" topics fail this: demand looks stable, but UI changes/acquisitions destroy them)
3. **Find the content refresh rate** — every topic has a cadence at which top-ranking pages get updated: high (financial products, software comparisons, AI tools: monthly/quarterly), medium (marketing guides, industry how-tos: annual/biannual), low (foundational concepts: update when stats/examples age). Check the average last-updated date across top 20 results; if you can't match that cadence, don't take the topic
4. **Match search intent** — verify before writing and re-verify when refreshing; intent shifts over time (e.g., "compound interest" SERPs demoted calculation-heavy pages in favor of explanatory ones); use SERP-comparison views to spot changes
5. **Cover required subtopics** — run a content-gap analysis across top-ranking pages for common subtopics; consistent subtopics across results = stability signal; wildly varying subtopics = topic less stable than it looks
6. **Avoid short-lifespan angles** — no pop-culture framings, trend-dependent hooks, or embedded timestamps ("earlier this year", "in 2025") in timeless pieces; write to the underlying concept, not the current example (exception: "best of" lists where freshness IS the angle)
7. **Maintain on a cadence** — meaningful refresh = replace dated stats with sourced current figures, swap aged examples, add emerged subtopics, update screenshots. Trigger updates on: traffic down 20% from peak, dropped from an AI Overview, superseded stats, new subtopics, competitor refreshes, rankings without conversions

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Check the trend graph for every evergreen candidate (flat > spiky)
- [ ] Run the no-web-search AI test for semantic stability
- [ ] Look up the topic's average top-20 last-updated date before committing
- [ ] Reject topics whose refresh cadence exceeds your resources
- [ ] Strip timestamps and trendy references from timeless pieces
- [ ] Schedule per-page refresh reminders at the topic's cadence
- [ ] Track the 6 refresh triggers (traffic −20%, lost AI citation, dated stats, etc.)
- [ ] Re-check search intent at every refresh, not just publication

## Data points & benchmarks
- Google's top 20 results for "compound interest": all updated within ~10 months on average (freshest: 4 days) — nothing ranks untouched
- In one study, 85% of AI Overview citations came from content published in the last 2 years
- AI-cited content is 25.7% fresher on average than content that only ranks organically (one study)
- AI Overview answers change every ~2.15 days but keep 0.95 cosine similarity (one study) — surface churns, meaning stays
- Average #1 ranking page is 5 years old; 72.9% of top-10 pages are 3+ years old (older study of 1M URLs) — old + maintained wins
- Google reportedly considers multiple freshness signals (byline date, URL date, on-page date, last significant update); treat this as a hypothesis, not a confirmed ranking factor
- Refresh payoff: updating one stable post to cover new AI tools produced an immediate traffic spike

## Mistakes to avoid
- Treating evergreen as "write once, never touch" — every top result gets updated
- Confusing stable search demand with stable answers (tool tutorials)
- Cosmetic date changes without substantive improvement (doesn't work)
- Choosing evergreen angles with built-in expiry dates (pop culture, trend hooks)
- Taking high-refresh-rate topics (best credit cards: 1–2 updates/month) without the resources
- Publishing an MCU-themed marketing post — the topic was evergreen, the content wasn't (traffic died with the hype)

## Key principles
- Evergreen = topic strategy + operational maintenance commitment. Both required.
- Freshness and authority together win in both classic and AI search.
- The competitive set defines your refresh rate — measure it, then match it.
- If it should feel accurate in two years, don't timestamp it.
