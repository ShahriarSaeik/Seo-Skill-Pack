# Content Marketing Goals: How Many and Which Ones

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
The classic list of "content marketing goals" (awareness, leads, loyalty, retention...) confuses outcomes with goals. One useful model: content directly achieves three things, which are educate, inspire, and entertain. Set those as goals, express them via controllable output targets, and let the business outcomes follow.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Reject the traditional goal list.** Awareness, lead gen, thought leadership, conversion, loyalty, retention are *outcomes* of good content, not goals. And you can't optimize one piece for a single "goal" — lead-targeted content that ignores trust and quality converts nothing.
2. **Adopt the three real goals:**
   - **Education** — content on problems your product solves, things it improves, or adjacent audience challenges (even when your product isn't the solution). Primary outcome: interest in the product.
   - **Inspiration** — the spark to act; works on imagination and emotion rather than complete solutions. Reaches people before they even have the problem (beats competitors to the punch), builds emotional connection, drives spontaneous returns. Primary outcome: interest in the brand.
   - **Entertainment** — lighter content that catches attention and evokes experience; broadest reach and earliest journey stage; viral potential. Only if your audience responds to it. Primary outcome: attention and awareness.
3. **Express goals as controllable outputs.** Good: "Publish 9 educational + 2 inspirational articles in Q3", "Test 20% more entertaining content for 6 weeks", "See if focusing content on features X/Y/Z raises their usage." Bad: "Generate 400 leads with the ebook", "Become a thought leader this year", "Decrease churn 2%" (those are outcomes you can't directly control).
4. **Track simple KPIs:** publication rate (more good topics = more chances), social engagement (relative, within your own profile), organic share of voice, NPS, impact on product usage.
5. **Find your proportions** — start with a guesstimate (e.g., 70% education / 20% inspiration / 10% entertainment) or a prioritization rule (product-led content: prioritize topics where the product fits naturally). Experiment from there.
6. **Think like a farmer** — squeeze everything from what you grow: repurpose, build owned audiences, promote, syndicate, build links, update underperformers. Repetition works because audiences are fragmented across channels and messages are more effective when repeated.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Rewrite any "goal" that's actually an outcome (leads, awareness, retention) as educate/inspire/entertain targets
- [ ] Set quarterly output goals you fully control (pieces published per goal type)
- [ ] Define your content proportions and revisit quarterly
- [ ] Pick 3–4 KPIs max from: publication rate, engagement, SOV, NPS, product usage
- [ ] Survey NPS: "How likely are you to recommend our blog to a colleague?"
- [ ] Run one proportion experiment per quarter (e.g., +20% entertainment)
- [ ] Add one repurposing/update play per existing asset before net-new content

## Data points & benchmarks
- NPS rule of thumb: 30–70 is great, 70+ is excellent (benchmarks vary by industry).
- Starting proportion heuristic: 70/20/10 education/inspiration/entertainment.
- One email-brand documentary about a candy-store owner exists purely to "attract people who've never heard of us and don't need us yet" — entertainment reaching the earliest journey stage.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Judging every piece by customers it brings — bosses may expect it; outcomes don't work that way.
- Strict time frames on goals — good content takes time to produce and to show effects.
- Complicated KPI stacks before basics are consistent.
- Letting content quality deteriorate by over-focusing on a single "goal".
- Publishing and forgetting — leaving harvested value in the field.

## Key principles
- Content influences only education, inspiration, entertainment; everything else is a downstream outcome.
- Good content is user-centric, not business-centric — like exercise, it benefits the whole body only if done right.
- Control outputs, not outcomes.
- Treat the three-goal model as a compass, not a map.
