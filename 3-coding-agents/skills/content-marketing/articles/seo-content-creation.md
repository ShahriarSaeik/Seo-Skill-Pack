# SEO Content Creation Process: A 6-Step Workflow

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A working writer's 6-step process for creating SEO content: find keywords with traffic potential (via competitor top pages), score business potential, match search intent from the SERP, outline and get feedback before drafting, write with lived experience, then apply a light on-page checklist. No secret tricks — process and editing quality do the work, and no process guarantees a #1 ranking.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Target a topic with search traffic potential** — look at keyword search volume AND traffic potential (total traffic across all keyword variants at #1; one page ranks for many variations). Source ideas from competitors' top pages report (pages with the most organic traffic reveal proven topics)
2. **Score business potential** — how naturally can you pitch your product while covering the topic? Prioritize topics scoring ≥2 on a 0–3 scale ("not essential, but a huge time-saver" = 2)
3. **Figure out search intent** — study the SERP: dominant format (step-by-step guide) and audience (beginners). Identify intent from the SERP overview; matching intent is, as a rule of thumb, the biggest part of on-page SEO
4. **Put together an outline** — fields: target keyword, search intent, pitch/angle, potential titles, H2–H4 structure. Benefits: test idea logic before committing thousands of words, easier feedback, no blank-page syndrome. For subtopics: run a content-gap report across the top 3–5 pages and include common keyword topics. Editor review here decides efficiency vs. uniqueness (repurpose an old post vs. write something new with lived experience — reserve the extra effort for higher-TP topics)
5. **Write your draft** — some drafts need outreach first (expert roundups), some are heads-down writing. Write, don't generate: "writing is thinking." Draft standards: accurate statements (no hype; qualifiers like "could/may"), no fluff, jargon only when needed, natural product use cases woven into narrative, images where possible. Submit for editorial feedback; iterate
6. **Sprinkle on-page SEO magic dust** — checklist: target keyword in title/URL/H1/intro; engaging meta description; internal links to useful pages; alt text on all images. Most important: the title — brainstorm 10+ variations in different styles (use an AI assistant to expand options), it's both the ranking hook and the human click driver

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pull 5 competitors' top pages monthly; shortlist keywords by traffic potential
- [ ] Assign business potential score; only pursue ≥2
- [ ] Confirm intent (format + audience) before outlining
- [ ] Fill outlines with content-gap subtopics from top-ranking pages
- [ ] Get outline approved before drafting
- [ ] Decide deliberately: repurpose (efficient) or create unique (worth it for high-TP topics)
- [ ] Brainstorm 10 titles; pick the one that earns the click
- [ ] Run the on-page checklist as the last step, not the strategy

## Data points & benchmarks
- One writer's track record: 111 articles published, ~80–90% SEO content, ~121,000 monthly search visits in total (at the time of writing)
- Example keyword: 500 monthly searches, 1,100 traffic potential (variants multiply traffic)
- A competitor's single page ranked for ~3,000 keywords and drew ~24,000 monthly visits at the time of the analysis
- Intent matching is the rule-of-thumb heavyweight of on-page SEO (often quoted as ~80%, not a measured figure); the rest is icing

## Mistakes to avoid
- Targeting topics with no business potential (traffic that never converts)
- Drafting before the outline is approved
- Efficiency everywhere — pure repurposing makes content no one wants to read; AI can do that cheaper
- Publishing without the final on-page pass (title/URL/H1/meta/alt text)

## Key principles
- Competitor top pages are a pre-validated topic list
- Content goals beyond rankings: links, shares, thought leadership — uniqueness buys them
- If you lack experience on a topic, interview people who have it — or go get the experience
