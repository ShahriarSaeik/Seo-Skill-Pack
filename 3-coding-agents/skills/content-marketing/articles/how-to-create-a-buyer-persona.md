# How to Create a Buyer Persona for Your Business

- **Updated**: 2026-10-03

## TL;DR
Most buyer personas are brainstormed in a meeting room and never used — real personas come from interviewing customers, users, and lost prospects, then segmenting by jobs-to-be-done (not demographics). Use the Five Rings of Buying Insight to structure interviews, and put the personas to work in positioning, journey-stage content, and sales/product alignment.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**The process (5 steps):**
1. **Find interviewees** — three groups, not just customers:
   - **Customers** (start with best buyers: longest tenure, highest spend — from your CRM)
   - **Users** — trial/free users who didn't convert (they reveal friction; customers only confirm what went right)
   - **Sales prospects** — people who engaged sales but didn't buy
   - No customers yet? Find target users in communities, groups, professional networks, events; interviews double as customer-development research
2. **Recruit** — honest ask: learning about customers' experience; state the time commitment (20–30 min); promise it's NOT a sales call; sweeten with a product discount or a charitable donation in their name
3. **Interview** — record (with permission), then:
   - Opener: "Take me back to the day you first decided to evaluate [category] — tell me what happened"
   - Structure around the **Five Rings of Buying Insight:** (1) Priority initiatives — why invest now? (2) Success factors — expected outcomes? (3) Perceived barriers — concerns and blockers? (4) Buyer's journey — how do they evaluate options? (5) Decision criteria — what aspects matter most?
   - Technique: allow silence; never defend or pitch; ask "why" and follow up using THEIR words; guide back when they skip around
4. **Organize the data** — transcribe; read for patterns; when a phrase repeats 2+ times, make it a category (easiest categories: funnel stages — Awareness/Interest/Consideration/Purchase — or the Five Rings); do it in a spreadsheet
5. **Segment into personas** — segment by: jobs-to-be-done, pain points, usage, company size, industry. One clear job = one persona; different expectations = new persona (validate with more interviews or a survey). The right count question isn't "how many personas?" but "how many different stories must we tell to win buyers?"

**Using personas (3 applications):**
1. **Positioning** — deliberately define how you're best at something your defined market cares about; fix positioning with what interviews revealed they care about
2. **Journey-stage content** — map each persona's stages (e.g., "struggling to get traffic" → awareness topics), then find stage-appropriate topics via question keywords (one example surfaced 1,600+ candidate topics for a single awareness-stage theme)
3. **Sales and product alignment** — give sales anticipatable objections and rebuttals; give product teams real friction points

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Pull 10+ interviewees across customers, non-converting users, and lost prospects
- [ ] Send non-salesy interview invites with time commitment + incentive
- [ ] Record interviews; open with the "take me back to the day..." question
- [ ] Work through the Five Rings; follow up with "why" and their vocabulary
- [ ] Transcribe; mark repeated phrases; bucket by funnel stage or Five Rings
- [ ] Segment by job-to-be-done, not demographics
- [ ] Write persona docs from qualitative quotes, not invented biographies
- [ ] Turn each persona journey into a topic list per funnel stage
- [ ] Schedule interviews monthly — personas are living documents
- [ ] Share findings with sales and product teams

## Data points & benchmarks
- Minimum ~10 interviews to start; interviews are ongoing (monthly), not a one-off campaign
- 20–30 minutes suffices per interview
- One persona's awareness-stage theme yielded 1,600+ question-keyword topic candidates
- Demographics only matter when they change the buying decision (married status matters to wedding photographers, not martech sellers)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Brainstorming personas in a meeting and filing them away (the Google Drive graveyard)
- Interviewing only happy customers — you'll only hear what went right
- Treating interviews as sales calls (interviewees clam up)
- Excessive note-taking mid-interview; relying on memory afterward
- Surveys first — surveys can only validate what you already know; they can't discover the unexpected (and carry designer bias)
- Demographic-driven segmentation (Charlie CMO / Claire CMO / CMO Chen all share one job — one persona)
- Interviewing the final decision-maker who barely participated in evaluation

## Key principles
- Personas are research artifacts, not creative-writing exercises.
- Segment by job-to-be-done: personas = the number of distinct stories you must tell to win.
- The best insight source is a real conversation; surveys validate, interviews discover.
- A persona that doesn't change your positioning, content, or sales playbook isn't a persona.
