# 3 Unique Content Strategy Examples (And How to Build Your Own)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Every content strategy reduces to three questions (adapted from Good Strategy, Bad Strategy): What challenge are we solving? What leverage do we uniquely have? Which content type fits that challenge×leverage combo? Three contrasting companies show how different the answers — and the resulting strategies — should be.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **What challenges do you want to overcome?** — diagnose specifically: pipeline from scratch, over-reliance on paid ads, converting existing traffic, churn. Specific diagnosis → obvious solution
2. **What leverage can you exploit?** — your secret weapon: unique data (user/product), personal brands, team expertise, a network of experts. Leverage makes strategy uncopiable
3. **Which types of content can overcome those challenges?** — match type to problem: search content = predictable compounding traffic; thought leadership = word of mouth + links but spiky traffic; sales enablement = closes deals but attracts no visitors

The three worked examples:
**A. Automation platform — organic search at scale (~8M monthly visits at the time of the analysis)**
- Challenge: freemium model needs huge visitor volume; horizontal product spans dozens of industries; users need help getting value
- Leverage: integrations with hundreds of tools — writing about popular apps converts their users
- Content: "best apps" lists per industry + programmatic app-integration landing pages (1,000+ pages for a single spreadsheet tool subfolder) + how-to guides (integration pages only work when each page adds unique, useful detail — near-duplicate templated pages risk scaled content abuse); ~93% of organic traffic from blog + integration pages combined
**B. Content agency — thought leadership**
- Challenge: doesn't need volume — needs to attract a handful of experienced marketing execs; must stand out from SEO-churning competitors; must prove its product (writing IS the product demo)
- Leverage: inside visibility into hundreds of companies' content programs → benchmark data no one else has
- Content: opinion pieces, inside-looks at how the company works, research reports; SEO is an afterthought, but word-of-mouth distribution earns dozens of quality links naturally
**C. Healthcare compliance tool — sales enablement**
- Challenge: customers arrive via tight-knit word of mouth; the problem isn't demand, it's persuading risk-averse buyers (lawyers, compliance, IT can all veto) and explaining jargon-heavy topics
- Leverage: hyper-focus — every piece solves one problem (compliant healthcare marketing) for one audience
- Content: use-case explainers, FAQ/integration articles, detailed documentation

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Write your single most important challenge in one sentence — if you can't, diagnose further
- [ ] List your leverage assets: data, personal brands, expertise, expert network
- [ ] Map the challenge to the right content type; refuse types that don't serve it
- [ ] Check your strategy against all three archetypes — which are you, and what are you mistakenly copying?
- [ ] Identify what competitors CAN'T copy about your plan; if nothing, add leverage

## Data points & benchmarks
- Search archetype: ~8M monthly organic visits; ~93% from blog + programmatic pages; 1,000+ pages per popular-app subfolder
- Thought leadership: spiky traffic but strong passive link acquisition
- Sales enablement: near-zero traffic goals, deal-closing success metrics

## Mistakes to avoid
- Copying another company's strategy without their leverage (or their challenge)
- Choosing content types by trend instead of problem-fit
- Vague challenge statements ("raise awareness") that select no strategy

## Key principles
- Strategy = diagnosis + leverage + coherent action
- Volume strategies fit freemium; attention strategies fit agencies; enablement strategies fit complex B2B sales
- The best strategies are deliberately bad at things outside their challenge
