# Inbound Marketing: What It Is and How to Do It

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Inbound marketing pulls customers in with useful content instead of interrupting them with ads. It runs as a flywheel: Attract (search content) → Engage (teach solutions, naturally featuring your product) → Delight (help customers succeed so they refer others).

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Attract** — rank in organic search for problems your product solves. Organic is chosen for two properties: consistent (rankings deliver traffic month after month) and highly targeted (readers arrive at the exact moment they have the problem).
   - Topic selection: pull keyword ideas from a keyword research tool, then score each topic for **business potential** — how naturally you can pitch the product while genuinely solving the problem. Score 0–3; only pursue topics scoring ≥2.
2. **Engage** — inside the content, teach readers to solve their problem, with your product as part of the solution where it genuinely fits. Never force it: if the product isn't the best way to solve the problem, leave it out — this is why business-potential scoring happens at the Attract stage.
3. **Delight** — delighted customers refer others; the flywheel only spins if the product actually delivers. Delight mechanics: keep improving the product; teach customers to get results (blog + help documentation); invite customers into a community; run a public feature-request board so feedback loops back into the product.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List the problems your product solves; find the search demand behind each
- [ ] Score every topic 0–3 for business potential; publish only ≥2
- [ ] Write the content so the product appears exactly where it's the natural solution
- [ ] Exclude the product from content where it isn't genuinely the answer
- [ ] Build onboarding/help content that drives customer results
- [ ] Create a customer community and a visible feature-request channel
- [ ] Review flywheel health quarterly: attract metrics, engage metrics, referral signals

## Data points & benchmarks
- One #1-ranked how-to post on one large SEO blog attracted 58,000 clicks in three months — every reader a potential customer because the topic directly precedes product need.
- Inbound works as a compounding flywheel: each delighted customer generates referrals.
- Question-type keywords (who/what/how/why around your niche) are the richest attract-stage topic mine.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Forcing the product into content where it doesn't fit — it breaks trust and engagement.
- Skipping the business-potential filter, making the Engage stage impossible.
- Investing in delight tactics to compensate for a product that doesn't deliver results ("you can't put lipstick on a pig").
- Copying another company's inbound tactics wholesale — principles transfer, tactics don't.
- Stopping at attraction: without engagement and delight mechanics, traffic never compounds into referrals.

## Key principles
- Create content your audience seeks out when they need it; don't interrupt.
- The right time to pitch is when the reader's problem and your solution coincide — that's what topic selection engineers.
- Delight is a product function first, content function second.
- Inbound compounds: attract → engage → delight → referrals → more customers.
- Each stage feeds the next; weakness in one stage throttles the whole flywheel.
