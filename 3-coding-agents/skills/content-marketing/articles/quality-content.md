# How to Create Quality Content (A 5-Level Framework)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
"Quality content" is measurable on a 5-level ladder: simple listicles → advanced listicles → deep dives → experiments/surveys → research studies/original ideas. Each level needs more expertise, effort, and investment — and is harder for AI to replicate. Treat the levels as a portfolio, not a race to Level 5; vet each idea with the BREW framework.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**The 5 levels:**
1. **Simple listicles** — Aggregate widely available info; no deep knowledge needed; creatable by interns/freelancers/AI. Still drive massive search traffic (one SEO blog's top-traffic pages are simple listicles). Find topics via a competitor's top-pages report. Level up with personal experience: test/use what you list, add mini-reviews, pros/cons, lessons learned.
2. **Advanced listicles** — Curated, opinionated selection ("Shein vs. boutique"). Small, intentional lists where the writer selects, omits, categorizes. Requires domain knowledge — or expert interviews to compensate (e.g., travel SEO piece built from interviews because the author never worked in travel). Level 1 = information dumping (big numbers); Level 2 = curation.
3. **Deep dives** — Exhaustive guides requiring tons of research + SME interviews (one author interviewed a dozen link builders of all backgrounds: agency, consultant, affiliate, in-house, e-commerce). Information architecture is the hard part ("thinking is easy, information architecture is hard" — Visakan Veerasamy). Use an AI content optimization tool to identify required subtopics and structure.
4. **Experiments and surveys** — First-hand data: run the experiment, gather responses, share insights. Expensive, rare, but link magnets (one agency's annual State of Link Building survey earned 1,400 backlinks from 488 referring domains). Idea source: interview co-workers about experiments they've run; sales teams hold unanswered customer questions.
5. **Research studies and original ideas** — Large datasets, new methodologies, industry-reshaping ideas. One brand's top-linked pages are mostly research studies. Shortcut-free (like academia) — but you CAN publish "replications": find proven studies to replicate with a content research tool (keyword + "study", filtered to pages with 50+ referring domains, 500+ words, and strong domain authority).

**BREW framework (deciding what to produce):**
- **B**usiness potential (0-3: how well can you pitch your product?)
- **R**each (who + how many? decision-makers may beat raw traffic)
- **E**ffort (time/resources; consider minimum viable article — 3 interviews instead of 12)
- **W**ho (does the assigned person have the skills? Don't waste seniors on listicles)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Classify every planned article by level (1-5); keep a balanced portfolio
- [ ] Add real experience to Level 1 (test, screenshot, review what you list)
- [ ] Use expert interviews to compensate for missing domain knowledge (L2-L3)
- [ ] Structure deep dives from AI-generated subtopic suggestions
- [ ] Mine colleagues + sales for experiment ideas (L4)
- [ ] Find link-proven studies to replicate with a content research tool (L5)
- [ ] Vet every idea with BREW before committing resources
- [ ] Splinter Level 5 content into multiple Level 1-3 pieces (repurposing)

## Data points & benchmarks
- One blog's highest-traffic pages = simple listicles (SEO statistics, free tools, etc.)
- One agency's State of Link Building survey: 1,400 backlinks / 488 referring domains
- "Link building" topic: business potential 3, traffic potential ~4,400 (older figure) → justified CMO-led deep dive
- Replication filters used: 50+ referring domains, 500+ words, strong domain authority
- BREW scores business potential 0-3 ("social media marketing" = 0, "link building" = 3 for an SEO software company)

## Mistakes to avoid
- Chasing only Level 5 content — "The long run is just a collection of short runs" (Morgan Housel); you must survive the short term
- Publishing bare "here are #1, #2, #3" listicles that AI can reproduce
- Information dumping when curation is expected (and vice versa)
- Assigning work without matching skills to level (interns on Level 5, seniors on Level 1)
- Assuming prompting replaces expertise — without domain knowledge you can't write good prompts or judge if output is original vs. regurgitation

## Key principles
- The higher the level, the harder for AI to keep up — expertise is the moat.
- Levels are a portfolio: all are helpful to readers at different skill levels.
- Curation is an opinion: selecting and omitting IS the value of Level 2.
- Repurposing multiplies ROI: one Level 5 study → many Level 1-3 derivatives.
