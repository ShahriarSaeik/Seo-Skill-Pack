# 23 Beginner Blogging Tips to Get Better at Blogging

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Blogging improvement is a stack: pick a balanced niche, know your audience, publish proven demand (your own and competitors' keyword research), write readable, well-titled, hooked posts, then promote, measure, refresh, and repeat — a lot. Improvement comes from reps: commit to a "write 100 posts" project.

## Framework / Step-by-step
**Foundation (1–4):**
1. **Establish a niche** — balance three factors: passion (sustains motivation), reach (too broad = giant competition; too narrow = no ceiling; pick the middle, e.g., "bbq chicken recipes" not "recipes" or "Indian tomato chicken"), profit potential (ads appearing on niche searches = commerciality)
2. **Choose a platform** — a self-hosted CMS you control; WordPress, the most popular, powers roughly 40%+ of all websites and has tens of thousands of free plugins
3. **Know your audience** — audience dictates language, tips, and examples (budget-travel tips vs. business-travel tips for the same topic); build buyer personas
4. **Cover popular search topics** — organic search drove 53.3% of all website traffic in one older study; use question-form keyword ideas and prioritize by traffic potential, not just volume

**Writing (5–8, 18–19, 22):**
5. **Compelling headlines** — ABC formula: Adjective + Benefit + Confidence booster ("10 Amazing SEO Tips That Actually Work (From a 10-Year Veteran)")
6. **Hook intros** — PAS formula: Problem, Agitate, Solution
7. **Readability** — short sentences/paragraphs, bullets for skimmers, descriptive subheads, images to break text, simple words, write as you speak, read copy aloud when editing
8. **Detailed outlines** — intro (hook) + body (categorize and order subtopics; steal common subtopics from competing articles) + conclusion (resolve the intro's problem); outlines are the defense against writer's block
18. **Expert quotes** — interview practitioners for areas outside your expertise; find them by topic-author searches
19. **Examples** — examples substitute for long explanations
22. **Search intent** — Google the keyword, study the dominant format of top results (e.g., listicles), match it

**Growth (9, 11–13, 15–16, 20–21):**
9. **Competitors' popular topics** — pull their top pages by search traffic and write your own version of winners (one grooming brand's beard-styles page draws tens of thousands of monthly visits)
11. **Editorial calendar** — treat posts as projects with deadlines in any project tool; stick to the schedule
12. **Social sharing** — be present where people search for brands; some platforms surface content via internal search without needing a following
13. **Email list from day one** — newsletter opt-ins are people *asking* for more from you; free email provider + an on-site opt-in form is enough to start
15. **Active promotion** — quality alone doesn't grow traffic: SEO, helpful participation in communities (Q&A sites, Reddit — can spike traffic when genuinely useful; disclose your affiliation and follow each community's self-promotion rules), guest blogging for new-audience exposure (no paid links without rel="sponsored")
16. **Monitor performance** — look for insights, not just pageviews (for one large blog, original data studies were consistently the most-linked content, so it kept making them)
20. **Internal links** — pass authority between pages; use a site crawler or audit tool's internal-link suggestions to place them systematically
21. **Refresh older content** — updates can visibly spike traffic (one refreshed tools post jumped after update); run content audits to pick targets

**Mindset (10, 14, 17, 23):**
10. **Feedback before publish** — swap reviews with colleagues; doing everything alone is the classic beginner mistake
14. **Use AI for menial tasks** — meta descriptions, title brainstorming, rewording
17. **Optimize monetization** — don't stop at one revenue stream; one test across dozens of sites found display ads did NOT reduce affiliate commissions (not guaranteed for every site); layer affiliate + ads + products, test networks (RPMs vary), and disclose affiliate/sponsored relationships (rel="sponsored" on those links)
23. **"Write 100 blog posts" project** — one post per week until 100; the 100th will be unrecognizably better than the 1st; one practitioner's biggest skill jumps came from these projects

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Score your niche on passion / reach / profit; adjust toward the middle
- [ ] Build a written audience persona before writing
- [ ] Find question keywords with traffic potential for your next 10 posts
- [ ] Pull competitor top pages; shortlist their winners for your own spin
- [ ] Apply ABC to every title and PAS to every intro
- [ ] Outline every post (intro hook / ordered subtopics / conclusion)
- [ ] Check search intent (dominant format) before drafting
- [ ] Get one peer review per post
- [ ] Maintain an editorial calendar with per-post deadlines
- [ ] Add an email opt-in this week
- [ ] Share each post on social + one relevant community
- [ ] Add internal links to every new post from related pages
- [ ] Refresh old posts on a schedule (quarterly works)
- [ ] Run a monetization audit: what second stream can you add?
- [ ] Commit publicly to a 100-post writing project

## Data points & benchmarks
- 53.3% of all website traffic came from organic search (older study)
- Roughly 40%+ of all websites run on WordPress
- One grooming brand's single "beard styles" page draws tens of thousands of monthly search visits — topic selection beats volume of posts
- One test across dozens of sites: display ads did not reduce affiliate commissions
- 100 posts ≈ the practice threshold for major skill jumps

## Mistakes to avoid
- Niche too broad (unwinnable competition) or too narrow (no audience)
- Writing blind — no audience definition
- Publishing without external feedback
- Depending on one traffic source or one monetization stream
- Ignoring search intent (wrong format = no ranking)
- Never refreshing old content as your knowledge grows
- Waiting for "quality content" to promote itself

## Key principles
- Blogs grow on proven demand (keyword + competitor research), not originality alone.
- Headline earns the click; intro earns the reader; subheads keep them.
- Email is the only audience you own — start it early.
- Analytics should change what you make (double down on what earns links/shares).
- Volume with feedback loops is the training program: write 100.
