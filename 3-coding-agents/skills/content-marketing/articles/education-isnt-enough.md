# "Education" Isn't Enough: How to Market When Information Is Dirt Cheap

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Educational content was a winning strategy only because information used to be scarce. With AI making information impossibly cheap and abundant, information arbitrage is worthless — the three viable moves are offering a unique "flavor" of existing information, creating genuinely new information, or moving past education into entertainment.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Two eras:
**Information scarcity (then)** — specific info was hard to find (obscure forums, esoteric PDFs, personal blogs); content was costly; simple arbitrage (find + repurpose into accessible format) was useful and appreciated; first-mover wins; the source barely mattered. Search was signal-hunting in noise.
**Information abundance (now)** — specific answers are virtually guaranteed (even AI Overviews generate them on the fly); content marginal cost is near zero; arbitrage is worthless; everyone is a second-mover; the SOURCE is everything — trusted brands and people win; bad content looks like good content (LLM polish over garbled nonsense). Search is now signal-hunting in signal, much of it AI slop regurgitating AI slop.

Three strategies when information is dirt cheap:
1. **Offer new flavors of information** — same core facts, radically different curation/presentation/experience (like news: same events, different lenses for different audiences). "Ultimate guide to X" becomes "the SaaS founder's guide to X" or "your first 10 links" or a docuseries of actually doing it. Trade-off: narrower focus = smaller TAM, but search is increasingly zero-sum — owning a low-volume topic beats contesting a high-volume one
2. **Create new information** — experiments, hard problems, weird edge cases: information that can't be found on competitors' sites or in LLM output. Harder and costlier, but longer-lasting
3. **Move past rote information → entertainment** — education is table stakes; most consumed media is entertainment. Benefits: larger TAM (opposite of niche flavors), moat (entertainment is genuinely hard — subjective, risky, audience-dependent), quicker time-to-value (reaches people before they're even problem-aware, where there are virtually no competitors)

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Audit your content: what % is repackaging publicly available information? Assume competitors have it too
- [ ] Reflag existing guides for one specific audience or experience (role, maturity level, format)
- [ ] Add an original-data component (experiment, survey, edge-case exploration) to tentpole pieces
- [ ] Test entertainment formats where ROI is hard to calculate but TAM is large
- [ ] Build named, trusted creators — the source now matters as much as the information

## Data points & benchmarks
- Marginal cost of content ≈ zero: brands can publish dozens of articles a day for very little money (rough estimate) — which is why volume alone no longer differentiates
- "Education is only relevant when you need it" — entertainment reaches audiences pre-problem-awareness
- Established brands can milk arbitrage a few more years on authority alone; smaller brands must differentiate now

## Mistakes to avoid
- Competing as a generalist publisher on volume
- Assuming accuracy + helpfulness is differentiation — it's baseline
- Judging content quality by polish (AI makes bad content look good)

## Key principles
- Education's marketing value came from scarcity; abundance destroyed the arbitrage
- In abundance, differentiation = flavor, novelty, or entertainment
- Trust migrates to sources: brands and people, not pages
