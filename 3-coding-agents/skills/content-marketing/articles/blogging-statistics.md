# Blogging Statistics: The Benchmarks That Matter

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
A curated set of blogging benchmarks: who earns what, what content habits correlate with success, how SEO really distributes outcomes, and how AI is (and isn't) changing blogging. Figures come from assorted surveys and studies of varying age, so use them as directional planning and expectation-setting numbers and re-check source dates.

## Framework / Step-by-step
Organized by decision area — pick the benchmarks that inform the decision you're making:

**Market size & behavior**
- 600M+ blogs among 1.9B websites; ~3B posts published yearly; ~70M new posts/month on WordPress alone (estimates).
- 77% of internet users read blogs; ~26% of UK 5–18-year-olds read them.
- 71% of marketers say content marketing grew more important in the last year.
- 44% of buyers consume 3–5 pieces of content before engaging a vendor.

**Revenue & monetization**
- Most popular monetization: display ads (via ad networks), then affiliate — but high-income bloggers are 2.5x more likely to sell their own product/service; 45% of $50K+/yr bloggers sell their own product vs 8% of lower-income bloggers.
- Most profitable niche by median income: food ($9,169/mo median). Niches with the most 50K+ monthly-session blogs: food (42.8%), lifestyle (13.3%), travel (10%).
- 72% of bloggers earning $2,000+/mo use premium ad management.

**Content production & quality**
- Average post: 1,427 words, 3h51m to write; only 3% regularly publish 2,000+ words.
- 35% of bloggers spending 6+ hours/article report strong results; longer posts correlate with strong results; businesses spending $4,000+/post are ~2.6x more likely to call their strategy very successful (vs $0–$500).
- 47% of bloggers conduct original research; ~3x more bloggers work with editors than a decade ago; 76% publish educational content; 25% use video.
- Engagement drops past 7 minutes reading time; 73% of readers skim; 75% prefer articles under 1,000 words.

**SEO outcomes (older industry studies of ~1B pages)**
- 96.55% of pages got zero organic traffic; only ~1.94% get 1–10 monthly visits.
- Only 5.7% of pages reached the top 10 within a year of publication (2017 study; a newer study found a lower rate).
- The top-ranking page gets the most traffic only 49% of the time; the average top-10 page ranks for ~1,000 other keywords.
- Links: positive correlation between linking websites, traffic, and position. Word count: backlinks correlate positively up to 1,000 words then negatively; traffic up to 2,000 words then negatively.
- Readability scores show no ranking correlation.
- 74% of bloggers update old content; 66% drive traffic via SEO.

**Social & AI**
- 92% of bloggers promote via social media; short-form video is the #1 format by reported ROI (31% of marketers).
- AI usage: 43% idea generation, 29% headlines, 28% outlines, 21% first drafts, 3% complete drafts — and AI users are NOT more successful than non-users.
- 57% of content marketers have considered replacing internal/external resources with AI.

**Guest blogging**
- 64.9% of link builders use guest posting; average cost of a paid guest post: $77.80; 60% of bloggers write 1–5 guest posts/month. Warning: paid placements that pass links need rel="sponsored" (or nofollow); untagged paid links violate Google's spam policies.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Benchmark your cadence, word count, and production time against the averages before judging performance
- [ ] If monetizing: plan your own product, not just ads/affiliate
- [ ] Set expectations: the large majority (~95%+ in older studies) of pages get no organic traffic; SEO needs quality + time
- [ ] Target sub-2,000-word depth unless the SERP demands more
- [ ] Add original research or editor review as quality differentiators
- [ ] Use AI for drafts/outlines, not full automation — no success advantage observed

## Mistakes to avoid
- Assuming longer = better (link/traffic correlations flip past 1–2K words).
- Expecting quick rankings — 5.7% of pages crack the top 10 in year one.
- Reading these as causal — they're correlations and survey self-reports.

## Key principles
- Most blogs get no traffic; that's the base rate to beat with deliberate quality and distribution.
- Success correlates with time invested per post, publishing frequency, editing, and original research.
- Monetization maturity path: ads/affiliate → own products.
