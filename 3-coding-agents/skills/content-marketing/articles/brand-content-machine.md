# How One SaaS Brand Built a Content Marketing Machine

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One automation software brand grew to 2M+ monthly blog readers by (1) building a programmatic directory of integration pages targeting long-tail tool pairs, (2) publishing exhaustive "best of" roundups and tutorials mapped to reader lifecycle stages, (3) republishing content as ebooks to rank in non-Google search, and (4) constantly experimenting.

## Framework / Step-by-step
1. **Target the product's complements, not the product.** Nobody searched for the new tool — but they searched for the tools it connected. Build a directory page for every integration and every pair ("email app + CRM", "form tool + spreadsheet"). 4,403 integration pages + 38,612 pair pages drive 299K+ monthly organic visits. Ranking was hard for popular apps but near-guaranteed for obscure pairs with no competing content. Caution: templated pages only work when each page offers something genuinely useful and unique; near-duplicate pages at scale risk thin-content and scaled-content-abuse actions.
2. **Fatten thin programmatic pages with unique content.** ~500-word tested walkthroughs with screenshots per app; ~100-word descriptions for ready-made workflow templates (one "automatic daily email" template still pulls ~2,400 organic visits/mo).
3. **Prioritize blog categories by search demand.** Cover the biggest categories first (to-do apps: 46K searches/mo; CRM: 36K/mo), drill into related questions ("what is CRM") for companion posts, then follow up with narrower roundups ("best CRM for small business").
4. **Go exhaustive on roundups.** Test every tool, screenshot everything, differentiate each entry: one CRM roundup covered 25 tools; a project-management roundup ran 8,000+ words across 50 tools. 171 "best of" posts now drive ~1.1M organic visits/mo, years after publication. Long form captures more long-tail keywords and gives every featured company a reason to link back (let each one know it was featured; never pay for or trade links for placement).
5. **Match content type to lifecycle stage.** Roundups capture discovery-phase readers (pageviews); tutorials capture development-phase readers (highest customers per pageview); productivity/thought pieces retain readers and syndicate easily for backlinks.
6. **Republish as ebooks.** Bundle category posts into ebooks on ebook-store marketplaces — a second search engine where people find your brand, plus email signups and longer session time.
7. **Experiment without data.** A hastily written fix-it tutorial (forcing a public Wi-Fi login page to open) peaked at 100K+ visits/mo — beating most well-researched posts.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] List every tool/service your product integrates with; build a page per pairing
- [ ] Add unique descriptions/templates to every programmatic page to avoid thin content
- [ ] Rank software categories by search volume; write the head term roundup first
- [ ] Write the "what is X" companion article for each major roundup
- [ ] Notify every company you feature in a roundup (no link trades or payments)
- [ ] Plan three content tracks: discovery (roundups), activation (tutorials), retention (productivity)
- [ ] Bundle finished topic clusters into ebooks for marketplace search visibility
- [ ] Ship quick itch-scratch posts alongside data-driven ones

## Data points & benchmarks
(Snapshot figures from one published case study; they date quickly.)
- 2M+ monthly readers built primarily on organic search content.
- 4,403 integration pages + 38,612 pair pages = 299K monthly organic visits.
- 171 "best of" roundups = ~1.1M organic visits/mo (≈4x the directory).
- One roundup: 13,000 monthly organic visits; its "what is" companion adds hundreds more.
- One 10-minute experiment post: 100,000+ visits/mo at peak, still top-3 for its terms years later.
- Roundup depth benchmark: 25–50 tools tested, up to 8,000+ words.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Programmatic pages generated from a handful of preset sentences — thin-content risk.
- Featuring your own product in "best of" roundups without disclosure or honest ranking — destroys the independence that earns reader trust.
- Judging experiments by today's search volume — search data is historical; emerging problems show no volume until they explode.
- Only writing roundups: great pageviews, weak per-visitor conversion vs. tutorials.

## Key principles
- Rank for what your audience already searches for (your complements), then introduce yourself.
- Exhaustive research beats quick takes for compounding search traffic — but shortcuts sometimes win big.
- Different content types serve different funnel stages; a healthy program mixes all three.
- Publish, then publish again: every asset can live a second life as an ebook or syndicated post.
- Never stop experimenting; write the thing no search data supports yet.
