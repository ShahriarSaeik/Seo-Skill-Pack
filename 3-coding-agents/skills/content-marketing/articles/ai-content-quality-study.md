# Search Engines Don't Punish AI Content; They Punish Bad Content (Study of 331k Pages)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
One industry study of ~331k pages found no evidence of a binary penalty on AI-generated content: fully AI-written pages rank in positions 1–3 and get indexed. But every measure of search performance worsens gradually as AI content share rises — because quality tends to fall as AI use rises. Fear spam and scaled content, not AI.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The study answered four questions:
1. **Can fully AI-generated pages rank at the top?** Yes — a small minority of top-3 pages are 100% AI-generated.
2. **How common is AI content across the top 10?** Every position contains heavily AI-generated pages; prevalence rises gently from position 1 to 10.
3. **Does high-AI-likelihood content get indexed?** Yes, at a somewhat lower rate than low-AI content — but far from blocked.
4. **Does AI content tank after 3–6 months?** No precipitous drops found; impressions for high-AI pages were stable, just lower overall.

Interpretation framework: AI use correlates with lower quality signals (no new information, no links/images/first-hand experience, flat academic tone, factual errors). The data is correlational, but it suggests quality, not AI usage, is what performance tracks. Scaled low-value publishing can also fail on crawl economics: search engines may "burst-crawl" new pages out of curiosity, then throttle and drop them when the domain lacks authority to sustain that scale.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Judge every AI-assisted draft on information gain, not AI percentage
- [ ] Add what AI can't: first-hand experience, original data, links, visuals
- [ ] Avoid scaled publishing beyond your domain's authority to sustain
- [ ] Track whether your pages retain impressions 3–6 months after publish (the "mount" traffic graph — spike then collapse — is the failure signature)
- [ ] Use AI detection only for large-scale directional analysis, never to convict individual articles

## Data points & benchmarks
- 5.3% of top-3 ranking pages were estimated 100% AI-generated; 9% were ≥80% AI content
- Pages under 50% AI content hold 82.2% of top-3 rankings; 54.7% of top rankers are <20% AI
- Share of pages with ≥80% AI content per position: 8.4% (position 1) to 11.7% (position 10)
- Average AI content level: 27.1% at position 1 → 30.9% at position 10 (median 17.1% → 19.5%)
- Indexation rate: 49.28% for low-AI pages vs 40.35% for very-high-AI pages — 40% of very-high-AI pages still indexed
- Low/moderate-AI pages earned 2–3x the organic impressions of high/very-high-AI pages, with no cliff-edge decline over time
- Method: ~1M pages sampled from top-10 results of 100k SERPs; detection runs only on pages ≥350 words, so the analysed set (~331k pages) is smaller than the sample

## Mistakes to avoid
- Reading the correlation as suppression: sites leaning on AI are often newer/lower-authority to begin with
- Assuming detection is perfect — detectors are probabilistic, like the models they detect
- Publishing common-knowledge rewrites at scale with no links, images, or experience
- Treating initial indexing as a verdict — crawl resources can be withdrawn later

## Key principles
- There is no hard AI cutoff in ranking or indexing — only a gradual quality gradient
- Companies using an "AI content strategy" are usually running a different, worse strategy: repeated common knowledge, no experience, no information gain
- Be scared of spam and scaled content, not of AI-assisted creation
