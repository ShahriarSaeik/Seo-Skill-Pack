# Content Marketing Analytics, Without the Fluff

- **Updated**: 2026-10-05

## TL;DR
Don't try to measure everything. Track a small set of decision-useful metrics (output, traffic, share of voice, links, audience, engagement, conversions), report in under five minutes, and treat analytics as directional — not perfect attribution.

## Framework / Step-by-step
The 7 core metrics that matter:
1. **Content output** — posts/videos/emails/social published per month vs. a set goal. Consistency compounds; track in any sheet.
2. **Traffic growth** — monthly visitors from organic, email, social; filter by landing page in GA4 (or your analytics tool) to isolate content visits. LLM referral traffic is undercounted (AI tools often strip referral data).
3. **Organic share of voice** — how often you appear in search vs. competitors for target keywords. Rising SOV with falling traffic still means relative wins (e.g., everyone dropped, you dropped less).
4. **Referring domain growth** — track only for link-focused assets (data studies, tools); monthly cadence; quality over quantity. Early sites naturally have few links.
5. **Audience growth** — newsletter subscribers, YouTube/LinkedIn/podcast followers. Audience = repeat demand; traffic is fleeting.
6. **Engagement** — per-platform bundled numbers (reactions, comments, shares; email opens/CTR). Look for trends, not precision.
7. **Conversions** — leads, trials, downloads, signups, revenue contribution. Look for trends and correlations, not perfect causality.

## Actionable checklist
- [ ] Set a monthly output goal and track every published asset in a shared sheet
- [ ] Review traffic by source monthly; filter by landing page to see content-only visits
- [ ] Track share of voice for your priority keyword set alongside raw traffic
- [ ] Monitor referring domains monthly, only for link-worthy assets
- [ ] Record audience counts (email + followers) monthly
- [ ] Note engagement trends per channel; reallocate effort to where engagement lives
- [ ] Correlate traffic spikes with conversion events and document the link
- [ ] Keep reports under 5 minutes to read, written for the specific reader (CEO/manager/team), with recommendations

## Data points & benchmarks
- A $100M ARR SaaS company's monthly content report can be a short chat message — minimal reporting suffices
- Report rule: <5 minutes to read, lead with insights, bullets over prose
- Example useful framing: "Email subscribers grew 18% and convert 50% better than social traffic"

## Mistakes to avoid
- Trying to measure everything — data overload with no clarity
- Trusting bounce rate, time on site, or scroll depth as quality signals (skimming, one-visit reads, and link-heavy short posts distort them; GA4's engagement rate is a better starting point than legacy bounce rate)
- Publish-and-pray on high-effort content without multi-channel promotion
- Ignoring list hygiene when email opens decline

## Key principles
- Attribution is structurally broken (multi-device, private browsing, zero-click AI answers) — use directional indicators
- Consistency beats perfection: content results compound like a snowball
- Form follows function in reporting: anything beyond the reader's purpose is noise
- Every insight in a report should lead to an action

## Warning signs playbook
- **Stalled traffic** → re-check keyword targeting, promotion, refresh decaying posts
- **High-effort piece flops** → validate big bets early with teasers/search volume; bundle into hubs
- **Sudden traffic drop** → algorithm update, technical issue, or competitors; run a dedicated drop diagnosis
- **Email opens falling** → cadence too high, stale list, weak subject lines; clean and segment
- **Traffic up, conversions flat** → wrong audience or funnel leak; build a content→signup funnel to find the drop-off step
- **Audience plateau** → cross-promotions, guest appearances, new channels/formats, sharper hook
